# Humor and Sensitivity

## Humor rule

**Explicit first, clever second.**

The reader should understand the underlying event before the joke depends on it.

Good humor sources:
- ordinary administrative formatting applied to an extraordinary but real biblical event;
- recognizable personality meeting modern workflow;
- harmless equipment/version/reporting confusion;
- comments that slowly reveal why a reviewer is concerned;
- an unresolved thread whose silence is itself funny.

Example pattern: Moses requests replacement stone tablets and plainly explains that he broke the first pair after descending Sinai and seeing the golden calf. The comedy is the replacement-request format, not distortion of Exodus.

David can submit a worship song whose individual sections are strong but whose Psalmists reviewer cannot understand the progression. A long reviewer comment can explain the problem and remain unanswered. David does not need to deliver a punchline.

## Comments

Comments are the preferred place for freer contemporary banter. Longer comments are useful when they establish context, escalate naturally and make the joke accessible to readers unfamiliar with the biblical personality.

Do not require every funny comment to receive a response. Open/unresolved is legitimate state.

## Serious boundary

Do not use grave sin, death, sexual violence, abuse, trauma or human suffering as comedy. Examples requiring serious treatment include David/Bathsheba/Uriah, Tamar/Amnon, deaths in warfare, Absalom's death and other comparable material.

A surrounding administrative detail can occasionally be light only if it does not trivialize the harmed person or the moral weight of the account.

## Character dignity

Do not turn Thomas into a perpetual evidence joke, Jonah into nothing but reluctance, Peter into an idiot, Martha into a kitchen stereotype or David into a musician who never leads. Humor should reveal character, not replace character.

## Disciplinary/post-mortem humor

An unusual historical incident may be framed as a modern review when the underlying harm is not the joke. David's behavior before Achish at Gath can be reviewed as conduct under fear and judgment: the oddness of the method is funny, while fear and leadership decision-making remain real topics.

## Fiction boundary

Never invent a fake biblical quote for comedy. Contemporary fictional comments should be visibly part of the 2026 Data Play interaction, not presented as ancient dialogue.
