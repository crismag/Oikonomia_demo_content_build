# Style Reference — Data Play Reports

These sample reports show the approved **tone and style** for Data Play records. They are reference material, not canonical character content, and are not imported.

## How to read them

- **Record date is present day; the substance is the past.** Each report is filed in 2026. David's Ziklag report, Elijah's wellbeing report and Joshua's Ai report present lived biblical history with the immediacy of an operational report. That is intended. See "Record date versus event" in `WORLD-AND-TIMELINE.md`.
- **What to take from them:** plain operational structure (accomplishments, concerns, follow-up, prayer); honest emotion without melodrama; a biblical truth made explicit; small, character-true humor (Peter's three-seconds follow-up); restraint (Barnabas on John Mark, Priscilla and Aquila on Apollos); leadership notes that apply the history to the present church.
- **Scripture inspiration** lines point to the account the report draws on.

## When turning a sample like these into a canonical record

Write it in `RECORD-FORMAT.md` form:

- `**Oikonomia section:**` and `**Record type:**` instead of `Report Type`;
- a 2026 `**Date:**` inside the populated window (January–September 2026); a quarter summary for September–December 2026 is future planning;
- `**Visibility:**` in Oikonomia words (`Restricted` → `Named people` + `Shared with`; "Leader + Mentor" → `Named people` naming the mentor);
- ministries named as in `organization/ministries.md` ("Ministry: Kingdom Leadership", "Facilities & Restoration" and similar labels here are illustrative);
- people named as on the roster, with non-roster biblical people (Asaph, Jethro, Jezebel) kept in prose;
- `**Biblical account:**` instead of `Scripture Inspiration`, checked against the text;
- follow-up and concern lists kept in the body, with anything needing a response as `**Attention:**` or `**Action requested:**`.

# Data Play — Biblical Leadership Demo Reports

## 1. Nehemiah — Ministry Accomplishment Report

**Ministry:** Facilities & Restoration  
**Report Type:** Ministry Progress  
**Status:** Positive / On Track  
**Scripture Inspiration:** Nehemiah 2–4

Work on the Jerusalem Wall Restoration continues to make good progress. Several sections have now been completed, and the teams assigned to the gates have been especially encouraged by seeing the different families and groups working alongside one another.

One of the strongest outcomes has been the willingness of people to take ownership of the section nearest to them. Rather than treating the wall as somebody else's responsibility, families, craftsmen, priests, and community leaders have begun treating the work as a shared responsibility.

We have encountered opposition and discouraging reports from outside the project. This initially affected morale, particularly among some workers who were already tired from carrying materials and maintaining their normal responsibilities. We adjusted the work arrangement so that the teams could continue safely while remaining prepared for possible disruption.

There is still considerable work ahead, but the progress is visible and morale has improved.

**Accomplishments**

* Multiple wall sections completed or substantially repaired.
* Families and ministry teams are taking clearer ownership of assignments.
* Work continued despite opposition and discouragement.
* Communication between section leaders has improved.
* Security concerns have been incorporated into the working plan.

**Prayer / Hope**

Please pray that the people will not become discouraged as the work continues. Pray also for wisdom in responding to opposition without allowing conflict to distract us from the assignment.

My hope is that we finish not simply with a repaired wall, but with a people who have rediscovered that they can accomplish difficult things when they work together.

## 2. David — Leadership Situation and Prayer Report

**Ministry:** Kingdom Leadership  
**Report Type:** Leader Situation  
**Status:** Attention Required  
**Scripture Inspiration:** 1 Samuel 30

We returned to Ziklag expecting rest after a difficult period, only to find the city burned and our families taken captive. The loss affected everyone deeply. The men wept until they had no strength remaining, and grief quickly began turning into anger.

The situation became personally difficult when some of the men began speaking of stoning me. I understand that their words came from tremendous grief, but it was still one of the lowest moments we have experienced together.

Before deciding our next action, I needed to seek the Lord rather than respond out of fear or defend myself before the men. After prayer and seeking direction, we received encouragement to pursue those responsible.

We are preparing to move. Some of the men are exhausted, so I expect that not everyone will be able to complete the pursuit.

**Current Concerns**

* Families remain missing.
* Team morale has been severely affected.
* Several men are physically and emotionally exhausted.
* Anger within the group could damage relationships if it is not handled carefully.

**Prayer**

Lord, give us strength to pursue what has been lost without allowing anger to control us. Give me wisdom to lead men who are hurting, especially when their pain is directed toward me. Protect our families and guide us in the decisions ahead.

I am choosing to strengthen myself in the Lord before asking the men to find strength in my leadership.

## 3. Jehoshaphat — Battle and Prayer Report

**Ministry:** Judah Leadership Council  
**Report Type:** Crisis / Prayer  
**Status:** Attention Required  
**Scripture Inspiration:** 2 Chronicles 20

We received word that a large combined force is advancing against Judah. Based on the reports available to us, we do not have sufficient strength to approach this situation with confidence in our own military capacity.

Our immediate response has therefore been to call the people together and seek the Lord. Families have gathered from across Judah. The seriousness of the situation is understood, but I have also been encouraged by seeing the people respond together rather than allowing fear to scatter us.

My prayer before the assembly was simple: **we do not know what to do, but our eyes are on the Lord.**

We are still preparing responsibly for what may happen. Seeking God is not an excuse for poor preparation. However, I want our leaders and people to understand that our confidence cannot rest merely on numbers, weapons, or strategy.

**Prayer Focus**

* Protection for the people of Judah.
* Wisdom for leaders making decisions under pressure.
* Courage among families who are afraid.
* Unity rather than blame or panic.
* Clear direction regarding our response.

Whatever the outcome, I want this season remembered as one in which fear caused us to seek God together rather than turn against one another.

## 4. Elijah — Personal Ministry and Wellbeing Report

**Ministry:** Prophetic Ministry  
**Report Type:** Personal / Confidential  
**Visibility:** Restricted  
**Scripture Inspiration:** 1 Kings 18–19

The confrontation at Mount Carmel resulted in a significant public victory. The people witnessed God's answer, and there was a clear turning point in their response. Rain also returned after the long drought.

Despite these positive outcomes, I need to report honestly that I experienced a significant personal low immediately afterward.

After receiving a threat from Jezebel, I left the area and eventually reached the wilderness. The combination of prolonged pressure, confrontation, travel, and fear appears to have affected me more deeply than I recognized during the ministry activity itself.

I became exhausted and discouraged enough that I no longer wanted to continue carrying the responsibility.

The immediate need was surprisingly ordinary: rest, food, quiet, and time with God. I am learning that a major ministry victory does not automatically mean the leader is personally strong afterward.

**Current Situation**

* Physically recovering.
* Emotionally tired but improving.
* Need clarity regarding the next assignment.
* Need to avoid making long-term conclusions while exhausted.

**Prayer**

I am asking for renewed perspective and the ability to hear God without demanding that He always speak through dramatic events.

**Leadership Note**

We may need to consider how we care for leaders immediately following demanding ministry seasons. The period after a major event may require as much attention as preparation before it.

## 5. Joshua — Campaign Progress Report

**Ministry:** Israel Operations  
**Report Type:** Accomplishment / Lessons Learned  
**Scripture Inspiration:** Joshua 6–8

The initial campaign at Jericho was successful and greatly strengthened confidence among the people. However, our next engagement at Ai resulted in an unexpected defeat.

The defeat was difficult because we entered the engagement expecting it to be straightforward. Our initial assessment underestimated the situation, and we discovered afterward that there was also an internal obedience issue that had not been addressed.

This has been an important correction for the leadership team. Previous success created confidence, but some of that confidence became presumption.

After addressing the underlying issue and seeking direction again, we returned with a different strategy and successfully completed the objective.

**Lessons**

* Yesterday's victory does not replace today's dependence on God.
* Small assignments still require careful preparation.
* Hidden problems within the community can affect the wider mission.
* Failure should be investigated rather than merely blamed on individuals.
* A defeat does not necessarily mean the assignment is finished.

**Prayer / Hope**

My hope is that we become a people who can learn from both victory and defeat. We thank God for the recovery at Ai, but I do not want the successful second attempt to erase what the first defeat taught us.

## 6. Esther — Leadership Intervention Report

**Ministry:** Community Care / Advocacy  
**Report Type:** Critical Situation  
**Status:** Attention Required  
**Scripture Inspiration:** Esther 4–8

A serious threat against our people required intervention at the highest level. Approaching the king without being summoned involved personal risk, so I asked the community to fast with me before taking action.

The support of the community during those days was significant. Although I was the person who needed to enter the king's presence, the responsibility did not feel like mine alone.

Rather than presenting everything immediately, I chose a measured approach and arranged opportunities for the matter to be addressed at the appropriate time. The circumstances eventually changed dramatically, and the threat against our people was exposed.

There is still work required. A favorable decision does not instantly undo the consequences of previous decisions, so leaders must now help communities understand what has changed and what actions remain necessary.

**Thanksgiving**

* Opportunity was given to speak.
* The threat was exposed.
* Community fasting brought remarkable unity.
* Mordecai has been given greater responsibility.
* Hope has returned among the people.

**Reflection**

There are moments when leadership means accepting personal inconvenience or risk because silence would leave others without a voice.

## 7. Peter — Fellowship and Lifegroup Report

**Group:** Galilee Lifegroup  
**Report Type:** Gathering Report  
**Status:** Positive  
**Scripture Inspiration:** Acts 2

We had another encouraging gathering this week. Attendance was strong, but more importantly, people remained after the formal gathering to talk, pray, share meals, and help one another.

Several newer members participated more openly than during previous gatherings. There is a growing sense that people are not simply attending an event; relationships are forming outside the scheduled meeting.

Teaching and prayer were followed by a shared meal. A few families mentioned practical needs, and other members immediately offered assistance. We should follow up to make sure those offers actually become actions.

There was also considerable enthusiasm during the discussion. I may have answered several questions before everyone had finished speaking, so that remains an area for personal improvement.

**Positive Outcomes**

* Strong participation.
* Newer members becoming more comfortable.
* Members voluntarily supporting one another.
* Prayer participation increasing.
* Fellowship continuing beyond the scheduled program.

**Follow-up**

* Check practical needs raised by two families.
* Encourage quieter members to contribute.
* Assign hospitality responsibilities earlier next gathering.
* Peter: allow at least three seconds before answering every question.

**Prayer**

May this group continue growing in devotion to teaching, fellowship, breaking bread, prayer, generosity, and sincere care for one another.

## 8. Paul — Mission Journey Report

**Ministry:** Missions & Church Development  
**Report Type:** Ministry Journey  
**Status:** Mixed / Progressing  
**Scripture Inspiration:** Acts 16–18

The current ministry journey has included both significant opportunities and unexpected changes of direction.

Several locations we originally intended to enter did not open to us as expected. At the time this was frustrating because our plans appeared reasonable. We eventually received clearer direction toward Macedonia and adjusted the journey.

In Philippi we met Lydia and others gathering for prayer. Lydia responded positively and opened her home, which has become an important point of connection for the growing community.

The ministry also resulted in opposition. Silas and I were imprisoned following an incident involving a young woman who had been exploited for profit. The imprisonment was painful and unjust, but even there an unexpected ministry opportunity developed. The jailer and his household became receptive after the events of the night.

We have since continued through other cities. Responses remain mixed. Some receive the message eagerly; others strongly oppose it.

**Accomplishments**

* New relationships established in Macedonia.
* Lydia's household has become an important local connection.
* New believers are being gathered into communities.
* Local leaders are beginning to emerge.
* Ministry continued despite imprisonment and opposition.

**Challenges**

* Travel changes and uncertainty.
* Physical fatigue.
* Opposition in several locations.
* Need for stronger local leadership before moving onward.
* Follow-up required with communities already established.

**Prayer**

Please pray for endurance, wisdom about where to remain longer, and discernment regarding when a community is ready for greater local responsibility.

The journey has not followed the itinerary we originally expected, but the closed doors have sometimes been as important as the open ones.

## 9. Barnabas — Leader Development Report

**Ministry:** Leadership Development  
**Person:** John Mark  
**Report Type:** Development / Mentoring  
**Visibility:** Restricted  
**Scripture Inspiration:** Acts 13–15

I have continued spending time with John Mark following the difficulties experienced during the previous journey.

There are legitimate concerns regarding his earlier decision to leave the team. The impact on the mission should not be minimized, and I understand why confidence in him was affected.

At the same time, I do not believe that one failure should permanently define his usefulness.

Mark appears willing to grow and should be given meaningful responsibility with appropriate support. My recommendation is not to pretend the previous incident never happened, but to create a path through which reliability can be demonstrated again.

**Development Goals**

* Complete assigned responsibilities consistently.
* Communicate earlier when struggling.
* Build endurance during demanding ministry periods.
* Continue learning alongside experienced leaders.
* Review progress after the next ministry assignment.

**Leader Observation**

Restoration requires both grace and evidence of growth. We should make room for both.

**Prayer**

May Mark become not merely someone who recovered from failure, but someone whose recovery becomes useful to others.

## 10. Priscilla & Aquila — Lifegroup / Teaching Report

**Report Type:** Fellowship + Follow-up  
**Status:** Positive

Because he is already an effective communicator, we felt it would be better to speak with him personally rather than publicly correct him in a way that could embarrass him or distract from the good work already happening.

We plan to spend additional time together before his next journey.

* Review several teaching points together.
* Encourage his existing strengths.
* Help connect him with believers in Achaia.

Sometimes leadership development is not about correcting a bad leader. It is helping a gifted leader become more complete.

## 11. Timothy — Personal Leadership Progress Report

**Ministry:** Emerging Leaders  
**Report Type:** Leadership Progress  
**Visibility:** Leader + Mentor  
**Scripture Inspiration:** 1 & 2 Timothy

This period has involved more responsibility than I previously carried, particularly in teaching, handling difficult conversations, and helping maintain order within the church.

I am becoming more comfortable taking responsibility, although I still sometimes hesitate when dealing with older or more experienced people.

Paul has continued encouraging me not to allow my age to become the defining issue. My focus needs to remain on character, speech, conduct, love, faith, and purity rather than trying to prove authority through position.

One area I want to improve is consistency in using the gifts and responsibilities already entrusted to me rather than waiting until I feel completely prepared.

**Current Goals**

* Prepare teaching earlier in the week.
* Address difficult issues promptly but respectfully.
* Continue mentoring with Paul.
* Develop additional local leaders instead of carrying every task personally.
* Protect time for prayer and personal study.

**Prayer**

Pray for courage without arrogance, humility without timidity, and faithfulness in responsibilities that sometimes feel larger than my experience.

## 12. Paul — Difficult Ministry Report: Corinth

**Ministry:** Church Development  
**Report Type:** Pastoral Situation  
**Status:** Attention Required  
**Scripture Inspiration:** 1 Corinthians

I continue to receive reports regarding divisions within the Corinth community. People appear to be forming identities around individual leaders rather than remembering that those leaders are servants participating in the same work.

There are also several matters involving conduct, relationships, worship gatherings, spiritual gifts, and disagreements between members that require direct teaching.

This is not a report of failure alone. The community has significant gifts, enthusiasm, and potential. My concern is that giftedness without maturity can produce considerable confusion.

I intend to address the problems clearly while continuing to remind them who they are called to become.

**Attention Required**

* Divisions around preferred leaders.
* Serious relational and conduct concerns.
* Disorder during gatherings.
* Confusion regarding spiritual gifts.
* Need for greater consideration of weaker members.

**Hope**

I remain hopeful because correction itself means we have not abandoned the relationship.

They do not primarily need a more impressive church. They need to learn the more excellent way: love.

## 13. Moses — Leadership Capacity Report

**Ministry:** Community Leadership  
**Report Type:** Leadership Development / Organizational  
**Status:** Attention Required  
**Scripture Inspiration:** Exodus 18

The current approach to resolving disputes and questions among the people is no longer sustainable.

People have been waiting for extended periods while matters are brought directly to me. I initially viewed this as part of faithfully serving the community, but Jethro observed that the arrangement is exhausting both the people and me.

His assessment is correct.

We need to identify trustworthy leaders who can handle appropriate matters at different levels, while the most difficult cases continue to come to senior leadership.

This will require me to release responsibilities I have become accustomed to carrying personally.

**Proposed Actions**

* Identify capable and trustworthy leaders.
* Define levels of responsibility.
* Establish a clear escalation process.
* Teach leaders the principles required for decisions.
* Review difficult cases together.
* Evaluate the arrangement after implementation.

**Personal Goal**

Delegate without disappearing.

**Prayer**

Give me wisdom to distinguish between responsibilities I must carry and responsibilities I must equip others to carry.

**Status:** Attention Required

A new restriction has been established prohibiting petitions to anyone other than the king for a specified period.

The policy appears to have implications beyond normal government administration and directly affects my established practice of prayer.

I have decided not to alter my regular prayer practice merely to create the appearance of compliance. At the same time, I do not intend to respond provocatively or use the situation for unnecessary public attention.

**Positive Outcomes**

* An unexpected ministry opportunity emerged in prison.
* The jailer's household responded positively.

## 16. David — Worship Ministry Report

**Ministry:** Worship & Creative  
**Report Type:** Ministry Reflection  
**Status:** Positive

The worship team has been working on several new songs drawn from recent seasons of thanksgiving, fear, deliverance, repentance, and confidence in God.

I want the team to understand that worship does not require pretending every week has been good. Some of our strongest expressions of worship have come from difficult seasons when the honest starting point was confusion or distress.

We are therefore developing a collection that includes celebration as well as lament.

Several musicians have shown significant growth. Asaph has been given greater responsibility, and we are organizing musicians more intentionally rather than relying on whoever happens to be available.

**Goals**

* Develop additional worship leaders.
* Organize musicians into sustainable rotations.
* Preserve songs that can serve the wider community.
* Encourage songwriting rooted in genuine experience.
* Make room for both celebration and lament.

**Prayer**

May our worship remain honest enough to express sorrow, strong enough to remember God's faithfulness, and joyful enough to celebrate when deliverance comes.

## 18. Paul — End-of-Quarter Ministry Summary

**Period:** September–December 2026  
**Ministry:** Missions & Leadership Development  
**Report Type:** Quarterly Summary  
**Status:** Progressing

This quarter has included travel, new relationships, opposition, leadership development, difficult correspondence, unexpected changes of plan, and several encouraging signs of growing local leadership.

The most encouraging development is that ministry is increasingly being carried by people rooted in their own communities. Timothy continues growing in responsibility. Priscilla and Aquila have demonstrated strong ability to develop others. Lydia's home has become a stable gathering point. Apollos is developing into an effective teacher with greater understanding.

There remain communities requiring considerable attention. Corinth continues to present complicated pastoral concerns. Some teams are carrying fatigue from travel and opposition. We also need to become more disciplined about following up with communities after leaving them.

**Quarter Accomplishments**

* New communities established and strengthened.
* Emerging leaders identified and mentored.
* Additional homes opened for fellowship.
* Teaching resources and correspondence distributed.
* Mission teams continued despite significant opposition.

**Attention Required**

* Corinth leadership and relational concerns.
* Team fatigue.
* Consistency of follow-up.
* Leadership succession in established communities.
* Travel planning and support.

**Prayer and Hope**

My hope for the coming quarter is not simply that we visit more places. I want what has already been planted to become stronger.

I pray that the leaders we are developing will eventually do things we could never accomplish ourselves, and that we will have enough humility to consider that success rather than replacement.
