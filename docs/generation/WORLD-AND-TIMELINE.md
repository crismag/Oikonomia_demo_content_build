# World and Timeline

## World rule

All selected characters coexist in the present-day Data Play organization. Their biblical history remains real lived history; their Oikonomia records are contemporary fictional activity.

Use biblical history as memory, testimony, leadership formation and theological reflection. Do not relocate ancient miracles, wars, journeys or institutions into 2026 merely to make a modern scene.

## Time windows

### Populated/current history — January through September 2026
Create accumulated records: calendars, gatherings, ministry work, reports, goals, meetings, tasks, follow-ups, prayers, development, incidents and completed work. The demo should feel lived-in rather than freshly seeded.

### Future planning — September 2026 through December 2027
Create future schedules, plans, goals, assignments, anticipated events, milestones and open follow-ups. September is the bridge month and may contain both current records and future-facing plans.

## Whole-life memory

A September 2026 report can reflect on any point in the character's biblical life. Biblical chronology does not need to be replayed month-by-month in 2026.

## Record date versus event

**The record's date is the present-day conversation; the substance is the character's past.**

A record is dated, filed and discussed in 2026. Its content may be the character's lived biblical history reported in the immediacy of an Oikonomia report: "We returned to Ziklag and found the city burned…", "We are preparing to pursue…", "Current concerns: families remain missing." Present tense and operational framing inside the report are allowed and encouraged. The reader understands this as David filing his account of Ziklag in Oikonomia, not as Ziklag burning in 2026.

What stays out of bounds:

- 2026 settings for the ancient event (Ziklag is not a Toronto neighbourhood; the Red Sea is not on the church calendar).
- Modern details inside the ancient event (no phones at Carmel, no Production crew at the Red Sea).
- Changing what happened, who was there or how it ended.

Present-day elements — who the report is filed with, comments from colleagues, Cris's follow-up, goals, Lifegroup discussion and prayer — stay in 2026 around the account. See `STYLE-REFERENCE-REPORTS.md` for the intended tone.

## Continuity

Consequences persist. A difficult meeting should create actions; actions should later be completed, delayed or escalated; development goals should show movement; relationships should remember previous conflict; operational decisions should affect later schedules.

Do not generate a crisis and forget it.

## Density

Featured characters receive richer histories. Supporting characters can appear through attendance, shared meetings, ministry activity, comments, assignments and occasional reports. Do not create artificial daily filler.

Ordinary weeks may have concise reports. Significant weeks deserve richer treatment.

## Calendar versus report

- Calendar/activity: what happened and when.
- Gathering/meeting/ministry record: what happened inside the activity.
- Weekly/reporting record: what mattered, changed, needs attention and happens next.

Do not duplicate identical prose across all three.
