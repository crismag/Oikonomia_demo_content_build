# Character Development Guide

## Goal

Build a whole person whose Oikonomia history remains recognizable across many records. Do not generate a mascot based on one famous incident.

## Development sequence

### 1. Survey the whole biblical account
Identify major life arcs, relationships, responsibilities, victories, failures, corrections, prayers, testimony, unresolved tensions and later development.

### 2. Establish identity
Define contemporary voice, leadership posture, strengths, vulnerabilities, recurring concerns and how the person differs from nearby characters.

Examples already established:
- Moses: senior leadership, governance, overload, delegation, intercession, succession.
- Joshua: execution, operations, courage, transition from assistant to independent leader.
- David: worship, emotional honesty, leadership under pressure, relationships, grave failure, repentance, family crisis and next-generation preparation.

### 3. Map relationships
Preserve important biblical relationships and add plausible 2026 cross-era relationships where useful. Relationships should generate records, not remain a static list.

### 4. Map church roles
Assign ministries/responsibilities according to actual work and character fit. Do not force every biblical event into the character's primary ministry.

### 5. Define development goals
Goals should arise from the character's real history: habits to strengthen, failures not to repeat, people to develop, responsibilities to hand off and truths to practice.

### 6. Select record families
Choose a varied set: weekly reports, special reports, Lifegroups, mentorship, goals, meetings, schedules, prayer, testimony, relationship reports, tasks, comments, operational reviews and future plans.

### 7. Build continuity
Allow earlier records to cause later records. Track open items, changed relationships, corrected behavior, incomplete goals and recurring concerns.

### 8. Add humor last
First make the character and record true, coherent and useful. Then identify harmless administrative or personality-driven humor. Do not invent foolishness solely to make the person funny.

## Character target versus character content

`/targets/<group>/<character>.md` is the generation task: identity hypothesis, biblical coverage, roles, topic backlog, relationships, record opportunities, sensitivity and status.

`/characters/<character>/` is canonical authored content. Once a target topic is authored, the target may be checked off or marked complete; the target file is not itself imported as character content.

## Completion test

Before calling a character sufficiently developed, ask:

- Can a reader explain who this person is beyond one famous event?
- Are major biblical relationships represented?
- Are strengths and failures both visible where Scripture provides them?
- Are biblical truths explicit enough for a low-biblical-literacy reader?
- Does the person have plausible 2026 church activity?
- Are there positive, ordinary and difficult records?
- Are at least some records connected to other people/modules?
- Do open items and future plans exist where appropriate?
- Is humor subordinate to characterization and truth?
