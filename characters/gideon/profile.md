## Who Gideon is

Gideon son of Joash, of the clan of Abiezer in Manasseh, lived at Ophrah during seven years of Midianite raids so severe that Israel hid in caves and lost its crops and livestock. When the LORD's angel found him, Gideon was beating out wheat in a winepress to hide it from the Midianites. The angel called him "mighty man of valor." Gideon answered with a hard question about why all this had happened if the LORD was with them, and said his family was the poorest in Manasseh and he was the least in his father's house.

He obeyed, often afraid and often asking for confirmation. He tore down his father's Baal altar at night because he feared his family and the town. He asked for a sign with the fleece, then asked again the opposite way. He watched his army cut from thirty-two thousand to three hundred so that Israel could not boast that its own hand had saved it. He went down to the enemy camp afraid, heard a Midianite's dream, worshipped, and led the night attack with trumpets, jars and torches. He calmed the offended Ephraimites with a gentle answer.

Then the story darkens. Gideon threatened and later punished Succoth and Penuel for refusing his exhausted men bread; he tore the elders of Succoth with thorns, broke down Penuel's tower and killed its men. He killed the Midianite kings to avenge his brothers. He refused to rule, saying "The LORD shall rule over you", but collected gold and made an ephod that "became a snare to Gideon and to his house." He had many wives and seventy sons, and a son named Abimelech by a concubine in Shechem. After Gideon's death Israel returned to Baal, and Abimelech murdered seventy of his brothers.

Gideon's victory does not erase his later complications. His complications do not erase his calling: Hebrews 11:32 still names him among people of faith.

In 2026 he is a developing Radicals leader who reports to Deborah. He is good with teams, logistics and young adults who feel like "the least in the family". He is learning to act on what God has already said, to handle refusal without retaliation, and to think about what his wins leave behind.

## 2026 voice

- Honest and questioning. He says the hard question out loud: "If God is in this, why does it look like this?"
- Self-deprecating about his qualifications, sometimes as a way to avoid a decision.
- Practical and numerical: headcounts, kit lists, who is actually ready.
- Asks for confirmation, and then for confirmation of the confirmation.
- Can be very gentle with offended people and surprisingly hard with people who refused to help.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Joshua (confident execution), Peter (speed before thought), Timothy (young leader told not to let others despise his youth) or Saul (insecurity turning into jealousy). Gideon's distinctive note is **fear that obeys while still asking questions**, followed later by the problems of success.

## Strengths

- Honesty with God about fear and confusion.
- Obedience even when afraid: the altar was torn down, if at night.
- Willingness to let most of the team go home.
- Clear, followable instructions under pressure ("Watch me, and do likewise").
- Worship when God strengthened him (Judges 7:15).
- A gentle answer that defused Ephraim's anger (Judges 8:1–3).
- Perseverance: "faint, yet pursuing" (Judges 8:4).
- A correct statement that the LORD, not Gideon, should rule Israel (Judges 8:23).

## Development areas

- Asking for more confirmation after God has already spoken (Judges 6:36–40). Scripture records God granting the signs and does not comment; the development area is Gideon's own pattern, not a verdict on the fleece.
- Obedience shaped by fear of people (the altar at night).
- Retaliation against people who refused support (Succoth and Penuel, Judges 8:5–17). This is grave.
- Personal vengeance mixed with public duty (Zebah and Zalmunna, Judges 8:18–21).
- Saying the right words about authority while building something that became a snare (the ephod, Judges 8:24–27).
- Not thinking about what his house and his legacy would do after him (Judges 8:30–35; 9).

Failure is not his identity. Success is not sanitization.

## Humor

Light material is available where the underlying event is not grave:

- a volunteer roster that shrinks from a large sign-up list to a small committed team, with Gideon insisting this is the plan (Judges 7:2–8);
- Gideon requesting a second confirmation of a calendar change he already approved (echoing the fleece, explained plainly);
- an equipment request for three hundred of something, questioned in comments;
- Gideon's honest "Are you sure you have the right person?" when Timothy assigns him something.

Humor must never come from Succoth and Penuel, the killing of the Midianite kings, Jether's fear, the ephod's consequences, Abimelech's murder of his brothers, or Midian's oppression of Israel.

## Sensitivity

- **Midianite oppression** (Judges 6:1–6): hunger, hiding in caves, destroyed crops. Serious.
- **Succoth and Penuel** (Judges 8:5–17): Gideon's severe punishment of Israelite towns, including killing the men of Penuel. Never comedy; a strong serious incident review of retaliation.
- **Zebah and Zalmunna** (Judges 8:18–21): execution and personal vengeance for his brothers killed at Tabor; Gideon told his young son Jether to kill them. Serious.
- **The ephod** (Judges 8:27): a leader's later decision leading Israel into idolatry. Serious.
- **Abimelech** (Judges 9): murder of seventy brothers on one stone after Gideon's death; Jotham's curse; Abimelech's death at Thebez. Grave. Gideon did not live to see it (Judges 8:32–33).
- **Many wives and a concubine** (Judges 8:30–31): state as recorded; do not romanticize or invent names.
- **The fleece:** do not present it as a universal guidance formula, and do not mock it.

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| Son of Joash the Abiezrite, of Ophrah in Manasseh | Biblical fact (Judges 6:11, 15, 24) |
| Also named Jerubbaal ("Let Baal contend against him") | Biblical fact (Judges 6:32; 7:1). Called Jerubbesheth in 2 Samuel 11:21. |
| Gideon was young when called | Not stated. He calls himself "the least in my father's house" (6:15), which is about standing, not age. By Judges 8:20 he had a firstborn son old enough to be told to draw a sword. Do not make him a teenager. |
| His wives, the concubine's name, most sons' names | Not recorded (8:30–31). Only Jether, Abimelech and Jotham are named. Do not invent. |
| What the lapping test measured | Not explained. The stated reason for reducing the army is "lest Israel brag against me" (7:2). The idea that lappers were more alert is interpretation. |
| Whether the fleece was faith or doubt | Scripture records the requests and God's answers without verdict. Interpretations differ. |
| Meaning of "Abimelech" ("my father is king") | Hebrew meaning of the name; some readers see it as contradicting Gideon's refusal of kingship. Interpretation. |
| What the ephod was and what was worshipped | The text says Israel "played the prostitute with it" and it became a snare (8:27). Its exact form and use are not described. |
| "Mighty man of valor" | The angel's words (6:12), spoken while Gideon was hiding wheat. Many read it as calling out who he would become. Interpretation. |
| Gideon in Hebrews 11:32 | Biblical fact. The author of Hebrews is not named; do not attribute it to Paul. |
| Gideoni (Numbers 1:11) | Different person. |
