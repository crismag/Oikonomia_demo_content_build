## Names

Records use **Thaddaeus** as his roster name. He may also be referred to as **Judas son of James**, with the qualification below.

- Matthew and Mark list **Thaddaeus** (Mark 3:18). The WEB of Matthew 10:3 reads "Lebbaeus, who was also called Thaddaeus"; ancient manuscripts differ here, and many read only "Thaddaeus."
- Luke and Acts list **Judas the son of James** in the same general place (Luke 6:16; Acts 1:13).
- John records a question from **"Judas (not Iscariot)"** (John 14:22).

Most readers conclude these are one man known by different names, because the lists otherwise match. That is a harmonisation of the lists, not a statement in the text.

## Who Thaddaeus is

He was one of the Twelve. He shared what Scripture records of the Twelve together: chosen after Jesus' night of prayer (Luke 6:12–16), sent out to preach and heal (Mark 6:7–13), scattered at the arrest (Matthew 26:56), commissioned on the mountain in Galilee (Matthew 28:16–20), praying in the upper room after the ascension (Acts 1:13–14), and very probably among "the twelve" who asked the church to appoint seven men over the daily distribution (Acts 6:2–6).

### His one recorded question — John 14:21–24

At the Last Supper Jesus said that whoever has His commandments and keeps them loves Him, and that He would reveal Himself to that person. Judas (not Iscariot) asked, "Lord, what has happened that you are about to reveal yourself to us, and not to the world?" Jesus answered, "If a man loves me, he will keep my word. My Father will love him, and we will come to him and make our home with him." Thomas and Philip had asked their own questions earlier in the same conversation (John 14:5, 8).

**Interpretation:** His question may reflect an expectation that the Messiah would be revealed publicly to everyone. The text does not say what prompted it.

**Truths:** He asked about the world outside the room. Jesus' answer did not describe a public display; it described love, obedience and God making His home with the one who keeps His word. John distinguishes him carefully from Judas Iscariot, and his faithfulness should not be overshadowed by a shared name.

**2026 use:** Lifegroup questions about mission and why faith seems hidden; team discussions; a single development goal about keeping Jesus' word in ordinary work.

In 2026 he serves on Nehemiah's Facilities team. He sets up rooms, locks up afterwards and asks, occasionally, the question that makes a planning meeting stop and think.

## 2026 voice

- Quiet, courteous and thoughtful. Speaks rarely, and usually with a question.
- Asks "why" and "for whom," not only "how."
- Practical in his work, reflective in discussion.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Thomas (candid "how can we know?" and evidence), Philip (practical "show us") or Nehemiah (project drive and ownership).

## Humor

Very light and administrative only: a planning meeting that was nearly finished until Thaddaeus asked who the event was actually for. Humor never involves confusion with Judas Iscariot. Betrayal is grave, and John was careful to distinguish them.

## Sensitivity

- **Judas Iscariot's betrayal and death** are grave. Never joke about the shared name.
- Do not import the stories of other men named Judas or Jude: Judas Iscariot; Judas the brother of Jesus (Mark 6:3; Matthew 13:55); Judas called Barsabbas (Acts 15:22); Jude who wrote the letter (Jude 1:1).

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| One of the Twelve | Biblical fact (Matthew 10:3; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:16; Acts 1:13) |
| Thaddaeus and Judas son of James are the same man | Widely accepted harmonisation of the lists; not stated. |
| "Lebbaeus" in Matthew 10:3 | Manuscript variant; some manuscripts read only "Thaddaeus". Do not build on it. |
| "Judas (not Iscariot)" in John 14:22 is Judas son of James | Probable identification; John does not give his father's name. |
| "Son of James" | Luke's Greek says "Judas of James." "Son" is the usual reading; some older translations read "brother." This James is not identified. |
| Author of the letter of Jude | Not established. Jude 1:1 calls the author "brother of James," which fits Jesus' brother Judas (Mark 6:3) better for many readers, and Jude 1:17 is often read as referring to the apostles as other people. Do not treat Thaddaeus as the author. |
| Later mission (e.g. Edessa, Armenia, Persia) and martyrdom | Church tradition only. Not used. |
| What prompted his question | Not recorded. |
