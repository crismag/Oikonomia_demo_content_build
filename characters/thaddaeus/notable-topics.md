# Thaddaeus / Judas son of James — Notable Topics

Use these as source topics for later Oikonomia records; they are not prompts and do not imply exclusive ownership. Name and identification notes are in `profile.md`.

## His own record
- **Included among the Twelve under naming variants** — Matthew 10:3; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:16; Acts 1:13. Thaddaeus (Matthew, Mark) and Judas son of James (Luke, Acts) are usually taken as one man; this is a harmonisation, not an explicit statement. "Lebbaeus" in Matthew 10:3 is a manuscript variant.
- **Judas (not Iscariot) asks why Jesus will reveal Himself to the disciples and not the world** — John 14:21–24: thoughtful question about revelation/mission. Jesus answered with love, keeping His word, and the Father and Son making their home with the one who loves Him. Follows Thomas' and Philip's questions (John 14:5, 8). Identification with Thaddaeus probable, not explicit.

## Shared with the Twelve
- **Chosen after a night of prayer; sent out** — Luke 6:12–16; Mark 6:7–13.
- **All fled at the arrest** — Matthew 26:56. Shared, serious.
- **Commissioned on the mountain** — Matthew 28:16–20.
- **Present with apostolic community after ascension** — Acts 1:13–14. Upper room prayer.
- **Very probably among the Twelve who delegated the distribution** — Acts 6:2–6 (inference).

## Identity cautions
- Not Judas Iscariot (John is explicit), not Judas the brother of Jesus (Mark 6:3), not Judas Barsabbas (Acts 15:22).
- Not established as Jude the author of the letter (Jude 1:1 "brother of James").
- Later mission traditions are not Scripture.

## Strong Oikonomia surfaces
- **Lifegroup:** teaching questions about mission and why faith seems hidden from the world (attends; does not write gathering reports).
- **Facilities (2026 role under Nehemiah):** setup, lock-up and attendance; one development goal about keeping Jesus' word in unseen work.
- **Meetings:** the thoughtful "who is this for?" question in planning.
- Shared mission, prayer, attendance.

Keep source limitations explicit and avoid filling gaps with unsupported legends. Never joke about the shared name with Judas Iscariot.
