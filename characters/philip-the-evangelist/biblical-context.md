# Philip the Evangelist — Biblical Context Library

This file records the lived biblical history of Philip the Evangelist that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are Philip's memories, testimony and formation.

**This is Philip of the Seven, not Philip the apostle.** Do not merge them.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

**Verse numbering:** Acts 8:37 is absent from the earliest manuscripts and from the WEB main text. References here use standard numbering: 8:38 baptism, 8:39 caught away, 8:40 Azotus to Caesarea.

---

## Chosen among the Seven — Acts 6:1–7

When the Jerusalem church grew, the Hellenists (commonly understood as Greek-speaking Jewish believers) complained that their widows were neglected in the daily distribution. The Twelve asked the community to choose "seven men of good report, full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom" to be appointed over the work. The community chose Stephen, Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas and Nicolaus. The apostles prayed and laid hands on them.

**Fact and inference:** All seven names are Greek, which suggests the church chose men from the overlooked group; Acts does not say so. Acts records no distribution work done by Philip.

**Truths:** Philip's public ministry began with an appointment to practical care for overlooked people.

**2026 use:** More Than Enough membership; Philip's link with Stephen; care for people easily missed.

## Scattered, and preaching in Samaria — Acts 8:1–8

On the day Stephen was killed, a great persecution arose against the church in Jerusalem, and all except the apostles were scattered throughout Judea and Samaria. Saul was dragging believers off to prison. "Therefore those who were scattered abroad went around preaching the word." Philip went down to the city of Samaria and proclaimed the Christ. The crowds listened with one accord as they heard and saw the signs he did: unclean spirits came out of many, crying with a loud voice, and many who were paralysed or lame were healed. "There was great joy in that city."

**Context:** Jews and Samaritans had long-standing religious and ethnic hostility (see John 4:9). Jesus had told the apostles they would be witnesses "in Jerusalem, in all Judea and Samaria, and to the uttermost parts of the earth" (Acts 1:8).

**Truths:** A persecuted and displaced believer became a messenger rather than only a refugee. The gospel crossed a boundary many would not cross.

**2026 use:** Reach-Out across cultural lines; testimony about serving after upheaval; Lifegroup with people from different backgrounds. Stephen's death and the persecution are serious; do not describe the scattering as fortunate. No 2026 reenactment of healings or exorcisms.

## Simon the sorcerer, and the apostles' follow-up — Acts 8:9–25

A man named Simon had practised sorcery in the city for a long time. People from least to greatest called him "that great power of God." When people believed Philip's preaching about the kingdom of God and the name of Jesus Christ, men and women were baptized. "Simon himself also believed. Being baptized, he continued with Philip," amazed at the signs. When the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word, they sent Peter and John, who prayed for the believers to receive the Holy Spirit, "for as yet he had fallen on none of them." They laid hands on them and they received the Spirit. Simon offered money for that power. Peter rebuked him: "May your silver perish with you, because you thought you could obtain the gift of God with money!" and told him to repent. Simon asked Peter to pray for him. Peter and John preached in many Samaritan villages on their way back.

**Silence:** Acts does not say whether Philip was present when Peter and John arrived, or record any conversation among them.

**Interpretation:** Why the Spirit was received only after the apostles came is understood differently across Christian traditions. Whether Simon's belief was genuine is debated. Do not settle either question in Data Play.

**Truths:** Philip's work was followed up and built upon by others. A person's amazement and even baptism did not guarantee a changed heart. The gift of God cannot be bought.

**2026 use:** Reach-Out handoffs with Peter (consistent with Peter's files); discernment about impressive responses; integrity. Simon's sin and Peter's rebuke are serious.

## The desert road and the Ethiopian official — Acts 8:26–39

An angel of the Lord told Philip, "Arise, and go toward the south to the way that goes down from Jerusalem to Gaza. This is a desert." He went. There was a man of Ethiopia, a eunuch of great authority under Candace, queen of the Ethiopians, in charge of all her treasure, who had come to Jerusalem to worship. He was returning, sitting in his chariot and reading the prophet Isaiah. The Spirit said to Philip, "Go near, and join yourself to this chariot." Philip ran to him, heard him reading Isaiah, and asked, "Do you understand what you are reading?" He answered, "How can I, unless someone explains it to me?" and invited Philip to sit with him.

He was reading the passage about one led like a sheep to the slaughter and silent like a lamb before its shearer, whose life was taken from the earth (Isaiah 53:7–8). He asked Philip, "Who is the prophet talking about? About himself, or about someone else?" Philip, "beginning from this Scripture, preached to him about Jesus." As they travelled they came to water, and the official said, "Behold, here is water. What is keeping me from being baptized?" He ordered the chariot to stop; both went down into the water and Philip baptized him. When they came up, the Spirit of the Lord caught Philip away. The official did not see him again, "for he went on his way rejoicing."

**Fact and inference:** The official's name is not recorded. Whether he was Jewish by birth, a convert or a God-fearing Gentile is debated. "Candace" is commonly understood as a royal title. The tradition that he took the gospel to Ethiopia is not Scripture. Isaiah 56:3–5 promises eunuchs who hold fast to God's covenant "a memorial and a name better than of sons and of daughters"; many readers connect this to the account, but Acts does not.

**Truths:** Philip left a city full of joy for an empty road because he was sent. He ran. He asked a question before explaining. He began from the passage the man was already reading. He baptized him and then was gone, and the man's joy did not depend on Philip staying.

**2026 use:** Core of Philip's Reach-Out identity; one-to-one follow-up; question-led Bible explanation; releasing people rather than creating dependence; Goals 1–3. Dignity for the official throughout. The Spirit catching Philip away is not reenacted and not a joke.

**Cross-era note:** Isaiah is a Data Play character. Philip explained Isaiah 53 as pointing to Jesus. Isaiah has no memory of Philip. In 2026 they may discuss the passage in a teaching or Lifegroup setting; neither claims historical contact, and the record should not settle debates about how Isaiah himself understood the prophecy.

## From Azotus to Caesarea — Acts 8:40

"Philip was found at Azotus." Passing through, he preached the good news in all the towns until he came to Caesarea.

**Silence:** Acts is silent about Philip from here until Acts 21.

**Truths:** Philip kept preaching in ordinary towns between the dramatic moments.

**2026 use:** Field Ministry offsite follow-up; steady, unspectacular outreach.

## A household in Caesarea; Paul and Agabus — Acts 21:8–15

Years later, Paul's companions, including the narrator of Acts ("we"), came to Caesarea and "entered into the house of Philip the evangelist, who was one of the seven, and stayed with him." Philip "had four virgin daughters who prophesied." During their stay of some days, a prophet named Agabus came from Judea, took Paul's belt, bound his own feet and hands, and said the Holy Spirit declared that the Jews in Jerusalem would bind the owner of the belt and hand him over to the Gentiles. "Both we and the people of that place begged him not to go up to Jerusalem." Paul answered that he was ready not only to be bound but to die in Jerusalem for the name of the Lord Jesus. When he could not be persuaded, they said, "The Lord's will be done." Some disciples from Caesarea went with them to Jerusalem.

**Fact and inference:** Whether Philip himself was among "the people of that place" who begged Paul is not stated, though it is a reasonable reading. The narrator is traditionally Luke. The daughters' names, ages, mother and prophecies are not recorded.

**Silence:** Paul, as Saul, had persecuted the church after Stephen's death, the persecution that scattered Philip. Acts records no conversation about this in Philip's house. Do not invent one or supply Philip's feelings.

**Truths:** Philip was still called an evangelist decades later. His home received a mission team. His daughters prophesied, and Scripture mentions it without explanation or apology. A household can hold hospitality, grief at a warning, and submission to God's will together.

**2026 use:** Household hospitality for visiting leaders; Paul relationship (carefully); recognising spiritual gifts in the next generation without inventing the daughters' lives.
