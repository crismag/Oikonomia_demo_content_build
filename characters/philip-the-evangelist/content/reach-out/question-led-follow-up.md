## 2026-04-17 — Distribution Follow-up: Start With the Question

**Record type:** Reach-Out / First Conversation  
**Date:** 2026-04-17

After Friday distribution, an adult guest asked why Christians speak about Jesus rather than simply telling people to be good. I asked what had prompted the question before I tried to answer it. The guest said a relative had sent a passage from Isaiah about a servant who suffers, but the passage made no sense to them.

I explained that Acts records a treasury official reading that same part of Isaiah on the road to Gaza. I asked whether the official understood it; he invited me to sit with him, and I began with the passage he was already reading and told him about Jesus. I gave the guest the location of Isaiah 53 and offered a Thursday Lifegroup conversation where questions can be asked without pressure.

The guest did not give contact details and did not ask for a visit. That is respected. Andrew has been told only that a guest may arrive Thursday with a question about Isaiah; he does not need a name or a personal history to welcome them.

**Action requested:** Be ready to welcome a guest asking about Isaiah 53 — from Andrew, by 2026-04-23
**Related goal:** Goal 1 — Start where the person already is
**Biblical account:** Acts 8:26–35; Isaiah 53:7–8.

## 2026-04-24 — Handoff Check: Thursday Guest Connected

**Record type:** Reach-Out / Follow-up  
**Date:** 2026-04-24

The guest from Friday attended Thursday Lifegroup, stayed through the discussion, and asked to return. Andrew welcomed them at arrival and then left the Bible conversation to the group rather than trying to manage both roles. That is the handoff I hoped for: clear, warm, and not dependent on one person.

I will not write personal details the guest did not offer. The next step is simply an invitation to the next gathering and space for their questions. In Samaria, Peter and John later supported work that had begun through my preaching; on the Gaza road, the official went on rejoicing after I was gone. A beginning should lead people toward Christ and His church, not toward dependence on the person who first spoke to them.
