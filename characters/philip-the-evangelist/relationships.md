# Philip the Evangelist — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Jesus
Philip proclaimed the Christ in Samaria and, beginning from Isaiah, preached Jesus to the Ethiopian official (Acts 8:5, 35). Jesus is not an ordinary demo account.

### Stephen — one of the Seven
Named together among the Seven (Acts 6:5). Stephen's death set off the persecution that scattered Philip to Samaria (Acts 8:1–5).

**Silence:** Acts records no interaction between them beyond their appointment.

**2026 fiction:** Stephen is Philip's ministry head in More Than Enough. Philip serves Fridays and takes Reach-Out follow-ups from distribution. Stephen's roster comments about Philip's availability are gentle. Philip's records never treat Stephen's death as the lucky cause of his own ministry, and never mention it in routine or shared contexts (see Stephen's `profile.md`).

### The other five of the Seven (not on the Data Play roster)
Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas and Nicolaus (Acts 6:5). Prose only, if at all.

### The apostles; Peter and John
The apostles laid hands on the Seven (Acts 6:6). After Philip's preaching in Samaria they sent Peter and John, who prayed for the believers to receive the Holy Spirit and confronted Simon (Acts 8:14–25).

**Silence:** Acts does not say Philip was present when Peter and John arrived.

**2026 fiction:** Consistent with Peter's files: Reach-Out follow-up and handoff. Philip starts, Peter supports, neither takes the other's credit. Philip is a Field Ministry member under Peter for offsite follow-up. John may be the careful documenter of a handoff Philip described in three lines.

### Simon the sorcerer (not on the roster)
Believed, was baptized and "continued with Philip" (Acts 8:13), then tried to buy spiritual power and was rebuked by Peter (Acts 8:18–24). Prose only. Serious material about discernment.

### The Ethiopian official (not on the roster)
A eunuch and treasury official of the queen of the Ethiopians, whom Philip taught and baptized (Acts 8:26–39). His name is not recorded. Prose only; always with dignity.

### Paul and his companions
Stayed "some days" in Philip's house in Caesarea; Agabus' warning was given there (Acts 21:8–14). Earlier, as Saul, Paul had ravaged the church after Stephen's death (Acts 8:3), the persecution that scattered Philip.

**Silence:** Acts records no conversation between Philip and Paul, and nothing about how either regarded that history.

**2026 fiction:** Philip's home remains open to visiting leaders, including Paul. Their 2026 relationship is warm and practical: hospitality, mission updates, Reach-Out stories. Do not stage conversations about Saul's persecution for the demo; any such record needs a deliberate lead-author decision and restricted visibility.

### Luke (qualified)
The "we" narrator who stayed in Philip's house (Acts 21:8) is traditionally identified as Luke. Not explicit.

**2026 fiction:** Luke may ask Philip for careful detail about Samaria and the desert road, and receive fewer details than he hoped.

### Agabus (not on the roster)
Prophet who delivered the warning in Philip's house (Acts 21:10–11). Prose only.

### Philip's four daughters (not on the roster)
Four virgin daughters who prophesied (Acts 21:9). **Do not invent their names, ages, mother, marriages or prophecies, and do not give them Data Play accounts or 2026 dialogue.** Philip may mention his daughters generally, with evident respect for their gift.

### Philip the apostle — namesake, not the same man
Philip of Bethsaida, one of the Twelve (John 1:43–46; Acts 1:13). A separate Data Play character. Scripture keeps them distinct; some later writers confused them.

**2026 fiction:** The two Philips work in different ministries (Greeters and Ushers for the apostle; More Than Enough and Reach-Out for the Evangelist). Misrouted notifications and room bookings are harmless recurring humor that also teaches readers the difference. Neither Philip is given the other's biblical memories.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Isaiah
Philip began from Isaiah 53 to preach Jesus (Acts 8:32–35). Isaiah has no memory of Philip. They may discuss the passage together in teaching or a Lifegroup; neither claims historical contact, and the record does not settle how Isaiah understood the prophecy.

### Cornelius
Both lived in Caesarea in Acts (8:40; 10:1; 21:8), but **Scripture never connects them**. Do not imply they met historically. In 2026 they serve together in More Than Enough and in cross-cultural welcome: Cornelius knows what it is to be the outsider someone came to; Philip knows what it is to be the one sent.

### Jonah
Both were sent to people outside their own community. Jonah fled; Philip ran toward the chariot. In 2026, Jonah (a reluctant outreach leader in Field Ministry) and Philip may be paired on follow-up visits. The contrast must not become mockery of Jonah; Jonah's own file governs his characterisation.

### Andrew and Mordecai (Greeters and Ushers)
Philip hands newcomers he meets through Reach-Out to the welcome team. Andrew, also a connector, understands Philip's instinct; Mordecai wants the handoff written down.

### Matthew (MTE coordinator)
Matthew asks for Philip's Reach-Out numbers; Philip sends a story.

### Cris
Cris chases Philip's longer follow-up reports and connects Reach-Out contacts to Lifegroups. Cris does not see restricted follow-up details unless named.
