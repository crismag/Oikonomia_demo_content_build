## Who Philip the Evangelist is

Philip was one of the seven men the Jerusalem church chose when Greek-speaking widows were being overlooked in the daily distribution (Acts 6:1–6). He is named second, after Stephen.

After Stephen was killed and persecution scattered the believers, Philip went down to a city of Samaria and proclaimed the Christ. Crowds listened, unclean spirits came out, paralysed and lame people were healed, and "There was great joy in that city." Many were baptized, including Simon, a man who had amazed the city with sorcery. When the apostles heard, they sent Peter and John (Acts 8:4–25).

Then an angel told Philip to go south to the desert road from Jerusalem to Gaza. There he met an Ethiopian official, a eunuch in charge of the queen's treasury, reading Isaiah in his chariot on the way home from worshipping in Jerusalem. The Spirit told Philip to go near. Philip ran, asked, "Do you understand what you are reading?", sat with him, and beginning from that Scripture told him about Jesus. When they came to water the official asked to be baptized, and Philip baptized him. The Spirit caught Philip away; the official "went on his way rejoicing." Philip preached through the towns from Azotus to Caesarea (Acts 8:26–40).

Years later Paul's travelling party stayed "some days" in Caesarea in "the house of Philip the evangelist, who was one of the seven." He had four unmarried daughters who prophesied. During that stay the prophet Agabus warned that Paul would be bound in Jerusalem (Acts 21:8–14). Philip is the only person in the New Testament called "the evangelist."

**He is not Philip the apostle**, one of the Twelve from Bethsaida (John 1:43–46), who is a separate Data Play character. Scripture keeps them distinct: in Acts 8 the apostles stayed in Jerusalem while this Philip went to Samaria, and Acts 21:8 identifies him as "one of the seven."

In 2026 he does Reach-Out: the follow-up with one person who asked a question at distribution, on a Sunday or in the neighbourhood. He serves in More Than Enough, joins Field Ministry for offsite follow-up visits and leads a weekday Lifegroup. His home is open to visiting leaders.

## 2026 voice

- Brisk, warm and unhurried with the person in front of him, even though he is always on his way somewhere.
- Starts with questions. "What are you reading? Does it make sense to you?" He begins where the other person is.
- Reports in stories of one person at a time, with enough detail to follow up, and then moves on.
- Glad to hand work to others. He mentions who picked something up after him and doesn't need the credit.
- Sends short updates from wherever he is and forgets to file the longer ones until reminded.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Philip the apostle (practical calculations, "come and see", honest questions about who Jesus is), Stephen (steady, exact, long view), Peter (quick and physical) or Paul (argued correspondence). Philip the Evangelist is movement, openness and the next conversation.

## Strengths

- Turned a scattering caused by persecution into proclamation (Acts 8:4–5).
- Willing to cross a long-standing ethnic and religious divide into Samaria.
- Responsive to direction, including leaving a city full of joy for a desert road (Acts 8:26–27).
- Personal, question-led explanation of Scripture to one person (Acts 8:30–35).
- Released people well: the official went on rejoicing without him; Peter and John built on his work in Samaria.
- Faithful over the long term: still known as an evangelist and still one of the Seven decades later (Acts 21:8).
- A household that hosted a travelling mission team and whose daughters prophesied (Acts 21:8–9).

## Development areas

Scripture records no failure by Philip. Do not invent one. 2026 development grows out of his gifts and roles.

- **Leaving before the paperwork.** Data Play fiction: his speed means he moves on before follow-up records are complete. Reach-Out depends on the record surviving the conversation.
- **Being hard to schedule.** Data Play fiction: MTE rosters need him on fixed days, and he is often elsewhere.
- **Discernment about impressive responses.** Simon the sorcerer believed and was baptized and "continued with Philip," amazed at the signs, but later tried to buy the power to give the Spirit (Acts 8:13, 18–24). Scripture does not criticise Philip. It is a reasonable lesson for any evangelist that enthusiasm is not the same as a changed heart, and that others (here Peter) may see what the first person did not.

## Humor

Light material is available where nothing grave is involved:

- **Name routing.** Notifications, room bookings and roster messages meant for Philip the apostle reach Philip the Evangelist and vice versa. Andrew has started adding "(Greeters)" or "(MTE)" to every message. This is harmless and keeps the distinction clear for readers.
- **Always on the road.** Stephen's roster comments about Philip's availability; Cris chasing a Reach-Out report Philip "definitely filed."
- **Running.** Philip ran to the chariot (Acts 8:30). Volunteers in 2026 may ask him to walk in the hallway.

Humor must not come from the Ethiopian official's being a eunuch, from his nationality, from the persecution that scattered Philip, or from Stephen's death. The Spirit catching Philip away (Acts 8:39) is not a gimmick for jokes about Philip disappearing, and it is never reenacted.

## Sensitivity

- **Stephen's death and the persecution** (Acts 8:1–4) preceded Philip's Samaria ministry. Do not describe the scattering as a lucky break.
- **The Ethiopian official.** Treat his status as a eunuch with dignity; it is not comedy and not a subject for speculation. Do not exoticise his nationality. His name is not recorded; do not invent one.
- **Samaritans.** Samaritans and Jews had long hostility. Do not caricature either group.
- **Simon the sorcerer** is serious material about trying to buy spiritual power; Peter's response was a severe rebuke and a call to repent.
- **Philip's daughters.** Four unmarried daughters who prophesied (Acts 21:9). Their names, ages, mother and the content of their prophecies are not recorded. Do not invent them, do not give them Data Play accounts, and do not write them as a comic household.
- **Paul in Philip's house.** Paul, as Saul, had ravaged the church after Stephen's death, the persecution that scattered Philip (Acts 8:1–5). Scripture records no conversation about this in Caesarea. Do not invent one.

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| One of the Seven; "the evangelist"; house in Caesarea; four virgin daughters who prophesied | Biblical fact (Acts 6:5; 21:8–9) |
| Distinct from Philip the apostle | Strongly supported by the text (Acts 8:1, 5, 14; 21:8). Some later Christian writers confused the two. Data Play keeps them separate. |
| Philip lived in Caesarea for many years between Acts 8 and Acts 21 | Reasonable inference. Acts 8:40 has him reach Caesarea and Acts 21:8 finds him with a house there; the intervening years are not described. |
| Philip was a Hellenist (Greek-speaking Jew) | Reasonable inference from his Greek name and the context of Acts 6; not stated |
| Philip personally did distribution work | Inference from his appointment; Acts records no distribution work by him |
| Why the Samaritan believers did not receive the Spirit until Peter and John prayed (Acts 8:14–17) | Theological interpretation varies across traditions. Do not settle it. |
| Whether Simon the sorcerer's faith was genuine | Debated. Acts records that he believed and was baptized, then Peter's rebuke and Simon's request for prayer. |
| The Ethiopian official was a Jew, a convert or a God-fearing Gentile | Debated. Acts says he had come to Jerusalem to worship. |
| "Candace" is a royal title rather than a personal name | Common interpretation, not stated |
| Acts 8:37 (a verse in some later manuscripts giving a confession of faith by the official before his baptism) | Absent from the earliest manuscripts and from the WEB main text. Do not quote it as Scripture. References in these files use standard verse numbering: 8:38 baptism, 8:39 caught away, 8:40 Azotus to Caesarea. |
| The official took the gospel to Ethiopia | Church tradition, not Scripture |
| Philip's wife | Not mentioned. Four daughters imply a family history, but Scripture says nothing about a wife; do not invent one. |
| The "we" narrator at Philip's house is Luke | Traditional identification, not explicit |
| Philip met Cornelius, who also lived in Caesarea | Not recorded. Do not assume. |
| Later life, travels and death of Philip or his daughters | Church tradition, not Scripture |
