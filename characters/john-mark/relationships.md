# John Mark — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Barnabas
Mark's cousin (Colossians 4:10). Barnabas and Saul brought Mark from Jerusalem (Acts 12:25); he served as their attendant (Acts 13:5). When Paul refused to take Mark again, Barnabas insisted, separated from Paul, and took Mark to Cyprus (Acts 15:37–39).

**Silence:** What Barnabas said to Mark, and what they did in Cyprus, are not recorded.

**2026 fiction:** Mark reports to Barnabas as head of Mentorship. Barnabas gives him real responsibility with honest review (consistent with the Barnabas style sample in `STYLE-REFERENCE-REPORTS.md`). Because they are family, Barnabas involves a second Mentorship leader in Mark's reviews. Mark is grateful and does not use the relationship to avoid accountability.

### Paul
Mark travelled with Barnabas and Saul and left them at Perga (Acts 13:13). Paul would not take "someone who had withdrawn from them in Pamphylia, and didn't go with them to do the work" (Acts 15:38). Later Mark was with Paul, who told the Colossians to welcome him and named him a fellow worker and a comfort (Colossians 4:10–11; Philemon 24). Paul later asked Timothy to bring Mark, "for he is useful to me for service" (2 Timothy 4:11).

**Silence:** No conversation between Paul and Mark about Perga is recorded, nor any statement that Paul changed his mind about his original decision.

**2026 fiction:** Paul asks for Mark by name on specific work (Paul's Goal 3). Mark does not ask Paul to say he was wrong. Their working relationship is practical and respectful.

### Peter
After his release from prison, Peter went to the house of Mary, Mark's mother, where believers were praying (Acts 12:12). 1 Peter sends greetings from "Mark, my son" (1 Peter 5:13).

**Qualification:** That the Mark of 1 Peter is John Mark is probable, not explicit. The tradition that Mark's Gospel draws on Peter's preaching is not Scripture.

**2026 fiction:** Consistent with Peter's files: Peter treats Mark as a son in the faith and a developing leader, and does not define him by his withdrawal; Peter knows how failure feels. Mark serves in Field Ministry, which Peter heads.

### Luke
Named alongside Mark among Paul's fellow workers (Philemon 24) and in the same greetings (Colossians 4:10, 14). When Paul asked for Mark, "Only Luke" was with Paul (2 Timothy 4:11).

**2026 fiction:** Luke coordinates Field Ministry schedules and follow-up. Mark carries out practical assignments Luke schedules. Luke's careful records and Mark's brief ones create gentle friction.

### Aristarchus
Named with Mark in Colossians 4:10 and Philemon 24.

**2026 fiction:** Two quiet helpers who were in the same letters. Occasional shared practical work with Facilities for offsite services.

### Timothy
Paul asked Timothy to bring Mark (2 Timothy 4:11).

**2026 fiction:** Timothy and Mark are both in Mentorship. Mark is not "the other young leader"; Scripture gives no age for him. They respect each other's different stories.

### Silas
Took Mark's place, in effect, as Paul's companion after the separation (Acts 15:39–40). No recorded interaction between them.

**2026 fiction:** No rivalry. If Silas' probable connection to Peter's letter (1 Peter 5:12) is used alongside "Mark, my son," both identifications must stay qualified.

### People not on the Data Play roster
Refer to these in prose only: Mary, his mother; Rhoda; Demas; Jesus called Justus (Colossians 4:11).

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Joshua and Elisha
Both began as assistants to greater leaders before carrying responsibility themselves. Useful Mentorship Night conversations about serving well in a supporting role.

### Jonah
Both left an assignment early; their stories are different and must not be merged. Jonah fled a direct call from God; Scripture gives no reason for Mark's departure and does not describe it as fleeing God. Any 2026 conversation should respect both differences.

### Cris
Cris tracks Mark's Field Ministry assignments and asks for completion notes. Cris cannot see Mark's restricted development reports unless named.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Mark / Barnabas:** restricted development plan, quarterly reviews, Mark's own progress entries.
- **Mark / Paul:** a specific assignment Paul requests; Mark's completion note; Paul's brief comment that he was useful.
- **Mark / Peter:** Field Ministry support on a visit; Peter's encouragement.
- **Mark / Luke:** schedule and follow-up coordination; a comment thread where Luke asks for one more detail.
- **Mark / Paul / Barnabas / Peter:** the lightly humorous comment thread where three leaders request Mark for the same Saturday.
