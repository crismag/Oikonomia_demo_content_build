# John Mark — Notable Topics

Use these as source topics for later Oikonomia records; they are not prompts and do not imply exclusive ownership. Fuller retellings and fact/inference notes are in `biblical-context.md`. Scripture's account of Mark is short; keep the topic map honest to that.

## Family and early context
- **Mother's Jerusalem home used by believers** — Acts 12:12: community/hospitality context. The church prayed there for Peter while James had just been killed. Father not mentioned.
- **Cousin of Barnabas** — Colossians 4:10. Which parent connects them is not stated.

## Setback
- **Travels with Barnabas and Saul as helper** — Acts 12:25; 13:5. "Attendant": a supporting role on an experienced team.
- **Leaves team at Pamphylia and returns to Jerusalem** — Acts 13:13: setback/withdrawal. Reason not recorded; do not supply one.
- **Paul refuses to take him again; Barnabas insists** — Acts 15:36–40: development disagreement affecting teams. Mark's own words are not recorded; Scripture does not say who was right.
- **Sails to Cyprus with Barnabas** — Acts 15:39. A new assignment with the leader who advocated for him.

## Restoration and usefulness
- **With Paul again; the Colossians told to receive him** — Colossians 4:10–11. Named among fellow workers "who have been a comfort to me."
- **Named among Paul's fellow workers** — Philemon 24, alongside Aristarchus, Demas and Luke.
- **Paul asks for him: "useful to me for service"** — 2 Timothy 4:11. Mark is not with Paul at this point; Paul asks Timothy to bring him. Restoration must become visible.
- **Relationship with Peter** — 1 Peter 5:13: later ministry connection. "Mark, my son" is probably John Mark, not explicitly.

## Tradition (not Scripture)
- **Gospel of Mark and Peter's preaching** — early church tradition; the Gospel is anonymous. Use cautiously and label.
- **The young man who fled (Mark 14:51–52)** — speculation only; do not use as Mark's memory.

## Strong Oikonomia surfaces
- **Mentorship:** restricted development plan with Barnabas; quarterly reviews; honest progress entries on his goals.
- **Field Ministry (member):** practical support on visits and offsite services under Peter, with Luke coordinating; brief, accurate task updates.
- **Requested assignments:** Paul or Peter asking for Mark by name; completion evidence.
- **Personal development:** finishing an assignment or saying early that he can't; not being defined by other leaders' disagreement.
- **Testimony:** a restrained account of leaving, being refused, being taken on again, and becoming useful, without invented motives.
- Never freeze Mark at his early departure.
