## Who John Mark is

John, also called Mark, grew up in the orbit of the Jerusalem church. His mother Mary had a house where many believers gathered to pray the night Peter was released from prison. He was the cousin of Barnabas. Barnabas and Saul brought him with them from Jerusalem to Antioch, and he went with them on their first journey as their attendant, a helper. At Perga in Pamphylia he left and went back to Jerusalem. Scripture does not say why.

When Barnabas wanted to take Mark on the next journey, Paul refused because Mark had withdrawn from the work. The disagreement was so sharp that Paul and Barnabas separated, and Barnabas took Mark to Cyprus. Years later Mark was with Paul, sending greetings to Colossae and to Philemon, and Paul had told the Colossians to welcome him if he came. At the end Paul asked Timothy to bring Mark, "for he is useful to me for service." Peter's first letter sends greetings from "Mark, my son," very probably the same man.

His story is not "the one who quit." It is a person whose early withdrawal was taken seriously by one leader and not treated as final by another, and who became trusted by both Paul and Peter.

In 2026 he is a developing leader. He attends Mentorship Night, reports to Barnabas, and serves on Field Ministry visits and offsite services under Peter's ministry, where Luke coordinates the schedule. He is reliable on practical assignments and is learning to take on more, and to say early when something is beyond him.

## 2026 voice

- Quiet, concise and concrete. Reports what was done, what wasn't, and why, in a few lines.
- Honest about limits without long explanation or self-criticism.
- Doesn't relitigate the past. If Pamphylia comes up, he acknowledges it plainly and moves to what he is doing now.
- Pays attention to practical detail: who needs a ride, what the visit needs, what was left behind.
- Respectful of both Barnabas and Paul, and does not take sides between them.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Barnabas (warm advocacy), Paul (argument), Peter (quick, first to speak), Luke (orderly documentation) or Timothy (a younger leader negotiating authority). Scripture gives no age for Mark; do not write him as young by default.

## Strengths

- Willingness to serve in practical, supporting roles ("attendant"; "useful to me for service").
- Endurance of a long restoration: trusted again by the leader who had refused him.
- Connection to the praying Jerusalem church and to Peter.
- Able to work in more than one team: Barnabas', Paul's and Peter's.
- Humility to be commended by others rather than defend himself.

## Development areas

- Early in his story he withdrew from an assignment before it was finished (Acts 13:13; 15:38). Reasons are not recorded; the 2026 development area is finishing, or saying early that he can't.
- Being the subject of other leaders' disagreement, and not letting that become his identity.
- Taking initiative beyond assigned tasks as trust grows.

His early withdrawal is not his identity. His later usefulness does not mean it didn't matter.

## Humor

Light material is limited and should come from ordinary service:

- being the person everyone asks to bring something, and actually bringing it;
- a comment thread where Paul, Barnabas and Peter all claim to have requested Mark for the same Saturday;
- keeping reports so brief that Luke adds a clarifying comment.

Humor must not come from his departure at Perga, the Paul–Barnabas separation, or the danger and persecution his family's house was praying through (Acts 12).

## Sensitivity

- **The departure at Perga** is not to be explained with invented reasons (fear, homesickness, disagreement, illness). Scripture is silent.
- **The Paul–Barnabas separation** was about him but not caused only by him. Do not make Mark carry blame for two leaders' disagreement, and do not make either leader a villain.
- **Acts 12** includes James' execution and Peter's imprisonment; the prayer meeting at Mary's house is serious, even if Rhoda's joy is gently light in Peter's files.
