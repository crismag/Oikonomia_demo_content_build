# John Mark — Biblical Context Library

This file records John Mark's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. Scripture gives a short, scattered account: seven passages, one of them a probable identification. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are his memories, setback, restoration and service.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked. Verse numbers follow standard English numbering.

---

## His mother's house: the church praying for Peter — Acts 12:1–17

King Herod killed James the brother of John with the sword, then arrested Peter during the Passover season and held him under heavy guard, while the church prayed earnestly for him. After an angel led Peter out of prison, he went "to the house of Mary, the mother of John who was called Mark, where many were gathered together and were praying." A servant girl named Rhoda recognised his voice and ran in to tell them without opening the gate. They said she was out of her mind. Peter kept knocking; when they opened, they were amazed. He told them how the Lord had brought him out, asked them to tell James and the brothers, and left.

**Silence:** Whether Mark was present that night is not stated. His father is not mentioned.

**Inference:** A house with a gate and a servant suggests some means; not stated.

**Truths:** Mark's family home was a place where the church gathered to pray in danger and grief.

**2026 use:** Testimony about the praying community he came from; the Peter relationship. James' death is serious. Rhoda's joy belongs to Peter's files and is gentle only apart from that grief.

## Brought from Jerusalem; attendant on the first journey — Acts 12:25; 13:1–5

After delivering famine relief to Jerusalem, "Barnabas and Saul returned… also taking with them John who was called Mark." When the Antioch church sent Barnabas and Saul out, they sailed to Cyprus and preached in the synagogues at Salamis. "They also had John as their attendant."

**Interpretation:** "Attendant" (a helper or assistant) does not specify his tasks.

**Truths:** Mark began in a supporting role on a team led by more experienced people, one of them his cousin.

**2026 use:** Service roles, Field Ministry support, what it means to be brought along.

## Leaving at Perga — Acts 13:13; 15:38

From Paphos, "Paul and his company set sail from Paphos and came to Perga in Pamphylia. John departed from them and returned to Jerusalem." Later Paul described this as having "withdrawn from them in Pamphylia," saying he "didn't go with them to do the work."

**Silence:** Acts gives no reason. Suggested reasons (fear of the road ahead, homesickness, disagreement over Paul's leadership or the Gentile mission, illness) are all speculation. Do not put any of them in a record as fact, including in Mark's own voice.

**Truths:** The departure had consequences for the team and for trust. Paul regarded it as leaving the work, and Scripture reports his view plainly.

**2026 use:** Restricted personal development; Goal 1. Mark can acknowledge that he left without narrating reasons Scripture does not give.

## The disagreement about him — Acts 15:36–41

When Paul proposed revisiting the churches, "Barnabas planned to take John, who was called Mark, with them also." Paul thought it unwise to take someone who had withdrawn in Pamphylia. "Then the contention grew so sharp that they separated from each other. Barnabas took Mark with him and sailed away to Cyprus," while Paul chose Silas.

**Silence:** Mark's own words or reaction are not recorded. Acts does not say who was right.

**Truths:** Two leaders Mark respected disagreed about him in a way that divided a partnership. Mark was given another assignment, with Barnabas.

**2026 use:** Mentorship readiness conversations, being the subject of disagreement, Goal 3.

## With Paul again; "receive him" — Colossians 4:10–11; Philemon 24

From imprisonment, Paul wrote to Colossae: "Aristarchus, my fellow prisoner, greets you, and Mark the cousin of Barnabas (concerning whom you received instructions, 'if he comes to you, receive him')." Paul named him among "my only fellow workers for God's Kingdom who are of the circumcision, men who have been a comfort to me." In his letter to Philemon Paul sent greetings from "Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, my fellow workers."

**Silence:** Who gave the instructions about receiving Mark, and when Mark rejoined Paul, are not stated.

**Truths:** The man Paul once refused was later with him, called a fellow worker and a comfort, and commended ahead to another church.

**2026 use:** Restoration made visible; commendation; Goal 2.

## "Useful to me for service" — 2 Timothy 4:9–11

Near the end of his last letter, after saying that Demas had deserted him and others had gone elsewhere, Paul wrote: "Only Luke is with me. Take Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful to me for service."

**Uncertainty:** 2 Timothy's authorship is widely disputed. Data Play uses it as Paul's words.

**Truths:** At the end, Paul asked for Mark by name, because of his usefulness in service, the very thing in question at Perga.

**2026 use:** Testimony, Goal 2, Paul relationship.

## "Mark, my son" — 1 Peter 5:13

Peter's first letter closes: "She who is in Babylon, chosen together with you, greets you. So does Mark, my son."

**Interpretation:** That this is John Mark is probable, not explicit. "My son" is a figurative, spiritual relationship. "Babylon" is commonly understood as Rome.

**Tradition:** Early church tradition connects Mark with Peter's preaching and names him as the author of the Gospel of Mark. The Gospel itself is anonymous.

**Truths:** Mark was valued by both of the most prominent apostles in Acts.

**2026 use:** The Peter relationship; Field Ministry under Peter; Mentorship.
