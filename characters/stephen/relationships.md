# Stephen — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

Scripture records few named relationships for Stephen. Do not invent friendships, family or private conversations to fill the gap.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Jesus
Stephen was accused of saying Jesus of Nazareth would destroy the temple (Acts 6:14). Before his death he saw "the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God" and prayed, "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit!" (Acts 7:56, 59). Jesus is not an ordinary demo account. Stephen refers to Him in prayer, teaching and rare testimony; Jesus does not receive tasks, comments or reports.

### The Twelve — including Peter and Matthias
The Twelve called the community together, proposed choosing seven men, and prayed and laid hands on those chosen (Acts 6:2–6).

**Inference:** Acts says "the twelve," so Peter and Matthias (counted with the eleven in Acts 1:26) were presumably among them. Acts records no individual conversation between Stephen and Peter or Matthias.

**2026 fiction:** Stephen reports to Peter. Peter trusts him with MTE and does not micromanage it. Stephen brings fairness concerns to Peter plainly, and Peter listens (his own Goal 1). Matthias and Stephen share the experience of entering responsibility through a community's selection and prayer.

### Philip the Evangelist — one of the Seven
Named second in the list of the Seven (Acts 6:5). Philip later preached in Samaria after the scattering that followed Stephen's death (Acts 8:4–5), and was still known decades later as "one of the seven" (Acts 21:8).

**Silence:** Acts records no interaction between Stephen and Philip beyond their shared appointment.

**Qualification:** This is not Philip the apostle.

**2026 fiction:** Philip reports to Stephen as a member of More Than Enough. Stephen values Philip's instinct for the one person nobody else noticed at distribution and hands him Reach-Out follow-ups. Philip is harder to schedule than anyone on the team. Stephen does not use Philip's later ministry as commentary on his own death.

### The other five of the Seven (not on the Data Play roster)
Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas and Nicolaus, "a proselyte of Antioch" (Acts 6:5). Nothing else is recorded about them. Refer to them in prose only, if at all.

### Saul (Paul)
The witnesses laid their garments at the feet of "a young man named Saul," and "Saul was consenting to his death" (Acts 7:58; 8:1). Saul then ravaged the church (Acts 8:3). After his conversion Paul named this memory himself: "When the blood of Stephen, your witness, was shed, I also was standing by, consenting to his death, and guarding the cloaks of those who killed him" (Acts 22:20).

**Silence:** Scripture records no encounter between Stephen and Paul after Stephen's death, and nothing of Stephen's response to Paul. Stephen's prayer, "Lord, don't hold this sin against them!" (Acts 7:60), was for those killing him; do not use it as a recorded personal pardon of Paul or as a neat resolution.

**2026 fiction and handling rules:**
- Both serve in the same church. Their ordinary 2026 contact is professional and unremarkable: Titus and Epaphroditus report to Paul and serve in More Than Enough under Stephen, so MTE rosters and requests may involve Paul's delegates without any reference to the past.
- No shared, routine or comment-thread record mentions Acts 7 in connection with the two of them.
- Never comedy, never irony, never a "who guarded the coats" line, never a reconciliation scene staged for the demo.
- Any record that addresses this history requires a deliberate decision by the lead author. If written, it is restricted (`Only me`, or `Named people` naming the other person and at most one pastoral leader such as Peter or Barnabas), keeps the full moral weight of what Saul did, and does not invent what Stephen felt or said historically.
- Cris does not see such a record unless named.

### Opponents and the council (prose only)
Men of the synagogue of the Libertines, Cyrenians, Alexandrians, and those of Cilicia and Asia; the false witnesses; the high priest and council (Acts 6:9–7:1). They are not Data Play characters and are not written as 2026 people.

### The devout men who buried him (prose only)
"Devout men buried Stephen and lamented greatly over him" (Acts 8:2). Unnamed. This is after his death and is not his memory.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Moses, Aaron, Joshua, David and Solomon
Stephen retold their history before the council (Acts 7:17–47). **None of them has any memory of Stephen, and Stephen knows them only through Scripture.** In 2026, Moses may learn that Stephen summarised his life, including being refused as "a ruler and a judge," and find that it names something true about his early failure in Egypt. Aaron and the golden calf, and Solomon and the temple, are not banter material with Stephen. The context of the speech (his trial) keeps these connections serious and infrequent.

### Isaiah
Stephen quoted Isaiah 66:1–2 about heaven as God's throne. Isaiah has no memory of Stephen. A quiet teaching conversation is possible; neither claims historical contact.

### Martha (Victuals Ministry head)
Coordination between food served at church (Victuals) and food given away (MTE): shared suppliers, surplus handoffs, kitchen use on distribution days. Neither takes over the other's ministry.

### Matthew (MTE coordinator)
Matthew keeps the figures; Stephen asks who the figures leave out. Useful, affectionate friction in MTE reports and comments.

### Titus and Epaphroditus
Paul's delegates serving in MTE. Titus as a leader in the ministry, Epaphroditus as a member. Stephen treats them as his own team; their reporting line to Paul is simply organisational.

### Cornelius and Simon the Zealot
MTE members. Cornelius brings a generous giver's instinct and a Gentile newcomer's eye for who is missed; Simon the Zealot brings intensity that Stephen channels into steady work. Keep Simon the Zealot's identity sparse, as his own file does.

### Barnabas (Mentorship head)
Barnabas sold a field and brought the money to the apostles in the same community whose distribution Stephen was later appointed to (Acts 4:36–37). Acts records no contact between them. In 2026 Barnabas may help Stephen develop MTE volunteers into leaders.

### Cris
Cris follows up MTE attention items (shortages, volunteer gaps, pickup scheduling). Cris cannot see Stephen's restricted reflections unless named.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Stephen / Peter:** a fairness concern raised in the weekly MTE report that Peter takes to leadership.
- **Stephen / Matthew:** figures versus faces; a distribution report where the numbers improved but one group was still missed.
- **Stephen / Philip the Evangelist:** Reach-Out handoffs from distribution; roster comedy about Philip's availability.
- **Stephen / Martha:** Victuals-MTE surplus coordination meeting.
- **Stephen / Titus / Epaphroditus:** volunteer leadership development inside MTE.
- **Stephen / Paul:** no routine records. Lead-author decision only.
