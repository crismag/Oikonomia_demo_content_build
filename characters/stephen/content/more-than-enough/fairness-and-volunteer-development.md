## 2026-04-15 — Distribution Review: A Good Total Is Not Yet a Fair Result

**Record type:** Ministry / Distribution Review  
**Date:** 2026-04-15  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Follow-up Required  
**Tags:** #serious #compassion #discernment #accountability

Wednesday's distribution met its overall supply target. That is good news, but it is not the whole report. A small number of guests arrived after the first explanation of the line and did not understand where to begin. They were eventually served, but only after another guest pointed them toward a volunteer. The issue was not a shortage of food. It was that our instructions assumed people already knew the process.

The church in Jerusalem faced a complaint that Greek-speaking widows were being overlooked in the daily distribution. The apostles did not ask whether the overall quantity was sufficient; they changed the care structure. We will do the same at the scale of this problem: clear entry-point signs, one volunteer assigned to welcome and explain, and a check at the end of each line for anyone waiting apart from it.

Philip the Evangelist noticed the guests first and offered to make the initial connection after service. That is the kind of attention we want on this team. It should not depend on one observant person happening to be nearby.

### Changes agreed

- A welcome volunteer will be named before each distribution opens.
- Matthew will add an anonymous "needed help finding the line" count to the debrief sheet.
- Philip may offer Reach-Out follow-up only when a guest asks; receiving food is never made conditional on a conversation.

**Attention:** Review whether the new entry-point process is working after two Wednesday distributions.

## 2026-07-08 — Building a Lead Who Does Not Need Me Beside Them

**Record type:** Ministry / Volunteer Development Report  
**Date:** 2026-07-08  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Progressing  
**Tags:** #reflective #gratitude #discernment #faith

Titus led today's distribution briefing and closeout while I remained available but did not step in. He opened with a short prayer, assigned the welcome role before the doors opened, and paused the team at the end to confirm that the receipt envelope and debrief sheet had both been handed over. The day was orderly because the work was shared, not because I carried every detail.

The Seven were chosen precisely because one group could not carry the daily service alone. Scripture does not describe each of us doing the same task, but it does show a community choosing trusted people and setting them before the apostles. I want that same kind of visible trust here: people who know what they are responsible for, and a ministry that remains faithful when one leader is absent.

Titus is ready to lead another full day. The next development step is to give a second volunteer a bounded responsibility rather than waiting until the whole role can be transferred at once.
