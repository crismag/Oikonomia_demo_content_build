# Stephen — Biblical Context Library

This file records Stephen's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are Stephen's memories, testimony and formation.

Scripture about Stephen is concentrated in Acts 6–7, with later references in Acts 8, 11 and 22. When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

**Handling rule:** Stephen's arrest, speech and death are grave. Routine MTE records do not draw on them. See "Stephen's death in 2026 records" in `profile.md`.

## The widows' complaint and the choosing of the Seven — Acts 6:1–7

The Jerusalem church was growing fast. The believers included Hebrews and Hellenists, commonly understood as Aramaic-speaking Jewish believers rooted in the land and Greek-speaking Jewish believers, many with roots elsewhere in the Mediterranean. A complaint arose from the Hellenists "because their widows were neglected in the daily service." Earlier chapters describe believers sharing possessions so that distribution was made to anyone in need (Acts 4:32–35).

The Twelve called the whole community together. They said, "It is not appropriate for us to forsake the word of God and serve tables." They asked the believers to select "seven men of good report, full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom," whom the apostles would appoint over the work, while the apostles continued in prayer and the ministry of the word. The proposal pleased everyone. They chose Stephen, "a man full of faith and of the Holy Spirit," with Philip, Prochorus, Nicanor, Timon, Parmenas and Nicolaus, a proselyte of Antioch. The apostles prayed and laid their hands on them. The word of God spread, the number of disciples multiplied, and many priests became obedient to the faith.

**Fact and inference:** The seven names are all Greek, which suggests (but does not state) that the community chose men from the group whose widows had been overlooked. Acts does not call the Seven "deacons". It does not describe Stephen doing distribution work; it records his appointment.

**Truths:** The church took a complaint about unfair care seriously and changed its structure. Character (good reputation, the Spirit, wisdom) was required before a practical task. Delegation protected both the needy and the ministry of prayer and the word.

**2026 use:** More Than Enough foundation; fair distribution across language and cultural groups; volunteer selection by character; delegation and structure when a ministry grows; Stephen's reports to Peter.

## Full of faith and power; the disputes — Acts 6:8–10

Stephen, "full of faith and power, performed great wonders and signs among the people." Men from the synagogue called "The Libertines" (commonly understood as freedmen: former slaves or their descendants), and from Cyrene, Alexandria, Cilicia and Asia, argued with him. "They weren't able to withstand the wisdom and the Spirit by which he spoke."

**Silence:** Acts does not record what the signs were or what the arguments were about beyond the later accusations.

**Truths:** A man appointed to a serving role was also a powerful witness. Serving tables did not make him less of a spokesman for Jesus.

**2026 use:** Serving and teaching held together (Goal 3); short Scripture with distribution volunteers. Do not reenact signs in 2026.

## False witnesses and the council — Acts 6:11–15

Unable to answer him, his opponents secretly induced men to say they had heard him speak blasphemy against Moses and God. They stirred up the people, the elders and the scribes, seized Stephen and brought him before the council. False witnesses said he never stopped speaking against the temple and the law, and claimed he had said Jesus of Nazareth would destroy the temple and change the customs Moses handed down. Everyone in the council, looking at him, "saw his face like it was the face of an angel."

**Truths:** Stephen was misrepresented deliberately. His composure did not depend on the fairness of the process.

**2026 use:** Rare. Integrity under misrepresentation, in restricted or testimony records. Not incident-review humor.

## The speech (1): Abraham, Joseph and the famine — Acts 7:1–16

The high priest asked, "Are these things so?" Stephen began, "Brothers and fathers, listen." He told how God appeared to Abraham in Mesopotamia and called him to a land God would show him, gave him no inheritance there, and promised it to descendants he did not yet have. He described the patriarchs selling Joseph into Egypt out of jealousy; God was with Joseph, gave him wisdom and favour before Pharaoh and made him governor. When famine came over Egypt and Canaan and "our fathers found no food," Jacob heard there was grain in Egypt and sent his sons; on the second visit Joseph made himself known and brought the family down.

**Interpretation:** A theme runs through the speech: God met His people outside the land and the temple, and the people repeatedly rejected the leaders God sent (Joseph, then Moses). Many readers see this as Stephen's answer to the charge that he spoke against the holy place.

**Note:** Acts 7:16 speaks of the tomb at Shechem "that Abraham bought." Genesis 23 records Abraham buying the cave of Machpelah, and Joshua 24:32 records Jacob buying land at Shechem. Harmonisation is debated. Do not resolve it in Data Play.

**Truths:** God's faithfulness was not confined to one place. The brother rejected by his brothers became the means of feeding them in famine.

**2026 use:** Occasional MTE reflection on famine and provision through Joseph (carefully, since Joseph is not a Data Play character); Stephen's long-view voice.

## The speech (2): Moses, rejected and sent — Acts 7:17–43

Stephen told of Israel's growth and oppression in Egypt, Moses' birth and rescue by Pharaoh's daughter, and that Moses was "instructed in all the wisdom of the Egyptians." At forty he visited his people, defended an Israelite by striking an Egyptian, and supposed his brothers would understand that God was delivering them through him, "but they didn't understand." The next day an Israelite pushed him away: "Who made you a ruler and a judge over us?" Moses fled to Midian. Forty years later God spoke from the burning bush and sent him back. Stephen said, "This Moses whom they refused ... God has sent him as both a ruler and a deliverer." Moses led them out with wonders, told them God would raise up a prophet like him, and received living words at Sinai. Yet the fathers turned back to Egypt in their hearts, asked Aaron to make gods, made the calf and rejoiced in the works of their hands.

**Fact and inference:** Some details (Moses' Egyptian education, the age of forty, the three forty-year periods) appear in Stephen's speech rather than in Exodus. They are part of Acts' record of what Stephen said. They do not contradict the Moses files and should not be imported into them.

**Truths:** The people God sends are often refused first. Rejection of a leader can be rejection of the God who sent him.

**2026 use:** Rare teaching reflection. Stephen knows Moses' story through Scripture.

**Cross-era note:** Moses, Aaron and Joshua are Data Play characters. Stephen retold their history; they have no memory of Stephen. In 2026 fiction Moses may hear that Stephen summarised his life before the council, but neither claims historical contact. The golden calf reference must not become banter with Aaron.

## The speech (3): the tabernacle, the temple and resisting the Spirit — Acts 7:44–53

Stephen described the tabernacle made according to the pattern shown to Moses, brought in with Joshua and kept until David, who asked to find a dwelling for God. "But Solomon built him a house." Then: "However, the Most High doesn't dwell in temples made with hands," quoting Isaiah 66:1–2: "heaven is my throne, and the earth a footstool for my feet. What kind of house will you build me?" He concluded by calling his hearers stiff-necked and uncircumcised in heart and ears, always resisting the Holy Spirit as their fathers had; their fathers persecuted the prophets who announced the Righteous One, "of whom you have now become betrayers and murderers," and they had received the law and not kept it.

**Interpretation:** Stephen's charge stands in the tradition of Israel's own prophets confronting their people. He spoke as a Jew to Jews. It must never be used to express contempt for Jewish people.

**Truths:** God is not contained by a building. Religious devotion to a holy place can coexist with resistance to God Himself.

**2026 use:** Very rare, serious teaching only. Not a template for confrontational feedback.

**Cross-era note:** David, Solomon, Joshua and Isaiah are Data Play characters. They have no memory of Stephen. Solomon may find it sobering that a temple he built was discussed this way; no comedy.

## The vision, the stoning and the prayer — Acts 7:54–8:1

When they heard this, his hearers were cut to the heart and gnashed their teeth at him. Stephen, full of the Holy Spirit, looked into heaven and saw the glory of God and Jesus standing at God's right hand. He said, "Behold, I see the heavens opened and the Son of Man standing at the right hand of God!" They shouted, covered their ears, rushed at him together, threw him out of the city and stoned him. The witnesses laid their garments at the feet of a young man named Saul. As they stoned him Stephen called out, "Lord Jesus, receive my spirit!" He knelt and cried with a loud voice, "Lord, don't hold this sin against them!" "When he had said this, he fell asleep." Saul was consenting to his death.

**Interpretation:** Many readers note that Stephen's two prayers echo Jesus' words on the cross (Luke 23:34, 46). Why Jesus is described as standing is not explained.

**Silence:** Scripture does not describe his experience of dying beyond these words. Do not invent it.

**Truths:** Stephen saw Jesus before he saw his killers' stones. He entrusted himself to Jesus and asked forgiveness for the people killing him.

**2026 use:** Only in rare, deliberate testimony or restricted reflection, with restraint. Never in routine records, never in comments, never comedy. See `profile.md`.

## Burial, lament and scattering — Acts 8:1–4; 11:19–21

That day a great persecution broke out against the Jerusalem church, and all except the apostles were scattered through Judea and Samaria. "Devout men buried Stephen and lamented greatly over him." Saul ravaged the church, dragging men and women to prison. Those who were scattered preached wherever they went; Philip went to Samaria. Some of those scattered "by the oppression that arose about Stephen" reached Phoenicia, Cyprus and Antioch, where some began speaking to Greeks as well, and a great number believed.

**Boundary:** All of this happened after Stephen's death. It is church history Stephen may know, not his memory.

**Truths:** The church grieved openly; courage did not cancel lament. The persecution that followed his death scattered witnesses outward.

**2026 use:** Philip the Evangelist's and Barnabas' records may draw on the scattering. Stephen's own records do so rarely, and never as a neat "it all worked out" explanation of his death.

## Paul remembers — Acts 22:19–20; compare Acts 26:10

Years later, recounting his conversion to a Jerusalem crowd, Paul described telling the Lord that people knew he had imprisoned and beaten believers, and said: "When the blood of Stephen, your witness, was shed, I also was standing by, consenting to his death, and guarding the cloaks of those who killed him." Before Agrippa he said more generally that when believers were put to death he cast his vote against them (Acts 26:10), without naming Stephen.

**Truths:** Paul carried the memory of Stephen's death into his own testimony. Grace did not erase what he had done.

**2026 use:** Primarily Paul's material. Anything connecting Paul and Stephen in 2026 requires a deliberate lead-author decision and restricted visibility. See `relationships.md`.
