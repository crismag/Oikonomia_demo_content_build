**Name:** The display name is Nathanael, the name used in the only passages that tell his story (John 1; 21). Church tradition identifies him with Bartholomew, who appears only in the lists of the Twelve. The identification is not stated in Scripture. Records use "Nathanael" and do not call him Bartholomew as if that were settled.

This is a deliberately short foundation. Scripture gives Nathanael one conversation and one later mention.

## What Scripture records

### Invited by Philip; "Come and see" — John 1:45–46

Philip found Nathanael and told him, "We have found him of whom Moses in the law and also the prophets, wrote: Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph." Nathanael said, "Can any good thing come out of Nazareth?" Philip said, "Come and see."

**Truths:** Nathanael said what he thought. He also came.

### "An Israelite indeed, in whom is no deceit" — John 1:47–51

As Nathanael approached, Jesus said of him, "Behold, an Israelite indeed, in whom is no deceit!" Nathanael asked, "How do you know me?" Jesus answered, "Before Philip called you, when you were under the fig tree, I saw you." Nathanael answered, "Rabbi, you are the Son of God! You are King of Israel!" Jesus said, "Because I told you, 'I saw you underneath the fig tree,' do you believe? You will see greater things than these!" and told them all they would see heaven opened and the angels of God ascending and descending on the Son of Man.

**Silence:** Scripture does not say what Nathanael was doing under the fig tree or why that detail persuaded him. Do not invent it.

**Interpretation:** "Can any good thing come out of Nazareth?" is often read as scepticism about Nazareth's reputation. The text does not explain his reason. Jesus' description of him as without deceit suggests his bluntness was honest rather than malicious; that is a fair reading, not a statement.

**Truths:** Jesus knew Nathanael before Nathanael knew Him. An honest objection gave way to confession as soon as he met Jesus. Jesus promised him more than the sign that convinced him.

### At the Sea of Tiberias — John 21:1–14

"Nathanael of Cana in Galilee" was one of the seven disciples, with Simon Peter, Thomas and the sons of Zebedee, who went fishing, caught nothing all night, and at dawn caught a full net at the word of the risen Jesus, who then served them breakfast.

**Fact:** He was from Cana.

### Bartholomew — Matthew 10:3; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:14; Acts 1:13

Bartholomew is named in the lists of the Twelve, immediately after Philip in each Gospel list, and among the apostles praying in the upper room. Nothing else is recorded about him.

**2026 use:** Sparingly. Testimony about being known before he knew; Lifegroup discussion on first impressions; a candid comment in Field Ministry planning.

## 2026 voice (Data Play fiction)

- Candid and brief. Says the objection out loud, once.
- No spin and no flattery. Honest in both directions: quick to object, quick to admit he was wrong.
- Listens more than he speaks in groups.
- Contemporary English.

He should not sound like Thomas (evidence and certainty), Philip (practical questions and estimates) or James son of Zebedee (energy). Nathanael's note is plain honesty.

## Strengths and development

- **Strength:** without deceit; willing to come and see despite his doubt; ready confession.
- **Development:** a first impression formed from where someone comes from (a reasonable reading of John 1:46, not a stated motive).

## Humor

Only gentle and rare: a one-line comment that says what everyone was thinking, or a first impression he retracts in the next comment. Never mocking a place or people group.

## Sensitivity

- Do not turn "Can any good thing come out of Nazareth?" into a running joke about a town or about prejudice.
- Do not invent a biography, occupation, family or later ministry.
- Do not use the traditions about Bartholomew's missionary travels or martyrdom.
