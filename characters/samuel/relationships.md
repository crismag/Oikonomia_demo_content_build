# Samuel — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Hannah and Elkanah — parents (not on the roster)
Hannah prayed for a son, vowed him to the LORD, and brought him to Shiloh once he was weaned; each year she brought him a little robe (1 Samuel 1; 2:19). Elkanah went up yearly to worship and supported her vow (1 Samuel 1:21–23). Refer to them in prose only.

### Eli — priest and mentor (not on the roster)
Eli raised Samuel at Shiloh, taught him how to answer God's call, and insisted on hearing the whole message against his own house (1 Samuel 3:8–18). Eli failed to restrain his sons (1 Samuel 3:13) and died when the ark was captured (1 Samuel 4:18). 1 Kings 2:27 notes the later fulfilment of the word about Eli's house when Solomon removed Abiathar.

**2026 note:** Samuel remembers Eli with honesty in both directions: the man who taught him to listen and the leader who would not stop his sons. Records about Eli are serious and never mocking.

### Joel and Abijah — sons (not on the roster)
Samuel appointed them judges; they took bribes and perverted justice, and the elders cited this in asking for a king (1 Samuel 8:1–5).

**Silence:** Samuel's response is not recorded. Any 2026 reflection must say plainly that Scripture does not tell us how he dealt with them.

### Saul
Samuel anointed Saul privately, presented him publicly and renewed the kingdom with him (1 Samuel 10; 11:14–15). He confronted Saul at Gilgal (13:8–14) and after Amalek (15), where Saul tore Samuel's robe. Samuel went back with him once to worship, then "came no more to see Saul until the day of his death, but Samuel mourned for Saul" (15:35). Saul prophesied before Samuel at Naioth while pursuing David (19:23–24). After Samuel's death Saul sought him through the medium at Endor (28, debated).

**2026 fiction:** Saul reports to Samuel. That line is not a punishment and not a restoration of kingship. Samuel keeps a fortnightly restricted check-in with Saul, prays for him, and tells him the truth. Samuel does not pretend Gilgal and Amalek did not happen; he also does not treat Saul only as the man he rejected. Their records are `Only me` or `Named people` naming each other. The biblical distance of 1 Samuel 15:35 means their 2026 conversations begin awkwardly and slowly, not warmly.

### David
Samuel anointed David among his brothers after being corrected about Eliab (1 Samuel 16:1–13). David fled to Samuel at Ramah and told him everything Saul had done; they stayed together at Naioth (19:18–24). Samuel died before David became king (25:1); David was later anointed over Israel "according to the LORD's word by Samuel" (1 Chronicles 11:3).

**Silence:** No recorded conversation between them survives beyond the anointing.

**2026 fiction:** Fellow Leadership Council members. Samuel knew David as a shepherd and a fugitive; David is now the more visible senior leader. Samuel is not David's superior. Samuel may challenge David quietly on Council matters, and David respects it. Neither trades stories about Saul casually. David's canonical profile names Samuel as "Prophet who anointed David."

### The company of the prophets
At Naioth, Saul's messengers saw "the company of the prophets prophesying, and Samuel standing as head over them" (1 Samuel 19:20). Saul met a band of prophets after his anointing (10:5, 10).

**2026 fiction:** This is the basis for Samuel's oversight of prophetic and teaching leaders who report to him: Elijah, Isaiah and Jeremiah.

### Abiathar and the priests (not on the roster)
Eli's line continued through Ahitub's family (1 Samuel 14:3; 22:20). Samuel is not recorded meeting Abiathar. Refer in prose only.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Moses
Scripture pairs them as intercessors (Psalm 99:6; Jeremiah 15:1), and Samuel spoke of Moses and Aaron in his farewell (1 Samuel 12:6–8). Samuel knows Moses' history; **Moses has no memory of Samuel.** In 2026 they are two Council members who both carried people who rejected God's leadership and kept praying for them anyway. Moses speaks with more visible frustration; Samuel with fewer words.

### Elijah, Isaiah and Jeremiah
Report to Samuel. Samuel gives them room, asks what God has said before asking what they plan, and protects time for prayer. Jeremiah knows Jeremiah 15:1 names Samuel; Samuel does not remember it and should not be written as flattered by it. Elijah's wilderness exhaustion (1 Kings 19) and Jeremiah's lament give Samuel pastoral oversight material; those leaders own their records.

### Deborah
Both led Israel as judges in the era before kings (Judges 4–5; 1 Samuel 7:15). Council peers. Useful for conversations about leadership without formal title and about transitions of authority.

### Solomon
Solomon's removal of Abiathar fulfilled the word about Eli's house that Samuel heard as a boy (1 Kings 2:27; 1 Samuel 3:11–14). Samuel did not live to see it. In 2026 Samuel may notice that detail with sobriety when reading Kings; he does not claim to remember it.

### Barnabas
Mentorship head. Samuel contributes to Mentorship Night and respects Barnabas' leadership of it. Barnabas encourages; Samuel discerns. Together they shape which developing leaders are given responsibility.

### Ezra
Council peer. Samuel wrote the regulations of the kingdom in a book (1 Samuel 10:25); Ezra cares for teaching and documents. They agree that written expectations protect people.

### Cris
Cris coordinates Council follow-ups and schedules Samuel's check-ins. Cris cannot see Samuel's restricted records with Saul or other leaders unless named.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Samuel / Saul:** restricted fortnightly check-ins; a goal about continuing to pray for people he has corrected; careful acknowledgment of the past without replaying it.
- **Samuel / David:** Council disagreement handled quietly; a shared testimony of the anointing from two perspectives (David's own record exists in his biblical context).
- **Samuel / Barnabas / Mentorship Night:** selection of developing leaders; looking past appearance.
- **Samuel / Elijah, Isaiah, Jeremiah:** oversight and pastoral check-ins; prayer before plans.
- **Samuel / Moses:** Council conversation on intercession for a discouraged congregation.
- **Samuel / Cris:** comments on long reports ("Has anyone prayed about this?").
