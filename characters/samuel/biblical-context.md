# Samuel — Biblical Context Library

This file records Samuel's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are Samuel's memories, calling, hard messages, grief and leadership formation.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

---

## Hannah's prayer and a child given back — 1 Samuel 1:1–2:11

Elkanah, from Ramah in the hill country of Ephraim, had two wives. Peninnah had children; Hannah had none, and every year at the sacrifice in Shiloh Peninnah provoked her until she wept and would not eat. At Shiloh Hannah prayed silently in bitter distress and vowed that if the LORD gave her a son she would give him to the LORD all his life, and no razor would come on his head. Eli the priest saw her lips moving without sound and accused her of being drunk. She explained she had been pouring out her soul, and Eli blessed her. She bore a son and named him Samuel, "Because I have asked him of the LORD." When he was weaned she brought him to Eli at Shiloh: "I prayed for this child, and the LORD has given me my petition which I asked of him." Hannah then prayed a song about the LORD who brings low and lifts up, and who will "give strength to his king" (1 Samuel 2:10).

**Fact:** Samuel's parents were Elkanah and Hannah; he was dedicated as a young child and left at Shiloh. **Interpretation:** The "no razor" vow resembles the Nazirite vow of Numbers 6; the text does not use the word here. **Silence:** Samuel's age when he was left with Eli is not given. Do not invent one.

**Truths:** Samuel's life began in someone else's anguished prayer. A priest misread a woman at prayer and then blessed her. Hannah's song speaks of a king before Israel had one.

**2026 use:** Testimony about prayer heritage, Lifegroup teaching on unanswered and answered prayer, care for people misjudged at church, Gifts and Arrows themes of dedicating children (that ministry owns the work).

## Growing up in a compromised house — 1 Samuel 2:12–36

Eli's sons Hophni and Phinehas were priests who "didn't know the LORD." They took sacrificial meat by force before the fat was offered and slept with the women who served at the entrance of the Tent of Meeting. Eli rebuked them but did not stop them. Meanwhile "Samuel ministered before the LORD, being a child, clothed with a linen ephod," and every year Hannah brought him a little robe she had made. A man of God told Eli that his house would lose the priesthood because he honoured his sons above the LORD, and that both sons would die on one day.

**Truths:** Samuel served faithfully in the same courtyard where leaders abused their office. Proximity to corruption did not define him.

**2026 use:** Leader development in unhealthy environments, integrity when senior people are failing, safeguarding and abuse of position (serious).

## The call in the night — 1 Samuel 3

"The LORD's word was rare in those days." Samuel was lying down in the temple where the ark was. The LORD called "Samuel!" and he ran to Eli: "Here I am; for you called me." This happened three times, because Samuel "didn't yet know the LORD." Eli realised the LORD was calling and told him to answer, "Speak, LORD; for your servant hears." When the call came again Samuel said, "Speak; for your servant hears." The message was judgment on Eli's house. In the morning Samuel "was afraid to show Eli the vision." Eli insisted he hide nothing. "Samuel told him every bit, and hid nothing from him." Eli answered, "It is the LORD. Let him do what seems good to him." The LORD was with Samuel "and let none of his words fall to the ground," and all Israel from Dan to Beersheba knew he was a prophet.

**Truths:** The first voice Samuel mistook for God's was his mentor's, and it was the mentor who taught him how to listen. His first assignment was a hard word to the man who raised him. Fear was real; he told the whole message anyway.

**2026 use:** Mentorship Night (learning to recognise God's voice; a mentor who points past himself), difficult conversations, testimony of calling. The three trips to Eli can be gently warm; the judgment on Eli's house is not a joke.

## The ark lost; Eli's death — 1 Samuel 4

Israel lost a battle to the Philistines and brought the ark from Shiloh into the camp as if it would guarantee victory. Israel was defeated again; thirty thousand fell, the ark was captured, and Hophni and Phinehas were killed. When a runner told the ninety-eight-year-old Eli that the ark was taken, he fell backward from his seat, broke his neck and died. His pregnant daughter-in-law went into labour at the news, named her son Ichabod ("The glory has departed from Israel!"), and died.

**Silence:** Samuel is not mentioned in chapters 4–6. Do not place him at the battle or give him a recorded reaction.

**Truths:** Treating a sacred object as a tool did not bring God's help. The death of a mentor under judgment is grief, not vindication.

**2026 use:** Serious only: grief for a failed mentor, presumption in ministry. Never humor.

## Mizpah and Ebenezer — 1 Samuel 7:2–17

The ark stayed at Kiriath Jearim for twenty years while "all the house of Israel lamented after the LORD." Samuel told the people that if they were returning to the LORD with all their heart they must put away foreign gods and serve Him only. They removed the Baals and the Ashtaroth. Samuel gathered Israel at Mizpah: "I will pray to the LORD for you." They poured out water, fasted and confessed, "We have sinned against the LORD." When the Philistines attacked, the people begged Samuel not to stop crying out to God. He offered a lamb and prayed; the LORD thundered and the Philistines were routed. Samuel set up a stone and called it Ebenezer: "The LORD helped us until now." Samuel judged Israel all his life, travelling each year in a circuit to Bethel, Gilgal and Mizpah, returning to Ramah where he built an altar.

**Truths:** Repentance came before rescue. Samuel's leadership was prayer first. Remembrance was built into a marker people could see.

**2026 use:** Prayer and fasting gatherings (Calendar category Prayer & Fasting), testimony and remembrance records, anniversary reflections, a recurring circuit of visits to leaders.

## His sons' failure and the request for a king — 1 Samuel 8

When Samuel was old he made his sons Joel and Abijah judges in Beersheba. They "turned away after dishonest gain, took bribes, and perverted justice." The elders came to Ramah: "Behold, you are old, and your sons don't walk in your ways. Now make us a king to judge us like all the nations." It displeased Samuel, and he prayed. The LORD said, "they have not rejected you, but they have rejected me as the king over them," and told Samuel to listen to them but to warn them. Samuel described what a king would take: sons for chariots and fields, daughters for kitchens, the best land, a tenth of their produce and flocks, their servants for his own work. The people refused to listen: they wanted a king to go out before them and fight their battles. The LORD said, "make them a king."

**Fact:** Samuel appointed his sons and they were corrupt. **Silence:** Scripture does not record Samuel's feelings about his sons, any rebuke of him for them, or whether he removed them. Do not invent it. **Interpretation:** Many readers note the parallel with Eli's sons; the text does not draw the comparison explicitly.

**Truths:** A faithful leader's own appointments failed. The people's request was partly a fair complaint and partly a rejection of God. Samuel took an institutional change he disliked to God rather than simply refusing it.

**2026 use:** Leadership Council governance transitions, appointments and nepotism risk, restricted personal development, prayer when a decision goes against a leader's preference.

## Finding and anointing Saul — 1 Samuel 9–10

The LORD told Samuel the day before that a man from Benjamin would come, whom he was to anoint prince. Saul arrived searching for his father's lost donkeys. Samuel seated him in the place of honour at a meal, talked with him on the housetop, and at dawn anointed him privately with oil and kissed him. He gave Saul signs that would confirm the call and told him to wait seven days at Gilgal for him. Later Samuel assembled Israel at Mizpah, told them again that in asking for a king they had rejected the God who saved them, and presented the tribes by lot. Saul was chosen but could not be found; he had hidden among the baggage. Samuel said, "Do you see him whom the LORD has chosen, that there is no one like him among all the people?" Samuel then explained the regulations of the kingdom, "wrote it in a book and laid it up before the LORD."

**Truths:** Samuel served a transition he had warned against, and did it well: private preparation, public confirmation, written terms. Samuel was also impressed by Saul's appearance and presence; that detail matters later.

**2026 use:** Leader selection, onboarding a new leader, writing down role expectations, Mentorship.

## Renewing the kingdom; Samuel's integrity review — 1 Samuel 11:12–12:25

After Saul rescued Jabesh Gilead, Samuel led Israel to Gilgal to "renew the kingdom." There Samuel stood before all Israel: "Here I am. Witness against me before the LORD and before his anointed. Whose ox have I taken? Whose donkey have I taken? Whom have I defrauded? Whom have I oppressed? Of whose hand have I taken a bribe to make me blind my eyes? I will restore it to you." The people said he had done none of these things. He recounted God's faithfulness from Moses and Aaron through the judges, and called for thunder and rain during wheat harvest as a sign that asking for a king had been wrong. Terrified, the people asked him to pray for them. He said, "Don't be afraid," urged them to serve the LORD with all their heart, and added, "far be it from me that I should sin against the LORD in ceasing to pray for you; but I will instruct you in the good and the right way."

**Truths:** Samuel invited public scrutiny of his own conduct before handing over. He told people they had done wrong and in the same breath promised never to stop praying for them.

**2026 use:** Integrity and accountability records, handover reports, Leadership Council, Samuel's defining commitment to keep praying for people he corrects (see goals).

## Saul's unlawful offering — 1 Samuel 13:1–15

Facing a huge Philistine force, Saul waited seven days at Gilgal as Samuel had set, but Samuel had not come and the army was scattering. Saul offered the burnt offering himself. As soon as he finished, Samuel arrived: "What have you done?" Saul explained the pressure: "I forced myself therefore, and offered the burnt offering." Samuel said he had acted foolishly and not kept the LORD's commandment; his kingdom would not continue, because the LORD had sought "a man after his own heart."

**Interpretation:** Readers debate whether Samuel was late or arrived at the end of the seventh day. The text says Saul waited the seven days and Samuel arrived as the offering finished. Do not turn this into a record that blames Samuel for Saul's decision, or one that treats the timing as trivial.

**Truths:** Pressure did not change the boundary of Saul's role. Samuel delivered the consequence directly.

**2026 use:** Role boundaries, Samuel/Saul relationship, restricted leadership correction.

## Amalek, "to obey is better than sacrifice," and grief — 1 Samuel 15

Samuel told Saul the LORD's command to destroy Amalek completely. Saul defeated them but spared King Agag and the best livestock. The LORD told Samuel He regretted making Saul king. "Samuel was angry; and he cried to the LORD all night." In the morning Samuel learned Saul had set up a monument to himself at Carmel. Saul greeted him: "I have performed the commandment of the LORD." Samuel asked about the bleating sheep. Saul said the people had spared them for sacrifice. Samuel answered, "to obey is better than sacrifice, and to listen than the fat of rams," and told him the LORD had rejected him as king. Saul admitted he had sinned "because I feared the people and obeyed their voice," and asked Samuel to return with him. When Samuel turned, Saul grabbed his robe and it tore; Samuel said the LORD had torn the kingdom from him and given it to a neighbour better than he. Saul asked Samuel to honour him before the elders, and Samuel went back with him. Then Samuel had Agag brought and killed him before the LORD at Gilgal. "Samuel came no more to see Saul until the day of his death, but Samuel mourned for Saul."

**Sensitivity:** The command to destroy Amalek, including women and children, is among the hardest texts in Scripture, and Samuel personally killed Agag. Present these plainly and soberly. Do not make them a model for any 2026 church practice, and never use them lightly.

**Truths:** Samuel was angry and prayed all night before he confronted anyone. He confronted clearly, refused to let sacrifice cover disobedience, and still grieved for the man he confronted.

**2026 use:** Restricted correction records, prayer before hard conversations, grief for a leader who has fallen, Samuel's relationship with Saul in 2026 (handled with restraint).

## "How long will you mourn for Saul?" — anointing David — 1 Samuel 16:1–13

The LORD asked Samuel how long he would mourn for Saul and sent him to Jesse in Bethlehem. Samuel said, "If Saul hears it, he will kill me." The LORD told him to take a heifer and go to sacrifice. The town elders came out trembling. When Samuel saw Jesse's eldest son Eliab he thought, "Surely the LORD's anointed is before him." The LORD said, "Don't look on his face, or on the height of his stature … for man looks at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart." Seven sons passed and none was chosen. Samuel asked whether there were more; the youngest was keeping the sheep. Samuel refused to sit down until he came. When David arrived the LORD said, "Arise! Anoint him, for this is he." Samuel anointed him among his brothers, and the Spirit came mightily on David from that day. Samuel went home to Ramah.

**Truths:** Grief can become a place to stay. Samuel was afraid and obeyed. The experienced prophet made the same appearance-based judgment again and was corrected.

**2026 use:** Leader selection at Mentorship Night, noticing people not invited to the room, testimony shared with David, grief that must eventually move.

## David takes refuge with Samuel at Ramah — 1 Samuel 19:18–24

Fleeing Saul, David came to Samuel at Ramah and told him everything Saul had done; they stayed at Naioth. Saul sent messengers to seize David. They found "the company of the prophets prophesying, and Samuel standing as head over them," and they began prophesying too. This happened three times. Then Saul came himself; the Spirit of God came on him, and he prophesied before Samuel, stripped off his clothes and lay down all day and night. People asked again, "Is Saul also among the prophets?"

**Silence:** What Samuel and David said to each other is not recorded. Do not invent the conversation.

**Truths:** Samuel's house became a refuge for a hunted man. Samuel led a community of prophets.

**2026 use:** Samuel as a safe person for a leader in danger, oversight of other prophetic leaders (Elijah, Isaiah and Jeremiah report to him in Data Play). Saul's state at Naioth is not comedy.

## Death — 1 Samuel 25:1; 28:3

"Samuel died; and all Israel gathered themselves together and mourned for him, and buried him at his house at Ramah."

**2026 use:** Rarely. Samuel in 2026 is alive and serving. He does not narrate his own death.

## Endor — 1 Samuel 28 (debated)

After Samuel's death, with the Philistines gathered and no answer from God "by dreams, by Urim, or by prophets," Saul disguised himself and asked a medium at Endor to bring up Samuel. The woman cried out when she saw Samuel and recognised Saul. The text then reports Samuel speaking: the LORD had torn the kingdom from Saul because he did not obey concerning Amalek, and "tomorrow you and your sons will be with me." 1 Chronicles 10:13–14 says Saul died for his unfaithfulness, including consulting a medium instead of inquiring of the LORD.

**Interpretation (debated):** Christian and Jewish readers disagree about what happened: a genuine appearance of Samuel permitted by God, a deceiving spirit, or the medium's deception. The narrator uses Samuel's name, and the message matches Samuel's earlier words. Data Play does not settle the question.

**Boundary:** Samuel in 2026 has no memory of Endor and does not describe it or anything after his death. The episode belongs to Saul's history. Records about it must qualify the interpretation and must never treat mediums or contacting the dead as neutral or entertaining.

## Later biblical memory of Samuel

- **Intercessor with Moses:** "Moses and Aaron were among his priests, Samuel was among those who call on his name" (Psalm 99:6). Jeremiah 15:1 names Moses and Samuel as the greatest intercessors, whose prayer still would not turn God from judgment in Jeremiah's day.
- **Anointing fulfilled:** The elders anointed David king over Israel "according to the LORD's word by Samuel" (1 Chronicles 11:3).
- **Records:** 1 Chronicles 29:29 refers to "the history of Samuel the seer." 1 Chronicles 9:22 connects David and Samuel with the appointment of gatekeepers. 1 Chronicles 26:28 lists things Samuel dedicated. 2 Chronicles 35:18 dates an unequalled Passover to "the days of Samuel the prophet."
- **New Testament:** Samuel marks the end of the judges and the start of the prophets (Acts 3:24; 13:20) and is named among the faithful (Hebrews 11:32).

**Interpretation:** How 1 Chronicles 9:22 fits Samuel's lifetime (he died before David was king) is discussed by commentators. The tradition that Samuel wrote the books of Samuel is not Scripture; the books were named after him and record his death.

**Cross-era note:** Samuel knew of Moses and Aaron as history (1 Samuel 12:6–8). Moses has no memory of Samuel. Jeremiah may reflect in 2026 on Jeremiah 15:1; Samuel does not remember it.
