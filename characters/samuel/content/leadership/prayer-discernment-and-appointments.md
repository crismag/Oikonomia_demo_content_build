## 2026-02-09 — Leader Selection Review: Who Has Not Been Invited In?

**Record type:** Mentorship / Discernment Report  
**Date:** 2026-02-09  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Barnabas, David  
**Status:** Follow-up Required  
**Tags:** #reflective #serious #discernment #humility

At Mentorship Night we considered several developing leaders for visible responsibility. Their gifts were evident, and it would have been easy to choose from the names already on the agenda. I asked a different question first: who has not been invited into the room?

When I came to Jesse's house to anoint a king, I assumed the eldest son looked right. The Lord corrected me. The youngest was still with the sheep and had not been called in. This does not give us a formula for selecting the least visible person. It does require us to examine whether visibility, presence and confidence are doing more work in our judgment than character and faithfulness in small responsibilities.

We postponed no one as a punishment. Barnabas will gather conduct and service observations before the next review, including from people who work alongside each candidate in ordinary tasks.

**Attention:** Appointment discussion must include declared relationships and a written reason for recommendation.
**Attention to:** Barnabas
**Action requested:** Bring unseen-service observations and any conflict disclosures to the next selection review — from Barnabas, by 2026-03-10
**Related goal:** Goal 2 — Look past appearance when leaders are chosen
**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 9:2; 10:23–24; 16:6–13.

## 2026-06-08 — Written Expectations Protect Both the Leader and the People

**Record type:** Governance / Leadership Report  
**Date:** 2026-06-08  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Ezra, Solomon  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #administrative #serious #accountability #discernment

I met with Ezra and Solomon about a short expectations document for new ministry responsibilities. This is not a catalogue of rules meant to replace prayer. It is a way to make responsibilities, boundaries and review points plain before someone is publicly relied on.

When Israel asked for a king, I wrote down the regulations of the kingdom and placed them before the Lord. Earlier, I had made my own sons judges, and they did not walk in my ways; they took bribes and perverted justice. Scripture does not tell us how I dealt with them. It does tell us that my appointments became part of the people's complaint. My personal integrity did not excuse poor appointment practice.

The proposed document will include role purpose, authority limits, conflict disclosure, first review date and who receives a concern. Ezra will draft it; Solomon will test whether it is readable in an Events appointment.

**Attention:** Do not treat written expectations as proof that an appointment is wise; discernment remains required.
**Attention to:** Church leadership
**Action requested:** Draft the role-expectations template for Council review — from Ezra, by 2026-06-22
**Related goal:** Goal 4 — Keep appointments clean of family and favour
**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 8:1–5; 10:25; 12:1–5.
