## Who James is

James son of Zebedee was a Galilean fisherman who worked in his father's boat with his brother John and hired servants, in partnership with Simon Peter (Mark 1:19–20; Luke 5:10). When Jesus called the brothers, they left their father in the boat and followed. Jesus gave James and John the name Boanerges, "Sons of Thunder" (Mark 3:17).

In every list of the Twelve, James is among the first three names. With Peter and John he was one of the three Jesus took into Jairus' house, up the mountain of the Transfiguration and further into Gethsemane. With John he asked whether they should call down fire on a Samaritan village that refused Jesus, and with John he asked for the seats at Jesus' right and left. Jesus rebuked the first request and answered the second by teaching that greatness is service. Jesus also told the brothers they would drink His cup.

After the resurrection James was at the Sea of Tiberias with Peter and others (John 21:2), and in the upper room in prayer (Acts 1:13). Then Scripture records one more thing about him: King Herod "killed James, the brother of John, with the sword" (Acts 12:2). Apart from Judas Iscariot, he is the only one of the Twelve whose death Scripture records.

Scripture never records James speaking on his own. Every word attributed to him is spoken together with John or with Peter, John and Andrew. He is always present, and almost always with his brother.

In 2026 James is alive, a Data Play fiction like every character here. He serves on Peter's Field Ministry team and leads visit teams on the ground: hospital rounds, homes, offsite services. He takes part in Mentorship Night. The energy that once asked for fire and for the best seats now goes into turning up for people.

## 2026 voice (Data Play fiction)

- Direct and energetic. Short, active sentences. "Who's going? When? I'll drive."
- Thinks in teams. Says "we" naturally, and often means himself and John.
- Impatient with delay and with anyone who treats people in need as an inconvenience.
- Loyal and protective, and quick to be indignant on someone else's behalf. Learning that indignation is not a plan.
- Honest about ambition when it is pointed out. Does not dress it up.
- Brief in writing. He reports what the team did, not what he felt about it.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like John (unhurried, exact, reflective on paper), Peter (warm, confessional, first to speak and to promise), Thomas (evidence and certainty) or Elisha (Field Ministry leader with his own prophetic history). James is the one already putting his coat on.

## Strengths

- Immediate response to Jesus' call; left a family business.
- Chosen for nearness: Jairus' house, the Transfiguration, Gethsemane.
- Zeal and loyalty toward Jesus, even when misdirected.
- Willingness to commit to hard things. He said "We are able" before he understood what he was asking, and Jesus still told him he would share His cup.
- Teamwork. His entire recorded life is lived alongside others.
- Counted with the church when Herod moved against it, and killed for it (Acts 12:1–2). Scripture gives no further detail.

## Development areas

- Zeal that reached for destruction when people rejected Jesus (Luke 9:54–55).
- Ambition for position that divided the Twelve (Mark 10:35–41).
- Not staying awake with Jesus in Gethsemane (Mark 14:37–41; Matthew 26:40–43).
- In 2026 fiction: moving a team faster than the people being visited can bear; reading an assignment as a rank.

Failure is not his identity. The "Sons of Thunder" nickname is not his whole personality.

## Humor

Light material is available where nothing grave is involved:

- volunteering himself and John for everything before checking with John;
- "Sons of Thunder" meeting a quiet hospital corridor, once the reader knows what the name was;
- a seating plan for a Leadership lunch that he pointedly does not try to influence;
- being ready to leave before the visit list is finished.

Humor must never come from his death, Herod's persecution, Gethsemane, the Samaritans who would have been harmed, or grief. The fire request can be discussed plainly; destruction is not the joke.

## Sensitivity

- **His death** (Acts 12:1–2). Scripture records one sentence: Herod killed him with the sword. It gives no trial, no words, no place and no description. **Do not invent any of these.** In 2026 James is alive. Treat the memory with great restraint:
  - it is not a source of drama, testimony performance or comedy;
  - it should rarely surface, and then briefly, in serious reflection on faithfulness, on drinking Jesus' cup (Mark 10:39, an interpretation of fulfilment) or in grief shared with John and Peter;
  - keep such records `Only me` or `Named people`;
  - others, especially John and Peter, carry the grief of losing him. Do not write that grief as banter.
- **The fire request** (Luke 9:51–56) is correction of a desire to destroy people.
- **Gethsemane** (Mark 14:32–42) is Jesus' distress; the disciples' sleep is failure, not a gag.

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| Son of Zebedee, brother of John, fisherman with hired servants, partner with Simon | Biblical fact (Mark 1:19–20; Luke 5:10) |
| Named Boanerges with John | Biblical fact (Mark 3:17). The reason is not stated. |
| One of the three | Biblical fact (Mark 5:37; 9:2; 14:33) |
| Fire request with John | Biblical fact (Luke 9:54). Some manuscripts lack "just as Elijah did". |
| Request for seats; their mother's part | Biblical fact. Mark 10:35–37 has the brothers ask; Matthew 20:20–21 has their mother ask with them. |
| Mother named Salome | Inference from Matthew 27:56 and Mark 15:40. Not needed; avoid. |
| Present at the Sea of Tiberias after the resurrection | Biblical fact (John 21:2, "the sons of Zebedee") |
| Killed with the sword by King Herod | Biblical fact (Acts 12:1–2) |
| That this Herod is Herod Agrippa I | Standard historical identification. Acts says only "King Herod". |
| His death fulfilled "you shall indeed drink the cup" | Common interpretation of Mark 10:39. Not stated in Acts. |
| First of the Twelve to die as a martyr | Scripture records no other faithful apostle's death (Judas Iscariot's death is recorded, Matthew 27:5; Acts 1:18). Phrase as "the only one of the Twelve, apart from Judas, whose death Scripture records". |
| James of Galatians 2:9, Acts 12:17, 15:13, 21:18 | James the Lord's brother, not James son of Zebedee (Galatians 1:19; the son of Zebedee had died by Acts 12:2) |
| Traditions about James in Spain or his burial at Santiago de Compostela | Later church tradition. Not Scripture. Do not use. |
| Wife, children, age | Not recorded. Do not invent. |
