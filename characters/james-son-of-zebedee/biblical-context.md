# James son of Zebedee — Biblical Context Library

This file records the lived biblical history of James son of Zebedee that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are James' memories, correction and formation.

Scripture gives James no individual speech, no recorded conversation of his own and a one-sentence death. This file keeps to what is written. When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow it.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB).

**Namesakes:** James son of Zebedee is not James the Lord's brother (Galatians 1:19; 2:9, 12; Acts 12:17; 15:13; 21:18) and not James son of Alphaeus (Mark 3:18). Do not attach their passages to him.

---

## Called from his father's boat — Matthew 4:21–22; Mark 1:19–20; Luke 5:1–11

Jesus saw James son of Zebedee and his brother John in a boat with their father, mending their nets, and called them. They immediately left the boat and their father and followed Him. Mark adds that they left Zebedee "in the boat with the hired servants." Luke records that James and John were partners with Simon, and were amazed with him at the huge catch of fish that nearly sank both boats before they left everything to follow Jesus.

**Silence:** Nothing is recorded about Zebedee's response or the family beyond the hired servants. Do not invent it.

**Truths:** The call interrupted an established family business. James responded immediately.

**2026 use:** Testimony, calling, leaving security for an assignment.

## Sons of Thunder — Mark 3:13–19

When Jesus appointed the Twelve "that they might be with him" and be sent out, He gave James and John the name Boanerges, "which means, Sons of Thunder."

**Silence:** The reason for the name is not given. Linking it to the fire request is a common inference, not a statement.

**2026 use:** Mentorship and personal development; light humor only when the reader is told what the name was.

## Jairus' daughter — Mark 5:35–43; Luke 8:49–56

When word came that Jairus' daughter had died, Jesus told Jairus not to be afraid, only to believe. He let no one follow Him except Peter, James and John. In the house, among mourners who laughed at Him, He took the parents and the three disciples into the room, took the twelve-year-old girl by the hand and raised her. He told them to give her something to eat, and ordered that no one should know about it.

**Truths:** The three were brought into a family's grief, and then told to keep it private.

**2026 use:** Field Ministry home visits, grief, confidentiality. Do not reenact a raising in 2026.

## The Transfiguration — Mark 9:2–10; Matthew 17:1–9; Luke 9:28–36

Jesus took Peter, James and John up a high mountain. His face shone and His clothes became dazzling white. Moses and Elijah appeared, talking with Him. Peter offered to build three tents. A cloud overshadowed them and a voice said, "This is my beloved Son. Listen to him." Matthew says the disciples fell on their faces, very afraid, until Jesus touched them and said, "Get up, and don't be afraid." Jesus told them to tell no one until the Son of Man had risen from the dead.

**Truths:** Awe, fear and Jesus' reassuring touch. Silence kept until the right time.

**2026 use:** Worship, awe, confidentiality.

**Cross-era note:** James saw Moses and Elijah. They are Data Play characters and must not be given a memory of the event.

## Fire on a Samaritan village — Luke 9:51–56

As Jesus set His face toward Jerusalem, He sent messengers ahead into a Samaritan village to prepare for Him, but the village did not receive Him. "When his disciples, James and John, saw this, they said, 'Lord, do you want us to command fire to come down from the sky and destroy them, just as Elijah did?'" Jesus turned and rebuked them. In WEB He says, "You don't know of what kind of spirit you are. For the Son of Man didn't come to destroy men's lives, but to save them." They went to another village.

**Background:** The brothers were recalling 2 Kings 1:9–12, where fire came down on soldiers sent to seize Elijah.

**Textual note:** Some manuscripts lack "just as Elijah did" and the longer rebuke. All record the rebuke and the move to another village.

**Truths:** Rejection is not permission to retaliate. When a door stays shut, Jesus went to another village.

**2026 use:** Field Ministry visits that are refused; restraint; Mentorship on anger. Serious, not a Samaritan joke. Goal 3.

**Cross-era note:** Elijah has no memory of Luke 9.

## Asking for the seats of honour — Mark 10:35–45; Matthew 20:20–28

James and John came to Jesus: "Teacher, we want you to do for us whatever we will ask." They asked to sit at His right and left in His glory. Matthew records that their mother knelt with them and made the request. Jesus said, "You don't know what you are asking. Are you able to drink the cup that I drink, and to be baptized with the baptism that I am baptized with?" They said, "We are able." Jesus said, "You shall indeed drink the cup that I drink," but the seats were not His to give. When the ten heard, they were indignant with the brothers. Jesus called everyone together and said that rulers of the nations lord it over people, "But it shall not be so among you, but whoever wants to become great among you shall be your servant. Whoever of you wants to become first among you shall be bondservant of all. For the Son of Man also came not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."

**Interpretation:** Many readers see James' death in Acts 12:2 as fulfilling "you shall indeed drink the cup." Acts does not say so.

**Truths:** Asking for position created resentment in the team. Jesus did not shame the ambition away; He redefined greatness as service and pointed to His own sacrifice.

**2026 use:** Servant leadership, team conflict, Mentorship, Goal 1.

## Olivet questions — Mark 13:1–5

When Jesus said the temple would be thrown down, Peter, James, John and Andrew asked Him privately when and what the sign would be. He began, "Be careful that no one leads you astray."

**2026 use:** Discernment, avoiding speculation.

## Gethsemane — Mark 14:32–42; Matthew 26:36–46

Jesus took Peter and "the two sons of Zebedee" further into the garden, began to be sorrowful and troubled, and asked them to stay and watch with Him. He returned three times and found them sleeping, "for their eyes were heavy." When He was betrayed, all the disciples left Him and fled (Mark 14:50).

**Truths:** The disciples closest to Jesus could not stay awake with Him in His distress. James was among those who slept and fled.

**2026 use:** Presence with suffering people, prayer, Field Ministry night and hospital visits, Goal 2. Serious.

## After the resurrection: the sea and the upper room — John 21:1–14; Acts 1:12–14

"The sons of Zebedee" were among the seven disciples who went fishing with Simon Peter at the Sea of Tiberias, caught nothing all night, and at Jesus' word caught 153 large fish. Jesus served them breakfast of bread and fish. After the ascension James is named among the eleven in the upper room who devoted themselves to prayer.

**2026 use:** Testimony, restoration of the group, prayer.

## Killed by Herod — Acts 12:1–5

"Now about that time, King Herod stretched out his hands to oppress some of the assembly. He killed James, the brother of John, with the sword." Seeing that it pleased people, Herod then arrested Peter, and the church prayed constantly for him. Peter was released by an angel.

**Silence:** Scripture records no trial, no words, no place, no reaction from James, and no account of the church's response to his death beyond the prayer for Peter that followed. **Do not invent any of it.**

**Truths:** The same chapter holds James' death and Peter's deliverance. Neither is explained away. Faithful people are not always rescued in this life.

**2026 use:** Rare, brief and serious. James is alive in 2026 fiction. Use only in restricted reflection on faithfulness or the cup, or in shared grief with John and Peter. Never drama for its own sake, never comedy.
