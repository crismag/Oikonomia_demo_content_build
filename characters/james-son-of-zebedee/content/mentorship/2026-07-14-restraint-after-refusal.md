## When a Refusal Is Not a Fight to Win

**Record type:** Mentorship / Personal Development Report  
**Date:** 2026-07-14  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Barnabas, Peter  
**Status:** Practice Continuing  
**Tags:** #reflective #serious #restraint #humility

At Mentorship Night I described a recent refused visit. The refusal was brief and not hostile. I still came away ready to explain why the person was wrong to turn us away.

Barnabas asked what response I believed Jesus wanted from me. The question exposed how quickly I had moved from disappointment to judgment. I remembered the Samaritan village that would not receive Jesus because He was travelling toward Jerusalem. John and I asked whether fire should fall on it. Jesus rebuked us, and we went on to another village. The village's refusal did not make destruction righteous.

I also spoke with Elijah afterward because we had invoked his story when we made that request. Scripture records fire falling in a specific confrontation involving soldiers sent to seize him; it does not give me permission to borrow that event whenever someone says no. Elijah heard the account as a 2026 colleague, not as someone who had been there with us.

### Practice agreed

- Record a refusal without assigning a motive.
- Wait one week before proposing another attempt, unless there is a safeguarding concern.
- Ask Peter whether another worker is better placed to follow up.

**Attention:** I can call indignation loyalty when it is really wounded pride.
