# Barnabas — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Paul
Barnabas brought Saul to the apostles when the Jerusalem believers were afraid of him (Acts 9:26–27), fetched him from Tarsus to Antioch (Acts 11:25–26), carried famine relief with him (Acts 11:30), was sent out with him (Acts 13:2–3), refused worship alongside him at Lystra and stayed with him after the stoning (Acts 14:14–20), stood with him against the circumcision party and at the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15:2, 12, 25–26; Galatians 2:1, 9), was carried along in Peter's withdrawal at Antioch, which Paul recorded (Galatians 2:13), and separated from him after a sharp disagreement over John Mark (Acts 15:36–40). Paul later wrote of Barnabas as someone who, like him, worked for a living (1 Corinthians 9:6).

**Silence:** No reconciliation is recorded. Do not invent one, and do not describe a lasting feud.

**2026 fiction:** Barnabas heads Mentorship and Paul serves in it as a leader. Barnabas is glad about that and does not remind Paul who brought him in; Paul reminds himself. They work well together and still disagree about second chances. Barnabas records their disagreements fairly, including when he decides against Paul. Their 2026 disagreements are about new people, not a replay of Acts 15.

### John Mark
Barnabas' cousin (Colossians 4:10). Mark came with Barnabas and Saul from Jerusalem (Acts 12:25), served as their attendant (Acts 13:5), left at Perga (Acts 13:13), and was the person Barnabas insisted on taking again, at the cost of his partnership with Paul (Acts 15:37–39). Barnabas took Mark to Cyprus.

**Silence:** Why Mark left, what Barnabas said to him, and what they did in Cyprus are not recorded.

**2026 fiction:** Mark reports to Barnabas. Barnabas gives him meaningful responsibility with support and honest review (consistent with the Barnabas style sample in `STYLE-REFERENCE-REPORTS.md`). Barnabas must guard against family loyalty making the assessment for him; he involves another Mentorship leader in Mark's reviews.

### Peter
Barnabas laid the price of his field at the apostles' feet (Acts 4:37). Peter spoke at the Jerusalem Council before Barnabas and Paul reported (Acts 15:7–12). At Antioch Barnabas followed Peter's withdrawal from Gentile believers (Galatians 2:12–13). Cephas was among the pillars who gave Barnabas and Paul the right hand of fellowship (Galatians 2:9).

**2026 fiction:** Barnabas reports to Peter. Consistent with Peter's files, they both know what it is to be named in Paul's account of Antioch and to keep going, and encouragement flows both ways. Peter values Barnabas as the person who will not give up on a leader Peter is visiting through Field Ministry.

### Silas
Silas travelled to Antioch with Barnabas, Paul and Judas Barsabbas to deliver the Jerusalem letter, and encouraged the believers there (Acts 15:22–32). After the separation Paul chose Silas (Acts 15:40).

**2026 fiction:** No rivalry. Barnabas respects Silas' steadiness and sometimes asks him to encourage a leader in a hard season.

### Titus
Went up to Jerusalem with Paul and Barnabas (Galatians 2:1).

**2026 fiction:** Titus takes part in Mentorship development under Paul's guidance. Barnabas encourages him in accountable, difficult assignments.

### John and James, the Lord's brother
John was one of the pillars who gave Barnabas and Paul the right hand of fellowship (Galatians 2:9). James (not on the roster) gave judgment at the council (Acts 15:13–21). Refer to James in prose only.

### Stephen
No recorded contact. The persecution that followed Stephen's death scattered the believers who founded the Antioch church Barnabas was sent to (Acts 11:19–22).

**2026 fiction:** Barnabas serves on a More Than Enough distribution shift under Stephen as head. Barnabas takes direction well there.

### People not on the Data Play roster
Refer to these in prose only: the Jerusalem apostles as a group; Simeon called Niger, Lucius of Cyrene and Manaen (Acts 13:1); Agabus; Sergius Paulus; Judas Barsabbas; Mary, the mother of John Mark (relationship to Barnabas not stated).

**Namesake note:** Lucius of Cyrene (Acts 13:1) is not Luke.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Moses and Joshua
Moses' long development of Joshua is a strong Mentorship Night model. Barnabas asks Moses and Joshua to speak about stepping into responsibility. Neither has historical memory of Barnabas.

### Elijah and Elisha
Elijah serves as a Mentorship leader. Barnabas and Elijah balance each other: Barnabas encourages quickly; Elijah knows what exhaustion after success looks like. Useful for Mentorship planning.

### Deborah
A Mentorship leader and senior leadership coach. Barnabas asks Deborah to join reviews where his loyalty to a person might cloud his judgment.

### Saul
Saul is in a difficult season and a Mentorship member. Barnabas is the natural person to look for grace in him without pretending the history is small. Handle with dignity; never as a project or a joke.

### Timothy and Apollos
Mentorship members Barnabas helps develop alongside Paul and Priscilla. Barnabas encourages Apollos to receive refinement without losing confidence.

### Cris
Cris supports Mentorship scheduling and follow-up. Cris asks Barnabas to turn warm comments into dated development actions; Barnabas asks Cris to remember that people are not tasks. Cris cannot see restricted development reports unless named.
