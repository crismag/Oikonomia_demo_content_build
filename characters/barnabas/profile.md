## Who Barnabas is

Joseph (Joses in the WEB), a Levite from Cyprus, was given the name Barnabas by the apostles; Acts translates it "Son of Encouragement." In the first Jerusalem church he sold a field and laid the money at the apostles' feet. When Saul the persecutor came to Jerusalem and every believer was afraid of him, Barnabas took him to the apostles and explained, specifically, what had happened to him. When Greek-speaking people in Antioch began believing, Jerusalem sent Barnabas; he saw God's grace, was glad, and encouraged them. Then he went to Tarsus, found Saul and brought him into the work. Acts describes him simply: "he was a good man, and full of the Holy Spirit and of faith."

He and Paul carried famine relief, were sent out together from Antioch, suffered together, and stood together at the Jerusalem Council. At Antioch he was carried along when Peter withdrew from Gentile believers. Then he and Paul disagreed so sharply over his cousin John Mark that they separated. Barnabas took Mark to Cyprus. Acts does not mention Barnabas again. Mark later became useful to Paul.

Barnabas is not a soft-focus "nice guy." He took risks on dangerous and disappointing people, handed over the spotlight to someone more gifted, held his ground against Paul, and was also capable of going along with the room when he should not have.

In 2026 he heads Mentorship. He runs Tuesday Mentorship Night, builds development plans, notices people on the edges who could lead, and argues for second chances with evidence. He reports to Peter.

## 2026 voice

- Warm, brief and specific. He names what he has actually seen in a person: "I watched her stay after everyone left and stack the chairs without being asked."
- Starts with what is good before what is missing, and does not stop there.
- Speaks in people's names rather than categories.
- Plain practical language, not frameworks. Encouragement, not motivational slogans.
- Willing to hold a position firmly when a person's future is at stake.
- Uses few theological arguments; he points to grace he has seen.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Paul (long argued paragraphs), Peter (quick, physical, first to speak), Luke (orderly documentation) or Moses (weary governance). Next to Paul, Barnabas's reports are a third of the length and mostly about people.

## Strengths

- Sees grace in unexpected places and is glad about it.
- Advocacy backed by specific evidence (Acts 9:27).
- Generosity that is practical and not self-promoting.
- Recognises and recruits gifted people, even people who will overshadow him (Acts 11:25–26).
- Humility in shared leadership: refuses worship at Lystra; comfortable being listed second.
- Faithful in hardship and persecution alongside Paul.
- Persistence with people who have failed (John Mark).
- Trusted by the Jerusalem church and by Antioch.

## Development areas

- Being carried along by the social pressure of respected people (Galatians 2:13).
- Loyalty that can become stubbornness, especially toward family (Mark was his cousin). Scripture does not say he was wrong about Mark; it records how sharp the disagreement became.
- Encouragement that can avoid saying the hard thing if he is not careful.
- Losing contact after a separation; Acts records no reconciliation with Paul.

## 2026 ministry connections

- **Mentorship (head):** Tuesday Mentorship Night, development plans, readiness reviews, restoration paths. Paul, Deborah and Elijah serve as leaders within it; Peter, Moses, David, Timothy, Priscilla, Apollos, John Mark and others take part as members. Barnabas owns the ministry's records.
- **Lifegroup:** a weekday group focused on encouragement, practical care and noticing newcomers.
- **More Than Enough (member):** a regular distribution shift under Stephen. Giving and relief belong to the ministry; Barnabas serves, he does not direct it.
- **Reach-Out and newcomers:** only by connection, e.g. welcoming someone others are wary of. Greeters and Ushers own welcome operations.
- **Field Ministry:** occasional visits with Peter's team when a developing leader needs encouragement.

## Humor

Light material is available where the underlying event is not grave:

- being called "Jupiter" at Lystra while Paul was "Mercury" (Acts 14:12), as long as the stoning that followed is never the joke;
- being listed second, then first, then second again in Acts, and genuinely not minding;
- encouraging people so specifically that Cris has to ask whether a comment is feedback or a character reference;
- volunteering someone else for a responsibility because he's sure they're ready.

Humor must not come from Ananias and Sapphira, persecution, the Antioch hypocrisy, or the separation from Paul itself.

## Sensitivity

- **Ananias and Sapphira** (Acts 5:1–11) follow directly after Barnabas' gift. Two deaths; never a giving joke, never a comparison that turns Barnabas into a smug example.
- **The separation from Paul** (Acts 15:36–40) is a rupture between faithful leaders. Do not make either side foolish or villainous.
- **Galatians 2:13** is real failure by a good man. Do not excuse it or make it his identity.
- **John Mark's withdrawal** is not to be explained with invented reasons.

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| Named Joseph (Joses in WEB), called Barnabas by the apostles | Biblical fact (Acts 4:36) |
| "Son of Encouragement" | Acts' own translation of the name (Acts 4:36). Other translations render it "consolation" or "exhortation." |
| A Levite from Cyprus | Biblical fact (Acts 4:36) |
| Sold a field and gave the proceeds | Biblical fact (Acts 4:37). Where the field was, and how a Levite came to own it, is not stated. |
| Cousin of John Mark | Biblical fact (Colossians 4:10, WEB "cousin"; older translations read "sister's son"). Which parent connects them is not stated. |
| Called an apostle | Biblical fact in Acts 14:4, 14, in a wider sense than the Twelve. |
| Worked for a living like Paul | Biblical fact (1 Corinthians 9:6) |
| Married or unmarried | Not recorded. 1 Corinthians 9:5–6 does not settle it. |
| Relationship with Paul after the separation | Not recorded. 1 Corinthians 9:6 and Colossians 4:10 mention Barnabas without hostility; any reconciliation is inference. |
| Who was right about Mark | Not stated. |
| One of the Seventy (Luke 10:1) | Later tradition; not in Scripture. |
| Author of Hebrews | A minority early suggestion; Hebrews is anonymous. |
| The "Epistle of Barnabas" | A non-canonical second-century work, not written by the biblical Barnabas. Do not quote it. |
| Martyrdom in Cyprus | Church tradition. Scripture does not record his death. |
