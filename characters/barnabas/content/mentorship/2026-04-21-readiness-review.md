### Discussion

Apollos has been serving well in teaching and has asked for more opportunities beyond the Tuesday room. Priscilla named what she has seen: he prepares carefully, opens the Scriptures clearly, and receives a question without treating it as an interruption. She also named the next work plainly. When a listener praises his delivery or contrasts him with another teacher, he needs to redirect the room sooner, before appreciation becomes a banner.

Paul agreed that Apollos is ready to serve, and cautioned that the first wider invitation should be in support of a host leader's existing series, not an Apollos-centred event. That is not a reduction of his gift. It is how the gift helps rather than gathers people around itself.

I asked Apollos what feedback he wanted recorded. He said, “Put down the part about the question. I can answer it; I need to make room for it.” That is a useful answer.

We remembered why private correction matters. In Ephesus, Priscilla and Aquila heard Apollos teach boldly. He was learned, fervent and accurate about what he knew, but his understanding was incomplete. They took him aside and explained the way of God more accurately. Scripture does not give us their private conversation. It does show that Apollos later greatly helped believers in Achaia. We want the same pattern here: clear feedback, no public diminishment, and a real next step.

### Decisions

- Apollos will guest-teach one Radicals session in May at Timothy's invitation, built around Timothy's existing series.
- Priscilla will give feedback within a week using the restricted development record she owns; Apollos, Aquila and Paul remain the named readers.
- Apollos will close the session by naming the host series and inviting questions rather than offering a separate followership pathway.
