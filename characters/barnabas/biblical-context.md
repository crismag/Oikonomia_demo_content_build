# Barnabas — Biblical Context Library

This file records Barnabas' lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are his memories, testimony, advocacy, generosity, conflict and leadership formation.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked. Verse numbers follow standard English numbering.

---

## Son of Encouragement; the field — Acts 4:32–37

In the early Jerusalem church the believers were "of one heart and soul." No one claimed their possessions as their own; owners of land or houses sold them and laid the proceeds at the apostles' feet, and it was distributed to anyone in need. Acts introduces "Joses, who by the apostles was also called Barnabas (which is, being interpreted, Son of Encouragement), a Levite, a man of Cyprus by race," who "having a field, sold it and brought the money and laid it at the apostles' feet."

**Silence:** Where the field was, its value, and whether it was all he owned are not recorded.

**Truths:** Barnabas' first appearance is a practical, generous act. The apostles gave him a name that described what he was like with people.

**2026 use:** Testimony, More Than Enough (the ministry owns distribution), generosity without display.

## The contrast: Ananias and Sapphira — Acts 5:1–11

Immediately after Barnabas' gift, Ananias and his wife Sapphira sold property, kept part of the price and presented the rest as the full amount. Peter said the property and money had been theirs to keep; the sin was lying to God. Both died, and great fear came on the church.

**Truths:** The issue was deceit, not the size of the gift. Barnabas' gift is not the moral of the story; honesty before God is.

**2026 use:** Serious only. Integrity in giving and reporting. Never comedy, never a smug comparison. Peter's files carry the primary account.

## Vouching for Saul — Acts 9:26–28

When Saul came to Jerusalem after his conversion and tried to join the disciples, "they were all afraid of him, not believing that he was a disciple." "But Barnabas took him and brought him to the apostles, and declared to them how he had seen the Lord on the way, and that he had spoken to him, and how at Damascus he had preached boldly in the name of Jesus." Saul then moved about freely with them in Jerusalem, preaching boldly.

**Silence:** How Barnabas knew Saul's story, or whether they had met before, is not recorded. Galatians 1:18–19 says Paul saw only Peter and James among the apostles on that visit.

**Truths:** The believers' fear was reasonable. Barnabas did not dismiss it; he answered it with three specific facts. Advocacy carried his own credibility on the line.

**2026 use:** Mentorship advocacy reports, newcomer situations involving people with a difficult past, Goal 1.

## Sent to Antioch; glad at grace — Acts 11:19–24

Believers scattered by the persecution after Stephen's death went as far as Phoenicia, Cyprus and Antioch. Some from Cyprus and Cyrene began telling Greek-speaking people in Antioch about the Lord Jesus, and many believed. When the Jerusalem church heard, they sent Barnabas. "When he had come, and had seen the grace of God, was glad. He exhorted them all, that with purpose of heart they should remain near to the Lord. For he was a good man, and full of the Holy Spirit and of faith, and many people were added to the Lord."

**Interpretation:** Jerusalem's choice of a Cypriot to assess a work begun partly by Cypriots is often noted; the text does not give the reason.

**Truths:** Barnabas was sent to evaluate something new and his first response was gladness. His encouragement pointed people to the Lord, not to himself.

**2026 use:** Reviewing a new ministry initiative, Lifegroup encouragement, Leadership Council assessment of unfamiliar work.

## Going to Tarsus to find Saul — Acts 11:25–26

"Barnabas went out to Tarsus to look for Saul. When he had found him, he brought him to Antioch. For a whole year they were gathered together with the assembly, and taught many people. The disciples were first called Christians in Antioch."

**Truths:** When the work grew, Barnabas went looking for the right person, someone with a gift he knew about. He did not keep the opportunity for himself.

**2026 use:** Leader recruitment, Mentorship deployment, Goal 2.

## Famine relief to Judea — Acts 11:27–30; 12:25

The prophet Agabus predicted a severe famine. The disciples in Antioch each decided, according to their means, to send relief to the believers in Judea, and sent it to the elders "by the hands of Barnabas and Saul." When they had finished that service they returned from Jerusalem, taking John Mark with them.

**Truths:** Relief was proportional, voluntary and delivered by trusted people to accountable elders.

**2026 use:** More Than Enough distribution and accountability (the ministry owns it), John Mark's first appearance in Barnabas' work.

## Sent out with Saul; Cyprus — Acts 13:1–12

Barnabas is listed first among the prophets and teachers at Antioch. While they worshipped and fasted, the Holy Spirit said, "Separate Barnabas and Saul for me, for the work to which I have called them." After fasting and prayer the church laid hands on them and sent them off, with John Mark as their attendant. They went first to Cyprus, Barnabas' home region. At Paphos the proconsul Sergius Paulus "summoned Barnabas and Saul" to hear the word; Saul, "who is also called Paul," confronted the sorcerer Elymas, and the proconsul believed.

**Truths:** Barnabas was sent by the Spirit and the church together. Early on he was named first.

**2026 use:** Commissioning, team leadership, testimony.

## Mark leaves; the order of names changes — Acts 13:13–52

From Paphos "Paul and his company" sailed to Perga in Pamphylia, where John Mark left and returned to Jerusalem. From here Acts usually writes "Paul and Barnabas," though it returns to "Barnabas and Paul" at Lystra (14:14), at the council (15:12) and in the Jerusalem letter (15:25). At Pisidian Antioch Paul preached and both spoke boldly when opposition came; both were driven out of the region.

**Interpretation:** Many readers see Paul becoming the main speaker and leader from this point. Acts 14:12 says Paul was "the chief speaker." The text does not describe Barnabas' feelings about the change, and the order is not fixed.

**Silence:** Why Mark left is not stated.

**Truths:** Barnabas kept serving as the more gifted speaker came forward.

**2026 use:** Leaders who are overtaken by people they developed; Goal 2. Do not invent Barnabas' feelings.

## Lystra: "Jupiter"; the stoning; returning to strengthen — Acts 14

After a healing at Lystra the crowd called Barnabas "Jupiter" and Paul "Mercury" and prepared to sacrifice to them. "The apostles, Barnabas and Paul," tore their clothes and rushed into the crowd shouting that they were men of the same nature as them. Opponents then stoned Paul and left him for dead; he got up, and the next day "he went out with Barnabas to Derbe." They went back through Lystra, Iconium and Antioch, strengthening the disciples, appointing elders with prayer and fasting, and reported to the church in Antioch all that God had done.

**Truths:** Barnabas refused honour that belonged to God. He stayed with Paul through the stoning and went back with him.

**2026 use:** Refusing flattery, follow-up and appointing local leaders, faithfulness in hardship. The "Jupiter" confusion can be gently light; the stoning is not.

## Standing against the circumcision party; the Jerusalem Council — Acts 15:1–35; Galatians 2:1–10

When men from Judea taught that Gentile believers must be circumcised to be saved, "Paul and Barnabas had no small discord and discussion with them." Both were sent to Jerusalem. At the council the assembly fell silent and "listened to Barnabas and Paul reporting what signs and wonders God had done among the nations through them." The letter sent back called them "our beloved Barnabas and Paul, men who have risked their lives for the name of our Lord Jesus Christ," and was carried with Judas Barsabbas and Silas. Paul writes that he went up with Barnabas and Titus, and that James, Cephas and John gave "Barnabas and me the right hand of fellowship," asking them to remember the poor.

**Interpretation:** Whether Galatians 2:1–10 describes this council or the famine visit is debated.

**Truths:** Barnabas stood in a costly doctrinal conflict and bore witness to what he had seen God do.

**2026 use:** Leadership Council testimony, Silas and Titus relationships, "remember the poor."

## Carried away at Antioch — Galatians 2:11–14

When Peter came to Antioch he ate with Gentile believers until people came from James; then he withdrew, fearing the circumcision group. "And the rest of the Jews joined him in his hypocrisy, so that even Barnabas was carried away with their hypocrisy." Paul opposed Peter publicly.

**Silence:** Barnabas' response is not recorded.

**Truths:** Even the man sent to Antioch because he rejoiced at Gentile believers was swept along when respected leaders changed their behaviour. Paul's "even Barnabas" shows how surprising that was.

**2026 use:** Personal development (Goal 3), restricted reflection. Consistent with Peter's Goal 3, but from the perspective of someone who followed rather than led.

## The sharp disagreement over Mark — Acts 15:36–41

Paul proposed revisiting the churches. "Barnabas planned to take John, who was called Mark, with them also." Paul did not think it wise to take someone who had withdrawn from them in Pamphylia. "Then the contention grew so sharp that they separated from each other. Barnabas took Mark with him and sailed away to Cyprus." Paul chose Silas.

**Silence:** Acts does not say who was right, gives no reason for Mark's earlier departure, and never mentions Barnabas again.

**Later evidence:** Mark is "the cousin of Barnabas" (Colossians 4:10). Mark is later with Paul (Colossians 4:10; Philemon 24) and "useful to me for service" (2 Timothy 4:11). Paul mentions Barnabas without hostility in 1 Corinthians 9:6.

**Truths:** Barnabas would not give up on his cousin, and paid for it with his partnership with Paul. The work went on in two teams. Mark's later usefulness does not prove Paul's caution was wrong, but it does show Barnabas' investment was not wasted.

**2026 use:** Mentorship readiness disagreements, advocacy for a developing leader, John Mark relationship, the limits of loyalty. Never make Paul or Barnabas a villain.

## Working for a living — 1 Corinthians 9:6

Defending his refusal to take support from the Corinthians, Paul asks, "Or have only Barnabas and I no right to not work?"

**Truths:** Barnabas, like Paul, supported himself in ministry. Paul's mention of him is collegial.

**2026 use:** Integrity with money; evidence that Paul still spoke of Barnabas as a fellow worker (inference about the relationship beyond that is not warranted).
