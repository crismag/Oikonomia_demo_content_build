## Who Andrew is

Andrew was a fisherman from Bethsaida, Simon Peter's brother (John 1:40, 44; Mark 1:16). Before he followed Jesus he was a disciple of John the Baptist. When John pointed to Jesus and said, "Behold, the Lamb of God!", Andrew and another disciple followed Jesus and spent the day with Him. The first thing Andrew did was find his brother Simon and tell him, "We have found the Messiah!" He brought Simon to Jesus (John 1:35–42). Jesus later called Simon and Andrew from their nets by the Sea of Galilee (Mark 1:16–18). Jesus healed Simon's mother-in-law in "the house of Simon and Andrew" (Mark 1:29–31).

Andrew is always named among the first four of the Twelve, but he was not one of the three Jesus took into Jairus' house, up the mountain of the Transfiguration or further into Gethsemane. Scripture records no comment from Andrew about that, and Data Play does not invent one.

The Fourth Gospel records Andrew three times, and each time he brings someone or something to Jesus: his brother; a boy with five barley loaves and two fish, with the honest question "what are these among so many?"; and, with Philip, a group of Greeks who wanted to see Jesus (John 1:41–42; 6:8–9; 12:20–22). In Mark he joins Peter, James and John in asking Jesus privately about the temple's destruction (Mark 13:3–4). He is named among the apostles praying in the upper room after the ascension (Acts 1:13). After that Scripture is silent about him.

In 2026 Andrew is a Greeters and Ushers leader under Mordecai. He notices who has walked in, remembers names, and walks people over to someone they should meet. He co-leads a Thursday Lifegroup with Philip that many newcomers first attend. He is Peter's brother, and he doesn't need to be Peter.

## 2026 voice

- Friendly, unhurried and specific. He talks about people by name and what they need next.
- Introductions, not speeches: "You should meet my brother." "There's someone here who can help."
- Honest about limits. He will bring the small thing and say plainly that it looks small.
- Short, practical notes. His reports read like a list of people and next steps.
- Rarely talks about himself.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Peter (quick, physical, first to speak), Philip (numbers, estimates and practical questions), Mordecai (vigilant community awareness), Cornelius (cross-cultural hospitality from the other side of the door) or Barnabas (encouragement and advocacy for leaders). Andrew's gift is the introduction.

## Strengths

- Recognises who Jesus is and tells the people closest to him first.
- Brings people personally rather than only telling them where to go.
- Notices small resources and raises them, even while unsure what good they are.
- Works well with others on a handoff (Philip and the Greeks).
- Content to serve without being in the inner three. (Scripture shows the pattern; it records no statement of his feelings about it.)
- Earlier spiritual hunger: he was already following John the Baptist.

## Development areas

- "What are these among so many?" Seeing the resource and doubting it in the same breath (John 6:9).
- Scripture records no failure specific to Andrew beyond what all the disciples shared: sleeping disciples, fleeing at the arrest (Mark 14:50). Do not invent one.
- In 2026 fiction: introducing people and assuming someone else will follow up; being so comfortable out of the spotlight that he doesn't raise a concern he should.

## Humor

Light material is available:

- introducing someone to Peter, then to John, then to Martha, then to the whole Lifegroup, before the newcomer has found a seat;
- quietly submitting the one practical suggestion in a planning meeting while everyone else debates;
- being introduced at events as "Peter's brother" and correcting nobody.

Humor must not trivialise newcomers' needs, the hungry crowd, or anyone's grief.

## Sensitivity

- Newcomers' personal circumstances belong in restricted entries or referrals, not in shared Greeters reports.
- Do not invent rivalry or resentment between Andrew and Peter. Scripture gives none.
- The feeding of the five thousand is a sign about Jesus, not a food-logistics gag.
