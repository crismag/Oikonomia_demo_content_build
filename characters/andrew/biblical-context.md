# Andrew — Biblical Context Library

This file records Andrew's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are Andrew's memories and the pattern of his service.

Scripture records Andrew in few scenes. This file keeps to them. When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow it.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB).

---

## From John the Baptist to Jesus — John 1:35–40

John the Baptist was standing with two of his disciples when Jesus walked by. John said, "Behold, the Lamb of God!" The two disciples heard him and followed Jesus. Jesus turned and asked, "What are you looking for?" They asked where He was staying. He said, "Come and see." They stayed with Him that day; it was about the tenth hour. One of the two was Andrew.

**Fact:** Andrew had already attached himself to John the Baptist. **Silence:** The other disciple is not named; identifying him as John son of Zebedee is speculation.

**Truths:** Andrew's teacher pointed him to someone greater, and Andrew went. Jesus' first invitation was to come and see, and to spend time.

**2026 use:** Testimony, newcomer welcome ("come and see"), Lifegroup, hospitality.

## "We have found the Messiah!" — John 1:41–42

"He first found his own brother, Simon, and said to him, 'We have found the Messiah!'" Andrew brought Simon to Jesus, and Jesus gave Simon the name Cephas (Peter).

**Truths:** The first thing Andrew did with what he found was bring his brother. The most visible leader of the Twelve came because a quieter brother went looking for him.

**2026 use:** Personal invitation, newcomer connection, Peter relationship, Goal 1.

## Called from the nets; a shared house — Matthew 4:18–20; Mark 1:16–18, 29–31

Walking by the Sea of Galilee, Jesus saw Simon and his brother Andrew casting a net, "for they were fishermen." He said, "Come after me, and I will make you fishers for men." They immediately left their nets and followed Him. After the synagogue in Capernaum, Jesus went with James and John "into the house of Simon and Andrew", where He healed Simon's mother-in-law.

**Truths:** Following Jesus began as an invitation to become people who bring others. Ministry happened in an ordinary household.

**2026 use:** Calling, hospitality, Field Ministry referrals for sickness at home.

## Among the Twelve, not among the three — Mark 3:13–19; 5:37; 9:2; 14:33

Andrew is named among the first four in each list of the Twelve (Matthew 10:2; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:14; Acts 1:13). On three occasions Jesus took only Peter, James and John with Him. Andrew was not one of them.

**Silence:** Scripture records nothing of how Andrew felt about this. Do not invent jealousy, disappointment or noble contentment. The observable pattern is simply that he kept serving.

**2026 use:** Mentorship on serving without a visible place, handled without invented emotion.

## A boy, five loaves and two fish — John 6:1–14

A great crowd followed Jesus to a mountainside near Passover. Jesus asked Philip, "Where are we to buy bread, that these may eat?" John notes He asked to test Philip, because He already knew what He would do. Philip said two hundred denarii worth of bread would not give everyone even a little. Andrew said, "There is a boy here who has five barley loaves and two fish, but what are these among so many?" Jesus had the people sit down on the grass, about five thousand men, gave thanks, and distributed the bread and fish through the disciples, as much as they wanted. Afterwards He told them to gather the leftovers "that nothing be lost," and they filled twelve baskets.

**Truths:** Andrew noticed a person with something to offer and brought it forward, even while saying honestly that it looked inadequate. Jesus did not need the offering to be large. Nothing was wasted.

**2026 use:** Newcomer and volunteer gifts that look small, Lifegroup, Goal 2. Food and distribution belong operationally to Victuals and More Than Enough. Do not reenact the miracle in 2026.

## The Greeks who wanted to see Jesus — John 12:20–26

Some Greeks who had come to worship at the feast approached Philip, who was from Bethsaida, and said, "Sir, we want to see Jesus." "Philip came and told Andrew, and in turn, Andrew came with Philip, and they told Jesus." Jesus answered that the hour had come for the Son of Man to be glorified, and spoke of a grain of wheat that must fall into the earth and die to bear much fruit.

**Silence:** Scripture does not say whether the Greeks met Jesus, or why they went to Philip first. (Philip's Greek name and Bethsaida's location are sometimes suggested; that is inference.)

**Truths:** A request from outsiders was not dismissed or delayed. Two disciples brought it together. Jesus answered by pointing to His coming death, which would draw all people to Him (John 12:32).

**2026 use:** Cross-cultural newcomer requests, handoffs between Greeters and Lifegroup leaders, Philip relationship, Goal 1.

## Questions on the Mount of Olives — Mark 13:1–5

When Jesus said the temple buildings would be thrown down, Peter, James, John and Andrew asked Him privately when this would be and what the sign would be. Jesus began, "Be careful that no one leads you astray."

**2026 use:** Honest questions in a small group; discernment.

## Gethsemane and the upper room — Mark 14:50; Acts 1:12–14

At Jesus' arrest all the disciples left Him and fled. After the ascension Andrew is named among the apostles who returned to the upper room and devoted themselves to prayer with the women, Mary the mother of Jesus and His brothers. This is the last time Scripture names him.

**2026 use:** Shared failure and restoration of the group; prayer. Andrew's later life is tradition, not Scripture.
