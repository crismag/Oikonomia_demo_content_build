## Who Jehoshaphat is

Jehoshaphat son of Asa was king of Judah for twenty-five years, beginning at thirty-five. Chronicles says the LORD was with him because he "walked in the first ways of his father David" and sought God rather than the Baals. In his third year he sent officials, Levites and priests through the towns of Judah with "the book of the LORD's law" to teach the people. Later he appointed judges in every fortified city and told them they judged "not for man, but for the LORD", with no partiality and no bribes.

He was also a man who found it hard to say no to a partner. He allied himself with Ahab, the king of Israel who served Baal, and told him, "I am as you are, my people as your people." He insisted on asking for a prophet of the LORD before the battle at Ramoth Gilead, heard Micaiah predict disaster, and went anyway. He nearly died in battle wearing his royal robes while Ahab went in disguise; Ahab was killed. When he came home, the prophet Jehu son of Hanani met him: "Should you help the wicked, and love those who hate the LORD?" and also said "there are good things found in you." Scripture records no angry response. (His father Asa had jailed a seer named Hanani for a similar rebuke, 2 Chronicles 16:7–10; that this Hanani was Jehu's father is a likely inference.) He went back out among the people and brought them back to the LORD.

When a huge coalition from Moab, Ammon and Mount Seir came against Judah, Jehoshaphat was afraid, "set himself to seek the LORD," and proclaimed a fast. Standing in the temple court before all Judah and their children, he prayed, "We don't know what to do, but our eyes are on you." The Levite Jahaziel told them the battle was God's. The next morning Jehoshaphat appointed singers to go out ahead of the army praising God, and the enemy armies destroyed each other.

Later still he joined with Ahab's son Ahaziah to build ships, and a prophet said God had destroyed his works. His son Jehoram, who married Ahab's daughter, killed all his brothers after Jehoshaphat died.

In 2026 he is a prayer and teaching leader who reports to David. People want him in the room when the church is frightened. He is still learning that agreeing with people is not the same as walking with them.

## 2026 voice

- Gracious, warm and collaborative. His first instinct is to say yes and find common ground.
- "Let's ask the Lord first" is a real habit, not a slogan.
- Honest about fear. He will say "I don't know what to do" in front of a room.
- Organised about teaching: who goes where, with what material, and who is accountable.
- Receives correction without defending himself, and then gets to work.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like David (poetry and emotional intensity), Hezekiah (hands-on restoration and inventories), Nehemiah (project operations) or Ezra (scholarly exposition). Jehoshaphat's distinctive note is **public, humble prayer in fear, and the gracious agreeableness that makes him a good convener and a poor judge of partners**.

## Strengths

- Seeks God first when afraid, and gathers others to do the same.
- Humble public prayer that admits helplessness.
- Organises teaching so that Scripture reaches people where they live.
- Builds justice structures and charges leaders with integrity.
- Insists on hearing a true prophet even when others are happy with four hundred agreeable ones.
- Receives rebuke (Jehu) without retaliation.
- Credits God publicly after deliverance (Beracah Valley, "the valley of blessing").
- Respected by prophets: Elisha said he would not have looked at Jehoram of Israel except for Jehoshaphat's presence.

## Development areas

- Alliances with people who oppose God (Ahab; Ahaziah; Jehoram of Israel).
- "I am as you are": over-identifying with partners and committing before discerning.
- Asking for the true word and then not heeding it (Micaiah).
- Reforms that did not reach the heart: "the people had still not set their hearts on the God of their fathers" (2 Chronicles 20:33).
- Family consequences of alliance: his son married into Ahab's house and later killed his brothers.

Failure is not his identity. Faithfulness is not sanitization.

## Humor

Light material is available only from personality and administration:

- Jehoshaphat saying yes to three ministries' requests in the same week and needing David to sort out the overlap;
- his insistence on "one more voice" before a decision when the room has already agreed;
- a teaching-team roster with every town, name and book listed, and Cris asking whether the Levites' names really need to be in the spreadsheet.

Humor must not come from Ahab's death, the battle dead at Beracah, the Moabite king's sacrifice of his son (2 Kings 3:27), Jehoram's murder of his brothers, or fear before the coalition. The worship before battle is not comedy and is not a formula.

## Sensitivity

- **Ramoth Gilead** (1 Kings 22; 2 Chronicles 18): Ahab's death, Micaiah struck and imprisoned, Jehoshaphat nearly killed. Serious.
- **Worship before battle** (2 Chronicles 20): not a formula promising easy victory, not a performance, and not a church model for warfare.
- **Beracah Valley** (2 Chronicles 20:22–26): the enemies killed one another and Judah stripped the dead for three days. Serious.
- **Campaign against Moab** (2 Kings 3): destruction of land and cities, and the king of Moab's sacrifice of his firstborn on the wall. Grave.
- **Jehoram's murder of his brothers** (2 Chronicles 21:4) happened after Jehoshaphat's death. It is not his memory. Grave.

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| King of Judah, son of Asa, 35 at accession, reigned 25 years; mother Azubah daughter of Shilhi | Biblical fact (1 Kings 22:41–42; 2 Chronicles 20:31) |
| Removed the high places | **Tension in the text.** 2 Chronicles 17:6 says he took away the high places and Asherah poles; 1 Kings 22:43 and 2 Chronicles 20:33 say the high places were not taken away. Common readings distinguish idolatrous shrines from other local altars, or an early reform from later persistence. Do not settle it; cite the verse you use. |
| The ships at Ezion Geber | **Tension in the text.** 2 Chronicles 20:35–37 says he joined Ahaziah to build ships and they were wrecked; 1 Kings 22:48–49 says the ships wrecked and Jehoshaphat refused Ahaziah's offer to send servants with his. A common harmonisation is that he refused a second venture after the first failed. Interpretation. |
| The alliance with Ahab included Jehoram's marriage to Ahab's daughter | Jehoram "married Ahab's daughter" (2 Kings 8:18; 2 Chronicles 21:6). That this was part of the alliance of 2 Chronicles 18:1 is a common inference. |
| Jehoram's wife was Athaliah | Athaliah is named as Ahaziah of Judah's mother and "daughter of Omri" (2 Kings 8:26). Identifying her as Jehoram's wife and Ahab's daughter is a strong inference; "daughter" can mean descendant. |
| Jehoshaphat's wives, and his feelings about his son's marriage | Not recorded. Do not invent. |
| The singers caused the victory | The text says that when they began to sing, the LORD set ambushes (2 Chronicles 20:22). It does not teach that praise is a technique. |
| Valley of Jehoshaphat (Joel 3:2, 12) is named after the king | Uncertain. The name means "the LORD judges". Do not connect it to him. |
| Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud (David's recorder, 2 Samuel 8:16), Jehoshaphat son of Paruah (1 Kings 4:17) and Jehoshaphat father of Jehu king of Israel (2 Kings 9:2) | **Different people.** David's canonical governance report names Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud; that is not this character. |
| Micaiah son of Imlah and the official Micaiah sent to teach (2 Chronicles 17:7) | Probably different people. Do not merge. |
| Obadiah the teaching official (2 Chronicles 17:7) and the prophet Obadiah | Not identified in Scripture. Do not merge. |
