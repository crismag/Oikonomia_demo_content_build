## 2026-04-21 — Partnership Review: I Asked Before I Agreed

**Record type:** Leadership / Partnership Discernment Review  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** David, Elisha  
**Status:** Partnership Declined  
**Tags:** #serious #reflective #humility #discernment

I was ready to confirm a joint initiative before the responsibilities, safeguarding route, and reporting line were clear. Elisha said the proposal asked our church to carry a public commitment without the authority to make the necessary decisions. David agreed that the concern was not a lack of goodwill; it was an unclear partnership.

We declined the proposal and offered to reconsider a revised one with named ownership, a review date, and a written care pathway. I did not enjoy writing the decline, but I did not need to collect more agreeable voices until I felt permitted to say yes.

At Ramoth Gilead I asked Ahab whether there was a prophet of the LORD to consult. Micaiah gave the unwelcome answer: the campaign would end in disaster. He was struck and imprisoned, and I went into battle anyway, nearly dying while Ahab disguised himself. Scripture also records Jehu's rebuke when I returned: I had helped the wicked, though good things were still found in me. This current review is not an ancient alliance replayed in 2026; it is a present exercise in heeding counsel before a commitment is made.

**Attention:** A warm relationship must not substitute for a clear agreement.  
**Attention to:** Jehoshaphat  
**Related goal:** Goal 1 — Discern before I say "I am as you are"  
**Related goal:** Goal 2 — Heed the unwelcome answer  
**Biblical account:** 1 Kings 22:5–33; 2 Chronicles 19:1–3.

### Comments

**David:** Thank you for recording the decline plainly. A careful no is not disloyalty to the people who made the request.

**Elisha:** Counsel is useful only if it can change the decision.

## 2026-06-03 — Wednesday Lifegroup: Prayer That Does Not Pretend We Know

**Oikonomia section:** Lifegroup Gathering  
**Led by:** Jehoshaphat  
**Venue:** Fellowship hall  
**Attendance:** Esther, Ezra, Miriam  
**Tags:** #devotional #reflective #faith #humility

We met during a tense week for several ministries. I did not bring a forecast or promise a particular outcome. Instead, I explained why the prayer in 2 Chronicles matters to me. A large coalition had come against Judah; I was afraid, called a fast, and prayed publicly that we had no power against the threat and did not know what to do, but that our eyes were on God. Families stood together before the LORD.

The group noticed that this was neither passivity nor a formula. Judah still assembled; a word came through Jahaziel, a Levite in the gathering; and the next morning the people acted in response. Miriam helpfully insisted that the singers appointed before battle were not a technique for controlling God. We agreed that present praise must be honest response, not spiritual pressure on people who are afraid.

### Discussion

What practical preparation belongs beside prayer, and how can a leader admit uncertainty without transferring panic to everyone else?

### Prayer

**Visibility:** Lifegroup leaders

For courage to name what we do not know, wisdom to take the next faithful action, and freedom to praise without pretending every outcome is already known.

### Follow-up

**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Esther, Ezra

Esther will identify any current advocacy concern that merits a focused prayer response. Ezra will prepare a short Scripture guide for leaders who need help distinguishing prayer from avoidance.

**Related goal:** Goal 4 — Lead prayer in fear without turning it into a formula  
**Biblical account:** 2 Chronicles 20:1–22.
