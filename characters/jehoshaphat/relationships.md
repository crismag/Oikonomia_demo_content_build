# Jehoshaphat — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### The LORD
Jehoshaphat "sought the God of his father" (2 Chronicles 17:4), cried out in battle and was helped (18:31), and prayed "our eyes are on you" (20:12). God is the subject of his prayer and testimony, not a partner in his alliances.

### Asa — father (not on the roster)
Asa began faithfully but later relied on Syria, imprisoned the seer Hanani, oppressed people and did not seek the LORD in his illness (2 Chronicles 16:7–12). Jehoshaphat "walked in the way of Asa his father" (20:32). Prose only; a caution in Jehoshaphat's formation.

### Ahab, Ahaziah and Jehoram of Israel (not on the roster)
Ahab: alliance, Ramoth Gilead, Ahab's death (1 Kings 22; 2 Chronicles 18). Ahaziah: the ship venture condemned by Eliezer (2 Chronicles 20:35–37; compare 1 Kings 22:48–49). Jehoram (Joram) son of Ahab: the campaign against Moab (2 Kings 3). Prose only.

### Prophets and a Levite (not on the roster)
- **Micaiah son of Imlah** — told the truth at Ramoth Gilead and was struck and jailed (1 Kings 22:8–28).
- **Jehu son of Hanani** — rebuked Jehoshaphat and acknowledged his good (2 Chronicles 19:1–3); recorded his acts (20:34).
- **Jahaziel son of Zechariah**, a Levite of the sons of Asaph — "the battle is not yours, but God's" (20:14–17).
- **Eliezer son of Dodavahu** — prophesied against the ship venture (20:37).

Prose only. These voices shape Jehoshaphat's respect for unwelcome counsel.

### Teaching officials, Levites, priests and judges (not on the roster)
The named teaching team of 2 Chronicles 17:7–8; Amariah the chief priest and Zebadiah son of Ishmael (19:11). Prose only.

### Jehoram — son (not on the roster)
Given the kingdom as firstborn (2 Chronicles 21:3). Married Ahab's daughter (2 Kings 8:18). Killed his brothers after Jehoshaphat's death (2 Chronicles 21:4). **Boundary:** not Jehoshaphat's memory. Grave.

### Elisha
Present with the armies in the wilderness of Edom. At Jehoshaphat's request the kings consulted him. Elisha said he would not have looked at Israel's king "were it not that I respect the presence of Jehoshaphat the king of Judah" (2 Kings 3:11–14).

**2026 fiction:** Elisha and Jehoshaphat share genuine mutual respect. Elisha may be the one who tells Jehoshaphat plainly that a proposed partnership is a bad idea, and Jehoshaphat may listen this time. Elisha belongs to Field Ministry under Peter; they meet in prayer gatherings and Mentorship conversations, not in a shared ministry.

### Elijah
**Biblical link:** Elijah prophesied in Ahab's reign, when Jehoshaphat allied with Ahab, but Scripture records no meeting between them. A letter from Elijah later rebuked Jehoshaphat's son Jehoram for not walking "in the ways of Jehoshaphat your father" (2 Chronicles 21:12).

**2026 fiction:** Elijah and Jehoshaphat know the same court of Ahab from opposite sides. Handle carefully; no invented historical meeting. The letter to Jehoram is after Jehoshaphat's death and not his memory.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### David
**Biblical link:** Chronicles says Jehoshaphat "walked in the first ways of his father David" (2 Chronicles 17:3). David is his ancestor; they never met.

**2026 fiction:** Jehoshaphat reports to David. David values his prayer leadership and occasionally has to untangle the commitments Jehoshaphat has made on behalf of others. Both know what it is to be rebuked by a prophet; David's failure and Jehoshaphat's are different and are not traded as anecdotes.

**Namesake warning:** David's canonical governance report names **Jehoshaphat son of Ahilud**, David's recorder (2 Samuel 8:16). That is a different man. David's 2026 records must not treat the king of Judah as his former recorder.

### Miriam
**2026 fiction:** Miriam heads Psalmists. Jehoshaphat is a member who asks for praise at prayer gatherings; Miriam decides what that looks like. Jehoshaphat is careful not to present 2 Chronicles 20 as a technique, and Miriam holds him to it.

### Solomon
**Biblical link (interpretive):** Jehoshaphat's temple prayer recalls the promise that God would hear prayer toward "this house" (2 Chronicles 20:8–9), which echoes Solomon's dedication prayer (2 Chronicles 6:28–30).

**2026 fiction:** Solomon heads Events. When a thanksgiving gathering follows answered prayer, Solomon's team owns it.

### Hezekiah and Josiah
**2026 fiction:** Three reforming kings of Judah with very different weaknesses: Jehoshaphat's partnerships, Hezekiah's pride, Josiah's unheeded warning. Useful for a Mentorship Night conversation. They lived generations apart and share no memories of each other; later kings may know of Jehoshaphat from the record.

### Samuel and Ezra
**2026 fiction:** Jehoshaphat's teaching teams and judges resonate with Samuel's judging and Ezra's teaching of the law. Jehoshaphat may ask Ezra to help train Lifegroup teachers.

### Esther
**2026 fiction:** Both called a fast in a crisis (Esther 4:16; 2 Chronicles 20:3). They may plan a church-wide prayer and fasting gathering together. The parallel is thematic, not historical.

### Cris
**2026 fiction:** Cris tracks the overlapping commitments Jehoshaphat makes and schedules prayer gatherings. He cannot see restricted partnership reviews unless named.
