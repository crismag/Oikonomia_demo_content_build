# Hezekiah — Notable Topics

Use these as source topics for later Oikonomia records; they are not prompts and do not imply exclusive ownership. Fuller retellings, truths and fact/inference notes are in `biblical-context.md`.

## Restoration and reform
- **After Ahaz: a shut temple** — 2 Chronicles 28:22–25; 29:3. Inheriting locked doors and broken vessels; starting in the first month of the first year.
- **Temple reopening and reform** — 2 Chronicles 29–31. Facilities, worship restoration, staffing, contributions and organization.
- **Sixteen days of cleansing** — 2 Chronicles 29:3–19. People consecrated first, then the house from the inside out; work shared and reported complete.
- **Worship restored "according to the commandment of David"** — 2 Chronicles 29:25–30. Levites with David's instruments; songs of David and Asaph; willing-hearted offerings.
- **Too few priests; Levites step in** — 2 Chronicles 29:34. Covering staffing gaps with willing teams; not a judgment on any 2026 group.
- **Nehushtan** — 2 Kings 18:4. Breaking the bronze serpent Moses made because people burned incense to it; removing a revered object that became an idol. Moses has no memory of this.

## Invitation and provision
- **Passover invitation** — 2 Chronicles 30. Large event, outreach across regions, mixed responses, prayer for participants.
- **Moving the date; mocked couriers; some came** — 2 Chronicles 30:2–11. Flexibility for the purpose; invitation across an old division; ridicule as a cost.
- **"May the good LORD pardon everyone who sets his heart to seek God"** — 2 Chronicles 30:18–20. Praying for imperfectly prepared people instead of turning them away.
- **Seven more days of joy** — 2 Chronicles 30:23–26. Extended celebration; "nothing like this" since Solomon.
- **Contribution/storage administration** — 2 Chronicles 31. More-than-enough provision, rooms, officers, distribution.
- **Named, trusted officers; fair distribution** — 2 Chronicles 31:11–19. Conaniah, Shimei, Kore; "to the great as well as to the small."

## The Assyrian crisis
- **Paying Sennacherib with temple gold** — 2 Kings 18:13–16. Stripping the doors he had overlaid to buy safety; it did not end the threat. Serious.
- **Assyrian threat / Sennacherib** — 2 Kings 18–19; Isaiah 36–37. Crisis communication, intimidation, prayer, Isaiah counsel.
- **Water conduit and defenses** — 2 Kings 20:20; 2 Chronicles 32. Facilities, infrastructure, preparation under threat. Scripture records the pool, the conduit and Gihon's waters brought west of the City of David; identifying this with the Siloam tunnel is archaeology, not Scripture. *(Corrected from "water tunnel".)*
- **"There is a greater one with us than with him"** — 2 Chronicles 32:6–8. Encouraging frightened people while preparing practically.
- **Rabshakeh at the wall; "Don't answer him"** — 2 Kings 18:17–37. Hostile messaging in the people's language; leaders protecting people from a public argument. Threats of famine are serious.
- **Sackcloth; sending to Isaiah** — 2 Kings 19:1–7. "The children have come to the point of birth, and there is no strength to deliver them."
- **The letter spread before the LORD** — 2 Kings 19:14–19. Bringing the actual threatening document to prayer.
- **Deliverance and the 185,000** — 2 Kings 19:35–37; 2 Chronicles 32:21–23. God's answer; grave loss of life; honour that followed became a test.
- **Isaiah's warning about looking to preparations** — Isaiah 22:8–11. Interpretive link to this period; the passage does not name Hezekiah.

## Illness, pride and legacy
- **Illness and recovery** — 2 Kings 20; Isaiah 38. Mortality, prayer, healing, testimony.
- **"I have seen your tears"** — 2 Kings 20:1–7. A faithful man told he would die, weeping and praying; fifteen years added; figs on the boil.
- **Choosing the harder sign** — 2 Kings 20:8–11. The shadow back ten steps on the sundial of Ahaz. Not to be reenacted.
- **Hezekiah's written song** — Isaiah 38:9–20. Honest fear of death, then praise; "The father shall make known your truth to the children."
- **Babylonian envoys / showing treasures** — 2 Kings 20:12–19. Pride, disclosure, security, and long-term consequences.
- **"If peace and truth will be in my days"** — 2 Kings 20:19; Isaiah 39:8. Short horizon; tone debated.
- **Heart lifted up; humbled himself; tested** — 2 Chronicles 32:25–26, 31. Pride after blessing named plainly; humility mattered; consequence remained.
- **The men of Hezekiah copied Solomon's proverbs** — Proverbs 25:1. Preserving wisdom for later generations.
- **Honoured at death; Manasseh succeeds** — 2 Chronicles 32:32–33; 2 Kings 21:1–3. Manasseh's reign is not Hezekiah's memory; grave.
- **Remembered for sparing Micah** — Jeremiah 26:18–19. Receiving a hard prophecy without killing the prophet protected Jeremiah later.
