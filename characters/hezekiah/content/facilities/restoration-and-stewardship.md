**Record type:** Facilities / Restoration Completion Report

**Shared with:** Nehemiah, Stephen  
**Status:** Handover Complete  
**Tags:** #administrative #gratitude #hope #obedience

The south storage room has been cleared, repaired, labelled, and handed to More Than Enough for receiving supplies. The sequence mattered: we removed unusable material, repaired the door and shelving, completed the inventory, and then asked Stephen's team to test whether the room supported the work they actually do. They confirmed that receiving can happen without blocking the distribution route.

When I opened and repaired the doors of the LORD's house, the priests and Levites first consecrated themselves and then removed what did not belong. They began on the first day of the month, reached the porch on the eighth, and completed the cleansing on the sixteenth. The work did not end with a clean room; it moved into worship. That ancient restoration is not a modern storeroom project, but it teaches me that reopening a space is only complete when the people who need it can use it rightly.

## 2026-08-10 — Visitor Access and the Temptation to Show Everything

**Record type:** Leadership / Stewardship and Access Review  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Nehemiah, Mordecai  
**Status:** Guideline Awaiting Approval  
**Tags:** #reflective #serious #humility #accountability

A prospective donor asked for a broad tour of the church's work areas, including equipment storage and receiving. The request was not hostile. Even so, the right response is not to prove our competence by opening every door. I have proposed a visitor route that shows public ministry spaces and explains the work, while storage, sensitive records, and receiving remain accessible only to the teams who need them.

After I recovered from illness, envoys from Babylon came with a letter and gift. Scripture says I showed them everything in my house: silver, gold, spices, oil, armoury, and all that was found in my treasuries. Isaiah confronted me, and Chronicles says my heart had become proud. The report does not require us to distrust every visitor, nor does it authorize a modern rule by itself. It does require me to admit that being pleased by interest can make disclosure feel harmless.

This is a 2026 access decision, not a claim that the visitor is Babylonian or that the church's supplies are royal treasure.

**Approval requested:** Visitor access and storeroom-route guideline — from Nehemiah  
**Related goal:** Goal 3 — Steward what is entrusted without showing it off  
**Biblical account:** 2 Kings 20:12–19; 2 Chronicles 32:25–26, 31.
