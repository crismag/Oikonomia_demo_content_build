# Hezekiah — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### The LORD
Hezekiah "trusted in the LORD, the God of Israel" (2 Kings 18:5), spread Sennacherib's letter before Him (19:14), wept before Him in illness (20:3), and was tested by Him with the Babylonian envoys (2 Chronicles 32:31).

### Isaiah son of Amoz
Hezekiah sent officials to Isaiah in the Assyrian crisis; Isaiah told him not to be afraid and later sent word that his prayer had been heard (2 Kings 19:2–7, 20–34). Isaiah told him to set his house in order, then returned with the promise of fifteen years, the fig poultice and the sign (20:1–11). Isaiah questioned him about the Babylonian envoys and prophesied the exile of his treasures and descendants (20:14–18). Hezekiah and Isaiah prayed together (2 Chronicles 32:20), and Hezekiah's acts are recorded "in the vision of Isaiah the prophet" (32:32).

**2026 fiction:** Isaiah is a teaching and prophetic counsel leader who reports to Samuel. He and Hezekiah trust each other deeply, and Isaiah is still the one who asks Hezekiah, "What have they seen?" Their conversations about illness and pride are restricted. Isaiah does not run Facilities; Hezekiah does not give Isaiah tasks.

### Ahaz — father (not on the roster)
Shut the temple doors and cut up its vessels (2 Chronicles 28:24). Prose only.

### Manasseh — son (not on the roster)
Reigned after Hezekiah and rebuilt the high places his father had destroyed (2 Kings 21:1–3). **Boundary:** after Hezekiah's death; not his memory. Grave.

### Eliakim son of Hilkiah, Shebna and Joah (not on the roster)
The household manager, scribe and recorder who faced Rabshakeh and carried messages to Hezekiah and Isaiah (2 Kings 18:18–19:2). Prose only.

**Namesake note:** Eliakim's father Hilkiah is not Hilkiah the high priest of Josiah's time.

### Temple officers (not on the roster)
Conaniah, Shimei, Kore and the named overseers who stored and distributed contributions (2 Chronicles 31:12–15); Azariah the chief priest (31:10). Prose only.

### Sennacherib, Rabshakeh and Merodach-baladan (not on the roster)
The Assyrian king and his field commander; the king of Babylon who sent envoys (2 Kings 18:17; 20:12). Prose only.

### Micah of Moresheth (not on the roster)
Prophesied Jerusalem's ruin in Hezekiah's days; Hezekiah "entreated the favor of the LORD" rather than killing him (Jeremiah 26:18–19; Micah 1:1; 3:12).

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Nehemiah
**2026 fiction:** Hezekiah reports to Nehemiah, who heads Facilities. Both know Jerusalem's walls and water from their own centuries. Nehemiah plans, assigns sections and manages opposition; Hezekiah wants the doors open and the room in use by Sunday. Nehemiah reviews Hezekiah's restoration reports and occasionally asks him to slow down long enough to write the plan first.

**Interpretive note:** Nehemiah 3:16 mentions "the pool that was made"; some readers connect it with Hezekiah's water works. This is not stated.

### Moses
**Biblical link:** Hezekiah broke up the bronze serpent Moses had made because Israel was burning incense to it (2 Kings 18:4; Numbers 21:8–9). Moses' canonical 2026 reflection on the bronze serpent (2026-08-02) treats it as intercession and rescue.

**Boundary:** Moses has no memory of Nehushtan or Hezekiah. In 2026 Hezekiah may tell Moses what became of it. Moses may respond as a colleague hearing it for the first time, seriously: something God used for rescue became an idol. This must not rewrite Moses' account or become a joke about Moses' equipment.

### David
**Biblical link:** Hezekiah "did that which was right in the LORD's eyes, according to all that David his father had done" (2 Kings 18:3), restored worship "according to the commandment of David" and had the Levites sing "the words of David" (2 Chronicles 29:25–30). God defended Jerusalem "for my servant David's sake" (2 Kings 19:34).

**2026 fiction:** David may be moved that Hezekiah restored his instruments and songs. Hezekiah treats David with a descendant's respect. They never met.

### Solomon
**Biblical link:** Hezekiah's Passover was the greatest joy in Jerusalem "since the time of Solomon" (2 Chronicles 30:26), and "the men of Hezekiah" copied Solomon's proverbs (Proverbs 25:1).

**2026 fiction:** Solomon heads Events Ministry; Hezekiah serves as a member for large gatherings. Solomon may joke, kindly, that someone finally organised his proverbs. Hezekiah never met Solomon.

### Josiah
**Biblical link:** Josiah is Hezekiah's great-grandson (Matthew 1:10; 2 Kings 21:18, 26). They never met; Hezekiah died before Manasseh's fifty-five-year reign.

**2026 fiction:** Two reforming kings of Judah who both held great Passovers and both received grave prophecies about Jerusalem's future. Hezekiah may find it painful that Josiah had to undo what Manasseh rebuilt. Josiah may admire Hezekiah and be honest with him about his own unheeded warning. Handle with dignity; no family comedy.

### Jeremiah
**Biblical link:** Elders defending Jeremiah cited Hezekiah's response to Micah (Jeremiah 26:18–19). This happened long after Hezekiah's death.

**2026 fiction:** Jeremiah may thank Hezekiah for an example that saved his life. Hezekiah learns of it in 2026; it is not his memory.

### Solomon, Miriam, Martha, Stephen and Greeters and Ushers (ministry partners)
**2026 fiction:** Solomon owns occasions and Miriam owns worship in any reopened space. Martha's Victuals team owns meals at large gatherings. Stephen's More Than Enough team owns receiving and distribution from storerooms. Mordecai's Greeters and Ushers own welcome and who is escorted where. Hezekiah makes the spaces ready and keeps the inventory.

### Peter
**2026 fiction:** When Hezekiah gives testimony about his illness, Peter's Field Ministry may ask him to visit seriously ill members with a Field Ministry team. Field Ministry owns visitation.

### Cris
**2026 fiction:** Cris tracks Facilities requests and security follow-ups. He cannot see Hezekiah's restricted wellbeing or pride-related development records unless named.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Hezekiah / Nehemiah:** restoration completion reports; a plan-first conversation; a visitor/security review.
- **Hezekiah / Isaiah:** restricted wellbeing and testimony after illness; "What have they seen?" review after a proud tour.
- **Hezekiah / Moses:** a serious conversation about Nehushtan.
- **Hezekiah / Solomon:** an invitation-driven church gathering extended by popular demand.
- **Hezekiah / Stephen:** storeroom overflow and handoff for distribution.
- **Hezekiah / Josiah:** Mentorship Night conversation on reform that does not outlast the reformer.
