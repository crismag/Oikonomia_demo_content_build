# Esther — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Mordecai — cousin, guardian, adviser, and later her direct report (2026)
Mordecai was the son of Esther's father's brother. When her parents died he "took her for his own daughter" (Esther 2:7). He walked past the women's court every day to learn how she was (2:11) and instructed her not to reveal her people (2:10, 20); "Esther obeyed Mordecai, like she did when she was brought up by him." He passed her the assassination plot, which she reported in his name (2:22). When Haman's decree came, he urged her to plead with the king and challenged her: "Who knows if you haven't come to the kingdom for such a time as this?" (4:8, 13–14). Then the direction reversed. Esther gave instructions for the fast: "So Mordecai went his way, and did according to all that Esther had commanded him" (4:17). After Haman's fall, Esther told the king who Mordecai was to her and "set Mordecai over the house of Haman" (8:1–2). They wrote together to confirm Purim (9:29–31).

**Silence:** Scripture records their messages through Hathach but no face-to-face conversation after she entered the palace. Later rabbinic tradition that Mordecai married Esther is not Scripture and must not be used.

**2026 fiction:** Mordecai, head of Greeters and Ushers, reports to Esther. This mirrors the text's own reversal: the guardian who once instructed her now serves under authority she gave him. He still notices things first and still pushes her to act; she decides when and how. Their records should show deep trust, occasional disagreement about timing, and mutual respect. Neither treats the other as a child or as a subordinate only.

### Ahasuerus — the king (not on the roster)
The king who deposed Vashti, took Esther into his harem and made her queen (2:16–17), gave Haman his ring (3:10), extended his sceptre to Esther twice (5:2; 8:4), and granted her requests. Commonly identified with Xerxes I; qualify.

**2026 boundary:** Not a Data Play account. Esther's records must not romanticise him or describe their relationship as a love story.

### Haman — adversary (not on the roster)
Haman the Agagite sought to destroy all the Jews because Mordecai would not bow to him (3:1–15). Esther named him "an adversary and an enemy, even this wicked Haman!" (7:6). He was hanged on the gallows he built for Mordecai (7:10); his ten sons were killed and their bodies hanged at Esther's request (9:7–14).

**2026 boundary:** No 2026 presence. Do not cast any 2026 character as "Haman."

### Vashti (not on the roster)
The queen deposed for refusing to be displayed (1:10–22). Scripture records no contact between Vashti and Esther.

### Hegai (not on the roster)
Keeper of the women, who favoured Esther and whose advice she followed (2:8–9, 15).

### Hathach (not on the roster)
The king's eunuch assigned to Esther, who carried every message between her and Mordecai (4:5–12).

### Esther's maids
Seven young women assigned to her (2:9), who fasted with her (4:16).

### The Jews of Susa and the provinces
Fasted for her for three days (4:16–17); later kept Purim at her and Mordecai's written instruction (9:20–32).

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Deborah
**2026 fiction:** Two senior women on the Leadership Council with very different paths into leadership. Deborah judged Israel openly and gave commands (Judges 4–5); Esther entered power through coercion, concealment and risk. They respect each other's instincts on sensitive situations: Deborah tends to say, "Go"; Esther tends to say, "Not yet, and not like that." Scripture records no link between them.

### Solomon — Events head
**2026 fiction:** Esther is an Events leader under Solomon. Solomon brings planning and splendour; Esther brings purpose, guest-list discretion and occasions of remembrance. Solomon should not reduce her to hosting, and she should not be written as a hostess.

### Mordecai's team: Andrew, Philip, James son of Alphaeus and Cornelius
**2026 fiction:** Through Mordecai, Esther is attentive to how newcomers and vulnerable visitors are received and protected.

### Nehemiah
Both books are set in Susa (Esther 1:2; Nehemiah 1:1). **Scripture records no contact.** That Nehemiah served a later king in the same palace is historical inference only.

**2026 fiction:** Both know what it is to make a request of a Persian king at personal risk. Esther's risk of death under law was greater; Nehemiah should not equate them.

### Ezra and Daniel
**2026 fiction:** Council colleagues (Ezra) and fellow veterans of foreign courts (Daniel). Daniel served faithfully under hostile officials and faced death under an irrevocable law (Daniel 6:8, 15), as Esther's people did under Haman's decree. No biblical contact.

### Saul and Samuel
Mordecai's genealogy includes a Kish (Esther 2:5), and Haman is an Agagite (3:1). Many readers connect these to Saul son of Kish and to Agag, the Amalekite king Saul spared (1 Samuel 9:1; 15:8–9). **This is interpretation, not stated in Esther.** Saul and Samuel have no memory of Esther or Mordecai. Do not build 2026 drama on it.

### Peter, Paul and the Leadership Council
**2026 fiction:** Esther's Council contributions centre on who is at risk and how a request should be made. She is careful with confidential pastoral matters and does not widen visibility to make a decision easier.

### Cris
Cris supports Esther's advocacy follow-ups but cannot see her restricted records unless named. Esther names readers deliberately.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Esther / Mordecai:** a restricted concern Mordecai raises from the door; Esther deciding when and how to act; disagreement about timing; delegation after a favourable outcome.
- **Esther / Leadership Council:** an advocacy request for someone vulnerable, prepared carefully; an attention item that stays open after the first decision.
- **Esther / Deborah:** coaching conversation on sensitive leadership; different instincts about timing.
- **Esther / Solomon:** a remembrance occasion with gifts to people in need, coordinated with Stephen and More Than Enough.
- **Esther / church-wide Prayer & Fasting day:** calendar entry and a Leadership Report asking named leaders to fast and pray.
