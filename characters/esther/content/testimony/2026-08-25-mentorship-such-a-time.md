*Deborah invited me to speak at Mentorship Night on sensitive leadership. These are my notes, written up afterward.*

People quote "for such a time as this" as though it were a promotion. It wasn't said to me as encouragement. It was said to me as a warning.

A decree had gone out to destroy my people. Mordecai sent word asking me to go to the king. I sent back reasons I couldn't: anyone who goes to the king uninvited can be put to death, and the king hadn't called for me in thirty days. Mordecai's answer was sharp. He told me not to imagine I'd escape because I was in the palace. If I stayed silent, deliverance would come from somewhere else, and I and my father's house would perish. "Who knows if you haven't come to the kingdom for such a time as this?"

Notice he said "who knows." He didn't promise me I was chosen for a heroic moment. He said I might be, and that silence would cost me anyway.

So I asked for support before I went. Everyone was to fast for three days. Then I said I would go to the king, against the law, and "if I perish, I perish."

Here's what I want developing leaders to hear.

1. **Access is responsibility.** At some point you'll be in a room the person at risk isn't in. Your comfort in that room isn't neutral.
2. **Ask for support before you act.** You shouldn't carry a hard request alone, and you shouldn't make it on the adrenaline of the moment.
3. **Prepare the request.** I invited the king to a banquet, then another, before I spoke. That wasn't hesitation. It was choosing the moment.
4. **The first win isn't the end.** When the man behind the decree fell, the decree still stood. I went back and wept at the king's feet to have it undone.
5. **Watch yourself when you have power.** The day my request was granted, I had more power than ever, and what I asked for with it is still hard to talk about.

This year I've been involved in a sensitive matter in our church. You won't hear any details from me. What I can tell you is that it involved a young person without a voice in a room I was in, a friend of mine on the other side of it, a prayer and fasting day, and a situation that is better now and not finished.
