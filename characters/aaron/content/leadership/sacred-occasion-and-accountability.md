## 2026-03-29 — Baptism Sunday: Order That Made Room for Testimony

**Record type:** Sacred Occasion / Leadership Report  
**Date:** 2026-03-29  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Solomon, Miriam, Moses  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #pastoral #celebratory #gratitude #discernment

### What happened

I prepared the order for Baptism Sunday with a simple purpose: the people being baptized should be seen and heard, and the service should make clear that baptism is a public response of faith rather than an attractive interruption in a programme. Solomon coordinated the occasion; Miriam and Psalmists led worship; Production and Facilities carried their own work.

Before the service, a request came to add several unscheduled presentations. The room wanted to honour good things, but each addition would have shortened the testimonies and prayer for the candidates. I paused the additions and kept the agreed order. That was not a refusal to celebrate; it was a decision about what this occasion was for.

The candidates were prayed for by name. The closing blessing was received quietly and without hurry. Two families asked afterward for a follow-up conversation about future dedications; those conversations belong with Events and the families, not in this public report.

### What I learned

At Sinai, when people grew impatient for Moses to return, I let their demand define what I provided. The result was idolatry, disorder, death and plague. This Sunday was not comparable in scale or moral weight, but the small leadership question was familiar: can I hear a room without obeying its pressure? I am grateful that I did not confuse a full programme with a faithful one.

**Attention:** Keep testimony and prayer time protected when late additions are proposed for a sacred occasion.
**Attention to:** Solomon
**Related goal:** Goal 1 — Do not give the crowd the occasion it demands
**Biblical account:** Exodus 24:14; Exodus 32:1–6, 21–25, 35.

## 2026-06-30 — Accountability Follow-up: Naming My Part Before Context

**Record type:** Personal Development / Accountability Report  
**Date:** 2026-06-30  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Moses, Miriam  
**Status:** Follow-up Required  
**Tags:** #serious #reflective #humility #accountability

Moses' recent reflection on our family conflict has remained with me. Miriam and I spoke against him, and the Lord confronted us. When Miriam was afflicted, I said plainly that we had acted foolishly and sinned; I also asked Moses not to let her remain as one dead. Moses prayed for her, and the community waited until she was brought back.

I cannot explain why Miriam alone received that affliction. Scripture does not tell us. I can say that I was involved, that I escaped a consequence she bore publicly, and that I should not treat this as an old family story with no present claim on me.

My first sentence in difficult reviews has often been context: the pressure, the mood, the other person's part. Context can be true and still become cover. I have told Moses and Miriam that I want them to interrupt me when I begin there. My own part must be named before I explain the room.
