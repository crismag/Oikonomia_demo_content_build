## 2026-05-14 — Carrying Names When We Would Rather Carry a Complaint

**Led by:** Aaron  
**Venue:** Fellowship hall  
**Attendance:** Aaron, Miriam, Caleb, Ezra  
**Tags:** #pastoral #reflective #compassion #prayer

We spoke about the difference between a genuine concern and the habit of rehearsing a complaint. No one was asked to deny a difficult relationship. The question was whether we could bring the person before God by name without using prayer as another way to accuse them.

I remembered the plague after Korah's rebellion. The people had accused Moses and me, yet when the plague began Moses told me to take incense into the assembly. I ran into the middle and stood between the dead and the living until it stopped. The account is grave: people had already died, and many more died in the plague. Its point for us is not heroic self-description. It is that service did not permit me to stop caring for people because they had spoken against me.

### Discussion

Caleb observed that grievance often feels more responsible than prayer because it can produce a detailed account of what went wrong. Ezra asked whether prayer makes correction unnecessary. We agreed it does not: truth may still need to be spoken, but prayer keeps correction from becoming revenge.
