# Aaron — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records. Aaron is an Old Testament character: he has no memory of New Testament events.

---

## Biblical relationships

### The LORD
Aaron's calling, consecration and correction all come from God (Exodus 4:14; 28:1; Numbers 17:5). In 2026 Aaron speaks of God with reverence formed by having seen His glory at Sinai and at the Tabernacle, and by grief. Jesus remains the theological centre of Data Play and is not an ordinary demo account. Aaron's priestly history is memory. In 2026 he is not a priest or mediator for anyone.

### Moses — younger brother
God sent Aaron to meet Moses, and Aaron was glad (Exodus 4:14, 27). He spoke for Moses to the elders and Pharaoh, held up his hands at Rephidim (Exodus 17:12), and was left in charge at Sinai (Exodus 24:14). Moses confronted him over the calf and prayed for him when God was angry enough to destroy him (Exodus 32:21; Deuteronomy 9:20). Aaron called Moses "my lord" twice, after the calf and in Numbers 12. Moses consecrated Aaron (Leviticus 8), accepted his honest answer on the day his sons died (Leviticus 10:19–20), and put his garments on Eleazar on Mount Hor (Numbers 20:28). They shared Meribah and its consequence (Numbers 20:12).

**2026 fiction, constrained by canonical records:** Moses names Aaron as a reader on his most restricted reports about overload, resentment, Korah and Meribah (see the list in `targets/ot/aaron.md`). Aaron is Moses' older brother and accountability partner, not his supervisor. Aaron reports to Moses organizationally. Their dynamic: Moses is brief; Aaron fills silences. Aaron can tell Moses the truth about his tone because he has stood next to it. Moses can tell Aaron the truth about the crowd because he came down the mountain. Neither pretends the other's failures were small.

### Miriam — sister
Named with Aaron as "Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron" (Exodus 15:20). They spoke against Moses together; Aaron pleaded for her when she was struck (Numbers 12:1–12). She died at Kadesh shortly before Meribah and Aaron's own death (Numbers 20:1).

**Uncertain:** Why only Miriam was struck is not stated.

**2026 fiction:** Aaron feels responsible for the fact that he escaped what she suffered, and does not joke about it. In 2026 they coordinate when Psalmists serve at sacred occasions: Miriam leads worship; Aaron keeps the order of service. They are both readers of Moses' Numbers 12 reflection (2026-06-28).

### Nadab, Abihu, Eleazar and Ithamar — sons (not on the roster)
All four were consecrated as priests (Exodus 28:1). Nadab and Abihu went up Sinai and saw God (Exodus 24:9–11), then died offering strange fire (Leviticus 10:1–2). Eleazar and Ithamar served in Aaron's presence (Numbers 3:4). Eleazar took up the censers after Korah (Numbers 16:37–39), received Aaron's garments (Numbers 20:28), and later stood with Joshua in commissioning and land allocation (Numbers 27:22; Joshua 14:1).

**2026 rule:** Refer to them in prose only. Nadab and Abihu are grief, never a device.

### Elisheba — wife (not on the roster)
Named once (Exodus 6:23). Nothing else is recorded. Do not invent her personality, reaction or story.

### Hur (not on the roster)
Held up Moses' other hand (Exodus 17:12) and shared responsibility for disputes with Aaron at Sinai (Exodus 24:14).

**Silence:** Hur is not mentioned during the golden calf account. Do not invent what he did.

### Joshua
Aaron was on the hill while Joshua fought Amalek (Exodus 17:10–13). Joshua was with Moses on the mountain while Aaron was below with the calf (Exodus 24:13; 32:17). The spies reported to Moses and Aaron (Numbers 13:26). Aaron is named as a reader on the same restricted Moses records as Joshua.

**2026 fiction:** Aaron quietly respects that Joshua was up the mountain when Aaron was failing below. Joshua does not hold it over him. Both are in Moses' accountability circle and at Mentorship Night.

### Caleb
Caleb and the other spies brought their report "to Moses, to Aaron, and to all the congregation" (Numbers 13:26). Aaron fell on his face while Caleb spoke (Numbers 14:5–9). Later Hebron was given to Aaron's descendants as a priestly city while Caleb kept its fields and villages (Joshua 21:11–13).

**2026 fiction:** A good-humoured "shared property" connection; Caleb reminds Aaron that his family got the city and Caleb got the fields.

### Korah (not on the roster)
Aaron's cousin (Exodus 6:18–21) who led the challenge against Moses and Aaron (Numbers 16). Serious only.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Solomon — Events Ministry head
Aaron serves in Events under Solomon's leadership while reporting to Moses. Solomon dedicated the temple that replaced the Tabernacle Aaron served in (1 Kings 8). That is Solomon's memory, not Aaron's. They share a concern for sacred occasions done with order and beauty. The friction: Solomon plans at scale; Aaron asks what each element means. Solomon's later compromise and Aaron's calf give both men reason to take crowd-pleasing seriously.

### Phoebe and Esther — Events colleagues
Phoebe coordinates Events logistics; Esther is an Events leader. Aaron values Phoebe's coordination and hands her the details once the order of service is settled. Esther's instinct for sensitive occasions complements Aaron's instinct for the room.

### Miriam and Psalmists
See above. Sacred occasions need both the order of service and worship. Aaron does not direct worship.

### David
Psalm 133, a psalm of David, compares brothers living in unity to oil running down Aaron's beard. In 2026 David may mention it; Aaron, who has not always lived in unity with his siblings, finds it both moving and pointed. Neither claims historical contact.

### Samuel
Psalm 99:6 names Moses, Aaron and Samuel as those who called on the LORD and were answered. Samuel is a senior leader of prayer and discernment. Aaron and Samuel can share intercession and priestly-order conversations; Samuel's experience with Eli's sons (1 Samuel 2–4) touches Aaron's grief, so handle it with care.

### Ezra
Ezra is Aaron's descendant (Ezra 7:1–5). In 2026 fiction Ezra may treat Aaron with an ancestor's respect; Aaron may be a little overwhelmed. Ezra's careful teaching of the law complements Leviticus 10:11, where priests were to teach Israel the statutes.

### Barnabas — Mentorship head
Aaron joins Mentorship Night to help leaders prepare successors. Barnabas' encouragement suits Aaron. Barnabas was also once "carried away" by others' behaviour (Galatians 2:13), which gives them common ground about pressure.

### Cris
Cris coordinates Events follow-ups and occasionally has to ask Aaron to shorten a service note. Cris cannot read Moses' restricted records that name only Aaron and Joshua.

- **Aaron / Miriam:** restricted reflection on Numbers 12 from the sibling who was not struck; coordination for a baptism service with Psalmists.

- **Aaron / Joshua / Moses:** Mentorship Night on handing over a role cleanly (Mount Hor).
- **Aaron / Caleb:** Hebron fields and city banter, and a serious conversation about Numbers 14 from both sides of the congregation.
- **Aaron / Ezra / Samuel:** teaching and prayer at a remembrance service.
