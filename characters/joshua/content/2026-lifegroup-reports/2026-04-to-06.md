## 2026-04-09 — When People Still Ask Moses

Tonight we talked about the difference between receiving responsibility and feeling trusted in it.

Moses has been intentionally transferring decisions to me this year. I appreciate that. What I did not expect was how strange it would feel when people receive my answer and then ask Moses the same question.

I understand why they do it. Moses has carried their trust for a very long time. I have been one of the people who looked to him myself.

Still, I noticed some frustration in me this week. Part of me wanted Moses to prove publicly that my answer was his answer too.

That would defeat the point.

If I require Moses to validate every decision I make, then he has delegated the work but not actually been released from it.

**What I am taking from tonight:** Authority I have genuinely received must eventually be exercised without borrowing someone else's confidence every time.

**Prayer:** Help me lead without insecurity, and help me honor Moses without making him responsible for decisions You have entrusted to me.

**Related goal:** Lead the decisions that have actually been delegated to me.

## 2026-04-23 — Courage Is Not Volume

Someone asked tonight whether courageous leaders always feel confident.

No.

Before I led Israel forward, God repeatedly told me to be strong and courageous and not to be afraid or discouraged. I have sometimes heard those words quoted as though they describe my personality. I hear them differently because I remember receiving them.

You do not repeatedly tell someone not to fear because there is nothing frightening ahead.

Moses was gone. Responsibility was changing. We were preparing to cross the Jordan into a land containing fortified cities and people we had feared for years.

Courage did not mean convincing everyone that none of this affected me. Courage meant trusting that God would be with us as He had been with Moses and then taking the next obedient step.

**What I am taking from tonight:** I do not need to sound fearless to lead courageously.

**Related goal:** Practice courage without pretending I am never afraid.

**Biblical account:** Deuteronomy 31:7–8, 23; Joshua 1:1–9.

## 2026-05-07 — Commissioned in Front of Everyone

Tonight's discussion was about moments when someone else's confidence in us changed the way we understood our responsibility.

I remembered my commissioning.

Moses knew he would not lead Israel into the land. Rather than simply protecting his position until the end, he asked God to appoint someone over the congregation so the people would not be like sheep without a shepherd.

God identified me.

Moses brought me before Eleazar the priest and the whole congregation, laid his hands on me, and publicly commissioned me. Later he again told me before Israel to be strong and courageous because I would lead the people forward.

I had served beside Moses for years, but public commissioning changed something. Responsibility was no longer merely something I performed because Moses asked me to help. The people needed to understand that I was expected to lead.

I am thinking about that this year as responsibilities are transferred again in our current work. Private encouragement is valuable. Sometimes people also need public clarity about who has actually been entrusted to lead.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 27:15–23; Deuteronomy 31:7–8.

## 2026-05-21 — Do Not Make Me Another Moses

Tonight we discussed comparison.

For much of my life, Moses was the obvious reference point for leadership. That was appropriate. I learned from him, served with him, watched him pray, watched him carry responsibility, and watched him make mistakes he later taught me not to repeat.

But I have realized that comparison can become a strange burden during transition.

If I make a decision differently, someone can wonder what Moses would have done. Sometimes I wonder the same thing.

Moses has told me more than once that I do not need to become another Moses.

I am beginning to understand that honoring a mentor includes learning from him without trying to reproduce him.

**What I am taking from tonight:** I can inherit responsibility without borrowing someone else's identity.

**Prayer:** Help me receive what You taught me through Moses and still become the leader You have called me to be.

## 2026-06-04 — Twelve Stones and One Question

Tonight we talked about how easily the next generation can inherit a practice without knowing the story behind it.

When Israel crossed the Jordan, the river was at flood stage. As the priests carrying the ark stepped into the water, the flow stopped and the people crossed on dry ground.

God instructed us to take twelve stones from the river, one for each tribe, and set them up as a memorial.

The purpose was explained very clearly: one day children would see those stones and ask, "What do these stones mean?"

That question was the point.

Their parents were supposed to tell them that Israel crossed the Jordan on dry ground because the Lord stopped the river before us, just as He had dried up the Red Sea for the generation before us.

The stones did not preserve the testimony by themselves. Someone still had to tell the story.

**What I am taking from tonight:** Documentation is useful when it helps people remember what God has done and understand why something matters.

**Possible follow-up:** Share this principle with Gifts and Arrows as we think about how children encounter church traditions and testimonies.

**Related goal:** Preserve testimony so the next generation knows why.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 3–4.

## 2026-06-18 — What Does This Mean?

We continued our discussion about testimony tonight.

I realized that leaders sometimes become impatient with basic questions because we have forgotten what it is like not to know the history.

"Why do we do this?"

"Why is this important?"

"What does this mean?"

Those are not necessarily signs that someone is disengaged. In Joshua 4, God anticipated that children would ask exactly that kind of question about the memorial stones.

The answer was not supposed to be, "Because this is how we have always done it."

The answer was a story about God's faithfulness.

**What I am taking from tonight:** If we cannot explain why a practice exists, preserving the practice alone is not enough.

**Prayer:** Help us become patient storytellers of Your faithfulness rather than custodians of unexplained traditions.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 4:19–24.
