## 2026-07-02 — Jericho and Obedience

Tonight we talked about obeying God when His direction does not resemble the strategy we would have designed ourselves.

Jericho was the first major fortified city Israel faced after crossing the Jordan. Its gates were securely shut because of us. God gave instructions that would not have looked like a conventional siege plan: Israel was to march around the city once a day for six days. On the seventh day we were to march around it seven times, the priests would sound the trumpets, the people would shout, and the wall would fall.

We followed the instructions. The wall collapsed and Israel entered the city.

What stays with me is not that unusual methods are automatically spiritual. The lesson is not, "Do something strange and expect God to bless it."

The lesson is that when God has actually spoken, obedience is more important than making His instruction look respectable according to our preferred methods.

**What I am taking from tonight:** Faith is not creativity for its own sake. Faith listens to God and obeys.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 6.

## 2026-07-16 — The Assignment After a Victory

Tonight's discussion was about what happens immediately after success.

I shared that one of the most painful failures of my leadership came directly after Jericho.

Jericho had been an extraordinary victory. When men later examined Ai, they reported that it was small enough that the whole force would not be needed. About three thousand men went up.

They were defeated. Thirty-six men died, and Israel's courage collapsed.

There was sin in the camp that we did not yet understand, but I also remember how quickly we moved from a great victory into the next assignment.

Success can make a leader assume that whatever worked yesterday has somehow become permanent evidence of God's approval, our readiness, or our competence.

It is not.

**What I am taking from tonight:** Yesterday's testimony should strengthen today's faith, not replace today's dependence on God.

**Related goal:** Investigate failure instead of assuming yesterday's success guarantees today's result.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 6–7.

## 2026-07-30 — When Failure Needs Investigation

We talked tonight about the temptation to respond to failure either by blaming someone immediately or by refusing to ask difficult questions because we want to protect people.

After the defeat at Ai, I tore my clothes and fell before the ark of the Lord. I asked why God had brought us across the Jordan if we were going to be defeated.

God's answer forced us to investigate what had happened.

Achan had taken items from Jericho that God had commanded Israel not to take and had hidden them among his possessions. His sin had consequences for the community. The matter was exposed and dealt with before Israel returned to Ai.

The judgment in that account is severe and I do not speak of it lightly.

What I carry into leadership now is the need for truthful examination. Compassion does not require us to pretend that wrongdoing did not happen. Accountability does not require us to enjoy exposing it.

**What I am taking from tonight:** When something fails badly, prayer and investigation belong together.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 7.

## 2026-08-13 — We Examined the Evidence but Did Not Pray

Tonight we discussed decisions that looked reasonable at the time but later exposed something important we had neglected.

The Gibeonites once approached Israel pretending they had traveled from a distant country. They understood that the peoples of Canaan were under judgment, so they created evidence to support their story: worn-out sacks, old wineskins, patched sandals, worn clothing, and bread that was dry and crumbly.

Their evidence was convincing.

We examined their provisions and made a treaty with them.

Then we discovered that they lived nearby.

Scripture identifies our failure plainly: **we did not inquire of the Lord.**

I have thought about that sentence many times. We were not careless in the sense that we looked at nothing. We looked at quite a lot.

We simply treated information as though it were sufficient for discernment.

**What I am taking from tonight:** Good evidence matters. Due diligence matters. Prayer still matters.

**Prayer:** Keep me from becoming so impressed with a complete-looking assessment that I forget to ask You for wisdom.

**Related goal:** Seek God before committing the organization.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 9:1–27.

## 2026-08-27 — Learning to Give Responsibility Away

Tonight we discussed what it means to move from being mentored to mentoring others.

For years I could identify myself in relation to Moses. I was his assistant. I accompanied him. I learned from him. Eventually I was commissioned after him.

That history still shapes me, but I have realized that I can become so focused on proving that I am ready to carry what Moses gave me that I forget the next responsibility: I must also prepare other people.

If Moses develops Joshua and Joshua develops no one, multiplication stops with Joshua.

I have begun identifying responsibilities this year that can be given to developing leaders. I am finding that giving someone a real decision feels very different from giving them a task whose answer I have already decided.

I now understand some of what Moses has been practicing with me.

**What I am taking from tonight:** A leader is not finished developing when people finally trust him. That may be when his responsibility to develop others becomes more important.

**Related goal:** Develop leaders instead of remaining Moses' permanent successor.

## 2026-09-10 — Unfinished Work

Tonight we talked about the uncomfortable difference between significant progress and finished work.

When I was older, God told me plainly that I had become advanced in years and that there was still very much land remaining to be possessed.

That sentence has always corrected something in me.

A leader can look back at genuine victories—the Jordan crossed, Jericho fallen, battles completed, land distributed—and still be required to acknowledge what remains unfinished.

Gratitude and incompleteness can exist at the same time.

I do not want our reports to exaggerate problems, but I also do not want celebration language to make unfinished responsibilities disappear.

**What I am taking from tonight:** "We have accomplished much" and "there is still work to do" can both be true.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 13:1–7.

## 2026-09-24 — As for Me and My House

Tonight we talked about the difference between inheriting someone else's faith and choosing whom we ourselves will serve.

Near the end of my leadership, I gathered Israel and reviewed our history. I reminded them of what God had done long before my own leadership: calling Abraham, bringing Israel out of Egypt, delivering us from Pharaoh, leading us through the wilderness, bringing us across the Jordan, and giving us the land.

Then I challenged the people to choose whom they would serve.

I told them where I stood: **as for me and my house, we would serve the Lord.**

That statement was not a decorative family motto. It was a decision spoken after a lifetime of seeing both God's faithfulness and the people's repeated temptation to turn elsewhere.

Tonight I am reminded that nobody can live indefinitely on Moses' faith, Joshua's faith, a parent's faith, a pastor's faith, or a Lifegroup leader's faith.

Testimony can be inherited as a story. Faithfulness still has to become personal.

**Prayer:** Thank You for the people whose faith helped bring us here. Give each of us grace to answer for ourselves whom we will serve.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 23–24, especially Joshua 24:14–15.
