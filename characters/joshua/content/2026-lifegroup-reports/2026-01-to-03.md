## January 15, 2026 — Serving Before Leading

**Record type:** Lifegroup Gathering Entry  
**Category:** General / Personal Sharing  
**Visibility:** Lifegroup  
**Status:** Complete

Tonight we talked about seasons when serving feels less important than leading.

I shared that before I carried responsibility for Israel, much of my life was spent assisting Moses. Scripture sometimes identifies me simply as Moses' assistant. I accompanied him, waited, watched, learned, and was entrusted with responsibilities before I ever became his successor.

One of the things I appreciate now is that those years were not a delay before my real calling. They were part of it.

I have sometimes been impatient with quieter assignments because I know I am capable of more. Looking back, I can see how much God formed in me while I was serving someone else's leadership.

**What I am taking from tonight:** Faithfulness is not measured by how visible the assignment is.

**Prayer:** Help me serve faithfully in whatever responsibility You have actually given me, without treating preparation as wasted time.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 24:13; Exodus 33:11; Numbers 11:28.

## January 29, 2026 — When Support Is Part of the Victory

**Record type:** Lifegroup Gathering Entry  
**Category:** Testimony  
**Visibility:** Lifegroup  
**Status:** Complete

Our discussion tonight was about receiving help without feeling that help diminishes our contribution.

I remembered the battle against Amalek. Moses told me to choose men and go out to fight while he stood on the hill with the staff of God. I was leading the men in the battle below, but another part of the story was happening above us.

When Moses held up his hands, Israel prevailed. When his hands grew tired and dropped, Amalek began to prevail. Aaron and Hur eventually placed a stone under Moses so he could sit, then stood on either side and held up his hands until sunset.

From where I was, it would have been easy to describe that day only in terms of the people fighting beside me. But the victory was never mine to claim that way.

Moses needed Aaron and Hur. I needed the people with me. All of us needed God.

**What I am taking from tonight:** Needing support does not make a leader less faithful. Sometimes the support nobody sees is part of the reason the visible work succeeds.

**Prayer:** Give me humility to receive support and awareness to thank the people whose work is easy to overlook.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 17:8–16.

## February 12, 2026 — Staying Near God's Presence

**Record type:** Lifegroup Gathering Entry  
**Category:** Spiritual Development  
**Visibility:** Lifegroup  
**Status:** Complete

Tonight's question was simple: What happens to our relationship with God when nobody is asking us to lead anything?

I thought about the Tent of Meeting. Moses would go there to meet with the Lord, and afterward he would return to the camp. Scripture records a small detail about me: I did not leave the tent.

That was long before I was commissioned to lead Israel.

I do not want prayer to become something I value only because leadership now requires it. There were years when staying near God's presence did not make me the person in charge of anything.

Those years mattered.

**Personal reflection:** I need to guard against allowing increased responsibility to consume the very habits that prepared me to carry responsibility.

**Related 2026 goal:** Practice courage without pretending I am never afraid.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 33:7–11.

## February 26, 2026 — I Was Protective of Moses

**Record type:** Lifegroup Gathering Entry  
**Category:** Personal Development  
**Visibility:** Lifegroup  
**Status:** Complete

We discussed jealousy, loyalty, and what happens when our desire to protect a leader becomes too narrow.

I shared something I am not particularly proud of.

During the wilderness years, God instructed Moses to gather seventy elders because the burden of leadership had become too heavy for him. Two men, Eldad and Medad, remained in the camp but began prophesying there. Someone ran to report it to Moses. I told Moses to stop them.

I thought I was defending him.

Moses responded by asking whether I was jealous for his sake. He said he wished all the Lord's people were prophets and that the Lord would put His Spirit on them.

Moses saw multiplication where I saw a threat to order.

That correction still matters to me. Loyalty to a leader should not become insecurity when God works through someone outside the structure I expected.

**What I am taking from tonight:** I can honor the leaders who formed me without needing every good thing to pass through them—or through me.

**Related 2026 goal:** Develop leaders instead of remaining Moses' permanent successor.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 11:24–30.

## March 12, 2026 — The Majority Was Still Wrong

**Record type:** Lifegroup Gathering Entry  
**Category:** Faith / Leadership  
**Visibility:** Lifegroup  
**Status:** Complete

Tonight we talked about how difficult it is to disagree when almost everyone around you reaches the same conclusion.

I remembered when twelve of us were sent to explore Canaan. We all saw much of the same evidence. The land was fruitful. Its cities were fortified. The people living there were strong.

The disagreement was not mainly over what we had seen. It was over what those facts meant in light of what God had promised.

Ten of the men concluded that Israel could not go forward. Their report spread fear through the congregation. People wept, complained against Moses and Aaron, and began talking about choosing another leader and returning to Egypt.

Caleb and I disagreed. We did not deny the fortified cities or pretend the opposition was weak. We said that if the Lord was pleased with us, He could bring us into the land.

The congregation became so angry that they talked about stoning us.

I do not remember that moment as exciting. Standing with a minority is easier to admire afterward than it is to experience while the crowd is against you.

**What I am taking from tonight:** Consensus is useful, but consensus is not God. Fear can become popular without becoming faithful.

**Prayer:** Give us courage to listen carefully to others without allowing numbers alone to decide what obedience means.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 13:1–33; Numbers 14:1–10.

## March 26, 2026 — Caleb

**Record type:** Lifegroup Gathering Entry  
**Category:** Fellowship / Gratitude  
**Visibility:** Lifegroup  
**Status:** Complete

Tonight we were asked to name someone whose faithfulness made a difficult season easier to endure.

My answer was Caleb.

When the other spies gave their fearful report, I was not standing alone. Caleb had already spoken before the people and urged them to go forward. Later we stood together before the congregation and pleaded with the people not to rebel against the Lord or fear the inhabitants of the land.

There is a particular kind of gratitude you develop for someone who was beside you before history decided which side had been right.

Caleb and I did not agree because it was safe. We agreed while the congregation was discussing stoning us.

I am thankful for friends who help me remain faithful before the outcome is visible.

**Prayer:** Thank You for people who stand beside us in faith, especially when doing so costs them something.

**Follow-up:** Encourage Caleb personally this week rather than merely appreciating him privately.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 13:30; Numbers 14:6–10, 24, 30.
