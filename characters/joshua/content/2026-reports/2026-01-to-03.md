## 2026-01-04 — Learning Before Leading

**Record type:** Leadership Progress Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #reflective #serious #gratitude

A new year makes leadership goals easy to write and harder to understand honestly.

For much of my life, I was known in relation to Moses. I served as his assistant. I accompanied him when he went up the mountain. I remained near the Tent of Meeting. I received assignments before I received a title.

I used to think of those years mainly as preparation for later leadership. I understand them differently now. Serving was itself leadership formation. I learned how Moses listened to God, how he carried people through crisis, how he interceded for people who had frustrated him, and also how even a faithful leader could become exhausted or respond wrongly under pressure.

My goal this year is not to prove that I am finally ready to stop learning from Moses. It is to receive what has been entrusted to me without remaining dependent on him for every answer.

**2026 focus:** Exercise delegated authority faithfully and begin developing others.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 24:13; Exodus 33:11; Numbers 11:28; Numbers 27:15–23.

## 2026-01-11 — The First Major Assignment Moses Gave Me

**Record type:** Leadership Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #reflective #courage

One of the first major responsibilities Moses entrusted to me was during Amalek's attack on Israel.

He told me to choose men and go out to fight. Moses would stand on the hill with the staff of God in his hand.

I had to choose people, lead them into danger, make decisions in real time, and carry responsibility for those beside me. Yet while the battle was happening below, Moses was on the hill above. When he held up his hands, Israel prevailed. When his hands became tired and dropped, Amalek began to prevail. Aaron and Hur eventually supported his hands until sunset.

That experience permanently changed how I think about visible leadership.

The person at the front is rarely carrying the work alone. Some victories depend on people praying, supporting, preparing, encouraging, and sustaining leaders in ways most people never see.

This year, when responsibility is delegated to me, I want to remember both sides of that hill.

**Leadership truth:** Receiving authority does not make support unnecessary.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 17:8–16.

## 2026-01-18 — I Keep Wanting to Check with Moses

**Record type:** Personal Development  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Moses  
**Status:** Follow-up  
**Tags:** #serious #reflective #uncertainty #growth

I noticed a pattern this week.

Several decisions had already been assigned to me. None required escalation. I still found myself thinking, "I should check what Moses thinks before I finalize this."

There is nothing wrong with seeking counsel. The problem is that I can use counsel as a way of avoiding ownership.

Moses has spent years teaching me. If every decision still requires him to approve my answer, then he has not actually been released from the responsibility.

I also recognize that part of this is personal. If Moses agrees with my decision, I feel safer. If the outcome goes badly, I know I was not alone in choosing it.

That is not the kind of leadership I have been commissioned to practice.

**Action:** For decisions clearly within my responsibility, I will make the decision, document the reasoning, and bring the result—not a request for permission—to mentorship review unless genuine escalation is necessary.

**Related goal:** Lead the decisions that have actually been delegated to me.

## 2026-01-25 — Courage Is Not the Absence of Weight

**Record type:** Personal Development  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #devotional #serious #fear #courage #hope

God told me repeatedly to be strong and courageous.

I have heard people quote those words as though they describe someone who naturally walks into every responsibility without fear. I remember receiving them differently.

Moses was no longer going to lead Israel forward. The Jordan was ahead. Beyond it were fortified cities and people whose strength had frightened an earlier generation. The responsibility was enormous.

God did not tell me that the assignment was easy. He told me that He would be with me.

That distinction matters to me this year. Courage is not leadership theatre. I do not need to make every difficult assignment sound small so other people will believe I am strong enough for it.

I can acknowledge the weight and still obey.

**Prayer:** Lord, keep me from confusing confidence in You with confidence in myself.

**Biblical account:** Deuteronomy 31:7–8, 23; Joshua 1:1–9.

**Visibility:** Shared

**Tags:** #devotional #reflective #peace

Before I carried the responsibilities people associate with my name now, Scripture records that I remained at the Tent of Meeting after Moses returned to the camp.

I value that detail more as I grow older.

It is possible for leadership responsibility to consume the habits that prepared us to carry leadership responsibility. Prayer becomes another agenda item. Time with God becomes preparation for the next meeting. Scripture becomes material for something we need to teach.

I do not want that to happen.

The tent mattered before the title.

**2026 action:** Protect time with God that is not merely preparation for public leadership.

## 2026-02-08 — I Tried to Protect Moses From Multiplication

**Record type:** Personal Development  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #reflective #conviction #humility

There was a time when Moses was overwhelmed by the burden of leading the people. God instructed him to gather seventy elders and placed His Spirit upon them.

Two men, Eldad and Medad, remained in the camp and began prophesying there. Someone reported this to Moses. I immediately told Moses to stop them.

I believed I was defending him.

Moses asked whether I was jealous for his sake. He said he wished all the Lord's people were prophets and that the Lord would put His Spirit on them.

I was trying to protect the leader. Moses was thinking about multiplying leadership.

I need that correction again in 2026. Healthy leadership should not feel threatened whenever God uses someone outside the route I expected.

**Leadership truth:** Loyalty becomes unhealthy when it requires other people's usefulness to become smaller.

**Related goal:** Develop leaders instead of remaining Moses' permanent successor.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 11:24–30.

## 2026-02-15 — Caleb Was Already Speaking

**Record type:** Leadership Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #dramatic #serious #courage #gratitude

When the twelve spies returned from Canaan, all of us had seen the same land. We saw its fruitfulness. We also saw fortified cities and strong inhabitants.

Fear began shaping the interpretation of everything we had observed.

Caleb spoke up before the people and urged them to go forward. Later, when the congregation turned against Moses and Aaron and talked about returning to Egypt, Caleb and I tore our clothes and pleaded with the people not to rebel against the Lord.

The crowd became so hostile that they talked about stoning us.

I remember Caleb's presence more than I remember feeling brave.

There is a kind of courage that becomes possible because another faithful person is standing beside you before anyone knows how the story will end.

**Follow-up:** Continue investing intentionally in peer relationships, not only mentor relationships.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 13:25–33; Numbers 14:1–10.

## 2026-02-22 — Majority Report

**Record type:** Leadership Progress Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #dramatic #fear #courage #discernment

The spies' report remains one of the clearest examples in my life of why leadership cannot simply count opinions and call the result discernment.

Ten spies believed the land could not be taken. Caleb and I disagreed.

The ten were not fabricating every observation. The cities really were fortified. The people really were formidable. The error was in treating those facts as though God's promise had become irrelevant.

Their fear spread through the congregation until people wanted to appoint a leader and return to Egypt.

This is why I want our current leadership culture to make room for dissent without romanticizing it. A minority is not automatically right because it is a minority. A majority is not automatically right because it has consensus.

We still have to seek God.

**Leadership truth:** Consensus is information. It is not revelation.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 13–14.

## 2026-03-01 — Receiving Responsibility Publicly

**Record type:** Leadership Transition Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #reflective #gratitude #responsibility

When Moses learned that he would not be the one to lead Israel into the promised land, his response was not primarily to protect his legacy. He asked God to appoint someone over the congregation so the people would not be like sheep without a shepherd.

God identified me.

Moses brought me before Eleazar and the congregation, laid his hands on me, and commissioned me publicly.

I had already served for years. I had already led people in battle. I had already stood with Caleb when the congregation rejected our report. But commissioning made responsibility visible to the community.

I am thinking about that in our 2026 leadership transitions. Sometimes we tell someone privately that they are responsible while continuing to behave publicly as though the previous leader still owns everything.

That is unfair to both leaders.

**Action:** When authority is transferred, make the scope clear enough that the community knows whom to follow.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 27:12–23.

## 2026-03-15 — When People Bypass the Person You Appointed

**Record type:** Leadership Situation  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Moses  
**Status:** Follow-up  
**Tags:** #serious #frustration #uncertainty

Three matters this week were brought to Moses after I had already given an answer.

Moses redirected all three back to me.

Two returned a second time asking whether I was certain Moses agreed.

I understand the transition problem. Moses has led for a long time. Trust does not move automatically because an assignment chart changes.

But if every delegated decision remains subject to an unofficial appeal to Moses, then we have not really transferred responsibility.

I also need to watch my own response. Frustration can tempt me to prove authority rather than exercise it calmly.

**Actions**
- Clarify decision ownership in the affected work areas.
- Ask Moses to continue redirecting ordinary bypass attempts rather than re-answering them.
- Explain escalation criteria without treating reasonable questions as disloyalty.
- Review my own tone when authority is questioned.

**Related goal:** Lead the decisions that have actually been delegated to me.

**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete

I spent many years learning from Moses. That makes comparison inevitable.

Sometimes people ask what Moses would do before they ask what I believe should be done. Sometimes I ask the same question internally.

I cannot honor Moses by refusing to become responsible for my own leadership.
