## 2026-04-05 — Preparing to Cross

**Record type:** Leadership Progress Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #hope #courage

There are moments when preparation must become movement.

Before Israel crossed the Jordan, officers went through the camp telling the people to prepare. The ark would go before us. We were entering territory we had not traveled before.

Leadership during transition requires enough preparation to move responsibly without using preparation as a permanent substitute for movement.

I have noticed this in myself. When responsibility is new, another meeting can feel safer than a decision. Another review can feel wiser than committing to the next step.

Sometimes it is wise. Sometimes it is fear wearing administrative clothing.

**2026 action:** Identify decisions that are adequately prepared and move them forward instead of scheduling another review by default.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 1:10–18; Joshua 3:1–6.

## 2026-04-12 — The Jordan Was at Flood Stage

**Record type:** Testimony  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #dramatic #devotional #faith #courage #awe

When Israel reached the Jordan, the river was overflowing its banks.

God instructed the priests carrying the ark to step into the water. When their feet touched the edge, the water flowing from upstream stopped, and the people crossed on dry ground.

I remember the Red Sea from the generation before me. This crossing was different because now I was responsible for leading the people through it.

God's faithfulness did not end with Moses' generation.

That matters deeply to me in transition. A new leader does not need God to become someone different. The same God who was faithful before the transition remains faithful after it.

**Testimony:** We crossed because God made a way, not because Joshua became Moses.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 3.

## 2026-04-19 — Twelve Stones

**Record type:** Testimony / Documentation Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #reflective #devotional #gratitude #teaching

After the Jordan crossing, God instructed us to take twelve stones from the river, one representing each tribe, and establish a memorial.

The reason was specifically connected to future questions. Children would see the stones and ask what they meant. Their parents were to tell them that the Jordan stopped before the ark and Israel crossed on dry ground.

The memorial was not meant merely to preserve an object. It was meant to preserve testimony.

This has shaped one of my 2026 goals. Our reports should not become a pile of records whose meaning disappears as soon as the people involved move on.

Important decisions need enough context that someone later can understand why they mattered.

**Ministry connection:** Gifts and Arrows / teaching the next generation.

**Related goal:** Preserve testimony so the next generation knows why.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 4.

## 2026-04-26 — Jericho: Obedience Before Explanation

**Record type:** Leadership Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #dramatic #devotional #faith #obedience

Jericho was shut tightly because of Israel. It was a fortified city, and the strategy God gave us did not resemble the siege plan a military leader would normally design.

For six days we marched around the city once each day. On the seventh day we marched around it seven times. The priests sounded the trumpets. The people shouted. The wall collapsed.

I do not use that history to argue that unusual ideas are automatically inspired. Strange is not the same as spiritual.

The lesson is obedience.

When God has clearly spoken, my responsibility is not to redesign His direction until it resembles the strategy I would have preferred.

**Leadership truth:** Innovation is not faith. Obedience is faith when God has actually directed the step.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 6.

## 2026-05-03 — After Jericho

**Record type:** Leadership Progress Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #reflective #confidence #caution

Success creates its own leadership risks.

After Jericho, Ai appeared comparatively small. The men who examined it advised that only a limited force would be needed.

We had just watched the walls of Jericho fall.

It is easy after a major success to assume momentum is evidence that the next assignment will be straightforward. Familiar confidence can quietly replace dependence.

The next report from Ai was not a victory report.

**2026 application:** After major wins, maintain normal preparation, prayer, review, and accountability. Celebration should not cancel vigilance.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 6:27; Joshua 7:1–5.

## 2026-05-10 — Defeat at Ai

**Record type:** Leadership Hardship Report  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Moses, Caleb  
**Status:** Attention Required  
**Tags:** #serious #dramatic #grief #confusion #failure

Israel was defeated at Ai. Thirty-six men died.

The people who had crossed the Jordan and seen Jericho fall suddenly lost courage.

I tore my clothes and fell before the ark. My prayer was not composed. I asked why God had brought us across the Jordan if we were going to be handed over to our enemies.

God's answer did not allow me to remain only in grief. There was wrongdoing in the camp that had to be identified and addressed.

I record this because leaders sometimes want failure to become inspirational too quickly.

Some failures need to hurt before they teach us anything.

People died at Ai. I will not turn that into a clever leadership illustration.

**Attention:** Failure requires investigation, prayer, accountability, and care for those affected.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 7:1–12.

## 2026-05-17 — Achan and Accountability

**Record type:** Leadership / Accountability Reflection  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Moses, Caleb  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #grief #conviction #accountability

The investigation after Ai exposed Achan's disobedience. He had taken items from Jericho that God had forbidden Israel to take and hidden them among his possessions.

The consequences described in Joshua 7 are severe. This is not an account I want to soften into a generic story about improving team processes.

It teaches me that sin can have consequences beyond the person who tries to conceal it, and that leadership cannot call avoidance compassion.

At the same time, accountability should never become entertainment. Exposure of wrongdoing is not an opportunity for leaders to enjoy being correct.

**2026 leadership principle:** Investigate truthfully, act justly, protect appropriate confidentiality, care for affected people, and do not sensationalize failure.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 7:13–26.

## 2026-05-24 — Returning to Ai

**Record type:** Testimony / Recovery Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #hope #courage #relief #restoration

After the sin connected to the first defeat at Ai was addressed, God told me not to be afraid or discouraged and instructed us to return.

That command mattered.

Accountability was not the end of the story. Failure did not permanently disqualify the entire community from moving forward.

We returned with a different plan and Ai was taken.

I am learning again that healthy leadership needs both accountability and restoration. If we refuse accountability, we repeat what caused harm. If we refuse restoration after correction, failure becomes a permanent identity.

**Leadership truth:** Correction should make faithful return possible where restoration is appropriate.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 8:1–29.

**Record type:** Teaching / Leadership Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete

After Ai, I built an altar and read the words of the law before the assembly. Blessings and curses were read. Scripture notes that there was not a word Moses had commanded that I failed to read before the whole congregation, including women, children, and foreigners living among us.

After failure, we did not merely improve the tactical plan. We returned to God's word.

That sequence matters.

A leadership team can become so focused on correcting process that it never asks whether the deeper issue is obedience.

**2026 action:** When significant failures occur, review not only procedures but also the principles and commitments that should govern the work.

## 2026-06-07 — The Gibeonite Presentation Was Convincing

**Record type:** Decision Review  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #reflective #uncertainty #discernment

The Gibeonites came with a carefully constructed story.

Their sacks were worn. Their wineskins were old and cracked. Their sandals and clothes looked worn from travel. Their bread was dry and crumbly. They claimed they had come from a distant country.

We examined what they presented.

It looked credible.

We made an agreement with them.

Three days later, we discovered they lived nearby.

The biblical account identifies our failure in one sentence: **we did not inquire of the Lord.**

I want this account preserved because it corrects a false choice. We do not have to choose between careful analysis and prayer. We need both.

**Related goal:** Seek God before committing the organization.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 9:1–16.

## 2026-06-14 — Due Diligence Is Not Discernment

**Record type:** Personal Development  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #reflective #conviction #discernment #humility

I have reviewed the Gibeonite decision again this week.

What bothers me is that we were not obviously careless. We asked questions. We inspected evidence. We considered their explanation.

That is exactly why the account is useful.

A leader can follow a respectable decision process and still omit the most important question.

Prayer is not a ceremonial step added after analysis so a decision feels spiritual. Seeking God should be part of discernment before commitment.

**2026 action:** For commitments with significant long-term consequences, explicitly record whether spiritual discernment and prayer occurred before approval.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 9:14–15.

## 2026-06-21 — We Still Had to Honor the Agreement

**Record type:** Leadership Ethics Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #responsibility #regret #integrity

Discovering the Gibeonites' deception did not erase the oath Israel's leaders had made.

The congregation complained against the leaders, but the leaders would not attack the Gibeonites because of the oath sworn before the Lord.

Our failure to seek God before the agreement created consequences that could not simply be removed by saying, "We made the decision with incomplete information."

Leaders sometimes inherit the consequences of their own poor judgment.

Integrity can require honoring obligations even while acknowledging that the process that produced them was flawed.

**Leadership truth:** Repentance does not always remove consequences. Sometimes it changes how faithfully we carry them.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 9:16–27.

- I am learning to distinguish preparation from delay.
