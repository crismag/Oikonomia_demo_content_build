## July 5, 2026 — Significant Progress Is Not Finished Work

**Record type:** Leadership Progress Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #reflective #responsibility

There came a point when God told me plainly that I had become old and there was still very much land remaining to be possessed.

That statement came after real victories.

We had crossed the Jordan. Jericho had fallen. Ai had been taken after correction. Major campaigns had been fought. The work accomplished was substantial.

It was still unfinished.

Leaders need language capable of holding both truths at once. If we speak only about what remains, people become discouraged. If we speak only about what has been accomplished, unresolved responsibility disappears behind celebration.

**2026 reporting principle:** Celebrate progress honestly and report unfinished work honestly.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 13:1–7.

## July 12, 2026 — Administration Is Also Leadership

**Record type:** Leadership / Operations Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #administrative #serious #responsibility

Some of the work people remember from my life involves battles. A great deal of later responsibility involved boundaries, inheritance, tribal allocation, cities, unresolved claims, and making sure responsibilities were assigned.

That work is less dramatic than Jericho.

It is also leadership.

A community cannot live indefinitely on moments of breakthrough. Eventually somebody has to document what belongs where, settle responsibilities, address disputes, and make ordinary structures function.

I appreciate our operational and Facilities leaders more because of this history.

**Leadership truth:** The work after the breakthrough may be less memorable and more necessary.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 13–21.

## July 19, 2026 — Caleb Asked for the Difficult Place

**Record type:** Leadership Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #inspiring #courage #gratitude #joy

Caleb came to me during the allocation of the land and reminded me of what God had said decades earlier after we explored Canaan.

He was eighty-five years old and said he was still as strong for the work as when Moses sent us out. He asked for the hill country where the Anakites and their fortified cities were located.

I blessed him and gave him Hebron as his inheritance.

I have spent much of this year thinking about developing younger leaders. Caleb reminds me not to make the opposite mistake and assume experienced leaders are useful only for advice while meaningful assignments belong elsewhere.

**Leadership truth:** Development includes younger leaders, but healthy leadership also recognizes enduring strength and calling in experienced people.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 14:6–15.

## July 26, 2026 — Peer Leadership With Caleb

**Record type:** Leadership Progress Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #reflective #gratitude #fellowship

Moses was my mentor. Caleb was something different.

He stood beside me.

We saw the land together. We disagreed with the fearful report together. We faced the anger of the congregation together. We waited through the wilderness years together. We eventually entered the land together.

Leadership development discussions often focus on mentors and mentees. I do not want to neglect peers.

There are things Caleb understands without requiring a long explanation because he was there.

**2026 action:** Protect peer relationships where honesty does not depend on hierarchy.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 13–14; Joshua 14.

## August 2, 2026 — Giving Someone a Real Decision

**Record type:** Mentorship Progress Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Active  
**Tags:** #reflective #growth #hope #leadership

I assigned a developing leader a decision this week and then caught myself preparing to tell them exactly how to make it.

I stopped.

This is more difficult than I expected.

For months I have wanted Moses to trust me with real responsibility. Now I understand how tempting it is to delegate the task while retaining the decision.

If I already require the person to reach my answer using my method, I may be training compliance rather than leadership.

There are boundaries. There are decisions that need review. But development requires enough room for judgment to become real.

**Related goal:** Develop leaders instead of remaining Moses' permanent successor.

**2026 fictional activity:** Contemporary Data Play mentorship event.

**Tags:** #light #reflective #humility #growth

A developing leader made a decision differently from how I would have made it.

The decision was reasonable.

Nothing was wrong with it.

I still felt an immediate desire to improve it.

The experience exposed how easily "mentoring" can become a respectable word for keeping control.

**Action:** When a delegated decision is sound, resist revising it merely because I prefer another method.

## August 16, 2026 — Ask Before You Commit

**Record type:** Decision-Making Progress Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #discernment #conviction #growth

I reviewed our major commitments for this quarter against the lesson I learned from the Gibeonites.

The question was not simply whether documentation existed. It was whether we had paused long enough to seek God before making commitments that would bind other people later.

I found improvement. Prayer and counsel are happening earlier in the process rather than being added after the preferred option has already been selected.

One decision was delayed because the information was adequate but the leadership group did not yet have peace about proceeding. I consider that delay healthy.

**Progress:** Goal remains active, with measurable improvement.

**Related goal:** Seek God before committing the organization.

**Biblical foundation:** Joshua 9.

## August 23, 2026 — Tell the Children Why

**Record type:** Teaching / Ministry Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #devotional #pastoral #hope #teaching

I met with leaders thinking about how we explain church practices and testimonies to children.

My reference point remains the memorial stones from the Jordan.

God expected children to ask, "What do these stones mean?" The adults were expected to know enough of the story to answer.

I am concerned whenever our explanation for a practice becomes only, "This is what we do here."

Tradition can preserve something valuable, but unexplained tradition eventually becomes habit without testimony.

**Ministry connection:** Gifts and Arrows.

**Action:** Encourage ministry documentation to include a simple explanation of why significant practices exist and what biblical truth they are intended to teach.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 4:19–24.

## August 30, 2026 — Courage for the Next Leader

**Record type:** Mentorship Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #reflective #hope #courage #mentorship

I have spent much of my life receiving the words "be strong and courageous."

I am beginning to think more seriously about how I speak courage into leaders who will carry responsibility after me.

The goal cannot be to make them dependent on my reassurance. Moses' encouragement worked because he kept pointing beyond himself: the Lord would go with me and would not leave me.

Good mentorship should eventually make the mentor less central, not more.

**Leadership truth:** The greatest reassurance I can give another leader is not "I will always be here to answer you," but "God will be with you as you obey Him."

**Biblical foundation:** Deuteronomy 31:7–8, 23; Joshua 1:1–9.

## September 6, 2026 — Remember the Whole Journey

**Record type:** Leadership Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #reflective #gratitude #serious #testimony

As this season moves toward transition, I have been reviewing more than victories.

The story includes Amalek and the support of Aaron and Hur. It includes years assisting Moses. It includes the fear of the spies and the faith of Caleb. It includes commissioning, the Jordan, Jericho, the grief of Ai, the exposure of Achan's sin, recovery, the Gibeonite mistake, battles, land allocation, and unfinished work.

A testimony edited to contain only victories becomes less useful to the next generation.

They need to know that God was faithful when we obeyed, that our failures had consequences, that correction mattered, and that restoration was sometimes possible afterward.

**Related goal:** Preserve testimony so the next generation knows why.

## September 13, 2026 — Choose Whom You Will Serve

**Record type:** Teaching / Testimony Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #devotional #serious #conviction #commitment

Near the end of my leadership, I gathered Israel and reviewed the history of God's faithfulness—from Abraham, through Egypt and the Exodus, through the wilderness, and into the land.

Then I challenged the people to choose whom they would serve.

I said, "As for me and my house, we will serve the Lord."

That statement is often displayed by itself. In its original setting it came after a long retelling of what God had done and alongside a warning against turning to other gods.

It was not decoration. It was covenant commitment.

For the next generation, testimony should lead somewhere. Remembering what God has done should call us to faithfulness now.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 24:1–28.

## September 20, 2026 — Do Not Let the Story End With Joshua

**Record type:** Leadership Development Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Active  
**Tags:** #serious #reflective #hope #mentorship

One of my most important goals this year has been to stop thinking of leadership development only as something Moses did for me.

If the story is Moses developing Joshua, then ending with Joshua is not success.

I have begun transferring meaningful decisions to developing leaders and intentionally allowing them to exercise judgment. I am also documenting enough context that they inherit lessons rather than only responsibilities.

There is still work to do. I am better at receiving delegation than giving it.

That sentence has been humbling to write.

**Next step:** Continue identifying responsibilities that can move outward rather than upward.

**Related goal:** Develop leaders instead of remaining Moses' permanent successor.

## September 27, 2026 — Finishing Faithfully

**Record type:** Leadership Progress / Season Review  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #reflective #devotional #gratitude #hope #peace

This year began with a question about whether I could carry responsibility without continually looking over my shoulder for Moses' approval.

I finish this reporting season with a different question: am I preparing other people to carry responsibility without continually looking over their shoulder for mine?

The journey between those questions has included courage, failure, correction, testimony, discernment, administration, friendship, mentorship, and the repeated faithfulness of God.

I am grateful for Moses. I am grateful for Caleb. I am grateful for the people who followed when leadership changed and for those who questioned us when questions were necessary.

I also know that leadership is never the final object of the story.

Near the end of my biblical account, Israel served the Lord throughout my lifetime and the lifetime of the elders who outlived me and had experienced what the Lord had done for Israel.

That is a better measure of finishing than whether people remember Joshua as impressive.

**Prayer:** Lord, let what we build remain faithful when the leaders whose names are attached to it are no longer the ones carrying it.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 23–24, especially Joshua 24:29–31.
