# Joshua — Special Documents: Twelve Spies Personnel and Assessment Package

> **Ownership note:** Moses, acting at the Lord's command, sent the twelve men to explore Canaan. Joshua was one of the selected representatives, not the hiring manager. This package is included under Joshua because the assignment became a defining part of his leadership history. Numbers 13 provides the names and assignment instructions; Numbers 13–14 records the competing reports and consequences.

---

## Personnel Selection Record — Twelve Representatives

**Record type:** Personnel Selection / Special Assignment

One leader from each ancestral tribe was selected to explore the land of Canaan.

## Assignment Brief — Exploration of Canaan

**Record type:** Assignment / Scope Document  
**Issued by:** Moses  
**Status:** Completed  
**Tags:** #administrative #serious #assessment

### Scope

The twelve representatives were instructed to explore Canaan and assess:

- whether the people were strong or weak,
- whether they were few or many,
- whether the land was good or bad,
- whether the towns were unwalled or fortified,
- whether the soil was fertile or poor,
- whether trees were present.

They were also told to bring back some of the fruit of the land.

### Reporting expectation

Return with direct observations and evidence sufficient to inform the congregation about the land.

### Joshua reflection

The assignment itself was sensible. We were expected to observe carefully. Faith did not require us to enter the land without looking at it.

The later failure was not that the team gathered evidence. The failure was how most of the team interpreted the evidence in relation to God's promise.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 13:17–20.

## Field Evidence Record — Canaan

**Record type:** Reconnaissance Evidence  
**Status:** Completed  
**Tags:** #administrative #serious #evidence

### Observations recorded

The team explored the land from the wilderness of Zin as far as Rehob toward Lebo Hamath.

At the Valley of Eshkol, the men cut a branch bearing a single cluster of grapes so large that two men carried it on a pole between them. They also brought back pomegranates and figs.

The reconnaissance lasted forty days.

### Evidence

- Land described as flowing with milk and honey.
- Fruit brought back as physical evidence.
- People described as powerful.
- Cities described as fortified and very large.
- Descendants of Anak observed.
- Amalekites, Hittites, Jebusites, Amorites, and Canaanites identified in various regions.

### Assessment note

The later disagreement did not require Joshua and Caleb to deny these observations.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 13:21–29.

## Majority Assessment — Ten Representatives

**Record type:** Assessment Report  
**Outcome:** Majority recommendation — Do not proceed  
**Status:** Historical  
**Tags:** #dramatic #serious #fear #risk

Ten representatives concluded that Israel was not able to attack the people because they were stronger.

Their report emphasized the strength of the inhabitants, the fortified cities, and the intimidating size of the people they had seen. Numbers describes their report as a bad report about the land and records language portraying Israel as insignificant by comparison.

### Effect

The report spread fear through the congregation.

People wept through the night, complained against Moses and Aaron, wished they had died in Egypt or the wilderness, and proposed choosing a leader to return to Egypt.

### Joshua's later assessment

The problem was not that the ten recognized risk.

The problem was that risk became the controlling reality in their recommendation and God's promise was effectively removed from the decision.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 13:31–33; Numbers 14:1–4.

## Minority Assessment — Joshua and Caleb

**Record type:** Dissenting / Minority Report  
**Submitted by:** Joshua son of Nun and Caleb son of Jephunneh  
**Recommendation:** Proceed in faith and obedience  
**Status:** Historical  
**Tags:** #dramatic #serious #courage #faith #hope

### Assessment

The land we explored is exceedingly good.

If the Lord is pleased with us, He will lead us into the land and give it to us.

The people should not rebel against the Lord and should not be afraid of the inhabitants of the land.

### Important clarification

Our minority report did not claim:
- the cities were not fortified,
- the inhabitants were weak,
- there was no danger,
- the majority had fabricated every observation.

We disagreed about the conclusion.

The majority treated the obstacles as decisive. Caleb and I believed God's promise and presence were more decisive.

### Response received

The congregation discussed stoning us.

### Leadership lesson

A minority report should not be dismissed because it lacks votes, nor accepted merely because dissent appears courageous. The substance still has to be tested before God.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 14:6–10.

## Personnel Outcome Review — Twelve-Spies Assignment

**Record type:** Personnel / Assignment Outcome Review  
**Status:** Closed  
**Tags:** #serious #dramatic #accountability #courage

### Assignment objective

Explore Canaan and report findings.

### Operational completion

All twelve completed the forty-day reconnaissance and returned with observations.

### Leadership outcome

The assignment produced a severe division in interpretation and recommendation.

Ten representatives spread a report that contributed to fear and rebellion in the congregation. Joshua and Caleb urged faith and obedience.

Numbers 14 records God's judgment on the unbelieving generation and specifically distinguishes Joshua and Caleb from the men whose report caused the community to grumble.

### Joshua's personnel lesson

Selecting recognized leaders does not guarantee a faithful conclusion.

Representation matters. Experience matters. Evidence matters. Character, faith, and interpretation under pressure matter too.
