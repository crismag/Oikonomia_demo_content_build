# Joshua — 2026 Goals

**Oikonomia section:** Goals

These are Joshua's actual 2026 Oikonomia goals. They reflect a leader who spent years serving faithfully under Moses and is now learning to exercise delegated authority, make difficult decisions, recover from failure, seek God before commitment, and prepare other leaders rather than remaining permanently defined as Moses' successor.

## Goal 1 — Lead the decisions that have actually been delegated to me

**Category:** Leadership Development  
**Status:** Active  
**Period:** January–September 2026  
**Target:** 2026-09

I have spent much of my life serving alongside Moses. That history taught me loyalty, patience, courage, and the value of learning before assuming responsibility. It can also make it too easy for me to look toward Moses when a decision has already been entrusted to me.

Moses has been intentionally transferring responsibility during 2026. If I continue sending ordinary decisions back to him, I am not honoring the delegation. I am only preserving the old structure under a different name.

I want to receive responsibility fully: listen carefully, seek God, consult appropriately, make the decision, communicate it clearly, and remain accountable for the result.

**Measures**
- Make delegated decisions without routinely returning them to Moses for approval.
- Clearly identify which matters belong to me and which genuinely require escalation.
- Redirect attempts to bypass established leadership responsibility.
- Review difficult decisions during Tuesday Mentorship Night without turning mentorship into retroactive permission seeking.
- Accept responsibility for outcomes rather than attributing every decision to Moses.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 17:8–16; Numbers 27:15–23; Deuteronomy 31:7–8, 23; Joshua 1:1–9.

## Goal 2 — Practice courage without pretending I am never afraid

**Category:** Personal / Leadership Development  
**Status:** Active  
**Period:** January–December 2026  
**Target:** 2026-12

I have heard the words "be strong and courageous" many times. They were not given to me because leadership would always feel easy.

After Moses' season of leadership, I was responsible for leading the people forward into a work larger than anything I had previously carried. God repeatedly told me not to be afraid or discouraged and promised to be with me.

I do not want to turn those commands into a performance in which a leader must never admit uncertainty, pressure, or fear. Courage is not pretending that the responsibility is small. Courage is obeying God while understanding that it is not.

I remember standing with Caleb after we explored Canaan. Ten of the twelve men sent to examine the land concluded that the obstacles were too great. The congregation became frightened and wanted to turn back. Caleb and I believed that if the Lord was pleased with us, He could bring us into the land despite what we had seen.

Faith did not require us to deny the fortified cities or the strength of the people living there. It required us to refuse to treat those realities as greater than God.

**Measures**
- Name significant concerns honestly rather than hiding them behind confident language.
- Distinguish realistic risk assessment from fear-driven retreat.
- Seek prayer when responsibility feels heavier than I want others to know.
- Do not allow majority anxiety to determine a decision that belongs to obedience.
- Encourage other leaders to practice the same kind of honest courage.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 13–14; Deuteronomy 31:7–8, 23; Joshua 1:1–9.

## Goal 3 — Seek God before committing the organization

**Category:** Spiritual Leadership / Decision Making  
**Status:** Active  
**Period:** January–December 2026  
**Target:** 2026-12

I want prayer and discernment to occur before major commitments, not merely after we have already decided what appears reasonable.

The Gibeonites once came to Israel pretending to have traveled from a distant country. They brought worn-out sacks, old wineskins, patched sandals, and dry, crumbly bread as evidence. Their presentation was convincing. We examined their provisions and accepted the explanation they gave us.

Scripture records the part of that meeting I do not want to forget: **we did not inquire of the Lord.**

We had information. We inspected the evidence. We discussed the situation. We reached an agreement.

What we did not do was seek God before making the commitment.

I learned that due diligence and spiritual discernment are not competitors, but neither can replace the other.

**Measures**
- Include prayer and spiritual discernment in significant commitments before approval.
- Avoid treating urgency as permission to skip discernment.
- Ask what information has not been independently verified.
- Invite appropriate counsel when a commitment has long-term consequences.
- Record lessons when an apparently reasonable decision proves incomplete or mistaken.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 9:1–27.

## Goal 4 — Investigate failure instead of assuming yesterday's success guarantees today's result

**Category:** Leadership Development / Accountability  
**Status:** Active  
**Period:** January–December 2026  
**Target:** 2026-12

Jericho was an extraordinary victory. The next assignment looked smaller.

Men sent to examine Ai advised that only a limited force would be necessary because the population was small. Israel went up against Ai and was defeated. Men died, and the confidence of the people collapsed.

My first response was grief and confusion before God. The defeat eventually exposed something we had not addressed: Achan had taken items from Jericho that God had forbidden Israel to take.

The experience taught me not to assume that yesterday's victory guarantees today's outcome, and not to treat unexpected failure as something to hide quickly so everyone can move on.

Failure may require prayer, investigation, accountability, correction, a changed plan, and another attempt.

**Measures**
- Conduct honest review after significant failures or unexpected outcomes.
- Separate learning from blame while still addressing actual wrongdoing.
- Do not allow previous success to eliminate appropriate preparation.
- Document corrective actions and confirm they are completed.
- Return to appropriate work after correction rather than allowing one failure to define the team permanently.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 6–8, especially Joshua 7.

## Goal 5 — Develop leaders instead of remaining Moses' permanent successor

**Category:** Mentorship / Multiplication  
**Status:** Active  
**Period:** February–December 2026  
**Target:** 2026-12

For a long time my leadership identity could accurately be described in relation to Moses. I served him, learned from him, accompanied him, received responsibility from him, and was eventually commissioned to lead after him.

That history is a gift, but "Moses' successor" cannot be the final definition of my leadership.

If Moses prepares Joshua and Joshua prepares no one, the pattern stops with me.

During 2026 I want to identify leaders who can receive real responsibility, make decisions, learn through experience, and eventually develop others themselves.

**Measures**
- Identify emerging leaders for intentional development.
- Give at least two leaders meaningful responsibilities rather than only support tasks.
- Use Tuesday Mentorship Night for both receiving and giving mentorship.
- Allow developing leaders to make reasonable decisions differently from how I would make them.
- Record their progress without comparing them constantly to Moses, Caleb, or me.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 24:13; Exodus 33:11; Numbers 27:15–23; Deuteronomy 31; Joshua 1.

## Goal 6 — Preserve testimony so the next generation knows why

**Category:** Testimony / Teaching / Institutional Memory  
**Status:** Active  
**Period:** January–December 2026  
**Target:** 2026-12

When Israel crossed the Jordan, God instructed that twelve stones be taken from the river and set up as a memorial. The purpose was not decoration.

The stones were meant to create a question. When children in later generations asked, "What do these stones mean?" their parents were to explain that Israel crossed the Jordan on dry ground because the Lord stopped the river before them, just as He had done at the Red Sea.

The next generation should not inherit practices whose meaning has been forgotten.

I want our records, testimonies, celebrations, and teaching to preserve more than dates and attendance. They should help people understand what God has done, what we learned, why important decisions were made, and why we continue certain practices.

**Measures**
- Record significant testimonies and leadership milestones in language future readers can understand.
- Explain the meaning behind major practices rather than assuming everyone already knows it.
- Work with Gifts and Arrows when testimony should be passed intentionally to children and younger generations.
- Preserve important lessons from both victories and failures.
- Include enough biblical context that readers unfamiliar with the account can understand why it matters.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 3–4.

## 2026 goal relationship to Moses

Joshua's goals deliberately overlap with Moses' goals without duplicating them.

Moses is learning to give Joshua genuine authority. Joshua is learning to receive it without continually returning it.

Moses is learning to prepare a successor without requiring him to become another Moses. Joshua is learning to become a leader in his own right and eventually prepare others.

Their progress should therefore be visible from both sides of shared 2026 events.
