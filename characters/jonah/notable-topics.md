# Jonah — Notable Topics

Use these as source topics for later Oikonomia records; they are not prompts and do not imply exclusive ownership. Fuller retellings, truths and fact/inference notes are in `biblical-context.md`.

## Before Nineveh
- **Prophet under Jeroboam II** — 2 Kings 14:23–27. Undeserved mercy to Israel under an evil king; Jonah had already carried compassion to his own people.

## Flight and storm
- **Call to Nineveh and flight toward Tarshish** — Jonah 1:1–3. Reluctant outreach assignment, avoidance, direction opposite the task; paid his own fare; going "down."
- **Storm and sailors** — Jonah 1:4–16. Consequences affecting uninvolved teammates; sailors' fear and prayer; Jonah asleep in the hold; accurate confession ("I fear the LORD") contradicted by his flight; sailors row to save him and end up worshipping the LORD.
- **Thrown overboard / great fish** — Jonah 1:15–17. Crisis and deliverance; "huge fish," not "whale"; do not make suffering slapstick.
- **Prayer from the fish** — Jonah 2. Distress, remembrance, thanksgiving; "When my soul fainted within me, I remembered the LORD"; "Salvation belongs to the LORD."

## Nineveh
- **Second call and Nineveh preaching** — Jonah 3:1–4. Reassignment after failure with the same destination and actual obedience; one recorded sentence.
- **Nineveh's repentance** — Jonah 3:5–10. Unexpected ministry outcome; repentance from the greatest to the least; the king names the city's violence; "Who knows whether God will not turn and relent?"; God relents.

## Jonah's heart
- **Jonah's anger at mercy** — Jonah 4:1–4. Resentment when outreach succeeds for people he did not want helped; "I knew that you are a gracious God and merciful"; request to die (no clinical labels); "Is it right for you to be angry?"
- **Watching from the east side** — Jonah 4:5. Waiting for judgment instead of rejoicing in repentance.
- **Plant, worm and God's question** — Jonah 4:6–11. Compassion, priorities, and unresolved ending; the same "prepared" for fish, vine, worm and wind; 120,000 people "and also many animals." Jonah's answer is not recorded; never invent it.

## Later references (Jonah has no memory of these)
- **The sign of Jonah** — Matthew 12:38–41; 16:4; Luke 11:29–32. Three days and three nights; the men of Nineveh held up as an example of repentance; "someone greater than Jonah is here."
- **Nahum's oracle against Nineveh** — Nahum 1:1. Not to be used to vindicate Jonah.
- **Namesake** — Peter's father "Jonah" (John 1:42; 21:15–17) is a different person.

## Strong Oikonomia surfaces
- **Field Ministry and Reach-Out:** outreach and offsite assignments to people Jonah would not choose; minimal-but-accurate outreach reports; unexpected positive responses.
- **Personal development:** restricted coaching with Peter on anger, resentment and compassion; goals that stay honest about an unresolved question.
- **Reassignment and attention items:** a repeated assignment, a travel deviation, a delayed start.
- **Prayer and testimony:** prayer from the depths; a second chance.
- **Lifegroup:** attends Peter's group; discussion of God's final question.
- **Comments:** Cris's expense and travel questions; Peter's patient follow-ups; Elisha on Naaman.
- **Humor:** administrative humor may surround repeated outreach assignment, the Tarshish fare and the one-sentence sermon, but ethnic hatred, danger, requests to die and judgment must remain serious.
