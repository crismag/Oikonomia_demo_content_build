# Jonah — 2026 Goals

**Oikonomia section:** Goals

These are Jonah's 2026 Oikonomia goals. They come from his lived biblical history and are meant to gather progress through Field Ministry assignments, Reach-Out reports and coaching conversations with Peter. Progress entries are added as records are written; none are recorded yet.

---

## Goal 1 — Go the first time

**Status:** Active  
**Ministry:** Field Ministry  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #serious #obedience #accountability

### Why this matters

God told me to go to Nineveh. I went down to Joppa, paid my own fare and sailed for Tarshish, the other direction. Then I slept in the bottom of the ship while the crew threw their cargo overboard and prayed to every god they knew. They had nothing to do with my decision, and they nearly died for it. The word came to me a second time, with the same destination. I went.

Running didn't change the assignment. It just made other people pay for my avoidance first.

### 2026 actions

- When Peter gives me an assignment I don't want, tell him honestly within a day, and then go anyway unless he changes it.
- No quiet substitutions: if I think another assignment is better, ask; don't just do the other one.
- Before an offsite service or visit I am dreading, pray about the people I'm going to, not only about my reluctance.
- Notice who else is affected when I delay, and tell them.

**Biblical account:** Jonah 1:1–16; Jonah 3:1–3.

## Goal 2 — Let the question God asked me stay open until it changes me

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #reflective #anger #repentance

### Why this matters

When Nineveh repented and God relented, I was furious. I told God this was exactly why I ran: "I knew that you are a gracious God and merciful, slow to anger, and abundant in loving kindness, and you relent of doing harm." I asked Him to take my life. He asked me, "Is it right for you to be angry?" I went and sat outside the city to see if He would destroy it after all.

I can describe God's mercy correctly. I don't always want it to reach the people I resent. God didn't strike me for that. He kept asking.

### 2026 actions

- Name, in restricted coaching with Peter, the people and groups I most resent receiving mercy.
- When an outreach succeeds with people I didn't want helped, write it in my report as good news, not under "concerns."
- Pray by name for one person or community I would rather see judged, weekly.
- Don't pretend this goal is finished because a few weeks go well. God's question in my story has no recorded answer, and I won't invent one.

**Biblical account:** Jonah 3:10–4:5.

## Goal 3 — Care more about people than about my shade

**Status:** Active  
**Ministry:** Field Ministry  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #pastoral #compassion #humility

### Why this matters

Outside Nineveh, God grew a vine to shade my head, and I was glad. The next morning a worm killed it, the east wind blew, the sun beat on my head, and I said I was right to be angry enough to die. God said I cared about a plant I hadn't planted or grown, which came up in a night and died in a night. Then He asked whether He shouldn't care about a great city of more than 120,000 people who couldn't tell their right hand from their left, and many animals.

My comfort had become more important to me than a city full of people. God cared about the city and even its animals.

### 2026 actions

- On offsite services, stay after the programme ends to talk with people, even when it's inconvenient.
- In Reach-Out reports, write at least one specific thing I noticed about the people I met, not only what I said.
- When I catch myself upset about a small personal loss during ministry, ask what I am overlooking about the people in front of me.
- Serve at least once a quarter alongside More Than Enough or Greeters and Ushers with newcomers from backgrounds I find hard, under their leadership.

**Biblical account:** Jonah 4:5–11.
