# Jeremiah — 2026 Goals

**Oikonomia section:** Goals

These are Jeremiah's 2026 Oikonomia goals. They come from his lived biblical history and are meant to gather progress through his Thursday Lifegroup, Mentorship Night, counsel to leaders facing hard conversations and his reports to Samuel. Progress entries are added as records are written; none are recorded yet.

---

## Goal 1 — Say the hard thing with tears, not with contempt

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #serious #lament #compassion

### Why this matters

I spent decades telling people what they didn't want to hear. I wished I were wrong. When Hananiah announced that the exiles would be home in two years, I said, "Amen! May the LORD do so." I also wept for the people I warned: "Oh that my head were waters, and my eyes a spring of tears." And I prayed for God to pay my persecutors back, children and all. Those prayers are recorded because they are true, not because they were right. God told me, "If you take out the precious from the vile, you will be as my mouth."

In 2026 I help leaders say difficult things. A hard message delivered with contempt is only half a true message.

### 2026 actions

- When I help a leader prepare a difficult announcement, start with grief for the people affected before discussing wording.
- Refuse false reassurance in meetings; offer honest hope instead.
- Bring my anger about opponents to God and to Samuel in restricted reflection, not into shared records.
- Pray by name for the people who resisted a message I gave, at least once a month.

**Biblical account:** Jeremiah 9:1; Jeremiah 15:15–21; Jeremiah 18:19–23; Jeremiah 28:5–9.

## Goal 2 — Write it down, keep it safe, and rewrite it if it burns

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #administrative #conviction #hope

### Why this matters

I dictated God's words to Baruch, and he read them in the temple. The king had them read to him by a fire and cut the scroll up three or four columns at a time and burned it all. God told me to write it again. We did, and more was added. When I bought my cousin's field during the siege, I signed the deed, sealed one copy, left one open, called witnesses, and had Baruch put both in a clay jar so they would last.

Words that matter to other people's futures should be recorded carefully enough to survive a bad reader, a fire or a long wait.

### 2026 actions

- Keep a clear record of every significant piece of counsel or decision I am part of, shared with the right people.
- Work with Ezra on a simple standard for recording leadership decisions: who decided, who witnessed, where it is kept.
- When a document or plan is rejected or lost, rebuild it rather than abandon it, and note what was learned.
- Keep a backup of anything a ministry would need if its leader were unavailable.

**Biblical account:** Jeremiah 32:6–15; Jeremiah 36:1–32.

## Goal 3 — Name the people who pull me out, and look after the ones who carry the scroll

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #pastoral #gratitude #humility

### Why this matters

I didn't survive by myself. Ahikam son of Shaphan stood with me when the crowd wanted me dead. When I sank in the mud of the cistern, Ebed-melech the Ethiopian told the king it was wrong, then brought old rags so the ropes wouldn't cut me as thirty men pulled me out. Baruch wrote, read, hid and went to Egypt with me. He also said, "Woe is me now! … I am weary with my groaning, and I find no rest," and God answered him personally.

I have tended to experience ministry as me against everyone. It was never true, and it isn't fair to the people who carried it with me.

### 2026 actions

- Thank the people who support hard conversations, specifically and in writing.
- Check in with any volunteer or assistant working with me at least monthly about their load, not only the work.
- Stand with a leader under pressure the way Ahikam stood with me, when it is right to do so.
- Welcome practical help without insisting I can manage.

**Biblical account:** Jeremiah 26:24; Jeremiah 38:1–13; Jeremiah 39:15–18; Jeremiah 45:1–5.

## Goal 4 — Buy the field: practise hope in long seasons

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #reflective #hope #faith

### Why this matters

I wrote to the exiles in Babylon: build houses, plant gardens, have families, "seek the peace of the city where I have caused you to be carried away captive, and pray to the LORD for it." Their stay would be seventy years, not two. Later, locked in the court of the guard with the Babylonian army outside, I paid seventeen shekels for a field in Anathoth because God said fields would be bought in this land again. I obeyed first, and then I told God I did not understand.

People quote the promise of "hope and a future" from my letter without the seventy years around it. I want to teach hope that includes the wait.

### 2026 actions

- Teach a Lifegroup study on Jeremiah 29 and 32 that keeps the context of exile and waiting.
- Encourage leaders in a hard season to make one concrete, long-horizon commitment that expresses hope.
- Pray regularly for the peace of our city, not only for the church.
- Take one practical step in my own ministry that will mainly benefit people after 2027.

**Biblical account:** Jeremiah 29:1–14; Jeremiah 32:1–27, 42–44.
