# Jeremiah — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### The LORD
God knew Jeremiah before he was formed, called him, put words in his mouth and promised to be with him (Jeremiah 1:5–9). Jeremiah ate God's words with joy (15:16), argued with God (12:1), accused Him of being like a deceitful brook (15:18), said God had persuaded him (20:7), and could not hold the word in (20:9). God answered with correction, questions and renewed promises (12:5; 15:19–21; 32:27).

### Baruch son of Neriah — scribe and co-worker (not on the roster)
Received and stored the deeds for the field (32:12–16). Wrote the scroll at Jeremiah's dictation, read it publicly and to the officials, hid with Jeremiah, and wrote the replacement scroll (36). Was weary and grieving, and received a personal word from God through Jeremiah (45). Was blamed by Johanan's group for turning Jeremiah against them, and was taken to Egypt with Jeremiah (43:3, 6).

**Inference:** Seraiah son of Neriah, the quartermaster who carried Jeremiah's scroll against Babylon (51:59–64), is probably Baruch's brother.

**Silence:** Baruch's later life is not recorded. Later books bearing his name are outside the Protestant canon; do not treat them as Scripture.

**2026:** Baruch is not on the roster. Refer to him in prose. Jeremiah's care for assistants and volunteers is shaped by Baruch.

### Ebed-melech the Ethiopian (not on the roster)
A palace official who protested to the king and pulled Jeremiah out of the cistern with ropes and old rags (38:7–13). God promised him safety because he trusted God (39:15–18).

### Ahikam son of Shaphan and Gedaliah son of Ahikam (not on the roster)
Ahikam protected Jeremiah after the temple-sermon trial (26:24). Ahikam's son Gedaliah received Jeremiah after the fall and governed at Mizpah until he was murdered (39:14; 40:5–6; 41:1–3). Other members of Shaphan's family appear favourably: Elasah carried Jeremiah's letter to the exiles (29:3), and Gemariah's room was where Baruch read the scroll and he urged the king not to burn it (36:10, 25).

**Namesake:** Gemariah son of Shaphan (36:10) and Gemariah son of Hilkiah (29:3) are different people.

### Kings: Josiah, Jehoiakim, Zedekiah (Josiah is on the roster)
- **Josiah:** Jeremiah's call came in Josiah's thirteenth year (1:2); God spoke to him in Josiah's days (3:6). Jeremiah later contrasted Jehoiakim with his father Josiah, who did justice and righteousness and judged the cause of the poor and needy (22:15–16, paraphrased). Jeremiah lamented Josiah's death (2 Chronicles 35:25). **Silence:** No meeting between them is recorded.
- **Jehoiakim** (not on the roster): killed the prophet Uriah (26:20–23), burned Jeremiah's scroll and ordered his arrest (36:21–26).
- **Zedekiah** (not on the roster): consulted Jeremiah secretly several times, confined him to the court of the guard, gave him bread, handed him to the officials, allowed his rescue, and refused his counsel to surrender (37–38).

### Opponents (not on the roster)
- **Pashhur son of Immer**, who beat him and put him in the stocks (20:1–6). **Namesake:** not Pashhur son of Malchijah (38:1).
- **Hananiah son of Azzur**, the prophet who broke his yoke (28). **Namesake:** not the other Hananiahs in Scripture.
- **The men of Anathoth**, and his own family, who dealt treacherously (11:21; 12:6).
- **Shemaiah the Nehelamite**, who wrote against him from Babylon (29:24–32).
- **Johanan son of Kareah and Azariah son of Hoshaiah**, who asked for his guidance and took him to Egypt (42–43).

### Hanamel — cousin (not on the roster)
Sold him the field at Anathoth (32:7–12).

### Nebuzaradan (not on the roster)
Babylonian commander who released Jeremiah from chains (39:11–14; 40:1–5).

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Samuel — reporting leader
Jeremiah heard God say, "Though Moses and Samuel stood before me, yet my mind would not turn toward this people" (15:1). That line gives their 2026 relationship weight: Samuel, the great intercessor, now receives Jeremiah's reports. Samuel lets Jeremiah say the hard thing in full, and then asks what he needs.

### Josiah
The young reforming king in whose reign Jeremiah's call began. In 2026 Josiah is a young reform-minded leader in Radicals. Jeremiah, who called himself "a child" at his call, has particular sympathy for a leader who started young. Jeremiah knew Josiah's reign and death (2 Chronicles 35:25); Josiah has no memory of Jeremiah's later ministry. Their relationship can carry both honour and grief, never humor about Josiah's death.

### Daniel
Daniel understood the seventy years "by the books … the LORD's word came to Jeremiah the prophet" (Daniel 9:2). **Jeremiah has no memory of Daniel reading it.** In 2026 Daniel may tell Jeremiah that his writing sustained Daniel's prayer in exile. It is one of the few places where Jeremiah learns that his words were read and believed.

### Ezra
Ezra 1:1 opens with Cyrus's decree "that the LORD's word by Jeremiah's mouth might be accomplished." **Jeremiah has no memory of it.** Ezra, a senior teacher and keeper of documents, is Jeremiah's natural ally on careful records and teaching.

### Isaiah
A prophet of an earlier century with a very different voice. Both warned against trusting Egypt. They can teach together; Isaiah's composure and Jeremiah's rawness are complementary, not competitive.

### Hezekiah
Elders at Jeremiah's trial cited Hezekiah as the king who listened to Micah's warning and sought the LORD (26:18–19). In 2026 Jeremiah may tell Hezekiah that his example once helped save Jeremiah's life. Hezekiah has no memory of Jeremiah's trial.

### Elijah and Jonah
Fellow prophets who carried hard messages and at one point wanted to die or cursed their birth. A restricted peer conversation is plausible; keep each account distinct.

### Barnabas
Mentorship head. Barnabas's encouragement meets Jeremiah's honesty; both are needed when leaders face opposition.

### Cris
Cris receives Jeremiah's documents in duplicate and asks whether the clay jar is really necessary. Cris cannot see Jeremiah's restricted lament reflections unless named.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Jeremiah / Samuel:** restricted wellbeing and lament report after a difficult message; Samuel's follow-up.
- **Jeremiah / Josiah:** Mentorship conversation about beginning young; honouring Josiah's reforms.
- **Jeremiah / Daniel:** a comment or Lifegroup moment where Daniel tells Jeremiah his writings were read in exile.
- **Jeremiah / Ezra:** a documentation standard for leadership decisions (duplicates, witnesses, storage).
- **Jeremiah / a leader facing a hard announcement:** meeting notes preparing to deliver bad news honestly.
- **Jeremiah / Cris:** duplicate-document comment thread.
- **Jeremiah and a volunteer:** a Baruch-style conversation about weariness and not seeking great things.
