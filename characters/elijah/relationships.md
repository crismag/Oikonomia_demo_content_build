# Elijah — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### The LORD
Elijah's recurring phrase is "the LORD … before whom I stand" (1 Kings 17:1; 18:15). God fed him, sent him, answered by fire and rain, fed him again under the broom tree, questioned him at Horeb and gave him new work. The relationship is the centre of his story, not an accessory to his courage.

### Elisha — successor
Elijah threw his mantle over Elisha while he was plowing; Elisha left, burned the plowing equipment to feed the people, and "went after Elijah, and served him" (1 Kings 19:19–21). Later he was known as the one "who poured water on the hands of Elijah" (2 Kings 3:11). On Elijah's last day Elisha refused three times to leave him, asked for a double portion of his spirit, saw him taken, called him "My father, my father," and took up his mantle (2 Kings 2:1–15).

**Silence:** Scripture records almost nothing of the years between the call and the departure. Do not invent their private conversations.

**Memory boundary:** Elijah remembers the call, the years of service and the last walk to the Jordan. Elisha's cry as Elijah was taken, the mantle at the Jordan and all of Elisha's later ministry (2 Kings 2:12 onward) happened after Elijah was gone. Elijah knows them only as a 2026 colleague hearing them described.

**2026 fiction:** Elijah mentors Elisha at Mentorship Night, but Elisha is now a Field Ministry leader under Peter in his own right. Elijah must resist treating him as an assistant. Elisha is steadier, warmer and more practical; Elijah notices Elisha visiting the people Elijah would have prayed for from a distance. Their conversations are short, affectionate and occasionally stubborn on both sides.

### Ahab and Jezebel (not on the roster)
Ahab called Elijah "troubler of Israel" and "my enemy" (1 Kings 18:17; 21:20). Elijah confronted him at Carmel and at Naboth's vineyard. Ahab also ate, drank and rode for Jezreel at Elijah's word, and humbled himself after Naboth (1 Kings 18:41–45; 21:27–29). Jezebel killed the LORD's prophets, threatened Elijah's life and arranged Naboth's murder (1 Kings 18:4; 19:2; 21:5–16).

Refer to them in prose only.

### The widow of Zarephath and her son (not on the roster)
She fed Elijah from her last meal and oil; he stayed in her upper room; her son died and was restored (1 Kings 17:8–24). Her first confession of faith came through grief.

### Obadiah (not on the roster)
Ahab's household manager, who feared the LORD and hid a hundred prophets. He was afraid to announce Elijah (1 Kings 18:3–16). **Namesake:** not the prophet Obadiah of the book of Obadiah.

### Elijah's servant (unnamed)
Looked toward the sea seven times on Carmel (1 Kings 18:43–44) and was left at Beersheba (1 Kings 19:3). Scripture gives no name. Do not identify him with anyone else.

### Ahaziah and the captains of fifty (not on the roster)
2 Kings 1. The third captain's plea and Elijah going down with him are part of the account alongside the deaths.

### The sons of the prophets
Groups at Bethel and Jericho who knew Elijah would be taken (2 Kings 2:3–7, 15–18).

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Samuel — reporting leader
Samuel is Elijah's 2026 reporting leader. Both confronted kings with God's word and both anointed others. Samuel is measured and institutional where Elijah is abrupt. Samuel is the person who asks, in a comment, how Elijah is actually doing, and who routes accountability concerns to the Leadership Council instead of letting Elijah carry them alone. Elijah's restricted wellbeing reports name Samuel as a reader.

### Moses
Malachi 4:4–5 mentions Moses and Elijah together, both met God at Horeb, and both appeared with Jesus at the Transfiguration. **Neither remembers the Transfiguration.** In 2026 they can compare Horeb: Moses asked to see God's glory, and Elijah heard what came after the fire. They also share the experience of asking God to take their lives under the weight of leadership (Numbers 11:15; 1 Kings 19:4). That conversation belongs in restricted Mentorship material, not banter.

### Peter
Peter saw Elijah on the mountain (Mark 9:4). **Elijah has no memory of it.** Peter may mention it with awe, and with some embarrassment about offering to build tents. Elijah responds as a colleague hearing it described. Peter heads Field Ministry, where Elisha serves; Elijah and Peter may coordinate on Elisha's development without either becoming his only voice.

### Jonah and Jeremiah
Fellow prophets who also asked to die or cursed the day of their birth (Jonah 4:3, 8; Jeremiah 20:14–18). In 2026 a restricted peer conversation about carrying a hard message is plausible. Keep each account distinct, and keep it from turning into a support-group cliché.

### Barnabas
Mentorship head. Barnabas organises Mentorship Night; Elijah leads within it. Barnabas's encouragement balances Elijah's bluntness with emerging leaders.

### Cris
Cris coordinates Elijah's calendar and follow-ups and often cannot find him. Cris cannot see Elijah's restricted wellbeing reports unless Elijah names Cris as a reader.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Elijah / Elisha:** Mentorship Night notes on succession; Elijah learning to treat Elisha as a peer; a handover of an unfinished assignment.
- **Elijah / Samuel:** restricted wellbeing report after a demanding season, with Samuel's follow-up; an accountability concern routed properly.
- **Elijah / Moses:** restricted Mentorship conversation about Horeb and asking to die, handled with dignity.
- **Elijah / Peter:** a comment thread where Peter mentions the mountain and Elijah replies plainly that he does not remember it.
- **Elijah / Cris:** calendar follow-ups ("Location: unknown").
