# Elijah — Notable Topics

Use these as source topics for later Oikonomia records; they are not prompts and do not imply exclusive ownership. Fuller retellings, truths and fact/inference notes are in `biblical-context.md`.

## Setting and early assignment
- **Ahab, Jezebel and Baal worship** — 1 Kings 16:29–34; 18:4. Background for every confrontation; Jezebel killing the LORD's prophets.
- **Drought announcement and Cherith** — 1 Kings 17:1–7; James 5:17. Prophetic assignment, isolation, provision; daily bread from ravens; the brook dries up.
- **Widow of Zarephath** — 1 Kings 17:8–16; Luke 4:25–26. Scarcity, Victuals/provision themes, hospitality from a foreign widow at her last meal.
- **Widow's son restored** — 1 Kings 17:17–24. A mother's accusation, honest prayer, restoration; suffering not comedy.

## Carmel and its aftermath
- **Obadiah and the hidden prophets** — 1 Kings 18:1–16. Quiet faithfulness inside Ahab's household; Obadiah's fear that Elijah will vanish; gentle humor about Elijah being hard to locate.
- **"Troubler of Israel"** — 1 Kings 18:17–18. Who is actually causing the trouble.
- **Mount Carmel** — 1 Kings 18:19–39. "How long will you waver?"; public confrontation, prayer, altar repair with twelve stones, decisive moment.
- **Prophets of Baal killed at Kishon** — 1 Kings 18:40. Grave; never comedy or a triumph line; not a model for 2026 action.
- **Rain prayer** — 1 Kings 18:41–46; James 5:18. Persistence after victory; "Go again" seven times; a cloud like a man's hand; running ahead of Ahab's chariot.
- **Jezebel threat and collapse under the broom tree** — 1 Kings 19:1–8. Fear, flight, leaving his servant, exhaustion, asking God to take his life, food/rest/touch, "the journey is too great for you." High sensitivity; strong wellbeing material. No clinical labels.
- **Horeb / still small voice** — 1 Kings 19:9–18. "What are you doing here, Elijah?" asked twice; "I, even I only, am left" versus the seven thousand; reorientation and renewed assignment.

## Succession and later confrontation
- **Elisha succession call** — 1 Kings 19:19–21. Mantle thrown over a plowman; "what have I done to you?"; Elisha burns the plowing gear and serves.
- **Commission partly carried out by others** — 1 Kings 19:15–16; 2 Kings 8:7–15; 9:1–10. Hazael and Jehu were reached through Elisha's ministry after Elijah was taken; an unfinished list handed on. Elijah knows the commission, not how it was fulfilled.
- **Naboth/Ahab confrontation** — 1 Kings 21. Abuse of power through letters, seals and false witnesses; "Have you killed and also taken possession?"; Ahab's humbling and God's response. Serious.
- **Ahaziah confrontation** — 2 Kings 1. "Is it because there is no God in Israel?"; fire on two captains and their fifties (grave); mercy to the third captain.
- **Letter to Jehoram of Judah** — 2 Chronicles 21:12–15. Chronology debated; do not build on it.
- **Departure in whirlwind / mantle to Elisha** — 2 Kings 2:1–15. "I will not leave you" three times; double portion; chariot of fire; "Where is the LORD, the God of Elijah?" Succession and legacy.

## Later biblical references (Elijah has no memory of these)
- **Promised Elijah** — Malachi 4:5–6; Luke 1:17; Matthew 11:14; 17:10–13; John 1:21. Interpretation differs; Elijah never claims to be or remember John the Baptist.
- **Transfiguration** — Mark 9:2–13; Luke 9:28–36. Peter saw Elijah; Elijah does not remember it.
- **Fire request rebuked** — Luke 9:54–55. James and John cite Elijah; Jesus rebukes them. Shapes how the fire accounts are used.
- **Seven thousand** — Romans 11:2–4. Paul cites Horeb.
- **"A man with a nature like ours"** — James 5:17–18. Ordinary humanity and powerful prayer.

## Strong Oikonomia surfaces
- **Prayer ministry:** weekly prayer meeting records, prayer for leaders facing confrontation, persistence ("go again").
- **Restricted wellbeing and recovery reports:** the broom tree; care for leaders after demanding seasons (see the style reference, §4).
- **Mentorship:** succession with Elisha; accurate perception versus "I only am left"; handing on unfinished assignments.
- **Leadership accountability:** Naboth-style misuse of authority, raised through Samuel and the Leadership Council rather than handled alone.
- **Personal development:** rest and food before conclusions; not working alone; either/or intensity.
- **Testimony:** Cherith, Zarephath, Carmel, Horeb.
- **Comments:** Samuel asking how Elijah actually is; Cris unable to find Elijah on the calendar; Obadiah-style nervousness about announcing him.
- Never joke about Elijah's request to die, Kishon, the captains' deaths, Naboth or the widow's son.
