# David — Reports Concerning Women, Relationships, Power, and Accountability

> These reports are intentionally not a romantic catalog. David's history with women includes wisdom, conflict, marriage, political arrangements, abuse of power, grief, and family failure. Bathsheba and Tamar in particular must be treated with seriousness and dignity.

---

## Abigail — The Woman Who Stopped Me

**Record type:** Relationship / Personal Development  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Tags:** #serious #gratitude #anger #restraint #wisdom

When Nabal insulted my men, I prepared violent retaliation against his household.

Abigail learned what had happened, prepared provisions, came to meet me, and appealed to me not to take vengeance into my own hands.

I listened.

I thanked God for sending her and thanked her for keeping me from bloodshed.

The important part of this account is not that Abigail was diplomatic. It is that she confronted a man who was armed, angry, moving toward violence, and surrounded by men prepared to follow him.

She showed courage and wisdom when my judgment was failing.

**2026 lesson:** Never build a leadership culture where the only people allowed to interrupt a senior leader are people senior to him.

**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 25.

## Michal — Celebration and a Marriage With Deep Conflict

**Record type:** Family / Relationship Reflection  
**Visibility:** Restricted  
**Tags:** #serious #relationship #conflict #grief

Michal loved me and once helped me escape when Saul intended to kill me.

Our history later became deeply complicated. Saul gave Michal to another man while I was a fugitive. I later demanded her return as part of negotiations when the kingdom was being consolidated.

When the ark came into Jerusalem and I danced before the Lord, Michal watched from a window and despised me in her heart. Our confrontation afterward was harsh.

It would be convenient to turn that scene into a simple lesson where I represent joyful worship and Michal represents criticism.

Our marriage history deserves more honesty than that.

There was love, separation, political power, forced transitions, resentment, public celebration, and private conflict.

**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 18–19; 1 Samuel 25:44; 2 Samuel 3:13–16; 2 Samuel 6:16–23.

## Bathsheba — Authority Was Not Permission

**Record type:** Serious Leadership / Personal Accountability  
**Visibility:** Highly Restricted  
**Tags:** #serious #regret #conviction #accountability #repentance

My history involving Bathsheba must never be retold as a romance.

I saw her bathing, sent people to find out who she was, and was told that she was Bathsheba, wife of Uriah the Hittite.

I sent for her and slept with her. When she became pregnant, I tried to conceal what had happened. When Uriah would not go home, I eventually sent orders that placed him in the fiercest fighting and instructed Joab to withdraw from him so he would be killed.

I used authority, servants, military command, secrecy, and another man's loyalty to protect myself.

Nathan later confronted me.

### 2026 accountability

- Position never creates sexual entitlement.
- Consent and power must never be treated casually in leadership relationships.
- Secrecy used to preserve a leader's reputation is itself a warning sign.
- Loyal subordinates must never be used to execute unethical instructions.
- Confession does not erase consequences.

**Biblical account:** 2 Samuel 11–12.

## Tamar — A Family Matter I Did Not Handle Well

**Record type:** Family / Leadership Failure  
**Visibility:** Highly Restricted  
**Tags:** #serious #grief #anger #regret #family

Tamar, my daughter, was raped by her half-brother Amnon after he deceived her into being alone with him.

She resisted him clearly and appealed to him not to do this disgraceful thing. He ignored her.

Afterward he rejected her and had her put out.

When I heard what happened, I was furious.

But the family did not receive the justice and protection it needed from me. Absalom later killed Amnon and eventually became estranged from me.

This is not material for a clever leadership analogy.

It is a family tragedy involving sexual violence, failed protection, anger, revenge, and consequences that continued for years.

### 2026 leadership commitment

When serious harm is disclosed, anger from the leader is not the same thing as an adequate response.

Victim safety, truth, appropriate reporting, accountability, care, and action matter.

**Biblical account:** 2 Samuel 13.

## Women and Power — 2026 Personal Review

**Record type:** Personal Development  
**Visibility:** Restricted  
**Status:** Active  
**Tags:** #serious #reflective #conviction #accountability

My history does not allow me to discuss leadership relationships with women as though power were irrelevant.

Abigail confronted me when I was dangerous.

Michal's story reminds me that political authority and family relationships can become entangled in painful ways.

Bathsheba's story confronts my abuse of royal power.

Tamar's story confronts my failure to respond adequately to grievous harm inside my own household.

### Current questions

- Does a person feel genuinely free to disagree with me?
- Does my role make a personal request difficult to refuse?
- Am I using private communication in ways I would be unwilling to explain to accountable leaders?
- When harm is reported, am I protecting the vulnerable or merely protecting the institution from discomfort?

I do not want biblical repentance to become a way of speaking beautifully about failures I am unwilling to guard against repeating.
