## Worship Is Response Before It Is Performance

**Record type:** Worship Reflection  
**Owner:** David  
**Tags:** #devotional #reflective #gratitude

Many of my songs came from events before they became material for public worship.

Deliverance, fear, confession, grief, wonder, enemies, creation, God's law, and God's faithfulness all became language directed back to God.

This is why I become concerned when worship is evaluated only by whether the congregation enjoyed the arrangement.

Skill matters. Preparation matters. Music matters.

But worship begins with who God is and what He has done.

**Biblical foundation:** Davidic Psalms broadly; 1 Chronicles 16.

## Worship While Afraid

**Record type:** Worship / Personal Reflection  
**Tags:** #devotional #fear #hope #faith

Some prayers associated with my fugitive years do not sound like a man pretending he is fearless.

Psalm 56's superscription connects it with the Philistines seizing David in Gath. The psalm acknowledges fear and deliberately places trust in God.

That distinction matters to me.

Faith does not require editing fear out of the report before God is allowed to see it.

Sometimes worship sounds like: I am afraid, and I will trust You.

**Biblical account:** Psalm 56 superscription and text; compare 1 Samuel 21:10–15.

## Worship After Deliverance

**Record type:** Testimony / Worship Reflection  
**Tags:** #celebratory #devotional #joy #gratitude

Psalm 18 is associated with the day the Lord delivered me from my enemies and from Saul.

The song remembers distress, prayer, rescue, strength, and God's faithfulness.

The important sequence is that deliverance becomes testimony.

A testimony should not merely announce that something worked out. It should help the community remember who God was in the middle of it.

**Biblical foundation:** Psalm 18; 2 Samuel 22.

## Worship in Repentance

**Record type:** Worship / Confession  
**Visibility:** Restricted  
**Tags:** #devotional #serious #regret #repentance #conviction

Psalm 51 does not attempt to turn failure into inspirational branding.

It asks for mercy, cleansing, truth in the inner person, a clean heart, restored joy, and a willing spirit.

The worship leader is not exempt from repentance because he knows how to write about repentance.

In fact, knowing the language can become dangerous if I learn to sound broken without becoming honest.

**Biblical foundation:** Psalm 51 superscription; 2 Samuel 11–12.

## Worship in Grief

**Record type:** Worship / Lament  
**Tags:** #devotional #serious #grief #lament

When Saul and Jonathan died, I wrote a lament.

There was no requirement to turn the funeral into a victory service because my path to the throne had become clearer.

Some moments require grief.

Psalmists must be able to serve a congregation that is celebrating, repenting, waiting, frightened, exhausted, or mourning.

If every musical response sounds triumphant, we may be asking grieving people to pretend before God.

**Biblical account:** 2 Samuel 1:17–27; broader lament tradition in the Psalms.

## Dancing Before the Lord

**Record type:** Worship / Celebration  
**Tags:** #celebratory #joy #worship #light

When the ark entered Jerusalem successfully, I danced before the Lord with all my might.

I was not attempting to demonstrate royal dignity. I was celebrating before God.

Michal despised what she saw and confronted me afterward.

I still believe wholehearted celebration was appropriate.

I also recognize that this event occurred inside a marriage with far more history and pain than the sentence "Michal criticized my worship" communicates.

Worship reflection should not require flattening another person's story.

**Biblical account:** 2 Samuel 6:12–23.
