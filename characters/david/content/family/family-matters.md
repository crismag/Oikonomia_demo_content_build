## Family Leadership Review — Public Success Did Not Mean Home Was Healthy

**Record type:** Family / Personal Development  
**Visibility:** Restricted  
**Tags:** #serious #family #grief #conviction

My public leadership record and my family history cannot be evaluated as though they were the same thing.

I experienced extraordinary public success while my household also experienced rivalry, sexual violence, revenge, estrangement, political maneuvering, rebellion, and grief.

It is possible for people outside a leader's home to see strength while people inside it are living through unresolved pain.

### 2026 commitment

Do not use ministry fruit as evidence that family concerns do not require attention.

## Absalom — Estrangement Was Not Resolution

**Record type:** Family / Relationship Review  
**Visibility:** Restricted  
**Tags:** #serious #family #grief #regret

After Absalom killed Amnon, he fled and remained away for years.

Joab eventually helped arrange his return to Jerusalem, but I initially refused to see him. Absalom lived in Jerusalem without seeing my face.

Eventually we met and I kissed him.

The outward restoration did not resolve what had accumulated between us.

Absalom later built political support, declared himself king, and I fled Jerusalem.

### Family lesson

Physical return is not the same as relational repair.

A ceremony of reconciliation does not automatically address years of grief, anger, injustice, ambition, and silence.

**Biblical account:** 2 Samuel 13–15.

## Absalom's Death — Leadership and Fatherhood Collided

**Record type:** Bereavement / Family Report  
**Visibility:** Restricted  
**Tags:** #serious #grief #family #lament

Before the battle against Absalom's forces, I ordered Joab, Abishai, and Ittai to deal gently with Absalom for my sake.

Absalom was killed despite that instruction.

When I received the news, I was overwhelmed with grief and wished that I had died instead of him.

The victory became complicated for the people because their king was mourning the person who had led the rebellion against them.

Joab confronted me because the soldiers who had risked their lives felt ashamed rather than welcomed after victory.

### Leadership tension

I was a grieving father and a king at the same time.

Neither role made the other disappear.

**Biblical account:** 2 Samuel 18:5–19:8.

## Solomon — Preparing a Son to Carry Work I Cannot

**Record type:** Family / Mentorship / Succession  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Tags:** #family #mentorship #hope #leadership

Solomon would build the temple I wanted to build.

My responsibility was not to compete with his assignment but to prepare him for it.

I gathered resources, gave instruction, organized people, spoke publicly to leaders, and encouraged Solomon to serve God wholeheartedly.

### 2026 application

A parent who has experience can easily make a child's assignment feel like an extension of the parent's unfinished ambition.

I want Solomon to receive preparation without receiving the burden of becoming another version of me.

**Biblical account:** 1 Chronicles 22; 28–29; 1 Kings 1–2.

## Family Attention Item — Do Not Delay Difficult Conversations

**Record type:** Family / Attention Required  
**Visibility:** Restricted  
**Status:** Active  
**Tags:** #serious #family #conviction #attention

One pattern in my family history is that unresolved matters were allowed to remain unresolved until they became larger.

Anger after Tamar's assault did not become an adequate intervention. Absalom's anger became revenge. Estrangement became years. Return did not become real repair. Political ambition later became rebellion.

### 2026 action

When a serious family matter requires a conversation, safeguarding response, accountability, or outside help, do not mistake delay for peace.

Silence can look stable right up until it breaks.
