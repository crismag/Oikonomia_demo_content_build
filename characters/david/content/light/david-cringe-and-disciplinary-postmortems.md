## Disciplinary Action — Conduct at Gath

**Record type:** Disciplinary Action (DA)  
**Subject:** David  
**Status:** Post-Mortem Required  
**Tags:** #disciplinary #fear #judgment #cringe #funny

### Incident summary

David fled from Saul and went to Achish king of Gath.

Achish's servants recognized David and repeated the song associated with his military reputation: Saul had struck down thousands and David tens of thousands.

David became very afraid of Achish.

David then deliberately changed his behavior in front of them, pretended to be insane, made marks on the doors/gate, and allowed saliva to run down his beard.

Achish concluded that David was mad and questioned why his servants had brought him there.

### DA concerns

- Judgment leading into the situation.
- Reputational impact.
- Leadership conduct under fear.
- Whether safer alternatives had been considered.
- Need to understand the context before assessing the conduct.

**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 21:10–15.

---

## DA Post-Mortem — David Response

**Record type:** Disciplinary Post-Mortem  
**Visibility:** Restricted  
**Status:** Completed  
**Tags:** #disciplinary #reflective #fear #cringe #funny

### David's statement

I was afraid.

I had fled Saul, arrived in Gath carrying the sword of the Philistine champion I had killed, and then heard Achish's servants identify me and repeat the song about my military victories.

At that point I believed my life was in danger.

I pretended to be insane so that Achish would dismiss me as harmless.

It worked.

I understand why Leadership would still like to discuss the method.

### Findings

- Fear was genuine and supported by the circumstances.
- David intentionally used deceptive behavior as an escape strategy.
- Immediate survival objective was achieved.
- Conduct was sufficiently unusual to require post-mortem review.
- No recommendation to adopt the method as a standard Field Ministry safety protocol.

### Corrective / development actions

- Review decisions that led to entering Gath.
- Identify earlier points where safer alternatives might have existed.
- Continue work on fear, discernment, and crisis decision-making.
- Do not include "saliva presentation" in future leadership training material without context.

### Comments

**Cris:** For completeness, the incident report says you were making marks on the doors.  
**David:** Correct.  
**Cris:** And saliva was running down your beard.  
**David:** I was committed to the strategy.  
**Cris:** That is not the part of the report currently being questioned.
