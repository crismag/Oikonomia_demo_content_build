## Submitted Song

### Verse 1 — Surrounded

They gather around me and speak as though the morning will never come.  
The road behind me is closed and the hills ahead are filled with trouble.  
Still I remember the God who heard me when I called,  
The One who has carried me through places I thought would become my grave.

### Verse 2 — The Heavens

The heavens declare what anxious hearts forget.  
Day speaks to day and night carries knowledge into night.  
The moon does not ask my enemies whether it should rise,  
And the stars remain where the Lord has appointed them.

### Chorus — Shepherd

You are my Shepherd; I will not be abandoned.  
You lead me where fear cannot become my master.  
Your goodness follows farther than the enemy can chase me.  
I will dwell in Your presence and call You faithful.

### Verse 3 — The King

Some trust in strength that can be counted.  
Some trust in weapons they can hold.  
I will remember the Name that carried a shepherd into battle  
And taught a frightened heart that the battle belongs to the Lord.

### Bridge — Where Are You?

Why do You feel far away when trouble is near?  
Why does the night become long when I am waiting for You?  
I will speak the question instead of hiding it.  
I will wait here until lament remembers Your name.

### Chorus — Shepherd

You are my Shepherd; I will not be abandoned.  
You lead me where fear cannot become my master.  
Your goodness follows farther than the enemy can chase me.  
I will dwell in Your presence and call You faithful.

### Final Section — Jerusalem

Let the gates remember the King of glory.  
Let the city lift its voice.  
Let every generation receive what we have learned  
And carry praise beyond the years we were given.

## Psalmists Review

**Reviewer:** Psalmists Team Member  
**Review date:** Friday, 2026-09-11, 9:41 PM  
**Finding:** Coherence / Narrative Progression  
**Severity:** Needs clarification before Sunday use

David,

I think every individual section is good, and I want to make that clear before describing the problem because nobody on the team is objecting to the biblical themes themselves.

Verse 1 begins with being surrounded by enemies and remembering God's deliverance. That gives us a clear opening situation.

Then Verse 2 suddenly moves to creation: heavens, day and night, moon and stars. We initially assumed this was meant to show that God's order is larger than the immediate crisis, which could work, but the song does not actually tell the congregation that this is why we have moved from enemies to astronomy.

Then the chorus says God is our Shepherd. Again, good material, but we have now moved from conflict, to creation, to shepherding.

Verse 3 moves back into battle language, counting strength and weapons, and appears to reference your experience with Goliath. Several of us thought this meant the song was returning to the original conflict narrative.

Then the bridge asks why God feels far away and why the night is long.

At Thursday practice, three of us thought the bridge belonged to another draft because the chorus immediately before it had sounded fairly settled and confident. Friday practice established that the bridge does in fact belong to this song and that this placement is intentional.

After the bridge, we return to the Shepherd chorus, which seems to resolve the lament.

Then the final section arrives in Jerusalem, opens the gates, introduces the King of glory, addresses the city, moves into generational testimony, and appears to end several years later than where we started.

Again, I understand every section individually.

I am less certain that the sections have met each other.

Could you please add a short explanation of the narrative or theological progression before Sunday? Right now the team understands the individual ideas but not what journey the congregation is supposed to be taking through them.

If the intended progression is something like **distress → remembering God's created order → remembering His shepherding → recalling past victory → honest lament → renewed confidence → corporate praise → generational worship in Jerusalem**, that would help us considerably.

However, if that is the intended progression, I think at least some portion of that clarity may need to exist inside the song rather than only inside the explanation you give us at rehearsal.

Please confirm the intended narrative progression and the final file before Sunday preparation is locked.
