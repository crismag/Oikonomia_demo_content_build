## I Was Ready to Kill People Because Nabal Insulted Us

**Record type:** Personal Development  
**Date:** 2026-03-16  
**Visibility:** Only me  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #anger #regret #conviction #gratitude

Nabal's shepherds had been around my men in the wilderness, and we had treated them well. When I later sent men to ask Nabal for provisions during shearing time, he answered with contempt and questioned who I was.

I became furious.

I told my men to put on their swords and prepared to retaliate against Nabal's household.

Abigail intercepted us with provisions and a careful appeal. She asked me not to take vengeance into my own hands and warned me against carrying unnecessary bloodshed into my future.

She was right.

I thanked God for sending her and thanked her for the judgment that kept me from carrying out what I had intended.

I do not want to rewrite this memory as though I calmly considered her advice. I was already moving toward violence.

The lesson is not merely "listen to wise people." The lesson is that anger can make retaliation feel like justice while we are still angry enough to enjoy the idea of retaliation.

**2026 action:** No punitive leadership decisions while angry. Seek counsel before escalation.

**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 25.

## I Had the Opportunity to Kill Saul

**Record type:** Personal Development  
**Date:** 2026-04-02  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #restraint #fear #conviction

Saul repeatedly pursued me and tried to kill me.

At En Gedi, Saul entered the very cave where my men and I were hiding. My men saw the moment as God's delivery of Saul into my hands.

I cut off a corner of Saul's robe.

Even that troubled my conscience.

I refused to kill him and restrained my men because Saul was the Lord's anointed king.

Later I had another opportunity when Saul was sleeping in the camp. Again I refused to kill him.

This history matters because restraint is easiest to praise when retaliation is impossible.

I had opportunity. I had supporters who would have justified it. I had legitimate grievances.

I still did not have permission to make myself judge over Saul's life.

**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 24; 1 Samuel 26.

## Nathan Said, "You Are the Man"

**Record type:** Personal Development / Confession  
**Date:** 2026-06-08  
**Visibility:** Only me  
**Status:** Complete with continuing consequences  
**Tags:** #serious #conviction #regret #grief #repentance

Nathan came to me with a story about a rich man who took a poor man's only lamb to prepare a meal for a traveler.

I was furious at the injustice.

Nathan told me: "You are the man."

He confronted what I had done involving Bathsheba and Uriah.

I had committed adultery with Bathsheba. When she became pregnant, I attempted to conceal what had happened. When Uriah would not go home in the way I expected, I sent instructions through him that resulted in his being placed in the fiercest fighting and abandoned so that he would die.

I had used royal power to serve my own desire and then to protect myself from exposure.

My response to Nathan was simple: I had sinned against the Lord.

That confession did not make the consequences disappear.

I keep this report restricted because confession is not performance. But I also refuse to protect my leadership image by pretending the failure was smaller than Scripture says it was.

**Related goal:** Receive correction before defending myself; never confuse authority with permission.

**Biblical account:** 2 Samuel 11–12.  
**Repentance connection:** Psalm 51 is traditionally connected by its superscription to Nathan's confrontation after David went to Bathsheba.

## Create in Me a Clean Heart

**Record type:** Prayer / Personal Development  
**Date:** 2026-06-22  
**Visibility:** Only me  
**Status:** Active prayer  
**Tags:** #devotional #serious #regret #repentance #hope

My prayer is not merely that the consequences of sin become easier.

I need God to change me.

Psalm 51 asks God for cleansing, a clean heart, a steadfast spirit, restored joy, and a willing spirit. It recognizes that God desires truth in the inner person and that outward religious activity cannot substitute for a broken and contrite heart.

In 2026 I want repentance to remain more than saying the correct words after correction.

I want the part of me that made self-protection possible to remain under God's examination.

**Prayer:** Lord, keep me truthful before You. Do not let leadership experience make me skilled at explaining what I should instead confess.

**Biblical account:** Psalm 51; 2 Samuel 12.
