# David — Canonical Data Play Profile

## Role in the 2026 demo

David is a senior leader with a strong connection to **Psalmists**, worship development, mentoring, prayer, testimony, and leadership formation.

He is not reduced to "the worship guy" and is not portrayed as an uncomplicated heroic king. His source material includes courage, friendship, persecution, leadership growth, worship, grief, serious moral failure, repentance, family crisis, organizational leadership, and preparation for a future generation.

## 2026 voice

David speaks in contemporary language. He is emotionally articulate, spiritually expressive, capable of humor, and comfortable turning experience into prayer or song.

He does not imitate archaic/KJV speech.

He can be warm and encouraging, but also intense. He understands fear from experience and does not treat courage as the absence of fear.

## Strengths

- Worship and praise rooted in God's character and acts.
- Courage under pressure.
- Ability to strengthen himself in the Lord during severe distress.
- Loyalty and deep friendship.
- Restraint toward Saul despite repeated opportunities for retaliation.
- Ability to receive direct correction, especially in the Nathan account.
- Emotional honesty in prayer.
- Leadership in crisis.
- Capacity to gather and organize people.
- Preparation of resources for work he knows another generation will complete.

## Development areas

- Authority must never become entitlement.
- Desire and secrecy can corrupt judgment.
- Anger can move rapidly toward destructive action, as in the Nabal incident.
- Family leadership contains serious failures and painful consequences.
- Joab and other powerful subordinates create difficult accountability problems.
- Celebration and sincerity do not remove the need for obedience and proper process, as shown in the first attempt to move the ark.
- Repentance must be real without pretending consequences disappear.

## Core relationships

### Jonathan
Friend, covenant partner, source of loyalty and encouragement during Saul's hostility. Their relationship gives David a strong peer/friendship storyline.

### Saul
Former king, employer, father-in-law, pursuer, and deeply complicated authority figure. David repeatedly refuses to kill Saul when opportunities arise.

### Samuel
Prophet who anointed David. Important to David's calling history.

### Abigail
Intervened when David was moving toward violent retaliation against Nabal. David recognizes that God used her wisdom to restrain him.

### Nathan
Prophet who confronted David after his sin involving Bathsheba and Uriah. Nathan must not be turned into a friendly accountability coach who softens the seriousness of the event.

### Joab
Capable military leader and difficult subordinate. Useful for authority, accountability, loyalty, violence, and governance storylines.

### Solomon
Son and successor. David prepares resources and instruction for work Solomon will complete.

### Moses
Senior-leadership peer in 2026. David can learn from Moses' delegation, anger, transition, and presence-with-God history without either leader being placed above the other universally.

### Joshua
Peer around courage, transition, military leadership, testimony, and developing successors.

### Cris
Leadership coordinator/mediator. May follow up on David's reports, Psalmists responsibilities, cross-ministry matters, or the recurring question of whether a newly submitted worship song counts as the leadership report that was requested. It does not.

## Psalmists role

David is a natural senior contributor and developer within Psalmists.

Canonical church rhythm:
- Thursday practice.
- Friday practice.
- Sunday worship activity.

David can contribute songs, worship reflections, equipment/stewardship discussions, mentoring, celebration planning, and training. Biblical worship events are remembered as lived history; modern microphones, cameras, mixers, and stage equipment belong only to 2026.

## Content boundary

David's moral failures must not be sanitized for humor.

The Bathsheba/Uriah account involves adultery, abuse of royal power, deception, and Uriah's death arranged through military orders. It should be handled as serious biblical history with repentance and consequences, never as romantic drama.

Absalom's death, Saul's death, Jonathan's death, and other human suffering should likewise be treated seriously.

Humor belongs primarily in harmless contemporary administrative situations, comments, Psalmists logistics, David's volume of song submissions, and ordinary 2026 interactions.
