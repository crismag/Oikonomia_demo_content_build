# David — Biblical Context for Data Play

This document identifies major biblical arcs available for David's authored Oikonomia content. Biblical events remain David's lived history; 2026 records may remember, teach from, or apply them without pretending the ancient events happened in 2026.

## Major arcs

1. **Shepherd years — 1 Samuel 16–17**  
   David cared for sheep and described protecting the flock from lion and bear attacks. Useful for pastoral responsibility, unseen preparation, and courage.

2. **Anointed by Samuel — 1 Samuel 16:1–13**  
   Jesse's older sons were seen first, but God directed Samuel to David. Important for calling, appearance, and hidden preparation.

3. **Serving Saul with music — 1 Samuel 16:14–23**  
   David's skill with the lyre brought him into Saul's service. Strong Psalmists connection, but the text should not be converted into simplistic claims about music as medical treatment.

4. **Goliath — 1 Samuel 17**  
   David hears Goliath's challenge, rejects Saul's armor, uses sling and stones, defeats Goliath, and Israel advances. Excellent special operational package.

5. **Jonathan friendship — 1 Samuel 18–20**  
   Covenant friendship, loyalty, warning, grief, and encouragement.

6. **Saul's jealousy and attempts on David's life — 1 Samuel 18–27**  
   David becomes a fugitive despite faithful service.

7. **Nob and consequences — 1 Samuel 21–22**  
   David receives help from Ahimelek; Saul later orders the priests killed through Doeg. Serious material involving fear, decisions, responsibility, and grief.

8. **Cave of Adullam / distressed people gather — 1 Samuel 22:1–2**  
   David becomes leader of a difficult and vulnerable group.

9. **Keilah — 1 Samuel 23**  
   David seeks God, rescues Keilah, then learns the city would surrender him to Saul. Strong decision/discernment material.

10. **David spares Saul in cave — 1 Samuel 24**  
    Restraint when retaliation is available.

11. **Nabal and Abigail — 1 Samuel 25**  
    David becomes angry and prepares violent retaliation. Abigail intervenes wisely; David recognizes God used her to restrain him.

12. **David spares Saul again — 1 Samuel 26**  
    Refuses to kill Saul despite opportunity.

13. **Ziklag burned — 1 Samuel 30**  
    Amalekites raid Ziklag, take families captive, David's men speak of stoning him, David strengthens himself in the Lord, seeks God, pursues, recovers everyone and distributes spoils with a fairness principle.

14. **Saul and Jonathan die — 1 Samuel 31; 2 Samuel 1**  
    David grieves rather than celebrates Saul's death. Lament for Saul and Jonathan.

15. **David becomes king — 2 Samuel 2–5**  
    Gradual leadership consolidation; conflict between houses; eventually king over all Israel.

16. **Jerusalem captured — 2 Samuel 5**  
    Strategic/governance milestone.

17. **Ark transport failure / Uzzah — 2 Samuel 6; 1 Chronicles 13, 15**  
    First transport attempt ends in Uzzah's death. Later David recognizes proper instructions and Levite responsibility; second movement succeeds. Important operational/process/accountability content.

18. **David dances before the Lord — 2 Samuel 6**  
    Celebratory worship, humility, conflict with Michal. Strong Psalmists content.

19. **David wants to build temple — 2 Samuel 7**  
    Nathan initially approves, then God gives different direction. David will not build the house; God gives covenant promise.

20. **Mephibosheth — 2 Samuel 9**  
    David remembers covenant with Jonathan and shows kindness to Jonathan's son.

21. **Bathsheba and Uriah — 2 Samuel 11**  
    Grave moral and leadership failure: adultery, abuse of authority, deception, and arranging Uriah's death. Must remain serious.

22. **Nathan confronts David — 2 Samuel 12**  
    David acknowledges sin. Judgment and consequences follow. Psalm 51 is traditionally connected to this repentance.

23. **Amnon, Tamar, Absalom — 2 Samuel 13 onward**  
    Severe family crisis, sexual violence against Tamar, inadequate response, revenge, estrangement, rebellion. Handle with high sensitivity; never entertainment.

24. **Absalom rebellion — 2 Samuel 15–19**  
    David flees Jerusalem, receives mixed loyalty, grieves Absalom's death, returns. Rich but painful leadership material.

25. **Shimei — 2 Samuel 16; 19**  
    David tolerates cursing during flight and later handles return/pardon questions.

26. **Joab — across 2 Samuel**  
    Highly capable but repeatedly difficult and violent subordinate. Governance/accountability complexity.

27. **Census — 2 Samuel 24; 1 Chronicles 21**  
    David orders census, recognizes sin, judgment follows. Serious leadership/accountability material.

28. **Temple preparation — 1 Chronicles 22, 28–29**  
    David prepares materials, organization, plans, leaders, and encouragement for Solomon even though Solomon will build the temple.

29. **Organization of worship — 1 Chronicles 23–25**  
    Levites and musicians organized for service. Strong Psalmists operational source.

30. **Psalms**  
    David-associated psalms provide biblically grounded language for fear, praise, repentance, grief, confidence, longing, thanksgiving, anger, and hope. Superscriptions should be respected where they connect a psalm to an event.

## Data Play principle

David should not become a collection of inspirational quotes.

His value to Data Play is the range of his record: obscure preparation, visible victory, persecution, friendship, worship, restraint, anger, wise intervention, crisis recovery, power, grave sin, correction, repentance, consequences, grief, administration, succession, and preparation for work another person will finish.
