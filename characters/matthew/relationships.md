# Matthew — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first, and for Matthew they are few. Scripture records no conversation between Matthew and any other named disciple. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Jesus
Called him from the tax office and accepted his feast despite public criticism (Matthew 9:9–13; Luke 5:27–32). Appointed him one of the Twelve, sent him out, and commissioned the eleven after the resurrection (Matthew 10:1–4; 28:16–20). Jesus is the centre, not an ordinary demo account. Matthew refers to Him in testimony, Lifegroup and prayer; Jesus receives no tasks or comments.

### The guests at Levi's table
"A great crowd of tax collectors and others" (Luke 5:29). None is named. They are Matthew's biblical network, and the reason for the criticism.

### The Pharisees and scribes who objected
They asked the disciples why Jesus ate with tax collectors and sinners (Mark 2:16; Luke 5:30). Not roster characters. Refer to them in prose, and do not caricature every Pharisee; Scripture reports this objection, not a feud with Matthew personally.

### The Twelve
Matthew was chosen, sent out, present at the Last Supper, among those who fled at the arrest, and among the eleven who received the commission and prayed in the upper room (Mark 3:13–19; Matthew 26:20, 56; Acts 1:13–14). Individual interactions are not recorded.

### James son of Alphaeus
Mark names Levi "the son of Alphaeus" (Mark 2:14), and James is also "the son of Alphaeus" (Mark 3:18).

**Qualification:** Scripture never calls them brothers, and Alphaeus may be two different men. The link also depends on identifying Levi with Matthew. Do not write them as brothers. In 2026 they may notice the shared name and leave it unsettled.

### Simon the Zealot
Listed together among the Twelve (Matthew 10:3–4; Luke 6:15; Acts 1:13).

**Qualification:** Popular teaching often contrasts a tax collector and a Zealot as political opposites. That is interpretation: Scripture does not say what "Zealot" meant for Simon, or that the two men ever clashed.

### Stephen
One of the seven chosen when the Twelve handed over oversight of the daily distribution (Acts 6:1–6).

**Inference:** Matthew was very probably among "the twelve" in Acts 6, but no apostle is named there. Scripture records no personal interaction between Matthew and Stephen.

## 2026 Data Play relationships (fiction)

### Stephen — ministry head
Matthew reports to Stephen in More Than Enough. Matthew keeps the receiving records, distribution counts and volunteer rosters; Stephen owns the weekly report, the ministry's direction and its public voice. Matthew is quietly aware that the apostles once asked Stephen to take on this very kind of work, and does not make a joke of it. Their working friction is ordinary: Matthew wants the numbers reconciled before the report goes out; Stephen wants the report to say what the numbers mean for the people served.

### Simon the Zealot — fellow More Than Enough member
They serve side by side on distribution days. Simon cares intensely about whether the distribution is fair and whether anyone is being exploited; Matthew cares whether the records are right. Their differences are temperament and conviction in 2026, not a replay of a recorded ancient rivalry. They respect each other.

### Philip the Evangelist and Titus
Also serve in More Than Enough. Philip's Reach-Out follow-ups sometimes begin with someone Matthew logged at a distribution; Matthew asks Philip for numbers and Philip tends to send a story. Titus, a More Than Enough leader, does a monthly stewardship reconciliation with Matthew and insists on a second signature.

### Martha
Victuals head. When Matthew's Lifegroup or a More Than Enough volunteer meal needs food support, he asks Victuals rather than running a kitchen himself. He remembers a feast; Martha runs meals.

### Andrew and Mordecai
Greeters and Ushers. Matthew passes along newcomers who first came through More Than Enough so they are welcomed on Sunday, and leaves the welcome itself to that team.

### Luke
Both keep careful records. Luke's are narrative and pastoral; Matthew's are counts, logs and reconciliations. They occasionally compare notes about what a record is for.

### Cris
Follows up on More Than Enough attention items and on Matthew's Lifegroup follow-ups. Cris sees recipient details only when Matthew or Stephen names Cris as a reader.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Matthew / Stephen:** weekly More Than Enough numbers feeding Stephen's report; a fairness review of how distribution reaches people who don't ask (Acts 6 memory).
- **Matthew / Simon the Zealot:** a distribution-day disagreement about donor conditions or fairness, resolved in meeting notes.
- **Matthew / Andrew / Mordecai:** a newcomer pathway from More Than Enough to Sunday welcome.
- **Matthew / Martha:** a Lifegroup meal request that respects Victuals ownership.
- **Matthew / Cris:** a comment thread about the length of the receiving log.
