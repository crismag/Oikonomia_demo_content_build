# Matthew — Biblical Context Library

This file records Matthew's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are memories and formation, not 2026 reenactments.

Scripture gives Matthew one individual scene (the call and the meal) and otherwise places him within the Twelve. Most sections below describe what the Twelve experienced together. Where Matthew's own part is not recorded, the text says so. Do not fill the silence with invented speeches, bookkeeping episodes or motives.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Interpretation and tradition are marked.

---

## Called from the tax office — Matthew 9:9; Mark 2:14; Luke 5:27–28

Jesus had returned to Capernaum and was teaching crowds by the sea (Mark 2:1, 13). As He passed by, He saw a man sitting at the tax office. Matthew's Gospel calls him Matthew; Mark calls him "Levi the son of Alphaeus"; Luke calls him "a tax collector named Levi." Jesus said to him, "Follow me." He got up and followed. Luke adds, "He left everything, and rose up and followed him."

For a reader new to the Bible: tax collectors collected tolls and taxes on behalf of the ruling authorities. The Gospels show them treated by religious people as outsiders, grouped with "sinners." The texts do not say whether Matthew himself was dishonest.

**Fact and interpretation:** The call is fact. That Levi and Matthew are one man is a widely accepted identification from the matching accounts, not an explicit statement. No conversation before the call is recorded, and his reasons are not given.

**Truths:** The call came to a man at his desk, not after he had tidied up his reputation. Following Jesus meant leaving the booth, not merely adding Jesus to it.

**2026 use:** Testimony, calling, personal development, Lifegroup, mentoring people who think their past work disqualifies them.

## The feast and the criticism — Matthew 9:10–13; Mark 2:15–17; Luke 5:29–32

Luke says Levi made a great feast for Jesus in his house, with "a great crowd of tax collectors and others" reclining at the table. Pharisees and their scribes complained to the disciples: "Why do you eat and drink with the tax collectors and sinners?" Jesus answered, "Those who are healthy have no need for a physician, but those who are sick do." In Matthew's account He adds, "But you go and learn what this means: 'I desire mercy, and not sacrifice,' for I came not to call the righteous, but sinners to repentance." The quotation is from Hosea 6:6.

**Fact:** Luke places the feast at Levi's house. Matthew says "in the house" and Mark "his house"; only Luke is explicit about the owner.

**Truths:** Matthew's first response to being called was to bring his own network to meet Jesus. Jesus accepted criticism rather than decline the table. Mercy is not a lowering of standards; it is what the sick need.

**2026 use:** Lifegroup hospitality, newcomer welcome, Reach-Out follow-up, More Than Enough relationships with donors and recipients, criticism for the company a ministry keeps. The meal itself is a memory; Victuals owns 2026 food work.

## "Matthew the tax collector" — Matthew 10:2–4; Mark 3:13–19; Luke 6:12–16; Acts 1:13

Luke says Jesus spent the whole night in prayer before choosing twelve from His disciples and naming them apostles. Mark says He appointed them "that they might be with him, and that he might send them out to preach." Matthew is in every list. Only the list in Matthew's Gospel adds "the tax collector."

**Interpretation:** If Matthew wrote that Gospel, as tradition holds, he kept his old label in the official list. That reading depends on the tradition and should be offered as such.

**Truths:** The Twelve were first called to be with Jesus. A list of apostles could include a tax collector and a man called the Zealot without explanation.

**2026 use:** Team identity, testimony, team unity with Simon the Zealot (as interpretation, not recorded interaction).

## Sent out with nothing to sell — Matthew 10:5–16; Mark 6:7–13, 30; Luke 9:1–6

Jesus sent the Twelve out in pairs to preach, heal the sick and cast out demons. They were to take no money in their belts, no bag and no spare coat, to stay with whoever received them, and to shake the dust off their feet where they were refused. "Freely you received, so freely give." They went, preached repentance, anointed many sick people with oil and healed them, then came back and told Jesus everything they had done and taught.

**Silence:** Matthew's partner and destinations are not recorded.

**Truths:** A former collector of money was sent out without money. What was received freely was to be given freely. Ministry ended with reporting back to Jesus.

**2026 use:** More Than Enough stewardship ("freely give"), reporting, dependence on hospitality, Lifegroup teaching.

## What the Twelve left, and who is greatest — Matthew 19:27–30; Luke 22:24–27

Peter said, "Behold, we have left everything and followed you. What then will we have?" Jesus promised a future reward and warned that many who are first will be last. At the Last Supper the disciples argued about which of them was the greatest; Jesus told them the leader should be like one who serves, "But I am among you as one who serves."

**Silence:** Matthew's individual part in these conversations is not recorded.

**Truths:** Leaving everything is not a transaction with God. Service, not position, is the measure.

**2026 use:** Stewardship, coordinator versus head roles, personal development.

## The arrest, the resurrection and the commission — Matthew 26:56; Matthew 28:16–20; Acts 1:12–14

When Jesus was arrested, "all the disciples left him and fled." After the resurrection, the eleven went to the mountain in Galilee. "When they saw him, they bowed down to him; but some doubted." Jesus said all authority had been given to Him and sent them to make disciples of all nations, "teaching them to observe all things that I commanded you," promising, "I am with you always." After the ascension Matthew was among those in the upper room who "with one accord continued steadfastly in prayer."

**Silence:** Scripture does not say which of the eleven doubted. Do not assign it to Matthew or Thomas.

**Truths:** The Twelve's failure was shared, and so was their commission. Teaching people to obey is part of making disciples.

**2026 use:** Testimony, Lifegroup, prayer, humility about shared failure.

## The Twelve and the daily distribution — Acts 6:1–7

As the church grew, Greek-speaking believers complained that their widows were being neglected in the daily distribution. The Twelve called the believers together and said it was not right for them to leave the word of God to serve tables. They asked the church to select seven men "of good report, full of the Holy Spirit and of wisdom" to oversee the work. The church chose Stephen, Philip and five others; the apostles prayed and laid hands on them.

**Inference:** Acts 6 names no apostles, but Matthew is in the list of Acts 1:13 and there is no record of his leaving. It is reasonable to count him among "the twelve" here.

**Truths:** A fair distribution matters enough to stop and fix. Complaints from the overlooked group were taken seriously. Trustworthy people were given real ownership.

**2026 use:** More Than Enough operations, fairness in distribution, Matthew's working relationship with Stephen as his ministry head, delegation. In 2026 Matthew serves in the distribution the apostles once handed over; that reversal can be noted with gentle humility, not played as irony.

## The Gospel that bears his name — tradition

The first Gospel does not name its author. Early church writers attributed it to Matthew, and the tradition is long-standing. Data Play may mention the tradition and may let Matthew reflect on the teaching recorded there, but it should not present authorship as biblical fact or have Matthew speak of writing it.
