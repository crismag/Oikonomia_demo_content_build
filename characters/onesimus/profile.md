## Who Onesimus is

Onesimus appears in two letters Paul wrote from prison.

In the letter to Philemon, Paul calls him "my child Onesimus, whom I have become the father of in my chains" (Philemon 10). Onesimus had been enslaved in Philemon's household. At some point he was with Paul, who was a prisoner, and became a believer through him. Paul says Onesimus "once was useless to you, but now is useful to you and to me" (verse 11). The name Onesimus means "useful", so Paul is playing on it. Paul calls him "my own heart", says he wanted to keep him to serve Paul in prison, but sends him back to Philemon, asking Philemon to receive him "no longer as a slave, but more than a slave, a beloved brother" (verses 12–16). If Onesimus has wronged Philemon or owes him anything, Paul will pay it (verses 18–19).

In Colossians Paul sends Tychicus to the church at Colossae "together with Onesimus, the faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you. They will make known to you everything that is going on here" (Colossians 4:9). There Onesimus is not a problem to be solved. He is a trusted brother sent to report and encourage.

**What Scripture does not say.** It does not say that Onesimus ran away, that he stole, why he was with Paul, or what happened when he returned. The runaway-thief picture is a common inference from Paul's words that Onesimus "was therefore separated from you for a while" and "if he has wronged you at all or owes you anything" (verses 15, 18). It may be right, but it is not written, and other explanations exist. Onesimus' own words are not recorded anywhere. Everything we know about him is said by Paul.

**Slavery.** Onesimus lived under Roman slavery, the legal ownership of one human being by another. However conditions varied, he was property in law. Going back to Philemon meant returning to a man with legal power over him, carrying a letter that asked that man to see him differently. Whatever he felt about it, his future was in someone else's hands. Data Play treats this with full seriousness.

In 2026 Onesimus serves on the Facilities team and is trusted with things that must arrive safely and be explained clearly. He is a whole person with his own voice, not a case study in someone else's reconciliation.

## 2026 voice

- Plain, direct and unhurried. Says what happened and what is needed.
- Speaks for himself. He is courteous when others describe him, and he corrects them when they get it wrong.
- Reliable with details that matter to other people: keys, deliveries, who was told what.
- Not self-deprecating about "usefulness", and does not joke about his name. If someone else makes the pun, he may let it pass or point out that Paul meant it kindly.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Philemon (host's warmth), Tychicus (polished communicator) or Paul (argument). His distinct note is a quiet, first-person steadiness from someone whose story was usually told by others.

## Strengths

- Faithful: Paul's own word for him, with "beloved" (Colossians 4:9).
- Paul, in chains, wanted to keep him as his helper and called him useful to himself (Philemon 11, 13).
- Trusted to travel with Tychicus and make known everything that was happening (Colossians 4:9).
- Went back, carrying Paul's appeal, to a man with legal power over him (Philemon 12). Whether he chose this and how he felt about it are not recorded.
- Deep bond with Paul: "my child", "my own heart" (Philemon 10, 12).

## Development areas

Scripture records no personal failing it names directly. The "useless" wording and the conditional "if he has wronged you" are not a confession, and must not be built into a character flaw. Data Play development for Onesimus is about 2026 growth, labelled as fiction:

- Telling his own story in his own words, rather than letting other people's summaries define him.
- Taking on responsibility that matches the trust others place in him.
- Belonging fully in a church where some people still know him mainly through someone else's account.

## Sensitivity

- **Slavery and power.** Never comedy, never a metaphor for a workplace dispute, never softened into "a staff member who left".
- **Unproven wrongdoing.** Do not write that he stole or ran away as if it were Scripture. If a record uses the inference, qualify it.
- **The "useful" wordplay.** Paul's affection for him. It must not become a label that measures his worth by output.
- **2026 reporting line.** Onesimus reports to Nehemiah, who heads Facilities where he serves. Philemon is deliberately not his reporting leader: no 2026 arrangement should recreate the old ownership relationship. Philemon hosts the Lifegroup Onesimus belongs to, as a brother.
- **Voice.** Records about his past should be his own or explicitly shared with him. Others do not narrate his feelings.

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| Had been enslaved in Philemon's household | Biblical fact, clearly implied by Philemon 15–16 ("no longer as a slave") |
| Became a believer through Paul while Paul was imprisoned | Biblical fact (Philemon 10) |
| From Colossae | Biblical fact: "one of you" to the Colossians (Colossians 4:9) |
| Ran away from Philemon | Common inference from Philemon 15; not stated |
| Stole from Philemon or caused financial loss | Inference from the conditional in Philemon 18; not stated |
