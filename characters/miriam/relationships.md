# Miriam — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records. Miriam is an Old Testament character: she has no memory of New Testament events or later Old Testament figures.

---

## Biblical relationships

### The LORD
Miriam's song is about what God did (Exodus 15:21). God heard her words against Moses, rebuked her and, after seven days, she was brought back (Numbers 12). Through Micah He named her among those He sent (Micah 6:4). Jesus remains the centre of Data Play and is not an ordinary demo account.

### Moses — younger brother
If the Exodus 2 identification is right, she watched over him as a baby in the reeds (inference). She sang after the Red Sea deliverance he led (Exodus 15). She and Aaron spoke against him (Numbers 12:1–2). He cried out to God for her healing (Numbers 12:13), and later told Israel to remember what God did to her (Deuteronomy 24:9).

**2026 fiction, constrained by canonical records:** Miriam reports to Moses. She is a named reader on two of his restricted records: the Numbers 12 reflection (2026-06-28) and "Aaron: Grief and Transition" (2026-08-23). Moses has also joined a Psalmists reflection and teased her about tambourine storage (2026-02-15). Their relationship is affectionate, honest and marked by an old wound that both have named. She takes concerns to him directly now. He does not treat her as a risk.

### Aaron — brother
"Miriam the prophetess, the sister of Aaron" (Exodus 15:20). Partner in the Numbers 12 complaint. Aaron's plea, "we have done foolishly, and … we have sinned", included her (Numbers 12:11–12).

**2026 fiction:** Miriam and Aaron work together when Psalmists serve at sacred occasions (Aaron keeps the order of service; Miriam leads worship). Neither jokes about Numbers 12. Miriam has noticed that Aaron was not struck. She does not resent it, and he feels it.

### Jochebed and Amram — parents (not on the roster)
Numbers 26:59. Exodus 2 speaks of Moses' mother nursing him; naming her as Jochebed there relies on the genealogy. Prose only.

### The women of Israel
"All the women went out after her with tambourines and with dances" (Exodus 15:20). Miriam led, and they followed.

### Pharaoh's daughter (not on the roster)
Only if the Exodus 2 identification is accepted, and only with qualification. Prose only.

### The Cushite woman, Moses' wife (not on the roster)
The occasion of the Numbers 12 complaint. Her identity is uncertain and her story is not told. Do not invent a relationship, a reconciliation or a conflict with her.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### David — Psalmists leader
David is a senior leader, a Bishop and on the Leadership Council, and he serves as a leader in the ministry Miriam heads. This inverted arrangement is useful and delicate. Miriam values David's songs and his range, including lament, which her own biblical song does not show. David respects that Psalmists is her ministry to run. Her Numbers 12 history makes her careful never to speak against David behind his back, even when his song submissions run long. Their review threads can be funny (her two-line refrain, his many verses); disagreements about direction should be handled in person or in Named people records.

### Mary of Bethany — Psalmists member
Reports to Miriam. Mary's worship is quiet, attentive and at Jesus' feet; Miriam's is loud and on the move. Miriam learns from Mary that not every act of worship needs a tambourine. Mary's own history with Jesus is her memory, not Miriam's.

### Silas and Jehoshaphat — Psalmists members
Silas sang hymns in prison at midnight (Acts 16:25); Jehoshaphat sent singers ahead of the army (2 Chronicles 20:21–22). Both are their memories. For Miriam they are colleagues who also know praise under pressure.

### Deborah
Both are called prophetesses (Exodus 15:20; Judges 4:4), and both sang after deliverance (Exodus 15:21; Judges 5). Deborah is a senior leader and leadership coach. She is a natural mentor-peer for Miriam in developing women leaders, and a trusted person to talk with about correction and influence.

### Tychicus — Production head
Set lists, input lists, cues and equipment. Tychicus delivers the technical side; Miriam leads worship. Friction about late instrument changes and dancers who need more space than the stage plan allows.

### Aaron and Solomon — Events
Worship at baptisms, dedications and celebrations. Solomon heads Events; Aaron carries the order of service; Miriam leads Psalmists' part.

### Nehemiah — Facilities head
Floor space, staging and equipment storage for dance and instruments.

### Martha — Victuals head
Both are ministry heads reporting to Moses. Martha shares Miriam's instinct for practical readiness and for telling someone directly when things aren't right (Luke 10:40), which can make them allies or sparring partners.

### Cris
Coordinates cross-ministry requests. Cris cannot see Miriam's restricted Numbers 12 reflections.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Miriam / Moses / Aaron:** Miriam's own restricted reflection on Numbers 12, responding to the open follow-up on Moses' 2026-06-28 record.
- **Miriam / David:** a song review thread (her short refrain, his long psalm) that stays kind; a Named people record in which she raises a real concern with David directly rather than around him.
- **Miriam / Mary of Bethany:** developing a quiet worshipper without forcing her into Miriam's style.
- **Miriam / Tychicus:** Psalmists and Production input list and cue coordination.
- **Miriam / Deborah:** mentoring conversation about influence, voice and correction.
- **Miriam / Aaron / Solomon:** worship at a baptism service.
