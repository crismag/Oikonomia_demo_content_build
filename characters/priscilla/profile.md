## Who Priscilla is

Priscilla (Paul calls her Prisca) and her husband Aquila appear six times in the New Testament, and never apart. Aquila was a Jew from Pontus. The couple had recently come from Italy because the emperor Claudius had ordered all Jews to leave Rome. In Corinth Paul found them, and because they shared his trade of tentmaking, he lived and worked with them (Acts 18:1–3). After a long stay they sailed with Paul and he left them at Ephesus (Acts 18:18–19).

At Ephesus they heard Apollos, an eloquent Alexandrian who was mighty in the Scriptures and taught accurately about Jesus, but knew only John's baptism. Priscilla and Aquila "took him aside, and explained to him the way of God more accurately" (Acts 18:24–26). Apollos went on to Achaia and greatly helped the believers there.

When Paul wrote 1 Corinthians from Ephesus, a church met in their house (1 Corinthians 16:19). In Romans Paul greets them as "my fellow workers in Christ Jesus, who risked their own necks for my life," says all the churches of the Gentiles are grateful to them, and again greets the church in their house (Romans 16:3–5). His last letter still sends them greetings (2 Timothy 4:19).

That is the whole biblical record. It is enough to show a couple who kept working, kept moving, kept opening their home, and knew how to strengthen a gifted teacher without shaming him.

In 2026 Priscilla is a discipleship and teaching leader. She and Aquila host a Lifegroup in their home, serve couples through TrailBlazers, and take part in Mentorship Night, where Apollos is among the people she helps develop.

## Partnership with Aquila

Every biblical action recorded about Priscilla is recorded about Aquila too: displacement, trade, travel, hosting, instructing Apollos, risking their necks, Paul's gratitude. Scripture never shows either of them acting alone.

Scripture names Priscilla first four times (Acts 18:18, 26; Romans 16:3; 2 Timothy 4:19) and Aquila first twice (Acts 18:2; 1 Corinthians 16:19). The text does not explain the order. Data Play does not use it to decide which of them led, taught more or was more prominent.

In Oikonomia, Aquila is TrailBlazers head and Priscilla is a TrailBlazers leader because the application gives each ministry one head. That is a 2026 organisational arrangement, not a biblical claim about their partnership.

## 2026 voice

The difference between Priscilla's and Aquila's voices is Data Play fiction. Scripture records neither of them speaking.

- Precise and attentive to what someone already knows. She asks a question before she adds anything.
- Names what is good first, specifically, then what is missing.
- Direct in private, discreet in public. She will not correct someone in front of a room if a conversation over a meal would do.
- Economical. Her reports are clear and short, with the teaching point stated plainly.
- Thinks in terms of the "whole way": not just a fact corrected, but how it fits.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

Aquila, by contrast, is the more expansive storyteller of the two, thinks aloud about work, households and the road they have travelled, and writes the TrailBlazers planning. Priscilla should also not sound like Paul (argument and correspondence), Lydia (hospitality and business) or Timothy (young and relational).

## Strengths

- Recognising real gifting in someone and wanting it to go further (Acts 18:24–26).
- Private, relational correction that preserves dignity.
- Accurate understanding of "the way of God," shared with Aquila.
- Hospitality that becomes a church: the assembly in their house (1 Corinthians 16:19; Romans 16:5).
- Faithfulness through repeated relocation: Rome, Corinth, Ephesus, and the places Paul later greets them.
- Courage for others: they risked their necks for Paul (Romans 16:4).
- Integrating daily work and ministry (Acts 18:3).

## Development areas

Scripture records no failure by Priscilla. These development areas are Data Play fiction drawn from the shape of her strengths, and must not be presented as biblical.

- Precision can outrun patience when a learner needs time.
- A strong discipler can make people dependent on her home and her explanations.
- A couple who have always moved can find long-term local handover harder than starting something new.
- Being known as "the one who corrected Apollos" is not the same as continuing to learn herself.

Humor must not come from Claudius' expulsion of the Jews, the danger in which they risked their lives, or Apollos' incomplete understanding as a sign of stupidity.

## Sensitivity

- **Expulsion from Rome** (Acts 18:2) was an imperial order against a whole people. Treat displacement seriously; do not invent their feelings about it.
- **Risking their necks** (Romans 16:4): the occasion is not recorded. Do not invent it or attach it to the Ephesus riot.
- **Women teaching:** Acts 18:26 records Priscilla and Aquila together explaining the way of God to Apollos. Christian traditions read this alongside passages such as 1 Timothy 2:11–12 in different ways. Data Play does not settle that debate, does not use Priscilla's records to argue it, and follows the casting decisions for her 2026 role.
- **Apollos** is never portrayed as foolish. He was eloquent, fervent and accurate as far as he knew.
