# Priscilla — Biblical Context Library

This file records Priscilla's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are her memories and formation.

**Shared history:** Every biblical account of Priscilla is also an account of Aquila. This file is the canonical retelling of the couple's shared history. Aquila's profile summarises it and refers here rather than keeping a divergent copy. Apollos' file retells Acts 18:24–28 from his side.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

---

## Ordered out of Rome — Acts 18:1–2

After Athens, Paul came to Corinth. "He found a certain Jew named Aquila, a man of Pontus by race, who had recently come from Italy with his wife Priscilla, because Claudius had commanded all the Jews to depart from Rome."

**Fact:** They had recently arrived in Corinth because of an imperial order expelling Jews from Rome. **Silence:** How long they had lived in Rome, what they lost, whether they were already believers, and how they felt about the expulsion are not recorded. Priscilla's own background is not given.

**Truths:** God's work continued through a couple whose move was forced on them. A displacement became the place where Paul found a home and co-workers.

**2026 use:** Welcoming people who have relocated or are starting over, testimony about being uprooted told without invented detail, TrailBlazers couples navigating a move. Never comedy.

## Same trade, one household — Acts 18:3–5

Paul came to them, "and because he practiced the same trade, he lived with them and worked, for by trade they were tent makers." Every Sabbath Paul reasoned in the synagogue. When Silas and Timothy came down from Macedonia, Paul devoted himself fully to testifying that Jesus was the Christ.

**Context:** Paul elsewhere says he worked with his own hands so as not to burden the churches (Acts 20:34; 1 Thessalonians 2:9). **Uncertainty:** Exactly what "tentmaking" involved is debated. Keep it general.

**Truths:** Their home and their workshop were part of Paul's mission. Ordinary work, shared with a guest, was not a distraction from ministry but a setting for it.

**2026 use:** Work and ministry integration for TrailBlazers couples, hospitality to a travelling leader, Aquila's Goal 2.

## Sailing with Paul; left at Ephesus — Acts 18:18–21

Paul stayed in Corinth "after this many more days," then "sailed from there for Syria, together with Priscilla and Aquila." At Cenchreae he had his head shaved because of a vow. At Ephesus "he left them there," reasoned in the synagogue, declined to stay longer, and sailed on, promising to return if God willed.

**Note:** The vow was Paul's, not theirs.

**Truths:** They moved again, this time with the mission, and were left to carry on while Paul went elsewhere.

**2026 use:** Being left in charge of a new work, handover, faithfulness in a new city.

## Taking Apollos aside — Acts 18:24–28

"Now a certain Jew named Apollos, an Alexandrian by race, an eloquent man, came to Ephesus. He was mighty in the Scriptures. This man had been instructed in the way of the Lord; and being fervent in spirit, he spoke and taught accurately the things concerning Jesus, although he knew only the baptism of John. He began to speak boldly in the synagogue. But when Priscilla and Aquila heard him, they took him aside, and explained to him the way of God more accurately."

When Apollos decided to go to Achaia, "the brothers encouraged him; and wrote to the disciples to receive him." There he "greatly helped those who had believed through grace; for he powerfully refuted the Jews, publicly showing by the Scriptures that Jesus was the Christ."

**Fact:** Both of them heard him, took him aside and explained. **Silence:** Where they spoke with him, what exactly they explained, and how long it took are not recorded. The text does not say whether Apollos was baptised again (contrast the disciples Paul met in Acts 19:1–7). Whether Priscilla and Aquila were among "the brothers" who wrote for him is not stated.

**Interpretation:** "Took him aside" is widely read as private instruction rather than public correction. That is a fair reading of the words, and Data Play uses it; do not add a dinner, a house or a conversation as if Scripture recorded them.

**Truths:** They listened first. They recognised real gifting and real accuracy, and saw what was missing. They did not shame him in the synagogue. The result was a stronger teacher who helped many others.

**2026 use:** Mentorship Night with Apollos, private development feedback (restricted), helping a capable leader become more complete, Priscilla's Goal 1. The Style Reference sample Lifegroup report on Apollos illustrates the intended restraint.

## The assembly in their house at Ephesus — 1 Corinthians 16:19

Paul wrote 1 Corinthians while staying at Ephesus (1 Corinthians 16:8). He closed: "The assemblies of Asia greet you. Aquila and Priscilla greet you warmly in the Lord, together with the assembly that is in their house."

**Truths:** Their home was not only hospitable; a church met there. They also sent warm greetings to the church in Corinth where they had lived.

**2026 use:** Home Lifegroup, TrailBlazers home gatherings, sustaining a gathering in a household, relationships across congregations.

## Fellow workers who risked their necks — Romans 16:3–5

"Greet Prisca and Aquila, my fellow workers in Christ Jesus, who risked their own necks for my life, to whom not only I give thanks, but also all the assemblies of the Gentiles. Greet the assembly that is in their house."

**Silence:** When and how they risked their lives is not recorded. Do not invent it or connect it to a particular event. **Inference:** This greeting suggests they were then living where Romans was sent, usually taken to be Rome; whether Romans 16 went to Rome is debated.

**Truths:** Paul called them fellow workers, not helpers. Their courage was for another person's life. Their faithfulness was known and valued far beyond one congregation. Again, a church met in their house.

**2026 use:** Testimony about courage and loyalty stated without invented detail, recognition of lay leaders, shared leadership, Aquila's Goal 3.

## Greeted in Paul's last letter — 2 Timothy 4:19

In his final letter, written while expecting death, Paul asked Timothy: "Greet Prisca and Aquila, and the house of Onesiphorus."

**Inference:** If Timothy was in Ephesus, they were there again.

**Truths:** Long partnership lasted to the end of Paul's life. They were among the people Paul remembered.

**2026 use:** Long faithfulness, Timothy relationship, reflection on lasting co-worker friendships.

## A note on the order of their names

Priscilla is named first in Acts 18:18, 18:26, Romans 16:3 and 2 Timothy 4:19. Aquila is named first in Acts 18:2 and 1 Corinthians 16:19. Scripture gives no reason. Data Play does not draw conclusions about leadership, prominence or status from the order, and records should not speculate about it.
