## 2026-04-05 — Jethro Has Seen This Before

**Record type:** Personal Development / Mentorship Report  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Attention Required

My current workload has begun to resemble a problem I should already know how to recognize.

When Jethro visited us in the wilderness, he watched me spend the day hearing people's disputes while everyone waited from morning until evening. He asked why I was sitting alone while all the people stood around me. I explained that they came to me to seek God's will and resolve disputes.

Jethro's response was direct: what I was doing was not good. He said both I and the people would wear ourselves out because the work was too heavy for me alone.

This week I reviewed my 2026 assignments and found too many items still marked for my decision. Some are legitimately mine. Several are not.

I have learned this lesson before. Experience does not exempt me from repeating old habits.

**Attention:** Workload and delegation review at Tuesday Mentorship Night.  
**Related goal:** Delegate without disappearing.  
**Biblical account:** Exodus 18:13–18.

## 2026-04-12 — Decisions, Not Just Tasks

**Record type:** Leadership Development Report  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** In Progress

Following last week's review, I reassigned several routine matters and clarified which decisions Joshua and other leaders can make without returning them to me for approval.

Jethro's advice in the wilderness was not simply to find assistants who could do more work for me. He told me to appoint capable, trustworthy people over groups of the community and allow them to judge ordinary matters themselves. Only the difficult cases needed to come to me.

That distinction matters. Delegation fails when I give someone the task but keep every meaningful decision.

This week I also redirected one matter back to Joshua after someone came to me seeking a second answer. If I tell people Joshua has authority and then quietly become an appeals court whenever they dislike his decision, I have not actually given him authority.

**Progress:** Routine escalation list reduced. Continue measuring through May.

**Related goal:** Delegate without disappearing; prepare Joshua to lead.  
**Biblical account:** Exodus 18:19–26.

## 2026-04-19 — Sinai: Leadership Must Teach, Not Only React

**Record type:** Teaching / Leadership Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete

Much of leadership can become reactive: a complaint, a shortage, a conflict, a crisis, another question waiting for an answer.

Sinai reminds me that leadership is also about forming a people.

After God delivered Israel from Egypt, He brought us to Mount Sinai and established His covenant with the people. I repeatedly went between God's presence and the congregation as His commands were given. Those commands addressed worship, justice, relationships, property, responsibility, holiness, and how the community was to live before God.

The goal was not simply to survive the next emergency. Israel needed to learn what it meant to belong to God.

I want our 2026 leadership work to retain that dimension. Solving this week's problems matters, but so does teaching people principles that help them live faithfully when no leader is standing beside them.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 19–24.  
**Reflection theme:** Healthy leadership develops understanding, not dependence on constant intervention.

## 2026-04-26 — The Commandments Were Not Suggestions

**Record type:** Teaching Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete

This week's teaching discussion returned to the commandments given at Sinai.

God spoke to Israel as the God who had brought us out of Egypt, out of slavery, and then gave commands governing our loyalty to Him and our relationships with one another. We were commanded to have no other gods before Him, not to make idols for worship, not to misuse His name, to keep the Sabbath, honor father and mother, and reject murder, adultery, theft, false testimony, and coveting.

The order matters to me. God identified Himself as our Deliverer before giving the commands. Obedience was not a scheme for earning the Exodus. The commands were given to a people God had delivered.

This also reminds me that leadership policies and biblical commands are not the same category. Oikonomia can help us organize responsibilities. It does not get to rewrite what God has said.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 20:1–17.

## 2026-05-03 — The Golden Calf: Pressure Is Not Permission

**Record type:** Personal Development / Leadership Reflection  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Complete

I have been thinking about Aaron and the golden calf again.

While I was on Mount Sinai receiving God's commands, the people became impatient because I had been gone longer than they expected. They gathered around Aaron and demanded that he make gods who would go before them. Aaron collected their gold, fashioned it into a calf, and the people began worshiping it.

When I came down the mountain carrying the two stone tablets and saw the calf and the dancing, I was furious. I threw the tablets down and broke them at the foot of the mountain.

There is enough failure in that story for both of us.

Aaron allowed pressure from the people to influence what he knew was wrong. My anger was also real, and anger has been a recurring danger in my own leadership.

What stays with me today is how quickly a leader can begin serving people's demands instead of serving the people themselves. Leadership is not giving people whatever will make them stop complaining. Sometimes love requires disappointing the people you lead.

**Related goal:** Respond to frustration without allowing anger to lead me.  
**Biblical account:** Exodus 32:1–20.

I completed the replacement request concerning the two stone tablets.

For clarity: I damaged the first set myself. I broke them after coming down from Mount Sinai and finding the people worshiping the golden calf. God later instructed me to cut two new stone tablets like the first ones and return to the mountain.

I am grateful that God's restoration does not require pretending the first set was never broken.

## 2026-05-17 — Intercession Is Leadership Work

**Record type:** Prayer / Leadership Report  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Complete

A serious failure in a community creates pressure to choose between accountability and compassion. My history has taught me that this is often a false choice.

After the golden calf, God's anger against Israel was severe. I pleaded with Him for the people. I later returned to the Lord and acknowledged the seriousness of their sin while asking for forgiveness. Years later, when the people rebelled after the spies returned from Canaan, I again interceded for them.

Intercession did not mean calling rebellion harmless. Nor did accountability require me to stop loving the people enough to pray for them.

This week I want our leaders to remember that praying for people after they fail is not avoiding leadership responsibility. It can be one of its deepest expressions.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 32:11–14, 30–32; Numbers 14:13–19.

## 2026-05-24 — If Your Presence Does Not Go With Us

**Record type:** Prayer / Spiritual Development Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete

This week was productive. That is precisely why I needed to revisit this prayer.

After Israel's failure with the golden calf, there was still a destination ahead of us. But I told God that if His presence did not go with us, He should not send us from there. What would distinguish us as His people if He did not go with us?

That conviction remains important in 2026. A full calendar, completed project, growing attendance, successful event, or efficient ministry does not by itself tell us whether we are walking where God is leading.

I am not opposed to planning. I have spent enough of my life organizing people to know planning matters. But I do not want efficiency to become our substitute for dependence on God.

**Prayer:** Lord, do not allow us to become so capable of moving that we stop asking whether You are leading.

**Related goal:** Protect time for God's presence.  
**Biblical account:** Exodus 33:12–17.

## 2026-05-31 — The Tabernacle Required Skilled Practical Work

**Record type:** Ministry Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete

I met with Facilities this week and was reminded how easily practical ministry can be treated as secondary.

The Tabernacle did not assemble itself. God gave detailed instructions concerning its construction, furnishings, materials, coverings, and service. Bezalel and Oholiab were specifically equipped for skilled craftsmanship, and many people contributed materials and work.

Later, whenever the community moved, practical responsibilities surrounded the Tabernacle's transport and setup.

There is spiritual significance in work that involves carrying, building, arranging, cleaning, protecting, setting up, and taking down. The fact that a task is physical does not make it less worthy of faithful service.

Facilities should not be noticed only when something is missing, dirty, broken, late, or still in the van.

**Related ministry:** Facilities  
**Ministry connection:** Facilities.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 31:1–11; Exodus 35–40; Numbers 3–4.

## 2026-06-07 — Organizing a People Without Losing the People

**Record type:** Leadership / Organization Report  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Complete

This week's planning required clarity about teams, responsibilities, and who belongs where. I have lived through organization on a much larger scale.

Israel was organized by tribes, clans, households, leaders, camp positions, and assigned responsibilities. The Levites had distinct duties connected with the Tabernacle. When the camp moved, order mattered. When the community stopped, order still mattered.

Structure can serve people. It can also become a way of seeing only categories and forgetting names, families, burdens, and stories.

I want our organizational work to make responsibility clearer without making people feel like rows in a census.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 1–4; Numbers 10.  
**Reflection theme:** Organization should make faithful care possible, not replace it.

## 2026-06-14 — When Responsibility Starts Becoming Resentment

**Record type:** Personal Development  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Aaron, Joshua  
**Status:** Attention Required

I have noticed something in myself during the past several weeks: repeated complaints are beginning to affect the way I think about the people I am serving.

I know this pattern.

In Numbers 11 the people complained and became consumed with craving other food. I heard families crying throughout the camp. I became deeply troubled and told God that the burden was too heavy for me. I asked why He had placed the responsibility for all these people on me. My words reveal how far exhaustion had progressed.

I do not want to romanticize that moment. I was not merely tired after a long meeting. I was deeply distressed.

What concerns me now is the early stage of the same pattern: the thought, “After everything we have done for them...” That sentence can become resentment if I allow it to grow.

The people I lead are not a debt owed to me because I have served them.

I need rest, prayer, honest counsel, and shared responsibility before frustration hardens into contempt.

**Attention:** Discuss workload and resentment candidly at Tuesday Mentorship Night.  
**Related goal:** Delegate without disappearing; respond to frustration without allowing anger to lead me.  
**Biblical account:** Numbers 11:10–17.

## 2026-06-21 — God Did Not Tell Me to Become Seventy-One People

**Record type:** Leadership Capacity Report  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** In Progress

Following last week's report, I reviewed how God responded when I told Him I could not carry Israel alone.

He instructed me to gather seventy elders whom I knew to be leaders among the people. They were brought together, and God placed on them the Spirit that was on me so that they could share the burden of the people with me.

God did not answer my overload by telling me to become seventy-one people.

Certainly, leaders should pray. Certainly, we should grow in endurance. But sometimes the faithful answer to an overloaded structure is to change the structure.

This week we identified responsibilities that can be carried by other leaders and clarified several escalation boundaries. I will continue mentoring without reclaiming those responsibilities when the new owners handle them differently than I would.

**Progress:** Capacity redistribution started.

**Related goal:** Delegate without disappearing.  
**Biblical account:** Numbers 11:16–17, 24–30.

## 2026-06-28 — Family, Criticism, Correction, and Prayer

**Record type:** Leadership Relationship Report  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Aaron, Miriam  
**Status:** Complete with Follow-up

This week I reflected on a difficult period involving Miriam and Aaron because family relationships and leadership relationships can become tangled very quickly.

Miriam and Aaron spoke against me and questioned whether the Lord had spoken only through me. God intervened directly. Miriam was struck with a serious skin disease and had to remain outside the camp for seven days.

My response was to cry out to God for her healing.

The correction was serious, but Miriam was still my sister. Leadership conflict did not erase relationship or compassion. The entire community also delayed its journey until she was restored and brought back.

I want to remember both sides: criticism should not automatically make me defensive, and correction of another person should never become an excuse to enjoy their humiliation.

There are also practical consequences when a person is in crisis. Sometimes the schedule should move because caring for a person matters more than keeping the original itinerary.

**Connections:** Pastoral care; leadership; Facilities/travel scheduling; prayer.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 12.
