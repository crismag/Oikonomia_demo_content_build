## 2026-01-04 — When I Did Not Feel Ready

**Type:** Personal Development / Weekly Leadership Report  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Complete

This week I spent time with leaders who are beginning assignments they do not yet feel prepared to carry. I understand that feeling better than they may realize.

When God called me from the burning bush, He told me to return to Egypt and confront Pharaoh so that Israel could be brought out of slavery. My first response was not confidence. I asked, “Who am I?” I worried that the people would not believe me. I asked what I should say. I also told God that I was not eloquent and was slow of speech and tongue.

God did not pretend my limitations did not exist. He promised to be with me and provided Aaron to help speak. Looking back, I can see that I spent a great deal of time explaining why I was inadequate while God was explaining why His presence was enough.

That memory affected how I mentored this week. I do not want to answer an insecure leader merely with, “Believe in yourself.” Sometimes the better answer is: know what God has asked you to do, receive the help He provides, and obey even while you still feel inadequate.

**Attention:** Continue watching for emerging leaders who are interpreting nervousness as disqualification.  
**Biblical account:** Exodus 3–4.  
**2026 goal link:** Protect time for God's presence; prepare other leaders.

## 2026-01-11 — Obedience While Still Afraid

**Type:** Weekly Leadership Report  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Complete

Several conversations this week involved people waiting to feel completely confident before acting. I shared that returning to Egypt did not become easy simply because I had finally agreed to go.

Egypt was the place I had fled after killing an Egyptian and learning that Pharaoh wanted me dead. Returning meant going back toward my failure, my people, and the ruler I was being commanded to confront. I also still had the same concerns about speaking that I raised when God called me.

I went because God had told me to go, not because fear had disappeared.

This is worth remembering in leadership development. Courage is not always the removal of fear. Sometimes it is obedience while fear is still asking questions.

Aaron's presence also reminds me not to despise support. There are assignments that are genuinely ours while still requiring other people beside us.

**Follow-up:** Reinforce team-based mentoring rather than treating independence as maturity.  
**Biblical account:** Exodus 2:11–15; Exodus 3–4.

## 2026-01-18 — When Doing the Right Thing Makes Things Worse First

**Type:** Leadership Hardship Report  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Complete

A leader this week asked whether increasing resistance means a decision was wrong. I remembered our first confrontation with Pharaoh.

Aaron and I delivered God's command that Pharaoh let Israel go. Pharaoh did not respond by making life easier. He increased the people's workload and stopped supplying the straw they needed for making bricks while still demanding the same quota. The Israelite foremen were beaten when the quota was not met, and afterward they blamed Aaron and me for making their situation worse.

That was difficult because their suffering was real. It was not enough for me to say, “But I obeyed.” My obedience did not make their pain imaginary.

I brought the matter back to God. The eventual deliverance came from Him, but there was a period when obedience appeared to have made conditions worse.

This week I reminded our leaders not to measure every decision by whether opposition immediately decreases. We should listen carefully when people are hurting, examine whether we have acted faithfully, and continue depending on God rather than defending ourselves simply because our intentions were good.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 5–6.  
**Reflection:** Obedience and immediate comfort are not the same thing.

## 2026-01-25 — Perseverance Is Not Pretending Resistance Is Easy

**Type:** Weekly Leadership Report  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Complete

This week's leadership work was steady rather than dramatic. Several matters required repeated follow-up after earlier conversations had produced little visible movement.

Pharaoh taught me something about repeated resistance. There were times during the confrontation in Egypt when circumstances changed, Pharaoh appeared to yield, and then he hardened his position again. The process was exhausting and far beyond anything I could have solved by becoming more persuasive.

I do not want perseverance to become stubbornness. Leaders must remain correctable. But neither should we assume that a faithful assignment becomes invalid because the same obstacle appears again.

My responsibility is to obey God, speak truthfully, care about the people affected, and avoid making myself the hero of the story. Israel's deliverance was God's work.

**Attention:** No major escalation this week. Continue current follow-ups.  
**Biblical account:** Exodus 7–12.

## 2026-02-01 — Remembering Deliverance So the Children Know Why

**Type:** Testimony / Teaching Report  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete

This week I have been thinking about how easily a community can keep a practice while forgetting the reason for it.

Before Israel left Egypt, God gave instructions for Passover. The meal was not meant to become an unexplained tradition. The people were specifically told that when their children asked what the observance meant, they were to explain how the Lord passed over the houses of the Israelites and delivered them from Egypt.

The next generation was supposed to know the story.

That matters for our work with Gifts and Arrows and for every leader who teaches children. We should not only tell young people what the church does. We should tell them what God has done and why our worship, obedience, generosity, prayer, and remembrance matter.

**Ministry connection:** Gifts and Arrows; Events when remembrance is part of a church gathering.  
**Biblical account:** Exodus 12:21–27; 13:8–10.

## 2026-02-08 — The Sea in Front of Us

**Type:** Testimony  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete

I shared this testimony again because some of our leaders are facing situations where every visible option appears closed.

After Israel left Egypt, Pharaoh changed his mind and pursued us with his army. The people saw the Egyptians approaching and became terrified. The Red Sea was in front of us and Pharaoh's forces were behind us. Some of the people immediately asked why we had been brought out of Egypt and said it would have been better to remain there than die in the wilderness.

I told them not to be afraid, to stand firm, and to see the deliverance of the Lord. Then God told us to move forward. He divided the sea, Israel crossed on dry ground, and when the Egyptian forces pursued, the waters returned.

I remember both instructions: stand firm, and move forward.

There are moments when faith refuses panic and waits for God. There are also moments when God has given direction and continuing to stand still would become disobedience. Leadership requires discernment about which moment we are in.

The most important truth remains that God delivered us. The sea did not open because Moses finally developed a sufficiently impressive crisis-management plan.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 14:5–31.  
**Reflection theme:** Faith under pressure; God's deliverance; knowing when to wait and when to move.

## 2026-02-15 — Praise After Deliverance

**Type:** Ministry Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Related ministry:** Psalmists  
**Status:** Complete

I joined the Psalmists' reflection this week on worship as a response to what God has done.

After Israel crossed the Red Sea, we sang to the Lord because He had delivered us from Pharaoh's army. Miriam then took a tambourine, and the women followed her with tambourines and dancing.

That celebration was not entertainment added after the important part. The deliverance itself produced praise.

I encouraged the Psalmists to remember this whenever preparation becomes dominated by arrangements, equipment, rehearsal, choreography, or whether every tambourine is where it belongs. Excellence in preparation is useful, but the ministry exists because God is worthy of worship.

Miriam would probably also appreciate the reminder about returning tambourines to the correct equipment area after use.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 15:1–21.  
**Ministry connection:** Psalmists.

## 2026-02-22 — Bitter Water and Real Complaints

**Type:** Weekly Leadership Report  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Complete

A practical provision concern this week reminded me of Marah.

After crossing the Red Sea, we traveled for three days in the wilderness without finding water. When we finally reached Marah, the water was too bitter to drink. The people complained to me and asked what they were supposed to drink.

The need was real. Telling thirsty people that they should complain more politely would not have produced water.

I cried out to the Lord, and He showed me what to do so the water became fit to drink.

This remains useful for leadership. Complaints can expose unhealthy attitudes, but they can also point to an actual problem. A leader must learn to distinguish between the two without using one to dismiss the other.

**Ministry connection:** Facilities / practical logistics.  
**Biblical account:** Exodus 15:22–27.

## 2026-03-01 — Daily Bread and the Victuals Ministry

**Type:** Ministry Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Related ministry:** Victuals Ministry  
**Status:** Complete

I spent time with Victuals this week discussing provision, distribution, and the temptation to treat ordinary food service as spiritually insignificant.

In the wilderness the congregation complained that there was no food. God responded by providing bread from heaven. Each morning the people gathered what they needed. We called it manna. Households gathered according to their needs, and on the sixth day they gathered twice as much because there would be no collection on the Sabbath.

The provision was miraculous, but there was still organization around gathering, households, quantities, timing, and instruction.

I appreciate that Victuals deals with similarly practical questions every week: setup, preparation, serving, cleanup, quantities, timing, and whether enough food is available. Practical work can still be part of caring faithfully for God's people.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 16.  
**Ministry connection:** Victuals Ministry.

## 2026-03-08 — Please Do Not Keep the Manna Overnight

**Type:** Ministry Incident Reflection  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Related ministry:** Victuals Ministry  
**Status:** Complete

Last week's discussion with Victuals led to the less glamorous part of the manna account.

The people were instructed to gather the ordinary daily portion and not keep it until morning. Some ignored the instruction and stored it anyway. By morning it had developed worms and smelled. I was angry with them.

There was an exception later in the week: on the sixth day the people gathered a double portion for the Sabbath, and that portion did not spoil. The point was not that storing food is inherently wrong. The people were being taught to receive God's provision according to His instruction rather than according to fear and hoarding.

For Victuals, the immediate application is simpler: food-handling instructions are not suggestions, and “we saved it from last time” is not automatically a reassuring sentence.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 16:16–30.  
**Ministry connection:** Victuals Ministry.

## 2026-03-15 — Complaints, Cravings, and the Weight Behind the Food Issue

**Type:** Restricted Personal Development Report  
**Visibility:** Restricted  
**Status:** Follow-up Required

The food discussion this week brought back a more difficult memory from Numbers 11.

There was a period when people became consumed with craving other food. They complained about the manna and spoke nostalgically about the food they remembered in Egypt. Their complaints spread through the camp.

I became deeply distressed. I told God that the burden of carrying the people was too heavy for me and that I could not carry them alone. This was not an ordinary frustrating week. I had reached a point of severe emotional exhaustion.

God's response continues to challenge the way I think about overloaded leaders. He did not merely tell me to become stronger. He instructed me to gather seventy elders, and He shared the burden of leadership with them.

The chapter also records God's provision of quail and severe judgment connected with the people's craving. I do not want to turn that suffering into a joke about people asking Victuals for a different menu.

What I do want to remember is this: sometimes the visible complaint is about food while the leadership issue underneath it is much larger. And sometimes the spiritual response to an overwhelmed leader includes prayer and redistribution of responsibility.

**Attention:** Review my current workload during Tuesday Mentorship Night.  
**Biblical account:** Numbers 11.  
**2026 goal link:** Delegate without disappearing.

## 2026-03-22 — Water From the Rock: Similar Problem, Different Instruction

**Type:** Leadership Reflection  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Complete

I reviewed the first water-from-the-rock account this week because it is easy to confuse it with what happened much later at Meribah.

At Rephidim the people had no water and quarreled with me. Their anger became serious enough that I cried out to God and said they were almost ready to stone me. God told me to take the staff, go to the rock at Horeb, and strike it. I did, and water came out for the people to drink.

Years later, in another water crisis, God gave me a different instruction: speak to the rock. I struck it instead. The situations looked similar, but God's instruction was not the same.

Experience is useful, but it can become dangerous if I assume that because God led one way before, I no longer need to listen carefully now.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 17:1–7; compare Numbers 20:1–13.  
**Reflection theme:** Do not let previous success replace present obedience.

## 2026-03-29 — Leaders Need People to Hold Up Their Hands

**Type:** Leadership Wellbeing / Teamwork Report  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Complete

This week I encouraged several leaders to stop treating exhaustion as proof of faithfulness.

When Amalek attacked Israel, Joshua led the fighting while I stood above the battle with the staff of God in my hands. As long as my hands were raised Israel prevailed, but my hands became tired. Aaron and Hur brought a stone for me to sit on and held my hands up, one on each side, until sunset.

I could not physically sustain what was required alone. The answer was not pretending that my arms were still strong. Aaron and Hur helped me.

Joshua was doing his part below. Aaron and Hur were doing theirs beside me. I was doing mine. The work required all of us.

I want our leaders to understand that receiving practical support is not a lesser form of leadership.

**Follow-up:** Identify leaders carrying responsibilities without adequate support.  
**Biblical account:** Exodus 17:8–16.  
**2026 goal link:** Delegate without disappearing.
