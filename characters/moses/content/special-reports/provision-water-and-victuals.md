## Water Supply Incident — Marah

**Record type:** Resource / Water Incident  
**Date:** 2026-02-23  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Resolved  
**Tags:** #serious #administrative #frustration #relief

### Situation

After crossing the Red Sea, Israel traveled three days in the wilderness without finding water. At Marah, water was found but could not be consumed because it was bitter.

The people complained against Moses and asked what they were supposed to drink.

### Status

**Water located:** Yes.  
**Initially potable:** No.  
**Community complaint:** Active at time of incident.  
**Resolution:** The Lord showed Moses a piece of wood; after Moses threw it into the water, the water became fit to drink.

### Operational lesson

Finding a resource does not mean the resource is usable. Availability and suitability are different status fields.

### Spiritual lesson

The Lord provided after the people encountered a need they could not solve themselves.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 15:22–27.

## Victuals Provision Status — Manna

**Record type:** Provision / Victuals Status  
**Date:** 2026-03-02  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Recurring Provision Established  
**Tags:** #administrative #devotional #gratitude

### Situation

The congregation complained in the wilderness about food and spoke as though death in Egypt beside pots of meat and plentiful bread would have been preferable to hunger in the wilderness.

The Lord responded by providing bread from heaven.

### Distribution instructions

- Gather each day according to household need.
- Ordinary weekday portions were not to be kept until morning.
- Some ignored the instruction; stored portions developed worms and smelled.
- On the sixth day, households gathered twice the normal amount.
- The seventh day was Sabbath; no manna collection was scheduled.
- A portion was preserved for future generations as testimony.

### Operational ownership note

**Related leader:** Moses  
**Operational owner:** Victuals Ministry for Data Play mapping

Although Moses communicated the instruction, this is **Victuals work** in the Oikonomia demo: food provision, household distribution guidance, collection schedule, storage compliance, and testimony preservation.

### Status

**Provision:** Active daily.  
**Friday-equivalent preparation:** Double collection before Sabbath.  
**Sabbath collection:** None.  
**Storage incident:** Documented.  
**Preserved testimony sample:** Required.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 16.

## Victuals Compliance Incident — Overnight Manna Storage

**Record type:** Food Handling / Compliance Incident  
**Date:** 2026-03-09  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Closed with recurring guidance  
**Tags:** #administrative #light #frustration

### Issue

Some members kept ordinary manna overnight despite instructions not to do so.

### Result

The stored manna developed worms and began to smell.

Moses was angry with those who ignored the instruction.

### Corrective guidance

- Gather what is needed for the day.
- Do not preserve ordinary weekday portions overnight.
- Sixth-day double portions are the stated exception for Sabbath preparation.

### Data Play note

This is a useful ordinary operational record because the biblical account itself contains collection rules, storage rules, noncompliance, observable consequences, and corrective communication.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 16:19–30.

## Food Provision Escalation — Quail / Numbers 11

**Record type:** Critical Provision / Leadership Incident  
**Date:** 2026-03-16  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Closed — Serious Historical Event  
**Tags:** #serious #dramatic #exhaustion #frustration #grief

### Situation

The people began craving other food and complained about the manna, remembering fish, cucumbers, melons, leeks, onions, and garlic from Egypt.

This was not merely a menu preference request.

The complaint became part of a deeper rejection of God's provision and produced severe distress in Moses. Moses told God that the burden of carrying the people was too heavy for him.

### Leadership capacity response

God instructed Moses to gather seventy elders and distributed leadership responsibility among them.

### Provision response

God also promised meat. Quail came in extraordinary quantity around the camp.

The account then records severe judgment while the meat was still between the people's teeth.

### Operational boundary

This event should not be reduced to a humorous "members requested chicken" ticket. It combines provision, craving, rejection of God's provision, leadership exhaustion, delegation, and judgment.

### Leadership lesson

When Moses said the burden was too heavy, God's answer was not simply to tell him to become stronger. Responsibility was shared with seventy elders.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 11.

## Water Crisis — Rephidim

**Record type:** Resource / Water Escalation  
**Date:** 2026-03-23  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Resolved  
**Tags:** #dramatic #serious #frustration #pressure #relief

### Situation

The community camped at Rephidim and there was no water for the people to drink.

The people quarreled with Moses and demanded water. The dispute escalated to the point that Moses cried to the Lord that the people were almost ready to stone him.

### Resolution

God instructed Moses to take elders with him and strike the rock at Horeb with the staff he had used on the Nile. Water came from the rock for the people to drink.

### Status

**Water shortage:** Resolved.  
**Community conflict:** Severe during incident.  
**Leadership safety concern:** Moses reported risk of being stoned.  
**Provision:** Water supplied.

### Historical distinction

This event at Rephidim in Exodus 17 should not be confused with the later Meribah event in Numbers 20 where Moses was instructed to speak to the rock but struck it twice in anger.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 17:1–7.
