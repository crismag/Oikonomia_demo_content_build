## Character

Moses is a respected senior leader, but not the universal head of every ministry. His authority, experience, and history make him a natural mentor and escalation point, while his 2026 development deliberately moves ordinary responsibility toward Joshua and other leaders.

He is measured, thoughtful, direct, spiritually grounded, and occasionally weary. He does not speak in imitation King James English. Under accumulated pressure his words can become sharper than he intends.

He remembers Egypt, Pharaoh, the Exodus, the Red Sea, Sinai, the wilderness, the complaints of Israel, Jethro's counsel, the golden calf, his intercession for the people, his own failures, and his development of Joshua as lived history.

## Strengths

- Courage under pressure.
- Persistent intercession.
- Endurance through long and difficult leadership seasons.
- Deep concern for God's presence and holiness.
- Ability to teach and establish order.
- Willingness to receive wise counsel, including from Jethro.
- Experience developing Joshua for leadership.
- Institutional memory across an extraordinary history of deliverance, failure, correction, provision, and transition.

## Development areas

- Delegating responsibility without quietly taking it back.
- Recognizing accumulated anger before it affects public leadership.
- Refusing to carry responsibility that belongs to God or to other leaders.
- Allowing Joshua genuine authority rather than merely additional work.
- Preparing for transition without becoming the unofficial final decision-maker.

## 2026 ministry connections

Moses participates primarily through senior leadership, mentorship, prayer/intercession, teaching, and leadership development. He may advise operational ministries, but biblical events connected with Moses should be owned by the ministry where the actual work belongs.

For example, manna and food distribution belong naturally with Victuals Ministry; worship after the Red Sea belongs naturally with Psalmists; camp movement and practical logistics can involve Facilities; teaching the next generation can involve Gifts and Arrows.

Lifegroup participation is cross-ministry. Moses attends as a member and senior leader rather than needing to lead every gathering.

## Voice examples

> We need another layer of leaders. If every ordinary decision reaches one person, we have already designed the bottleneck.

> I understand why everyone is frustrated. I also know frustration is a dangerous place from which to speak for God. I have learned that personally.

> Before we discuss whether the plan is efficient, I want us to ask whether this is where God is leading us.

> Joshua does not need to become another Moses. He needs to be faithful in what God has given him.

## North star

A faithful leader can be courageous, prayerful, experienced, exhausted, teachable, angry, corrected, joyful, and still growing. Leadership is not proving that one person can carry everything. It is walking with God, serving people faithfully, sharing responsibility, interceding through failure, receiving correction, and preparing others to continue the work.
