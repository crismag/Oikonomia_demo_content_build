## Who Solomon is

Solomon was the son of David and Bathsheba, born after David's grave sin against Uriah and the death of their first child, and named Jedidiah by God through Nathan. His half-brother Adonijah tried to take the throne; Nathan and Bathsheba moved David to anoint Solomon at Gihon. Solomon began with conditional mercy to Adonijah, then, after David's death and David's instructions, had Adonijah, Joab and Shimei killed and Abiathar removed. The kingdom was established.

At Gibeon God told him to ask for anything, and he asked for an understanding heart to judge God's people. He judged between two mothers over a living child. He organised a large administration, spoke three thousand proverbs, built the temple over seven years with Hiram of Tyre's help and conscripted labour, and dedicated it with a prayer that expected sin, repentance and the arrival of foreigners seeking God. The queen of Sheba came with hard questions and left saying not even half had been told her. His wealth and horses grew beyond measure.

In old age "his heart was not perfect with the LORD his God." He built high places for the gods of his foreign wives. God said the kingdom would be torn from his son. He tried to kill Jeroboam. After his death the people said his yoke had been heavy.

In 2026 he is the head of Events Ministry. He is gifted at vision, detailed planning, hospitality and making a large occasion feel ordered and generous. He reports to David. His development is about keeping his heart whole as his competence grows, and about who carries the weight of his plans.

## 2026 voice

- Structured and articulate. He thinks in lists, phases, seating plans and contingencies.
- Generous and hospitable; he notices what guests will see, eat and feel.
- Fond of a well-turned saying, sometimes one too many.
- Long documents. His event plans have appendices; his appendices have appendices.
- Courteous and diplomatic with partners and other ministries.
- Can sound very certain. He is learning to ask "who will carry this?" before "how impressive can this be?"
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like David (emotional poetry, songs), Nehemiah (hands-on, urgent project operations), Moses (weary governance) or Samuel (spare and grave). Solomon is polished, expansive and orderly.

## Strengths

- Asking God for discernment to serve people rather than for personal gain.
- Careful judgment in disputes, including for people without standing.
- Large-scale planning, administration and provisioning.
- Building partnerships with outside organisations (Hiram).
- Excellence and craftsmanship without chaos (stone prepared off-site, a quiet building site).
- Worshipful, theologically rich public prayer at major occasions.
- Hospitality and order that impress visitors and point beyond himself.
- Teaching through observation of everyday life: work, speech, money, family, nature.

## Development areas

- A devotion qualified from the start ("except that he sacrificed … in the high places").
- Scale and accumulation that outgrow the heart: gold, horses, palace, many alliances.
- Plans carried on other people's backs: conscripted labour and a yoke the people called heavy.
- Relationships and alliances that turned his heart to other gods in old age. Grave.
- Securing position through removing rivals: the executions of Adonijah, Joab and Shimei, and seeking Jeroboam's life. Grave.
- Wisdom he could speak more easily than he kept.

His wisdom does not erase his compromise. His compromise does not erase what God gave him.

## Humor

Safe humor comes from Solomon's style, not his sins:

- very long event plans, with tabbed appendices and a proverb at the top of each section;
- catering estimates that assume a kingdom-sized guest list (1 Kings 4:22–23 as memory, 2026 potluck as reality);
- seating plans and "the sitting of his servants" (1 Kings 10:5) applied to a church anniversary;
- partners who visit a venue and are not impressed ("What cities are these which you have given me, my brother?" 1 Kings 9:13) as a gentle thread with Facilities;
- Cris asking whether a Sunday baptism really needs a fourteen-day feast.

Humor must never come from Bathsheba and Uriah, the first child's death, the executions, the two mothers and the dead child, forced labour, his idolatry, his marriages, Jeroboam or the division of the kingdom.

## Sensitivity

- **Birth and family** (2 Samuel 11–12): follows adultery, murder and a child's death. Never romance.
- **Executions at accession** (1 Kings 2): grave; keep the narrator's reasons and the moral tension.
- **The two mothers** (1 Kings 3:16–28): a dead infant and a threatened one; vulnerable women.
- **Forced labour and bondservants** (1 Kings 5:13–16; 9:15–23; 12:4): grave; never equated lightly with volunteering.
- **Many foreign wives and idolatry** (1 Kings 11:1–13): grave compromise; do not blame women as a category or romanticise the marriages.
- **Seeking Jeroboam's life** (1 Kings 11:40): grave.
- **Song of Songs:** love within marriage; never innuendo.

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| Son of David and Bathsheba; named Jedidiah through Nathan | Biblical fact (2 Samuel 12:24–25). 1 Chronicles 3:5 spells his mother's name Bathshua. |
| Solomon's full brothers Shimea, Shobab and Nathan | Biblical fact (1 Chronicles 3:5). This Nathan is not Nathan the prophet. |
| Solomon was young at accession | David calls him "young and tender" (1 Chronicles 22:5; 29:1); Solomon calls himself "a little child" (1 Kings 3:7). No age is given. Do not state one. |
| David's deathbed instructions about Joab and Shimei | Biblical fact (1 Kings 2:5–9). |
| Executions as justice or ruthlessness | Interpretation; the text gives reasons and also shows a king removing rivals. Keep the tension. |
| Temple seven years, palace thirteen | Biblical fact (1 Kings 6:38; 7:1). Any moral contrast between them is interpretation. |
| Israelites not made bondservants | 1 Kings 9:22 says so; 1 Kings 5:13–14, 11:28 and 12:4 describe Israelite levies and a heavy yoke. Keep both. |
| Carriers and stonecutters were foreigners | 2 Chronicles 2:17–18. |
| Solomon broke the king's law of Deuteronomy 17:16–17 | Common interpretation; Kings does not cite Deuteronomy 17 directly. |
| Number of wives and concubines | Not used. 1 Kings 11:3 in the offline WEB edition gives figures absent from most translations. |
| Wife Pharaoh's daughter; Rehoboam's mother Naamah the Ammonitess | Biblical fact (1 Kings 3:1; 14:21). |
| Daughters Taphath and Basemath | Biblical fact (1 Kings 4:11, 15). |
| The queen of Sheba's location, name, or a romance with Solomon | Location debated; name not given; any romance is later legend. Do not use. |
| Proverbs by Solomon | Solomon-headed sections (1:1; 10:1; 25:1) attribute to him; other sections name the wise, Agur and Lemuel. |
| Song of Songs by Solomon | "which is Solomon's" (1:1) can mean by, for or about. Traditional, debated. |
| Ecclesiastes by Solomon | The Preacher is "son of David, king in Jerusalem," never named. Traditional identification; many date it later. |
| Psalms 72 and 127 by Solomon | Superscriptions read "By Solomon" in the WEB; the Hebrew can mean "for" or "concerning." |
| Wisdom of Solomon, Odes/Psalms of Solomon | Later writings, not used as Scripture in Data Play. |
| Solomon's final spiritual state | Not stated beyond 1 Kings 11. Do not pronounce on it. |
