# Solomon — Biblical Context Library

This file records Solomon's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are Solomon's memories: a contested succession, wisdom asked for and given, a temple built and dedicated, a kingdom administered at scale, and a heart that turned away in old age.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered. Solomon's wisdom must not erase his later compromise; his compromise must not erase what God gave him.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

---

## Born to David and Bathsheba; named Jedidiah — 2 Samuel 12:24–25; 1 Chronicles 3:5

After David's adultery with Bathsheba, his arranging of her husband Uriah's death, Nathan's confrontation and the death of their first child, "David comforted Bathsheba his wife." She bore a son, and David named him Solomon. "The LORD loved him," and sent word through Nathan the prophet to name him Jedidiah. 1 Chronicles 3:5 lists Shimea, Shobab and Nathan as his full brothers.

**Sensitivity:** Solomon's birth follows grave sin, a man's death and a child's death. It is never romance. David's canonical records treat Bathsheba and Uriah seriously; Solomon's records must do the same.

**Namesake:** Solomon's brother Nathan (1 Chronicles 3:5) is not Nathan the prophet.

**Truths:** God's love for Solomon was announced into a family carrying deep failure and grief.

**2026 use:** Rarely and seriously: identity and grace, family history that a leader did not choose.

## Adonijah's claim and Solomon's anointing — 1 Kings 1

When David was old, his son Adonijah declared, "I will be king," gathered chariots and runners, and held a sacrificial feast with Joab and Abiathar the priest, inviting the king's sons but not Solomon, Nathan, Zadok or Benaiah. The narrator notes that David had never once corrected Adonijah by asking, "Why have you done so?" Nathan urged Bathsheba to remind David of his oath that Solomon would reign, and came in to confirm it. David ordered Zadok, Nathan and Benaiah to put Solomon on his own mule, anoint him at Gihon and blow the trumpet. The people shouted, "Long live King Solomon!" and the noise reached Adonijah's feast. His guests scattered. Adonijah clung to the horns of the altar and asked for an oath that Solomon would not kill him. Solomon said, "If he shows himself a worthy man, not a hair of his shall fall to the earth; but if wickedness is found in him, he shall die," and sent him home.

**Truths:** Solomon's accession was contested and depended on other people acting quickly. His first ruling was conditional mercy. David's failure to correct Adonijah shaped the crisis.

**2026 use:** Succession and handover records with David, contested leadership transitions (serious), links to David's canonical family material about delaying difficult conversations.

## David's charge, and the executions — 1 Kings 2

Before he died David charged Solomon to be strong, keep the law of Moses and walk in God's ways. He also told Solomon to deal with Joab "according to your wisdom" and not let his grey head go down to Sheol in peace, because Joab had killed Abner and Amasa in peacetime; to show kindness to the sons of Barzillai; and not to hold Shimei guiltless. After David's death Adonijah asked Bathsheba to request Abishag, David's attendant, as his wife. Solomon heard it as a claim to the kingdom and swore Adonijah would die that day; Benaiah killed him. Solomon banished Abiathar the priest to Anathoth instead of executing him, "that he might fulfill the LORD's word which he spoke concerning the house of Eli in Shiloh." Joab fled to the altar and refused to come out; Solomon ordered Benaiah to kill him there, saying Joab's innocent bloodshed would be removed from David's house. Solomon confined Shimei to Jerusalem on pain of death; three years later Shimei went to Gath after runaway slaves, and Solomon had him executed. "The kingdom was established in the hand of Solomon."

**Sensitivity:** Three men were killed and a priest removed as Solomon secured his throne, one of them at the altar. Present these plainly and soberly. Scripture narrates them without a simple verdict; Data Play does not turn them into a lesson about "decisive leadership" and never uses them for humor.

**Interpretation:** Readers debate how far the executions were justice, prudent security, or ruthlessness. The text gives reasons for each (Joab's bloodshed, Shimei's broken oath, Adonijah's request) and also shows a new king removing rivals. Keep that tension.

**Truths:** Solomon's reign began with his father's unfinished business handed to him. Authority was consolidated through death as well as wisdom.

**2026 use:** Serious restricted personal development about conflict and rivals; succession handover with David (David's canonical records do not yet address 1 Kings 2:5–9).

## Pharaoh's daughter and the high places — 1 Kings 3:1–3

"Solomon made a marriage alliance with Pharaoh king of Egypt." The people still sacrificed at high places because there was no temple yet. "Solomon loved the LORD, walking in the statutes of David his father, except that he sacrificed and burned incense in the high places."

**Truths:** The narrator qualifies Solomon's devotion from the start. Political alliance through marriage appears before the temple.

## "Ask for what I should give you" — 1 Kings 3:4–15; 2 Chronicles 1:3–13

At Gibeon, where the Tent of Meeting stood, Solomon offered a thousand burnt offerings. That night God appeared in a dream: "Ask for what I should give you." Solomon remembered God's kindness to David and said, "I am just a little child. I don't know how to go out or come in." He asked, "Give your servant therefore an understanding heart to judge your people, that I may discern between good and evil." God was pleased that he had not asked for long life, riches or the death of enemies, gave him a wise and understanding heart, and added riches and honour. God said that if Solomon walked in His ways as David did, He would lengthen his days. Solomon returned to Jerusalem, stood before the ark and held a feast for his servants.

**Truths:** The request was for the ability to serve people justly, not for personal gain. The gift came with a condition about walking in God's ways.

**2026 use:** Personal development, testimony, Mentorship, a goal about asking for wisdom before capacity.

## Two mothers and one living child — 1 Kings 3:16–28

Two women who were prostitutes came before the king. Both had given birth; one child had died in the night. Each claimed the living child. Solomon called for a sword and ordered the living child divided. The real mother cried out, "give her the living child, and in no way kill him!" The other said to divide him. Solomon said, "Give the first woman the living child, and definitely do not kill him. She is his mother." Israel "saw that the wisdom of God was in him to do justice."

**Sensitivity:** A baby died and another was in danger; two vulnerable women had no one else to judge between them. The judgment is not comedy, and the women are not a punchline.

**Truths:** Wisdom listened to people society overlooked, and found truth through love rather than evidence alone.

**2026 use:** Discernment in disputes, pastoral care, justice for vulnerable people. Serious.

## Officials, districts and provision — 1 Kings 4

Solomon's government had named officials: priests, scribes, a recorder, a commander, an officer over district governors, a chief minister called "the king's friend," a steward, and Adoniram "over the men subject to forced labor." Twelve district officers each provided food for the royal household for one month a year; two of them were married to Solomon's daughters. The daily provision was enormous. Judah and Israel were numerous, "eating and drinking and making merry," and lived safely, "every man under his vine and under his fig tree." God gave Solomon wisdom "like the sand that is on the seashore." He spoke three thousand proverbs and his songs numbered 1,005; he spoke about trees, animals, birds, reptiles and fish; people came from all nations to hear him.

**Truths:** Peace and prosperity were real. They rested on a system of rotating provision and, from the start, forced labour.

**2026 use:** Events planning and administration, rotas and responsibilities, the limits of any system that depends on other people's burden; light humor about the scale of catering lists (never about forced labour).

## Hiram and the preparations — 1 Kings 5; 2 Chronicles 2

Hiram king of Tyre, who "had always loved David," sent envoys. Solomon explained that David could not build the house for the LORD because of wars, but God had given rest, and asked for cedars from Lebanon, offering wages for Hiram's woodcutters. Hiram rejoiced and supplied cedar and cypress by sea in exchange for yearly wheat and oil; they made a treaty. Solomon raised a levy of thirty thousand men from Israel, sending ten thousand a month to Lebanon in rotation, with Adoniram over the forced labour, plus seventy thousand carriers and eighty thousand stonecutters and thousands of supervisors. 2 Chronicles 2:17–18 says the carriers and stonecutters were foreigners living in Israel.

**Namesake:** Hiram king of Tyre is different from Hiram (Huram-abi in 2 Chronicles 2:13), the bronze craftsman from Tyre whose mother was from Naphtali (1 Kings 7:13–14).

**2026 use:** Partnership and supplier relationships (continuing David's canonical Hiram record), Events and Facilities coordination, project resourcing.

## Building the temple and the palace — 1 Kings 6–7

Solomon began building the temple in the fourth year of his reign. The stone was prepared at the quarry, so no hammer, axe or iron tool was heard at the site. During construction God told Solomon that if he walked in His statutes, God would dwell among Israel and not forsake them. The temple took seven years. Solomon's own house took thirteen years. Hiram the bronze-worker made the pillars, the great basin and the temple furnishings.

**Interpretation:** Some readers contrast seven years for the temple with thirteen for the palace; the text states both without comment.

**Truths:** God's presence was tied to obedience, not to the building.

**2026 use:** Project planning, quality and quiet craftsmanship, facilities work (Facilities owns building operations; Solomon's records are planning and Events perspective).

## Dedication: the ark, the cloud and the prayer — 1 Kings 8; 2 Chronicles 5–7

Solomon assembled Israel at the feast in the seventh month. The priests brought the ark into the inner sanctuary; it held only the two stone tablets Moses had placed there. When the priests came out, a cloud filled the house, "for the LORD's glory filled the LORD's house." 2 Chronicles adds that this happened as the Levite singers Asaph, Heman and Jeduthun, with 120 trumpeters, praised together: "For he is good, for his loving kindness endures forever!" Solomon blessed the assembly, told how God had kept His word to David, and knelt with hands spread toward heaven. He prayed: "heaven and the heaven of heavens can't contain you; how much less this house that I have built!" He asked God to hear and forgive when people sinned and repented, in drought, famine, war and exile, acknowledging "there is no man who doesn't sin," and to hear the foreigner who came from far away "that all the peoples of the earth may know your name." Chronicles records fire from heaven consuming the sacrifices. The feast lasted fourteen days, and the people went home "joyful and glad."

**Truths:** The finest building could not contain God. Solomon's prayer expected sin, repentance and forgiveness, and welcomed outsiders.

**2026 use:** Events: dedications, large celebrations, coordination with Psalmists, Victuals, Facilities and Production. Testimony and prayer. The dedication is worship, not a production spectacle; do not project 2026 equipment into the ancient event.

## God's second appearance: a warning — 1 Kings 9:1–9; 2 Chronicles 7:12–22

After the building, God appeared to Solomon again. He had heard the prayer and consecrated the house. If Solomon walked in integrity like David, his throne would be established; if he or his children turned to other gods, Israel would be cut off from the land and the temple would become a ruin people would ask about. 2 Chronicles 7:14 includes, "if my people who are called by my name will humble themselves, pray, seek my face, and turn from their wicked ways, then I will hear from heaven, will forgive their sin, and will heal their land."

**Truths:** The height of Solomon's success came with the clearest warning of his life.

## Cities, Cabul and the labour force — 1 Kings 9:10–28

Solomon gave Hiram twenty towns in Galilee. Hiram came to see them and was not pleased: "What cities are these which you have given me, my brother?" He called them Cabul. Solomon's forced labour built the temple, his palace, the Millo, the wall of Jerusalem, Hazor, Megiddo, Gezer, storage cities and chariot cities. The remaining Canaanite peoples were made bondservants; the narrator says Israelites were not made bondservants but served as soldiers and officials. Solomon built a fleet at Ezion Geber, crewed with Hiram's sailors, which brought gold from Ophir.

**Tension:** 1 Kings 9:22 says no Israelites were made bondservants, yet 1 Kings 5:13–14 describes a rotating levy of Israelite men, 11:28 puts Jeroboam over the labour of the house of Joseph, and after Solomon's death Israel said, "Your father made our yoke difficult" (12:4). Preserve both statements; do not smooth them.

**Sensitivity:** Forced labour and the enslavement of the remaining Canaanite peoples are grave. Never humor. Never equate them lightly with volunteer rotas.

**2026 use:** The Cabul exchange can support gentle humor about a partner's unimpressed site visit. Forced labour supports serious reflection on who carries a leader's vision.

## The queen of Sheba — 1 Kings 10:1–13; 2 Chronicles 9:1–12

Hearing of Solomon's fame "concerning the LORD's name," the queen of Sheba came with a great caravan of spices, gold and precious stones "to test him with hard questions." Solomon answered all of them. When she saw his wisdom, his house, "the food of his table, the sitting of his servants, the attendance of his officials, their clothing, his cup bearers" and his ascent to the temple, "there was no more spirit in her." She said, "not even half was told me!" and blessed the LORD for making him king "to do justice and righteousness." They exchanged lavish gifts and she went home.

**Later reference:** Jesus said the Queen of the South came from the ends of the earth to hear Solomon's wisdom, "and behold, someone greater than Solomon is here" (Matthew 12:42; Luke 11:31).

**Truths:** Wisdom drew honest questions from outside. Order and hospitality were part of what she saw. Her praise pointed to God's purpose: justice and righteousness.

**2026 use:** Hospitality and guest reception with Greeters and Ushers and Events, answering hard questions from visitors, testimony. Jesus remains the centre; "someone greater than Solomon" is never a joke at Solomon's expense.

## Wealth, gold and horses — 1 Kings 10:14–29

Gold came in every year; Solomon made gold shields and a great ivory throne overlaid with gold with lions on its steps; silver became as common as stones in Jerusalem. He gathered 1,400 chariots and 12,000 horsemen and imported horses from Egypt, trading them on to other kings. "King Solomon exceeded all the kings of the earth in riches and in wisdom."

**Interpretation:** Deuteronomy 17:16–17 says a king must not multiply horses, return the people to Egypt for horses, multiply wives, or greatly multiply silver and gold. Many readers see 1 Kings 10–11 as showing Solomon breaking these one by one. Kings does not cite Deuteronomy 17 directly; 1 Kings 11:2 cites the command against intermarriage with the nations.

**2026 use:** Stewardship, scale and accumulation; restricted personal development.

## A heart turned away — 1 Kings 11:1–13; Nehemiah 13:26

"King Solomon loved many foreign women," including Pharaoh's daughter and women from the nations Israel had been told not to intermarry with, because they would turn their hearts to their gods. "When Solomon was old, his wives turned away his heart after other gods; and his heart was not perfect with the LORD his God, as the heart of David his father was." He followed Ashtoreth and Milcom and built high places for Chemosh and Molech on the mountain before Jerusalem, and did the same for all his foreign wives. The LORD was angry with Solomon "because his heart was turned away from the LORD … who had appeared to him twice." God said He would tear the kingdom from him, not in his lifetime for David's sake, and would leave one tribe to his son. Centuries later Nehemiah cited Solomon: loved by his God, without equal among kings, and still led into sin. The high places Solomon built stood until Josiah defiled them (2 Kings 23:13).

**Sensitivity:** This is grave spiritual compromise late in life by the man who dedicated the temple. The narrator says the women turned his heart; the LORD holds Solomon responsible. Data Play does not blame the women as a category, does not romanticise the marriages, and does not joke about them.

**Textual note:** 1 Kings 11:3 in the offline WEB edition gives numbers of wives and concubines that do not appear in most translations. Do not use a number.

**Truths:** Wisdom did not protect a heart that was not guarded. Decline came slowly and in old age.

**2026 use:** Restricted personal development about finishing well, relationships and alliances that shift loyalty, and accountability late in a successful tenure. Never humor.

## Adversaries and Jeroboam — 1 Kings 11:14–43

God raised adversaries: Hadad the Edomite, who had fled to Egypt as a child when Joab killed Edom's males, and Rezon, who ruled Damascus. Jeroboam son of Nebat, an industrious young man Solomon had put over the labour of the house of Joseph, met the prophet Ahijah, who tore his new cloak into twelve pieces and gave him ten, saying God would give him ten tribes because Solomon had forsaken Him. "Therefore Solomon sought to kill Jeroboam," who fled to Egypt until Solomon died. Solomon reigned forty years and was buried in David's city.

**Truths:** The young leader Solomon had noticed and promoted became the one to whom the kingdom was given. Solomon's response was to try to kill him.

**2026 use:** Serious: responding to rivals and succession without eliminating people.

## After Solomon: the heavy yoke — 1 Kings 12:1–20

At Shechem Israel asked Rehoboam to lighten "the hard service of your father, and his heavy yoke." The older men who had served Solomon advised serving the people; Rehoboam followed the young men and promised a heavier yoke. Israel rejected David's house; Adoram, over the forced labour, was stoned to death; the kingdom divided. Rehoboam's mother was Naamah the Ammonitess (1 Kings 14:21).

**Truths:** The people who carried the burden of Solomon's building described it plainly. Consequences arrived in the next generation.

**2026 use:** Serious reflection on volunteer and staff load, handover, and listening to experienced counsel.

## Solomon's writings and wisdom tradition

- **Proverbs:** "The proverbs of Solomon, the son of David, king of Israel" (Proverbs 1:1). Collections headed "The proverbs of Solomon" (10:1) and "These also are proverbs of Solomon, which the men of Hezekiah king of Judah copied out" (25:1). The book also includes "the words of the wise" (22:17), "sayings of the wise" (24:23), "the words of Agur" (30:1) and "the words of King Lemuel" (31:1). Records should attribute specific proverbs to Solomon only when they fall within the Solomon-headed sections.
- **Song of Songs:** "The Song of songs, which is Solomon's" (Song of Songs 1:1). The phrase can mean by, for, dedicated to or about Solomon, and Solomon appears as a character in the poem (3:7–11; 8:11–12). Authorship is traditional and debated. Its imagery of love within marriage belongs in serious or celebratory wedding contexts, never innuendo.
- **Ecclesiastes:** "The words of the Preacher, the son of David, king in Jerusalem" (Ecclesiastes 1:1). The book never names Solomon. The identification is traditional; many scholars date the book later. Records should say "the Preacher" or state that the link to Solomon is traditional.
- **Psalms 72 and 127:** Both superscriptions read "By Solomon" in the WEB. The Hebrew can also mean "for" or "concerning" Solomon, and Psalm 72 ends "This ends the prayers by David, the son of Jesse" (72:20). Psalm 127:1: "Unless the LORD builds the house, they who build it labor in vain."
- **1 Kings 4:32–33:** three thousand proverbs and 1,005 songs; most are not preserved.
- **Not Scripture in Data Play:** the Wisdom of Solomon and the Odes or Psalms of Solomon are later writings. Do not cite them as Solomon's words.

## Later biblical memory of Solomon

- **Nathan and Ahijah's records:** 2 Chronicles 9:29.
- **Temple items later carried off:** 2 Kings 24:13; 25:16; Jeremiah 52:20.
- **Jesus:** "even Solomon in all his glory was not dressed like one of these" (Matthew 6:29; Luke 12:27); "someone greater than Solomon is here" (Matthew 12:42; Luke 11:31). Jesus walked in "Solomon's porch" (John 10:23), where the early church also gathered (Acts 3:11; 5:12).
- **Stephen:** "But Solomon built him a house. However, the Most High doesn't dwell in temples made with hands" (Acts 7:47–48).
- **Genealogy:** Matthew 1:6–7 names Solomon's mother as "her who had been Uriah's wife."

**Cross-era note:** Solomon's porch (Acts 3:11; 5:12) is where Peter and John preached. Solomon has no memory of it; Peter may mention it in 2026 fiction.
