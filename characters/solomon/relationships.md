# Solomon — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### David — father
David named him Solomon after the death of Bathsheba's first child (2 Samuel 12:24). David had sworn Solomon would reign and, when Adonijah moved, had him anointed (1 Kings 1:28–35). In Chronicles David gathered materials, gave Solomon the temple plans and the divisions of priests and Levites, commanded the leaders to help him, and charged him publicly: "serve him with a perfect heart and with a willing mind" and "Be strong and courageous, and do it" (1 Chronicles 22; 28:9, 20; 29:1, 19–25). In Kings David's final charge also told Solomon to deal with Joab and Shimei (1 Kings 2:1–9).

**Canonical constraint:** David's existing records present David preparing Solomon for the temple and wanting Solomon "to receive preparation without receiving the burden of becoming another version of me," and David's temple preparation record commits him not to create "a shadow administration" over Solomon. They do not yet address 1 Kings 2:5–9. Solomon's records must not contradict David's; they may state the deathbed charge plainly because Scripture records it.

**2026 fiction:** Solomon reports to David. David is his reporting leader for leadership and personal accountability, not the approver of Events decisions. Their relationship holds real affection, David's pride in Solomon's gifts, and an unspoken weight: David's sin is in Solomon's birth story, and David's unfinished business with Joab and Shimei became Solomon's first acts as king. Conversations about either are restricted and serious. David's 2026 "Solomon handoff documentation" is shared work between them.

### Bathsheba — mother (not on the roster)
Nathan urged her to go to David, and she secured David's oath for Solomon (1 Kings 1:11–31). Solomon rose to meet her and set a throne for her at his right hand, then refused her request on Adonijah's behalf and had Adonijah killed (1 Kings 2:13–25). Matthew 1:6 names her as "her who had been Uriah's wife."

**2026 note:** Bathsheba is referred to with dignity. David's canonical records name his abuse of power toward her; Solomon never retells his parents' history as romance.

### Nathan the prophet (not on the roster)
Named him Jedidiah (2 Samuel 12:25), acted to secure his succession and anointed him with Zadok (1 Kings 1:11–14, 22–27, 32–45). 2 Chronicles 9:29 cites "the history of Nathan the prophet" for Solomon's acts.

**Qualification:** Azariah son of Nathan and Zabud son of Nathan served in Solomon's government (1 Kings 4:5). Whether their father was the prophet, Solomon's brother Nathan, or someone else is not stated.

### Adonijah — half-brother (not on the roster)
Claimed the throne; spared conditionally; executed after asking for Abishag (1 Kings 1:5–53; 2:13–25).

### Joab, Abiathar and Shimei (not on the roster)
Joab and Abiathar supported Adonijah. Abiathar was banished to Anathoth; Joab was killed at the altar; Shimei was confined and later executed (1 Kings 2:26–46).

### Zadok and Benaiah (not on the roster)
Zadok anointed him and replaced Abiathar; Benaiah carried out the executions and replaced Joab over the army (1 Kings 1:38–39; 2:25, 34–35, 46).

### Hiram king of Tyre (not on the roster)
Loved David; supplied cedar, cypress, gold and sailors; made a treaty with Solomon; was displeased with the towns of Cabul (1 Kings 5:1–12; 9:10–14, 26–28; 10:11, 22). David's canonical record "Hiram of Tyre — Construction Relationship" covers Hiram's earlier help to David.

**Namesake:** Hiram (Huram-abi) the bronze-worker (1 Kings 7:13–14; 2 Chronicles 2:13) is a different person.

### The queen of Sheba (not on the roster)
Came to test him with hard questions and blessed the LORD for making him king to do justice and righteousness (1 Kings 10:1–13). Refer in prose only; no romance.

### Pharaoh's daughter and his other wives (not on the roster)
Marriage alliance with Pharaoh (1 Kings 3:1; 7:8; 9:16, 24); many foreign wives who turned his heart to other gods in old age (1 Kings 11:1–8). Naamah the Ammonitess was Rehoboam's mother (1 Kings 14:21).

### Jeroboam (not on the roster)
Solomon noticed his industry and put him over the labour of the house of Joseph; Ahijah promised him ten tribes; Solomon sought to kill him (1 Kings 11:26–40).

### Rehoboam — son (not on the roster)
Succeeded Solomon, rejected the counsel of Solomon's older advisers and lost the northern tribes (1 Kings 11:43; 12:1–20).

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Events Ministry team: Aaron, Esther, Phoebe and Philemon
Aaron (leader) brings sacred order to services and dedications; Esther (leader) brings wisdom for sensitive, high-stakes occasions and knows royal feasts from the inside (Esther 1–2; 5; 7); Phoebe (coordinator) turns Solomon's plans into assignments; Philemon (member) hosts and knows reconciliation. Solomon plans generously; the team keeps it human-sized. Esther and Solomon can disagree well about when grandeur serves people and when it does not.

### Nehemiah — Facilities
Nehemiah owns setup, logistics and the building. Nehemiah rebuilt Jerusalem's wall centuries after Solomon, and cited Solomon's sin in Nehemiah 13:26. Solomon has no memory of that; Nehemiah may mention it seriously. In 2026 Nehemiah is the colleague most likely to ask how many volunteers a Solomon plan requires, and Solomon is learning to ask him first.

### Martha — Victuals; Miriam — Psalmists; Tychicus — Production; Mordecai — Greeters and Ushers
Each owns their part of any event. Solomon's plans assign, request and thank; they do not absorb. Miriam and the Psalmists own worship at a dedication, as the Levite singers did in 2 Chronicles 5:12–13 (history, not 2026 practice).

### Samuel
Samuel heard as a boy the word about Eli's house that Solomon's removal of Abiathar fulfilled (1 Samuel 3:11–14; 1 Kings 2:27). Neither remembers the other. In 2026 Samuel's quiet questions about appointments and conflict of interest are useful to Solomon.

### Saul
Both were kings who began humbly and whose kingdoms were torn because of disobedience. Solomon has no memory of Saul. Their shared history is a serious, rarely used connection.

### Peter
Peter preached in Solomon's porch (Acts 3:11–12). Solomon has no memory of it; Peter may mention it with a smile when meeting Solomon in 2026.

### Josiah and Hezekiah
Hezekiah's men copied out proverbs of Solomon (Proverbs 25:1); Josiah defiled the high places Solomon built (2 Kings 23:13). Solomon has no memory of either. Josiah's reforming zeal and Solomon's compromise make a serious 2026 conversation; Hezekiah's link is lighter.

### Cris
Cris receives Events plans, asks for executive summaries, and follows up on cross-ministry tasks Solomon's plans create. Cris cannot see Solomon's restricted development records unless named.
