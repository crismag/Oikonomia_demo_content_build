## Who John is

John son of Zebedee was a Galilean fisherman who worked in his father's boat with his brother James, alongside hired servants, and in partnership with Simon Peter (Mark 1:19–20; Luke 5:10). Jesus called both brothers, and they left their father and followed. Jesus named the two of them Boanerges, "Sons of Thunder" (Mark 3:17).

With Peter and James, John was one of the three Jesus took into Jairus' house, up the mountain of the Transfiguration and further into Gethsemane. The Gospels also record his harder edges plainly: he stopped a man who was casting out demons in Jesus' name "because he doesn't follow with us"; he and James asked whether they should call down fire on a Samaritan village; the two of them asked for the seats at Jesus' right and left in His glory. Jesus corrected each of these.

After the resurrection John appears in Acts as Peter's constant partner: at the temple gate where a lame man was healed, under arrest, before the council ("we can't help telling the things which we saw and heard"), and sent to Samaria, where he prayed for Samaritan believers and preached in many Samaritan villages. The man who once wanted fire on a Samaritan village went back to Samaritan villages with the gospel. Paul names John as one of the "pillars" in Jerusalem (Galatians 2:9).

Church tradition goes further. It identifies John with "the disciple whom Jesus loved" in the Fourth Gospel, and treats him as the author of that Gospel, of the letters of John and of Revelation. Those identifications are traditional or debated rather than stated in Scripture (see the table below). Data Play uses them carefully and never presents them as biblical fact.

In 2026 John is a teaching and pastoral care leader. He leads a Lifegroup, visits with Field Ministry, takes part in Mentorship Night, and is the person Peter and others ask to write the minutes when a meeting matters. The thunder is still there when truth is at stake. It now serves love instead of competing with it.

## 2026 voice

- Unhurried and exact. He gets the wording right and repeats it back to check.
- Plain, short, declarative sentences. Simple words carrying weight: truth, love, witness, light.
- Records what was actually said and done, then says what it means. He separates the two.
- Warm, but not soft. He will name a falsehood directly.
- Notices who is left out of a room, and who is standing at the edge.
- Does not put himself at the centre of his own accounts. His records say "we" and name others before himself.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation, and no mystical fog.

He should not sound like Peter (quick, physical, first to speak), James (energy, action, "who's on it?"), Paul (argument and correspondence), Luke (orderly research and careful case notes) or Matthew (figures and stewardship). John's documentation is about witness: getting what happened right so others can trust it.

## Strengths

- Faithful presence: one of the three at Jairus' house, the Transfiguration and Gethsemane.
- Partnership. He worked alongside Peter for years without competing for the lead voice.
- Courage under official pressure (Acts 4:19–20).
- Visible change: from exclusion and retaliation toward Samaritans to ministry among them.
- Can keep a confidence. He, Peter and James kept silent about the Transfiguration until the right time (Mark 9:9; Luke 9:36).
- Recognised by other leaders as a pillar.
- A teacher who holds truth and love together.

## Development areas

- Zeal that draws the circle too tightly ("he doesn't follow with us," Luke 9:49).
- Zeal that reaches for punishment when people reject Jesus (Luke 9:54–55).
- Ambition for position alongside his brother (Mark 10:35–41).
- In 2026 fiction: documenting so completely that discretion suffers, and treating accuracy as a reason to overrule someone else's concern about who reads a record.

Failure is not his identity. The "beloved disciple" tradition is not a reason to make him sentimental or to erase the thunder.

## Humor

Light material is available where nothing grave is involved:

- minutes that are more complete than anyone wanted, with exact wording;
- gently correcting a colleague's paraphrase in a comment ("That isn't quite what was said");
- declining to put his own name in his own report;
- the "Sons of Thunder" name meeting a calm, careful 2026 minute-taker, when the reader has been told what the nickname was about;
- Peter raising who reached the tomb first, and John declining to claim anything (the text names only "the disciple whom Jesus loved").

Humor must not come from James' death, the crucifixion, Gethsemane, the arrest and threats in Acts, or the Samaritans who would have been harmed. The fire request can be discussed seriously; it is not a joke about Samaritans.

## Sensitivity

- **James' death** (Acts 12:1–2) is John's brother killed by a king. Scripture gives one verse. Never comedy; do not invent details of it or of John's reaction.
- **The cross** (John 19:25–27) is, in the Gospel, witnessed by "the disciple whom Jesus loved". If John's records use it, they say so and keep the identification qualified.
- **The fire request** (Luke 9:51–56) involves a proposal to destroy a village. Treat it as serious correction, not a temperament gag.
- **Revelation** contains judgment and suffering imagery. Handle with care; do not use it for 2026 prediction, spectacle or humor.
- **Documentation versus discretion with Peter** can be affectionate and funny in comments, but the Malchus incident was a real injury that Jesus healed. The injury is not the joke.

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| Son of Zebedee, brother of James, fisherman working with hired servants, partner with Simon | Biblical fact (Mark 1:19–20; Luke 5:10) |
| Named Boanerges, "Sons of Thunder", with James | Biblical fact (Mark 3:17). Why the name was given is not stated; do not invent an explanation. |
| One of the three at Jairus' house, the Transfiguration and Gethsemane | Biblical fact (Mark 5:37; 9:2; 14:33) |
| Mother of the sons of Zebedee made the request for seats | Biblical fact in Matthew 20:20–21; Mark 10:35–37 has James and John ask. Both accounts can be cited for what each records. |
| Their mother was Salome | Inference from comparing Matthew 27:56 with Mark 15:40. Not explicit. Her name is not needed; avoid building on it. |
| Sent with Peter to prepare the Passover | Biblical fact (Luke 22:8) |
| "The disciple whom Jesus loved" is John son of Zebedee | Traditional identification. The Gospel never names that disciple. It is consistent with John 21:2, 7 (the sons of Zebedee are among those present), but not stated. |
| The unnamed disciple with Andrew (John 1:35–40) and "another disciple" known to the high priest (John 18:15–16) are John | Traditional or speculative. Not stated. |
| John wrote the Fourth Gospel | Traditional. John 21:24 says the beloved disciple "wrote these things", and that disciple's identity is itself traditional. Debated by scholars. |
| John wrote 1–3 John | Traditional. 1 John is anonymous; 2 and 3 John are from "the elder". Debated. |
| The John of Revelation is John son of Zebedee | Revelation's author names himself John (Revelation 1:1, 4, 9; 22:8), but which John is debated, and the book does not call him an apostle. |
| Care of Jesus' mother | The Gospel says "the disciple whom he loved" took her to his own home (John 19:26–27). Tied to the same traditional identification. |
| Long life, residence in Ephesus, natural death | Church tradition, not Scripture. John 21:23 corrects a rumour that the beloved disciple would not die. |
| With Peter at the temple, before the council, in Samaria | Biblical fact (Acts 3:1–4:23; 8:14–25) |
| A "pillar" in Jerusalem | Biblical fact (Galatians 2:9) |

### How Data Play uses the traditional identifications

- Foundation files and target files keep the qualification visible.
- In John's own 2026 records, he may draw on the Fourth Gospel, the letters and Revelation as Scripture he teaches from. He does not state as fact that he is the beloved disciple or the author. Where a record depends on the tradition, it says so, e.g. "the disciple whom Jesus loved, traditionally understood to be John."
- Whatever the tradition, John's records never claim the title "the disciple whom Jesus loved" for himself. That restraint fits the Gospel, which never names that disciple.
- `docs/CHARACTER-PLAN.md` contains a Peter/John/Malchus documentation scenario that assumes John wrote the Gospel. When the scenario is authored as a record, it should use this qualified framing: the Malchus account names Peter in John 18:10, in a Gospel traditionally attributed to John.

Scripture quotations in John's files follow the World English Bible unless marked as paraphrase.
