# John — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Jesus
The centre of John's story: calling, naming, correction, nearness at key moments, commission and witness. Jesus is not an ordinary demo account. John refers to Him in teaching, testimony and prayer; Jesus does not receive tasks, comments or reports.

### James son of Zebedee — brother
Called together from their father's boat (Mark 1:19–20). Named Boanerges together (Mark 3:17). Both among the three at Jairus' house, the Transfiguration and Gethsemane (Mark 5:37; 9:2; 14:33). Together they proposed fire on the Samaritan village (Luke 9:54) and asked for the seats of honour (Mark 10:35–41). Both were at the Sea of Tiberias after the resurrection (John 21:2). Herod killed James with the sword (Acts 12:2).

**Silence:** Scripture records no individual conversation between the brothers and nothing of John's response to James' death.

**2026 fiction:** Brothers who still finish each other's plans. James moves first; John writes it down. John is more reflective on paper, James more direct in the room. Neither treats James' death as material for banter. When it surfaces, it is serious and usually restricted.

### Zebedee and their mother
Zebedee was their father, in the boat with hired servants when they were called (Mark 1:20). Their mother asked Jesus for the seats (Matthew 20:20–21) and was among the women watching from a distance at the cross (Matthew 27:56).

**Qualification:** That she is Salome (Mark 15:40) is an inference. Neither parent is on the Data Play roster. Refer to them in prose only and do not invent further details.

### Peter
Fishing partner (Luke 5:10). One of the three with John and James. Sent with John to prepare the Passover (Luke 22:8). At the temple gate, under arrest and before the council together (Acts 3–4). Sent together to Samaria (Acts 8:14). Named with John among the pillars (Galatians 2:9).

**Qualification:** The Gospel scenes pairing Peter with "the disciple whom Jesus loved" (John 13:23–24; 20:2–10; 21:7, 20–22) depend on the traditional identification. Records name "the disciple whom Jesus loved" or state that the identification is traditional.

**2026 fiction:** Peter is John's reporting leader and closest collaborator, consistent with Peter's files. Peter acts and speaks first; John observes and documents carefully. John records more detail than Peter would like; Peter moves before John has finished writing. Their friction is affectionate and useful. The documentation-versus-discretion scenario in `docs/CHARACTER-PLAN.md` (Malchus) is a 2026 disagreement about a record. It must use the qualified framing: John 18:10 names Peter, in a Gospel traditionally attributed to John. Cris mediates; Malchus' injury, which Jesus healed, is not the joke.

### Andrew
Fellow fisherman and brother of John's partner Simon. Jesus went to the house of Simon and Andrew with James and John (Mark 1:29). The four asked Jesus privately on the Mount of Olives (Mark 13:3).

**Qualification:** The unnamed disciple who followed Jesus with Andrew in John 1:35–40 is sometimes identified as John. That is speculation.

**2026 fiction:** Andrew often brings newcomers into John's Lifegroup. John values that Andrew brings people rather than opinions about them.

### Philip, Thomas, Nathanael and the Twelve
Fellow apostles (Mark 3:13–19; Acts 1:13). Thomas and Nathanael were with the sons of Zebedee at the sea (John 21:2).

**Qualification:** Nathanael's identification with Bartholomew is traditional.

### Philip the Evangelist
Philip preached in Samaria; the apostles sent Peter and John to pray for the new believers (Acts 8:5–17).

**Qualification:** This Philip is one of the Seven (Acts 6:5), not Philip the apostle.

**2026 fiction:** Reach-Out follow-up. John follows up Philip the Evangelist's contacts without taking the credit, and does not hide that he once wanted a Samaritan village destroyed.

### Paul and Barnabas
James (the Lord's brother), Cephas and John gave Paul and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship (Galatians 2:9).

**Silence:** Scripture records no other interaction between John and Paul.

**2026 fiction:** Mutual respect. Paul argues in writing; John states things simply. Paul may find John's minutes too short on reasoning; John may find Paul's reports long. Neither is wrong.

### Mary Magdalene
Stood near the cross with Jesus' mother while "the disciple whom he loved" was there (John 19:25–26), and ran to tell Simon Peter and that disciple the tomb was empty (John 20:2).

**Qualification:** Both scenes depend on the traditional identification.

**2026 fiction:** Care and testimony colleagues on Field Ministry.

### Mary, the mother of Jesus (not on the Data Play roster)
Named in the upper room with the eleven (Acts 1:14). Jesus entrusted her to "the disciple whom he loved" (John 19:26–27), traditionally John. Refer to her in prose only, with reverence and the qualification. She is not a demo account.

### James, the Lord's brother (not on the Data Play roster)
A pillar with Cephas and John (Galatians 2:9). **Do not confuse him with John's brother.** Refer to him in prose only.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Elijah
James and John asked whether to call down fire "just as Elijah did" (Luke 9:54; see 2 Kings 1:9–12), and John saw Elijah with Jesus at the Transfiguration. **Elijah has no memory of either.** In 2026 John may tell Elijah, seriously and with some embarrassment, that he once wanted to repeat what Scripture records Elijah doing, and that Jesus rebuked him. Elijah may respond as a colleague hearing it for the first time. This is useful Mentorship material on zeal.

### Moses
John saw Moses with Jesus on the mountain. Same boundary: Moses does not remember it.

### Jonah
Both wanted judgment rather than mercy for people they considered outsiders, and both were corrected (Jonah 4; Luke 9:55). Useful Mentorship pairing about outsiders and anger. Neither claims historical contact.

### Luke
Both care about accurate accounts. Luke researches and orders; John records what he witnessed and states its meaning. Field Ministry colleagues. Luke's files will define his own voice; keep them distinct.

### Cris
Cris asks John to take minutes and relies on his accuracy. Cris mediates when Peter and John disagree about what a record should contain. John's precision does not give Cris access to restricted pastoral records; John names readers carefully.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **John / Peter:** documentation versus discretion (qualified framing), shared Field Ministry visits, Peter's comment about the tomb that John declines to claim.
- **John / James:** "Sons of Thunder" development at Mentorship; James' death handled as a restricted, serious reflection if at all.
- **John / Elijah:** Mentorship conversation about zeal and fire.
- **John / Philip the Evangelist:** Samaritan-style Reach-Out follow-up.
- **John / Andrew:** newcomers brought to John's Lifegroup.
- **John / Cris:** minutes that are more complete than anyone asked for.
