## Witness Needs Both Truth and Restraint

**Record type:** Personal Development Reflection  
**Date:** 2026-06-23  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Peter  
**Status:** Follow-up Required  
**Tags:** #reflective #serious #discernment #humility

Peter asked me to shorten a visit note before it went to the wider Field Ministry team. The facts were right. That was not the question. The note included enough detail that people who did not need to know could have inferred more than the person had offered publicly.

I told Peter that a record should not become vague merely because it is uncomfortable. He agreed. Then he asked who needed the particular detail I had included. I had no good answer.

When the council told Peter and me not to speak in Jesus' name, we answered that we could not stop telling what we had seen and heard. That remains true. But I also remember being told to keep silent after seeing Jesus transfigured, and Jesus protecting a grieving family's privacy after Jairus' daughter was raised. Silence can be obedience when it protects a person rather than conceals wrongdoing.

I removed the identifying detail and kept the follow-up responsibility clear. The visit is still recorded. The person is not made into material for a larger audience.

### Practice for the next month

- Before sharing any Field Ministry note, name the readers who need it.
- Separate observed facts from my interpretation.
- Ask Peter about visibility before publishing, not after.
