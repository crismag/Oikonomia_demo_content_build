# John — Biblical Context Library

This file records John's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are John's memories, correction, partnership and witness.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Accounts that depend on the traditional identification of John with "the disciple whom Jesus loved", or on traditional authorship, are grouped in a separate section and marked.

---

## Part 1 — Accounts that name John

### Called from the boat — Matthew 4:21–22; Mark 1:19–20; Luke 5:1–11

James and John, sons of Zebedee, were in a boat with their father mending nets when Jesus called them. Mark adds that they left Zebedee in the boat "with the hired servants" and went after Jesus. Luke records the great catch that came when Simon let down his nets at Jesus' word: the net was breaking, Simon's partners in the other boat came to help, and both boats began to sink. James and John, "partners with Simon", were amazed with the others. When they brought the boats to land, they left everything and followed Him.

**Fact:** A family fishing business with hired workers; a partnership with Simon. **Silence:** Nothing is recorded about Zebedee's response, John's age or the family's wealth beyond the hired servants. Do not invent them.

**Truths:** Calling came in the middle of ordinary work. Following Jesus meant leaving a family business and a father in the boat.

**2026 use:** Testimony, calling, Lifegroup, James relationship.

### Sons of Thunder; the Twelve — Mark 3:13–19; Luke 6:12–16

After a night in prayer, Jesus chose twelve and named them apostles. Mark says He appointed them "that they might be with him", and to send them out. He gave James and John the name Boanerges, "which means, Sons of Thunder."

**Silence:** Mark does not explain the name. Readers often connect it to the brothers' later zeal (Luke 9:54). That is an inference.

**Truths:** Jesus named the brothers honestly and still chose them.

**2026 use:** Personal development, Mentorship, light administrative humor when the setup is explained.

### The house of Simon and Andrew — Mark 1:29–31

After the synagogue in Capernaum, Jesus went with James and John into the house of Simon and Andrew, where He healed Simon's mother-in-law of a fever.

**2026 use:** Home visits, early friendship among the four fishermen.

### Jairus' daughter — Mark 5:35–43; Luke 8:49–56

Messengers told Jairus, a synagogue ruler, that his twelve-year-old daughter had died. Jesus told him, "Don't be afraid, only believe." He allowed no one to follow Him except Peter, James and John. Mourners laughed at Him. He put them out, took the parents and the three disciples into the room, took the girl by the hand and told her to get up. She rose and walked. Jesus ordered that no one should know about it and told them to give her something to eat.

**Truths:** The three were brought into a grieving family's most private room. They were told to keep it quiet. Jesus cared about the girl's next meal as well as the miracle.

**2026 use:** Field Ministry home visits, confidentiality in pastoral care, grief. Do not reenact a raising in 2026.

### The Transfiguration and keeping silent — Mark 9:2–13; Luke 9:28–36; Matthew 17:1–9

Jesus took Peter, James and John up a high mountain to pray. His face and clothing became dazzling. Moses and Elijah appeared, speaking with Him about His departure at Jerusalem. The disciples had been heavy with sleep. Peter offered to build three tents. A cloud overshadowed them and a voice said, "This is my beloved Son. Listen to him." Matthew records that they fell on their faces, very afraid, and that Jesus came, touched them and said, "Get up, and don't be afraid." Coming down, Jesus told them to tell no one until the Son of Man had risen from the dead. Luke says, "They were silent, and told no one in those days any of the things which they had seen."

**Truths:** Some things are witnessed long before they should be told. Keeping silence at the right time is part of faithful witness.

**2026 use:** Discretion in documentation, confidential records, worship and awe, Goal 2.

**Cross-era note:** John saw Moses and Elijah on the mountain. Moses and Elijah are Data Play characters. Scripture records the event from the disciples' side only. Do not give Moses or Elijah a memory of it.

### "He doesn't follow with us" — Mark 9:38–41; Luke 9:46–50

The disciples had been arguing about which of them was the greatest. Jesus set a little child beside Him and said the least among them would be great. John then said, "Master, we saw someone casting out demons in your name, and we forbade him, because he doesn't follow with us." Jesus answered, "Don't forbid him, for he who is not against us is for us." In Mark He adds that even a cup of water given in His name will not lose its reward.

**Truths:** John's zeal drew the line around his own group. Jesus drew it around His name.

**2026 use:** Cross-ministry cooperation, newcomers serving before they "belong", personal development, Goal 1.

### Fire on a Samaritan village — Luke 9:51–56

As Jesus set out for Jerusalem, He sent messengers ahead into a Samaritan village to prepare for Him. The village did not receive Him, because He was heading for Jerusalem. James and John said, "Lord, do you want us to command fire to come down from the sky and destroy them, just as Elijah did?" Jesus turned and rebuked them. WEB continues: "You don't know of what kind of spirit you are. For the Son of Man didn't come to destroy men's lives, but to save them." They went to another village.

**Textual note:** Some ancient manuscripts do not include "just as Elijah did" or the longer rebuke. Every text records that Jesus rebuked them and they went on. When a record quotes the longer wording, cite it as WEB.

**Background:** The brothers were recalling 2 Kings 1:9–12, where fire came down on soldiers sent to seize Elijah.

**Truths:** Rejection of Jesus did not authorise retaliation. Zeal can quote Scripture and still be the wrong spirit.

**2026 use:** Serious personal development, restraint, Samaritan contrast with Acts 8, Goal 1. Not a joke about Samaritans or about destruction.

**Cross-era note:** Elijah is a Data Play character. John may remember having wanted to imitate what Scripture records of Elijah. Elijah has no memory of Luke 9.

### Seats at the right and left — Mark 10:35–45; Matthew 20:20–28

James and John came to Jesus and said, "Teacher, we want you to do for us whatever we will ask." They asked to sit one at His right hand and one at His left in His glory. In Matthew their mother kneels and makes the request with them. Jesus said, "You don't know what you are asking," and asked whether they could drink His cup. They said, "We are able." He told them they would drink His cup, but the seats were not His to give. The other ten were indignant. Jesus called them all together: rulers of the nations lord it over people, "But it shall not be so among you, but whoever wants to become great among you shall be your servant," for the Son of Man came "not to be served but to serve, and to give his life as a ransom for many."

**Truths:** Ambition for position divided the team. Jesus answered with service and His own death, not with a better seating plan.

**2026 use:** Servant leadership, team conflict, Mentorship. Primarily James' goal material (see `characters/james-son-of-zebedee/`); John may reflect from his own perspective without duplicating it.

### Questions on the Mount of Olives — Mark 13:1–5

When Jesus said the temple's great buildings would be thrown down, Peter, James, John and Andrew asked Him privately when this would happen and what the sign would be. He began, "Be careful that no one leads you astray."

**2026 use:** Teaching, discernment, caution about speculation.

### Preparing the Passover — Luke 22:7–13

Jesus sent Peter and John to prepare the Passover. They asked where. He told them to follow a man carrying a pitcher of water to a house with a large furnished upper room. They found everything as He had said and prepared the meal.

**2026 use:** Practical preparation with Peter. Events and Victuals own the operational work in 2026.

### Gethsemane — Mark 14:32–42; Matthew 26:36–46

Jesus took Peter, James and John further into the garden, told them His soul was exceedingly sorrowful, and asked them to stay and watch. Three times He came back and found them sleeping. Mark says "they didn't know what to answer him." When Judas arrived, all the disciples left Him and fled (Mark 14:50).

**Truths:** The three closest to Jesus could not stay awake in His hour of distress. John was among those who fled.

**2026 use:** Prayer, presence with suffering people, humility. Serious.

### The upper room after the ascension — Acts 1:12–14

John is named among the eleven who devoted themselves to prayer with the women, Mary the mother of Jesus and Jesus' brothers.

### The temple gate, arrest and "we can't help telling" — Acts 3:1–4:31

Peter and John went up to the temple at the hour of prayer. A man lame from birth asked them for money. Peter, "fastening his eyes on him, with John," said, "Look at us," and healed him in the name of Jesus. The man held on to Peter and John as a crowd gathered. The temple authorities arrested both of them. The council saw "the boldness of Peter and John", perceived they were "unlearned and ignorant men" (without formal training), and "recognized that they had been with Jesus." Ordered not to speak or teach in Jesus' name, Peter and John answered, "Whether it is right in the sight of God to listen to you rather than to God, judge for yourselves, for we can't help telling the things which we saw and heard." Released, they reported to the believers, who prayed for boldness.

**Note:** Peter is the recorded speaker for the sermon and the defence; John is named with him throughout and in the joint answer of Acts 4:19–20.

**Truths:** Witness is telling what was seen and heard, even under threat. John's partnership did not require him to be the main voice.

**2026 use:** Testimony, courage, Peter partnership, Goal 2.

### Samaria: back to Samaritan villages — Acts 8:14–25

When the apostles in Jerusalem heard that Samaria had received the word of God through Philip the Evangelist, they sent Peter and John. The two prayed for the Samaritan believers and laid hands on them, and they received the Holy Spirit. Simon, a former sorcerer, offered money for that power and Peter rebuked him. Peter and John testified, then returned to Jerusalem, preaching "the Good News to many villages of the Samaritans."

**Interpretation:** Scripture does not comment on the contrast with Luke 9:54. Readers commonly note it. It is a fair reflection for John in Data Play, provided records do not invent what he felt.

**Truths:** The disciple who asked to burn a Samaritan village later prayed for Samaritans and preached in their villages.

**2026 use:** Testimony of change, cross-cultural ministry, Reach-Out follow-up with Philip the Evangelist, Goal 1.

### James killed by Herod — Acts 12:1–2

King Herod "killed James, the brother of John, with the sword." Peter was arrested next.

**Silence:** Scripture records nothing of John's response or of the circumstances beyond this verse. Do not invent them.

**2026 use:** Rarely; serious only; restricted visibility when personal. James is alive in 2026 Data Play fiction, which makes this memory delicate. See James' profile.

### A pillar — Galatians 2:9

Paul writes that James, Cephas and John, "those who were reputed to be pillars," gave Barnabas and Paul the right hand of fellowship, agreeing that Paul and Barnabas would go to the Gentiles, and asked them only to remember the poor.

**Qualification:** This James is not James son of Zebedee. By this time Herod had killed him (Acts 12:2). He is generally identified as James the Lord's brother (Galatians 1:19), a reasonable inference.

**2026 use:** Recognition of leadership with Paul and Barnabas; shared authority; "remember the poor" belongs operationally to More Than Enough.

## Part 2 — Accounts that depend on the traditional identification

The Fourth Gospel never names "the disciple whom Jesus loved." Church tradition identifies him with John son of Zebedee. The following accounts are available to John in Data Play only with that qualification visible. Records should say "the disciple whom Jesus loved" or state that the identification is traditional.

### Leaning on Jesus at the supper — John 13:21–26

When Jesus said one of them would betray Him, "one of his disciples, whom Jesus loved, was at the table, leaning against Jesus' chest." Simon Peter signalled to him to ask who it was. He asked, "Lord, who is it?" Jesus said it was the one to whom He would give the dipped bread, and gave it to Judas.

**2026 use:** Nearness to Jesus; Peter/John collaboration. Qualify.

### At the cross; "Behold, your mother" — John 19:25–27

Near the cross stood Jesus' mother, her sister, Mary the wife of Clopas and Mary Magdalene. Jesus saw His mother and "the disciple whom he loved standing there." He said to His mother, "Woman, behold, your son!" and to the disciple, "Behold, your mother!" From that hour the disciple took her into his own home.

**Truths:** At the point of His death, Jesus arranged care for His mother. Care was entrusted, and received into a home.

**2026 use:** Long-term care, Field Ministry home care, serious testimony. Qualify. The crucifixion is never comedy.

### "He who has seen has testified" — John 19:33–35

After a soldier pierced Jesus' side, the Gospel says, "He who has seen has testified, and his testimony is true. He knows that he tells the truth, that you may believe."

**2026 use:** Witness and truthful documentation. Qualify authorship.

### Running to the tomb — John 20:1–10

Mary Magdalene ran to Simon Peter and "the other disciple whom Jesus loved" and told them the Lord had been taken from the tomb. They ran together. The other disciple outran Peter and reached the tomb first, stooped and saw the linen cloths, but did not go in. Peter arrived and went straight in, and saw the linen cloths and the head cloth rolled up by itself. Then the other disciple went in, "and he saw and believed."

**2026 use:** Easter reflection, Peter comment threads. John's records do not claim he was the one who arrived first; Peter may raise it, and John may decline to.

### "It's the Lord!" and "what about this man?" — John 21:1–25

Simon Peter, Thomas, Nathanael of Cana, the sons of Zebedee and two other disciples went fishing and caught nothing. At dawn Jesus told them to cast on the right side, and the net filled. "That disciple therefore whom Jesus loved said to Peter, 'It's the Lord!'" After Peter's restoration, Peter saw the beloved disciple following and asked, "Lord, what about this man?" Jesus answered, "If I desire that he stay until I come, what is that to you? You follow me." The Gospel corrects the rumour that followed: Jesus did not say that disciple would not die. It adds, "This is the disciple who testifies about these things, and wrote these things."

**Fact:** The sons of Zebedee were present on this occasion (John 21:2). **Tradition:** That the beloved disciple is John.

**2026 use:** Peter/John comparison from John's side: John did not ask for it. Correcting a rumour accurately. Qualify.

### Written that you may believe — John 20:30–31; 21:25

The Gospel says Jesus did many other signs "which are not written in this book; but these are written that you may believe that Jesus is the Christ, the Son of God, and that believing you may have life in his name." It closes by saying the world could not hold the books if everything were written.

**Truths:** Faithful writing selects. The purpose of the record governs what goes in it.

**2026 use:** Documentation principles, Goal 2. Qualify authorship.

## Part 3 — Letters and Revelation (authorship traditional or debated)

### Seen, heard, touched; walking in the light — 1 John 1:1–7

1 John opens with what "we have heard", "seen with our eyes" and "our hands touched, concerning the Word of life," declared so that readers may have fellowship "with us" and with the Father and the Son. "God is light, and in him is no darkness at all." Claiming fellowship with God while walking in darkness is a lie.

**Authorship:** 1 John is anonymous. Traditionally attributed to John.

**2026 use:** Teaching, Lifegroup, witness and honesty.

### Love in deed and truth — 1 John 3:11–18; 4:7–12, 18–21

The letter teaches that "we should love one another." "By this we know love, because he laid down his life for us. And we ought to lay down our lives for the brothers." Someone who has goods, sees a brother in need and closes his heart has a question to answer about God's love. "My little children, let's not love in word only, or with the tongue only, but in deed and truth." "God is love." "There is no fear in love; but perfect love casts out fear." Whoever says he loves God and hates his brother is a liar.

**2026 use:** Lifegroup teaching, practical care, Goal 3. Practical giving belongs operationally to More Than Enough.

### The elder: truth, hospitality and Diotrephes — 2 John 1–12; 3 John 1–14

Both letters come from "the elder." 2 John urges the "chosen lady and her children" to love one another and warns against receiving deceivers who deny that Jesus Christ came in the flesh. 3 John commends Gaius for faithful hospitality to travelling brothers, "I have no greater joy than this: to hear about my children walking in truth," and names Diotrephes, "who loves to be first among them," who refused the elder's letter, spread wicked words, refused hospitality to the brothers and threw out those who offered it. It commends Demetrius. Both letters end with the writer preferring to speak "face to face" rather than write more with ink.

**Authorship:** "The elder" is traditionally identified as John. Debated.

**Truths:** Hospitality is faithful work. A leader who loves to be first harms the church. Some things are better said in person than written.

**2026 use:** Greeters and Ushers hospitality (owned by that ministry), leadership accountability, documentation discretion (Goal 2), Mentorship.

### John on Patmos — Revelation 1:9–20; 19:9–10; 22:8–9

The writer calls himself "I John, your brother and partner with you in the oppression, Kingdom, and perseverance in Christ Jesus," on the island of Patmos "because of God's Word and the testimony of Jesus Christ." A loud voice told him to write what he saw and send it to seven assemblies in Asia. He saw one like a son of man and fell at His feet as though dead; He laid His right hand on him and said, "Don't be afraid. I am the first and the last." Twice later the writer fell down to worship the angel who showed him the visions, and was told, "Worship God."

**Authorship:** The writer names himself John. Whether he is John son of Zebedee is debated. Revelation does not call him an apostle.

**Truths:** Faithful witness can mean suffering. Even a seasoned leader can misdirect worship and need correcting.

**2026 use:** Serious teaching on endurance and worship. No 2026 prophecy, prediction or spectacle; no humor about judgment imagery.
