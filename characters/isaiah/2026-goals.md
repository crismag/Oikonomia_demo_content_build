# Isaiah — 2026 Goals

**Oikonomia section:** Goals

These are Isaiah's 2026 Oikonomia goals. They come from his lived biblical history and are meant to gather progress through his Wednesday Lifegroup, Sunday teaching, Mentorship Night and counsel to leaders. Progress entries are added as records are written; none are recorded yet.

---

## Goal 1 — Begin with "woe is me" before "here I am"

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #devotional #humility #awe

### Why this matters

In the year King Uzziah died I saw the Lord on a throne, and the seraphim calling, "Holy, holy, holy." The first thing I noticed about myself was my mouth: "I am a man of unclean lips." My words, the tool I was about to be sent with, were the problem. A coal from the altar touched my lips before I heard, "Whom shall I send?" Only then did I say, "Here I am. Send me!" And the assignment I accepted was to speak to people who would mostly not listen.

I am good with words. That is exactly why my teaching has to start with confession and worship, not with a clever outline, and why I cannot measure faithfulness by applause.

### 2026 actions

- Prepare every Sunday teaching and Lifegroup study with time in prayer and confession before writing.
- At Mentorship Night, help emerging teachers prepare from worship first and technique second.
- Keep teaching faithfully when a series draws little response; review with Samuel at mid-year rather than changing the message to suit the room.
- Ask a trusted leader to tell me when my words are more impressive than they are clear.

**Biblical account:** Isaiah 6:1–13.

## Goal 2 — Counsel calm trust when leaders are afraid

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #pastoral #faith #peace

### Why this matters

When Syria and Israel came against Jerusalem, King Ahaz's heart shook like trees in the wind. God sent me to him with one message: "Be careful, and keep calm. Don't be afraid." He refused to ask for a sign and sent treasure to Assyria instead. Years later, when Assyria's official shouted threats at Jerusalem's wall, Hezekiah sent to me for prayer, and the word was again, "Don't be afraid." Hezekiah took the threatening letter, spread it before the LORD and prayed, and God answered because he prayed. Later still, after Hezekiah showed the Babylonian envoys everything he owned, I had to ask him, "What have they seen in your house?"

Counsel has three parts: calm in the crisis, prayer before the decision, and honest questions after the relief.

### 2026 actions

- Be available to leaders facing a crisis decision within a day, and pray with them before advising.
- Help leaders tell the difference between prudent action and panicked alliances.
- After a major success or recovery in a ministry, schedule a restricted follow-up conversation that asks what has been exposed or overlooked.
- Give counsel without trying to control the decision; that belongs to the leader and, where needed, to Samuel and the Leadership Council.

**Biblical account:** Isaiah 7:1–12; Isaiah 37:1–7, 14–21; Isaiah 39:1–8.

## Goal 3 — Keep worship and justice in the same sentence

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #serious #conviction #compassion

### Why this matters

Early in the book that carries my name, God refuses crowded festivals and many prayers from people whose hands were full of blood, and then says, "Learn to do well. Seek justice. Relieve the oppressed. Defend the fatherless. Plead for the widow." Later in the book, God says the fast He chooses is to break yokes, to share bread with the hungry, to bring the homeless poor into the house and to clothe the naked.

A church can grow in worship and prayer while quietly ignoring the oppressed among its own neighbours. I teach this so it becomes practice, not so it becomes a quotation.

### 2026 actions

- Teach a Lifegroup series on Isaiah 1 and 58 that ends with the group serving alongside More Than Enough, which leads the work.
- On church prayer and fasting days, include practical care for someone in need as part of the fast.
- In counsel, ask leaders how a decision affects the vulnerable people it touches.
- Avoid using these passages as slogans; explain their context each time.

**Biblical account:** Isaiah 1:10–18; Isaiah 58:1–12.
