# Isaiah — Notable Topics

Use these as source topics for later Oikonomia records; they are not prompts and do not imply exclusive ownership. Fuller retellings, truths and fact/inference notes are in `biblical-context.md`. Keep Isaiah's **life events** (memory) distinct from the **book's teaching** (what he teaches in 2026).

## Isaiah's life (memory)
- **Temple vision and call** — Isaiah 6. Holiness, confession ("a man of unclean lips"), cleansing, willingness to be sent; a hard assignment with no promise of response; the holy seed in the stump.
- **Ahaz and the "keep calm" counsel** — Isaiah 7:1–12; 2 Kings 16:7–9. Counsel during geopolitical fear; Ahaz's pious-sounding refusal to ask a sign; trust versus alliances.
- **Immanuel sign context** — Isaiah 7:13–25; 8:8; Matthew 1:22–23. Sign given to a leader who refused to ask; translation and fulfilment discussed carefully; no memory of Jesus' birth.
- **Children as signs; the witnessed tablet** — Isaiah 7:3; 8:1–4, 16–18. Shear-jashub and Maher-shalal-hash-baz; "the prophetess"; family life joined to message; sealing the teaching among disciples; waiting when God seems hidden.
- **Walking naked and barefoot for three years** — Isaiah 20. Sign against trusting Egypt and Ethiopia; captives' shame; cost of obedience. Restraint; no nudity or dress-code humor.
- **Shebna and Eliakim** — Isaiah 22:15–25. A steward carving his own tomb; office entrusted and removed; "the key of David's house"; even a secure peg gives way.
- **Hezekiah / Sennacherib crisis** — Isaiah 36–37; 2 Kings 18–19; 2 Chronicles 32:20. Counsel, prayer, hostile communications; "Don't be afraid of the words that you have heard"; God answers because Hezekiah prayed; prophet and king pray together.
- **Hezekiah's illness** — Isaiah 38; 2 Kings 20:1–11. Pastoral/prophetic support and testimony; honest terminal word, then a new word; figs and a sign.
- **Babylonian envoys** — Isaiah 39; 2 Chronicles 32:25–31. Long-term consequence warning after disclosure; "What have they seen in your house?"; pride after recovery (Chronicles); "peace and truth in my days."
- **Recorder of kings' acts** — 2 Chronicles 26:22; 32:32. Isaiah as a writer of history (those writings are lost).

## The book's teaching (for 2026 teaching, not memory)
- **Justice, fasting and worship** — Isaiah 1:10–18; 58:1–12. Religious activity joined to justice and mercy; "Seek justice … Plead for the widow"; the fast God chooses.
- **Vineyard song** — Isaiah 5:1–7. Looking for justice and finding oppression.
- **Assyria warnings and hope** — Isaiah 8–12. Crisis teaching and future hope; the great light; the child with the government on his shoulders; the shoot from Jesse.
- **Messages concerning nations** — Isaiah 13–23. International perspective and accountability.
- **Trust in quietness, not Egypt** — Isaiah 30:1–17; 31:1–3. "Tell us pleasant things"; "in quietness and in confidence"; "You refused."
- **Comfort and restoration** — Isaiah 40 onward. Hope, endurance, God's greatness; "Comfort, comfort my people"; the word that stands forever; renewed strength for those who wait. Authorship of 40–66 debated; not Isaiah's memory.
- **Servant passages** — Isaiah 42; 49; 50; 52–53. Messianic/theological material must be handled carefully and not casually assigned as Isaiah's own self-description. New Testament applies them to Jesus (Acts 8:32–35; Matthew 8:17).
- **Good news to the humble** — Isaiah 61:1–3; Luke 4:16–21. Mission and care; no memory of Nazareth.

## Later biblical references (Isaiah has no memory of these)
- **"He saw his glory"** — John 12:37–41.
- **Voice in the wilderness** — Matthew 3:3; John 1:23.
- **The Ethiopian reading Isaiah** — Acts 8:26–35.
- **Paul quoting Isaiah** — Acts 28:25–27; Romans 9:27–29; 10:16, 20; 15:12.
- **Death by sawing** — tradition only; Hebrews 11:37 names no one.

## Strong Oikonomia surfaces
- **Teaching:** series and notes on holiness, justice, comfort and hope; seasonal (Advent, Good Friday) teaching with careful explanation.
- **Lifegroup Gathering reports:** a Wednesday group reading Scripture closely, prayer, members' fears and hopes.
- **Leadership counsel:** crisis counsel to Hezekiah and others; restricted "after the win" follow-ups (envoys pattern); stewards and self-promotion (Shebna pattern).
- **Prayer:** praying with leaders under hostile pressure.
- **Mentorship:** preparing emerging teachers from worship and confession.
- **Fasting reflection:** Isaiah 58 connected to More Than Enough (operations stay with MTE).
- **Comments:** Cris asking for action items in a poetic report; Ezra approving a properly witnessed tablet; Peter wanting to act while Isaiah says "keep calm."
- Preserve prophetic context and avoid turning prophecy into generic motivational quotes.
