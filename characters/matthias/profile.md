## Who Matthias is

Matthias appears by name in Scripture only in Acts 1:23 and 1:26. Everything Scripture says about him comes from that one account.

After Jesus ascended, the apostles returned to Jerusalem and devoted themselves to prayer with the women, Mary the mother of Jesus and Jesus' brothers (Acts 1:12–14). Peter stood up among about 120 believers and spoke about Judas, who had guided those who arrested Jesus, and whose death Acts describes (1:15–20). Peter said that one of the men who had accompanied them "all the time that the Lord Jesus went in and out among us, beginning from the baptism of John to the day that he was received up from us" must become "a witness with us of his resurrection" (1:21–22).

They put forward two: Joseph called Barsabbas, also called Justus, and Matthias. They prayed, "You, Lord, who know the hearts of all men, show which one of these two you have chosen to take part in this ministry and apostleship from which Judas fell away ..." They drew lots, "and the lot fell on Matthias; and he was counted with the eleven apostles" (1:23–26).

That is all. Matthias is never named again. Scripture records nothing of his words, his background, his family, his ministry afterward or his death.

What the account does establish is significant. Matthias was put forward as someone who met the stated requirement: he had been with Jesus' followers from John's baptism to the ascension, and was a witness of the resurrection. Yet none of the Gospels names him. He was present through the whole story without appearing in it, and he stepped into responsibility after a painful vacancy caused by betrayal.

In 2026 he is a supporting leader who takes part in Mentorship Night. He is steady, quiet and faithful over long periods, and knows what it is to be appointed rather than to have been prominent. He is a colleague, not a demo persona, and he should remain lightly drawn.

## 2026 voice

- Quiet and economical. He says little and does not fill silence.
- Speaks as a witness rather than an authority: what he saw, plainly.
- Comfortable not being the name people remember.
- Takes appointment seriously and without self-importance.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Peter (quick, first to speak), Barnabas (warm encouragement), Joshua (execution and command) or Thomas (questions and evidence). Matthias is the unnoticed long-term witness, not an inspirational underdog.

## Strengths (from the account)

- Long faithfulness: among Jesus' followers from John's baptism to the ascension (implied by Acts 1:21–23).
- A witness of the resurrection (Acts 1:22).
- Considered qualified by the community (Acts 1:23).
- Entered a role through prayer and a shared process rather than self-promotion (Acts 1:24–26).

## Development areas

Scripture records none. Do not invent failings or insecurities. Any 2026 development is Data Play fiction and should stay modest, for example learning to share his testimony when invited, since Scripture records the requirement that he be a witness but none of his words.

## Relationships

Scripture gives too little for a separate relationships file. Biblical relationships first, then 2026 fiction.

- **Jesus** — Matthias accompanied His followers from John's baptism to the ascension and was a witness of His resurrection. Not an ordinary demo account.
- **Peter** — led the gathering and set out the requirement before the believers put forward two candidates, prayed and drew lots (Acts 1:15–26). This matches Peter's files. **2026 fiction:** Matthias reports to Peter, and Peter "takes an interest in how Matthias is supported in a role he entered by appointment rather than by long visibility" (Peter's `relationships.md`).
- **The eleven** — Peter, John, James, Andrew, Philip, Thomas, Bartholomew, Matthew, James son of Alphaeus, Simon the Zealot and Judas son of James (Acts 1:13), with whom he was counted (1:26). All eleven are Data Play characters. Use roster names, and keep the traditional identifications qualified: Bartholomew with Nathanael, and Judas son of James with Thaddaeus. **Inference:** Matthias was presumably among "the twelve" who called the community together in Acts 6:2 and among "the eleven" who stood with Peter at Pentecost (Acts 2:14); he is not named there.
- **Joseph Barsabbas (Justus)** — the other candidate (Acts 1:23). Not on the roster; prose only. Scripture records nothing about his response or later life. Do not write rivalry or resentment between them. Do not identify him with Judas Barsabbas of Acts 15:22 without qualification.
- **Judas Iscariot** — the vacancy Matthias filled came from Judas' betrayal and death (Acts 1:16–20, 25). Serious only. Matthias is not "the replacement hire" in any joke.
- **Barnabas (Mentorship head), 2026 fiction** — Matthias takes part in Mentorship Night under Barnabas' leadership.
- **Stephen, 2026 fiction** — both entered responsibility through a community's selection and prayer (Acts 1:23–26; 6:3–6). A possible quiet Mentorship conversation; never touching Stephen's death.
- **Joshua and Saul, cross-era 2026 fiction** — Joshua was prepared over a long time before succeeding Moses; Saul was chosen by lot when Samuel brought the tribes near (1 Samuel 10:20–21). Neither has any memory of Matthias. Mentorship conversations may compare kinds of appointment. Matthias must not be written as a parallel to Saul's later failures.

## 2026 ministry connections and rhythm

Matthias' 2026 presence is light, and there is no separate rhythm file.

- **Mentorship (member):** Tuesday Mentorship Night, especially conversations about succession, appointment after a painful vacancy, and serving without prior visibility.
- **Sunday:** present at main services as a colleague.
- **Records:** occasional attendance in Mentorship meeting notes, an occasional comment, and his single goal. No weekly reports.

## Humor

Very little, and gentle:

- Matthias is often left off lists of "the Twelve" made from the Gospel lists, which were written before he was added. A colleague's roster or name list may omit him, and someone quietly corrects it.

Humor must not come from Judas' betrayal or death, from the lot as a "hiring method", or from Matthias being a replacement.

## Sensitivity

- **Judas' betrayal and death** (Acts 1:16–20) are grave. Acts' description of his death is not to be repeated for effect.
- **Drawing lots** was a prayerful, shared act (Acts 1:24–26). Do not portray it as random or as a mistake. Some interpreters argue the apostles should have waited, for example for Paul; Scripture does not say so, and Data Play does not adopt that view.
