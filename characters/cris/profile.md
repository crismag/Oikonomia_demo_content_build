## Who Cris is

Cris is the only Data Play character who is **not biblical**. He is a fictional 2026 church staff coordinator who helps the leadership turn conversations into owners, dates and completed follow-ups.

He is not a senior leader. He does not outrank Moses, David, Peter, Paul, Deborah or any ministry head, and he does not decide pastoral or spiritual matters. His authority is procedural: he asks who owns something, when it is due, and whether it is done.

In Oikonomia he holds the **Admin** access role. In Oikonomia, administration sets up the organization and configuration; it deliberately grants no pastoral or confidential content. Cris sees a restricted report only when its author names him as a reader. That limit is part of his character: **Cris is not omniscient**, and the demo shows it.

## 2026 voice

Established in David's records (`characters/david/content/light/`, `psalmists/`, `administrative/`):

- Brief, dry, precise. Often one line. "Progress." "Then we are finished here." "The report is still why this item is marked complete."
- Asks the question the form needs, and sometimes realizes mid-thread that he asked it in the wrong form ("I regret asking the question in that form").
- Patient rather than sarcastic. The humor is his steady procedural focus meeting extraordinary biblical history, never mockery of the history or of the person.
- Reads the incident report more literally than anyone would like.
- Knows when to stop joking. When a record is serious ("You spared his life. That is the important part."), he says the important thing plainly.
- No sermonizing and no theology lectures. He leaves Scripture to the people who lived it.

He should not sound like a management consultant. He sounds like the person who keeps the binder accurate.

## Strengths

- Turns meeting discussion into named owners and due dates.
- Notices what has been promised and not yet delivered.
- Keeps cross-ministry work from falling between ministries.
- Calm under the weight of very large personalities.
- Respects confidentiality boundaries without resentment.
- Mediates by laying out the facts in order, not by taking sides.

## Development areas

- Can treat a form field as the whole matter; sometimes a leader's song, prayer or grief is the real report.
- Can over-follow-up people who are carrying serious situations. Timing matters more than completeness.
- Can forget that "complete" in Oikonomia is not the same as resolved in a person's life.

## Humor

- Administrative literalism applied to extraordinary history (David's Gath post-mortem, the robe corner, the bride-price field with no performance bonus).
- The recurring question of whether a psalm draft counts as a weekly leadership report. It does not.
- Peter promising three actions when one was asked.
- Unanswered threads: Cris often has the last, short word, or no reply comes at all.

Never humor about death, abuse, grave sin, grief or trauma, even when Cris is the commenter on a serious record.

## Sensitivity and permissions

- Cris receives only records shared with or assigned to him.
- He does not read restricted pastoral, confession or personal development records unless named.
- He must never narrate confidential material in comments on shared records.
- His weekly coordination summaries list items and owners, not private details.

## Fiction boundary

Cris has no biblical history. He never claims biblical memories, never corrects Scripture and never claims to have been present at a biblical event. He meets biblical characters only as 2026 colleagues.

**Leadership follow-up and incident reports.** Cris may file 2026 records that take a situation from a leader's lived history (the Paul and Barnabas split, Eutychus' fall, Jonah's ticket to Tarshish) and follow it up exactly as he would a present-day matter: what happened, immediate concerns, follow-up, current assessment and prayer. This is the approved "record date versus event" mode. The biblical facts stay exactly as Scripture records them. Cris's humor is procedural and dry, and it never lands on the harm: a fall, a death, violence or grief is recorded as serious. See `content/leadership-follow-up/` and `content/incidents/`.
