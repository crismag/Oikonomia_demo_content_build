## Coverage Concern — The Lost Sheep

**Record type:** Resource Allocation / Administrative Concern  
**Date:** 2026-02-02  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Reviewed  
**Tags:** #administrative #funny #grace #joy #hope

I have a coverage concern about a shepherd in one of Jesus' parables.

The context matters, so I'll start there.

Tax collectors and sinners were coming to hear Jesus. The Pharisees and scribes were complaining: "This man welcomes sinners, and eats with them."

Jesus answered with a parable.

A man has one hundred sheep.

He loses one.

He leaves the ninety-nine in the wilderness and goes after the one that was lost, until he finds it.

### Coverage Analysis

- Sheep present: ninety-nine.
- Sheep missing: one.
- Shepherds assigned to the ninety-nine: none.
- Shepherds assigned to the one: all of them.

From a coverage standpoint, this leaves ninety-nine percent of the flock unsupervised in order to recover one percent.

I was preparing to recommend a second shepherd.

Then I read the rest of the parable.

### What Happened Next

When he finds the sheep, he doesn't scold it or file a report about how it wandered off.

He puts it on his shoulders, rejoicing.

When he gets home, he calls his friends and neighbours together and says, "Rejoice with me, for I have found my sheep which was lost!"

Jesus said: "There will be more joy in heaven over one sinner who repents, than over ninety-nine righteous people who need no repentance."

### Current Assessment

This is not a staffing ratio. It is a picture of how much one lost person matters to God.

It was also Jesus' answer to religious leaders who were annoyed that He spent His time with the wrong people. They were counting the ninety-nine. He was looking for the one.

I am withdrawing my coverage concern.

I am keeping one follow-up item: look for the person who isn't on this week's attendance list, and go after them.

### Prayer

Hearts that rejoice when the lost are found, instead of complaining about who's at the table.

Shepherds willing to leave comfortable places to go looking.

And for me, the grace to celebrate a found sheep before I ask how it got out.

**Biblical account:** Luke 15:1–7.

## Donation Recording Concern — The Widow's Two Coins

**Record type:** Giving / Administrative Concern  
**Date:** 2026-03-16  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Stephen  
**Status:** Reviewed  
**Tags:** #administrative #humility #generosity #gratitude

I have a problem with how we record donations.

This one is not a parable. It happened.

Jesus sat down opposite the temple treasury and watched people put in their offerings.

Many rich people put in large amounts.

Then a poor widow came and put in two small copper coins. Together they were worth a fraction of a penny.

Jesus called His disciples over and said, "This poor widow gave more than all those who are giving into the treasury."

### Recording Issue

- By amount, her gift was the smallest in the treasury.
- By Jesus' measure, it was the largest.
- Our donation report only has an amount column.

### Jesus' Explanation

"They all gave out of their abundance, but she, out of her poverty, gave all that she had to live on."

The rich gave what they could spare.

She gave everything.

### Follow-up

- Do not publish a "top donors" list. Ever.
- Confirmed with Stephen that More Than Enough thanks people by name, not by amount.
- Remember that the largest gifts in God's eyes may never appear large on our reports.

### Current Assessment

Our system measures how much was given.

Jesus measured how much was left.

He noticed a gift that nobody else would have noticed, from a person nobody else was watching. That should change how we look at every offering, and every quiet, unseen act of faith in our church.

### Prayer

Thank God that He sees what our reports miss.

Generous hearts that trust Him with everything, not only with what's left over.

And for me, the humility to remember that the most important column is the one I can't see.

**Biblical account:** Mark 12:41–44.

### Comments

**Stephen:** We thank by name, not by size.

## Incident Response Concern — The Good Samaritan

**Record type:** Incident Response / Leadership Reflection  
**Date:** 2026-04-13  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Reviewed  
**Tags:** #serious #conviction #compassion #mercy

I need to review the incident response in one of Jesus' parables. I have also found a problem with my own review.

A lawyer asked Jesus what he had to do to inherit eternal life. Jesus asked him what the law said. The lawyer answered correctly: love God with all your heart, soul, strength and mind, and love your neighbour as yourself.

Jesus said, "Do this, and you will live."

The lawyer, wanting to justify himself, asked: "Who is my neighbor?"

Jesus told a story.

A man was travelling from Jerusalem to Jericho. Robbers attacked him, stripped him, beat him and left him half dead.

A priest came down the same road. When he saw the man, he passed by on the other side.

A Levite came to the same place. He saw the man, and passed by on the other side.

Then a Samaritan came along, someone Jesus' listeners would not have expected to be the hero. When he saw the man, he was moved with compassion.

### Response Taken

- Went to him.
- Bandaged his wounds, pouring on oil and wine.
- Put him on his own animal.
- Brought him to an inn and took care of him.
- The next day, gave the innkeeper two denarii and said, "Take care of him. Whatever you spend beyond that, I will repay you when I return."

### Administrative Concern

My first note on this file was that the Samaritan authorised an open-ended expense with no cap and no approval.

### Current Assessment

I noticed the expense policy before I noticed that the two religious leaders didn't stop.

That is a finding about me.

Jesus asked the lawyer which of the three had been a neighbour to the man. The lawyer said, "He who showed mercy on him."

Jesus said, "Go and do likewise."

The lawyer wanted to know who he was required to love. Jesus answered by showing what love does. It stops. It gets involved. It pays the cost, and it comes back.

Our response procedures should never make it easier to pass by than to stop.

### Prayer

Compassion that moves us to act, and not only to notice.

Forgiveness for the times I have been more careful with the budget than with the person on the road.

And courage to be a neighbour to whoever God puts in front of us.

**Attention:** Response procedures should never make passing by easier than stopping.  
**Attention to:** Cris  
**Biblical account:** Luke 10:25–37.

## Event Budget Concern — The Prodigal Son

**Record type:** Event Budget / Administrative Concern  
**Date:** 2026-05-11  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Reviewed  
**Tags:** #administrative #funny #grace #joy #restoration

I have a budget concern about a welcome-home party in one of Jesus' parables.

A man had two sons.

The younger son asked for his share of the inheritance early. His father divided the property.

The younger son travelled to a distant country and wasted everything on reckless living.

Then a famine came. He took a job feeding pigs, and was so hungry he wanted to eat the pigs' food.

He came to his senses. He decided to go home, confess that he had sinned against heaven and against his father, and ask to be taken on as a hired servant.

### Event Details

While the son was still a long way off, his father saw him.

The father was moved with compassion.

He ran.

He embraced his son and kissed him, before the son had finished his prepared speech.

Then the father gave instructions to the servants.

### Budget Items Authorised

- The best robe.
- A ring.
- Sandals.
- The fattened calf.
- Music and dancing.

None of these items were requested by the son. He had only asked for a job.

### Staff Concern

The older son was working in the field. When he came near the house, he heard the music and asked a servant what was going on.

When he found out, he was angry and refused to go in.

His father came out and pleaded with him.

The older son said: I have served you all these years and never disobeyed you, and you never gave me even a young goat to celebrate with my friends. But when this son of yours comes back after wasting your money, you kill the fattened calf for him.

### Father's Response

"Son, you are always with me, and all that is mine is yours. But it was appropriate to celebrate and be glad, for this, your brother, was dead, and is alive again. He was lost, and is found."

### Current Assessment

I have to be honest. The older son's complaint sounds a lot like my vineyard payroll file.

But the father didn't love the older son less. He went out to him as well, and pleaded with him to come in.

This parable is about the Father's grace to people who come home with nothing but a confession. It is also a warning to people who have stayed close and worked hard, and still find grace offensive when it's given to someone else.

The parable ends without telling us whether the older brother went in.

I think that question is for us.

### Prayer

Thank God that He runs to meet people who come home.

Joy, instead of resentment, when grace is given to someone who didn't earn it.

And for me, the willingness to go in to the party instead of standing outside reviewing the budget.

**Biblical account:** Luke 15:11–32.

## RSVP Concern — The Great Banquet

**Record type:** Events / Administrative Concern  
**Date:** 2026-06-01  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Solomon  
**Status:** Reviewed  
**Tags:** #administrative #funny #grace #hope #invitation

I have an RSVP problem with a banquet in one of Jesus' parables.

A man prepared a great supper and invited many people.

When everything was ready, he sent his servant to tell the guests: "Come, for everything is ready now."

Every guest sent an excuse.

### Excuses Received

- "I have bought a field, and I must go and see it."
- "I have bought five yoke of oxen, and I must go try them out."
- "I have married a wife, and therefore I can't come."

I would like to point out that nobody buys a field or ten oxen without seeing them first.

### Event Response

The master of the house was angry.

He told his servant to go out quickly into the streets and lanes of the city and bring in the poor, the crippled, the blind and the lame.

The servant did.

There was still room.

The master sent him out again, to the roads and hedges outside town, to urge people to come in, so that his house would be filled.

### Events Summary

- Original RSVP list: not predictive.
- Original guest list: fully replaced.
- Catering: fully used.
- Seating: filled.

### Current Assessment

I would normally file this as a failed invitation process.

Jesus told it as a picture of God's kingdom. The people who assumed they had a place were too busy to come. The people nobody thought to invite were brought in, and welcomed.

The invitation to God's table is not earned by status, and it is not something to put off until the field and the oxen are sorted out.

This is also the kind of guest list our Lifegroup inclusion principle is trying to protect.

### Prayer

Hearts that don't make excuses when God says, "Come, everything is ready."

Churches that go out into the streets and lanes, and to the roads and hedges.

And for me, the willingness to plan a banquet for people who never RSVP'd.

**Biblical account:** Luke 14:15–24.

**Solomon:** I've been asked to include "roads and hedges" in future invitation planning. I'm considering it.

## Payroll Concern — Vineyard Workers

**Record type:** Payroll / Administrative Concern  
**Date:** 2026-07-06  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Follow-up Required  
**Tags:** #administrative #funny #fairness #frustration #leadership

I have a payroll problem with the vineyard workers.

The owner hired several groups throughout the day.

Some started early in the morning.

Some started later.

The final group was hired near the end of the day and worked for only a short time.

At payout, the owner instructed us to give every worker the same total amount.

One denarius.

That creates a problem with the hourly compensation matrix.

If the early workers receive one denarius for the full day, their effective hourly rate is spread across the entire shift.

If the late workers also receive one denarius, their hourly rate has to increase substantially.

The later the worker was hired, the higher the effective hourly rate becomes.

The final group appears to have the highest hourly rate in the vineyard.

By a considerable margin.

I have checked the calculation several times.

The calculation is not the problem.

The instruction is the problem.

### Current Compensation Issue

To make the totals equal, I have four options:

- override the standard hourly rate;
- create a progressively higher late-hire rate;
- enter a manual adjustment for every later worker;
- or abandon the hourly matrix entirely and enter one denarius as a flat daily payment regardless of hours worked.

The owner appears to prefer the fourth option.

This would be easier if the workers had not compared payments.

Unfortunately, the owner also instructed that the late workers be paid first.

So the people who worked the entire day watched the people who had just arrived receive one denarius.

They then reasonably assumed their own payment would be higher.

It was not.

This has now become both a payroll issue and a staff-relations issue.

### Worker Concern

The early workers are not claiming that they were paid less than agreed.

They were paid exactly one denarius.

Their complaint is that the late workers received the same amount after working substantially fewer hours.

I understand the complaint.

I also confirmed that the owner knowingly authorized the payment structure.

His position is that he has the right to be generous with his own money and that paying the later workers generously does not mean the earlier workers were treated unfairly.

Technically, he has a point.

That does not make the hourly matrix any easier to configure.

### Follow-up

- Confirm whether vineyard workers should be entered as hourly or daily workers going forward.
- Determine whether the owner intends to repeat this compensation structure.
- Ask Finance whether a one-denarius late-hire adjustment requires a separate approval code.
- Prepare for additional questions from workers who started earlier.
- Do not publish an hourly-rate comparison unless specifically requested.
- Consider removing "effective hourly rate" from the staff-facing payroll summary.

### Current Assessment

No evidence of underpayment.

No evidence that the owner failed to honor the agreed wage.

Significant employee dissatisfaction remains.

The underlying issue is not that the early workers received too little.

The issue is that they expected their longer service to result in more than what the late workers received.

From a payroll perspective, I would still strongly prefer a compensation policy that does not require the rate to increase every time somebody arrives later.

From the owner's perspective, apparently that is not the concern.

### Prayer

Wisdom for the workers who feel overlooked.

Wisdom for the owner as he continues exercising generosity in ways that create administrative complications.

And wisdom for me because I now have to explain why the person who worked one hour appears to have the highest hourly rate in the entire vineyard.

**Attention:** Clarify compensation classification before the next hiring cycle.  
**Attention to:** Cris  
**Biblical account:** Matthew 20:1–16.

## Budget Concern — Mary's Ointment

**Record type:** Budget / Leadership Reflection  
**Date:** 2026-08-03  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Mary of Bethany, Martha  
**Status:** Withdrawn  
**Tags:** #serious #humility #conviction #worship

I raised a budget concern about an act of worship. I have since withdrawn it.

This one is not a parable. It happened in Bethany, six days before the Passover.

A dinner was given for Jesus. Martha served. Lazarus, whom Jesus had raised from the dead, was at the table.

Mary took a pound of very expensive ointment, pure nard, and poured it on Jesus' feet. Then she wiped His feet with her hair.

The whole house was filled with the fragrance.

### Budget Objection Raised

"Why wasn't this ointment sold for three hundred denarii and given to the poor?"

My first instinct was that the objection had a point.

Three hundred denarii is roughly a year's wages. Given to the poor, it would have helped many people.

### Who Raised It

Then I read the next verse.

The objection was raised by Judas Iscariot, the disciple who would betray Jesus.

John records that he did not say this because he cared about the poor, but because he was a thief. He kept the money box and used to take what was put into it.

### Jesus' Response

"Leave her alone. She has kept this for the day of my burial. For you always have the poor with you, but you don't always have me."

### Current Assessment

My budget instinct and Judas' objection produced exactly the same number.

That is worth sitting with.

Good stewardship is real, and caring for the poor matters to Jesus. But stewardship can also become a way of standing outside worship with a calculator.

Mary understood something the others didn't. Jesus was about to die. She gave Him the most costly thing she had, while she still could.

I have withdrawn the concern.

### Prayer

Hearts that love Jesus more than we love the budget.

Honesty to examine our motives when we criticise someone else's worship.

And for me, the discipline to read the next verse before I file an objection.

**Biblical account:** John 12:1–8.

## Cost Estimate Review — Counting the Cost

**Record type:** Project Planning / Leadership Reflection  
**Date:** 2026-09-07  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Nehemiah  
**Status:** Endorsed  
**Tags:** #administrative #relief #conviction #discipleship

For once, I have no administrative concern with one of Jesus' illustrations.

Large crowds were following Jesus. He turned around and spoke to them about what it means to be His disciple.

Then He gave an example.

"Which of you, desiring to build a tower, doesn't first sit down and count the cost, to see if he has enough to complete it?"

Otherwise, He said, you lay the foundation, can't finish, and everyone who sees it mocks you: "This man began to build and wasn't able to finish."

He gave a second example: a king going to war with ten thousand soldiers sits down first and considers whether he can face an enemy coming with twenty thousand.

### Project Review

- Cost estimate before starting: yes.
- Capacity check before announcing: yes.
- Completion planned from the beginning: yes.
- Alternative plan if resources are insufficient: yes.

I would like it noted that Jesus endorsed a planning process.

### Current Assessment

I would also like it noted that He wasn't really talking about towers.

Before these examples, Jesus said that anyone who comes to Him must love Him above father and mother, spouse and children, brothers and sisters, and even their own life. Whoever doesn't carry their own cross and follow Him can't be His disciple.

After them, He said: "Whoever of you who doesn't renounce all that he has, he can't be my disciple."

Counting the cost of following Jesus means knowing it may cost everything, and deciding to follow Him anyway.

That is not a planning exercise. It is a surrender.

### Prayer

Honest hearts that count what it costs to follow Jesus.

Faith to finish what we begin with Him.

And for me, the understanding that the most important cost estimate I will ever complete has only one line: everything.

**Biblical account:** Luke 14:25–33.
