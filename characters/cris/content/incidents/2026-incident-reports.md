# Cris — Incident Reports

**Oikonomia section:** Leadership Report

Cris files incident reports as a 2026 coordinator. The events are the leaders' lived history, told exactly as Scripture records them and followed up the way Cris would follow up any incident. Cris was not there. Where the incident involves sin, death or judgment, the report says so plainly and the humor stays out of it.

## Equipment Replacement — The Stone Tablets

**Record type:** Equipment / Replacement Review  
**Date:** 2026-02-16  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Moses  
**Status:** Approved  
**Tags:** #administrative #funny #serious #covenant #mercy

I need to process a replacement request for two stone tablets.

These were not ordinary tablets.

Scripture records that the tablets were the work of God, and the writing was the writing of God, engraved on both sides.

Moses was carrying them down Mount Sinai.

As he came near the camp, he saw the golden calf and the people dancing. His anger burned, and he threw the tablets out of his hands and broke them at the foot of the mountain.

### Damage Report

- Item: two tablets of the covenant.
- Manufacturer: God.
- Condition before incident: perfect.
- Condition after incident: broken.
- Cause of damage: thrown by the person carrying them.

I have reviewed the wording of the cause several times. It is accurate.

### Replacement

God told Moses: "Chisel two stone tablets like the first. I will write on the tablets the words that were on the first tablets, which you broke."

I would like to note that the replacement instruction already includes the incident finding.

No further cause analysis is required.

Moses cut two new tablets, went up the mountain early in the morning, and stayed with the LORD forty days and forty nights. The words of the covenant were written again.

### Current Assessment

The broken tablets look funny on a damage form. The reason they were broken is not funny at all.

Within weeks of hearing God's commandments, the people had made an idol and were worshipping it. Moses breaking the tablets was a picture of what the people had already done to the covenant.

The part that matters most is the replacement. After the people broke faith, God did not walk away from them. He wrote His words again.

That is not a standard replacement policy. That is mercy.

### Prayer

Thank God that He writes His word again after His people break it.

Reverence for how serious it is to turn away from Him.

And patience for me, because someone has already asked whether the replacement tablets need a carrying case, and I am not sure that is the right lesson.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 32:15–20; Exodus 34:1–4, 27–28.

### Comments

**Moses:** "Thrown by the person carrying them" is fair.

## Serious Incident — Aaron and the Golden Calf

**Record type:** Serious Incident / Leadership Accountability  
**Date:** 2026-03-02  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Aaron, Moses  
**Status:** Recorded  
**Tags:** #serious #grief #idolatry #accountability #leadership

I need to record a serious incident that happened while Moses was on the mountain.

Moses had been gone longer than the people expected.

The people gathered around Aaron and said, "Come, make us gods, which shall go before us; for as for this Moses, the man who brought us up out of the land of Egypt, we don't know what has become of him."

Aaron told them to bring him their gold earrings.

They did.

Aaron took the gold, shaped it with a tool, and made a calf. The people said, "These are your gods, Israel, which brought you up out of the land of Egypt."

Aaron built an altar in front of it and announced a feast to the LORD for the next day.

The next morning, the people offered sacrifices, sat down to eat and drink, and rose up to play.

This was idolatry. It was a grave sin, and its consequences were grave. There is no humor in this record.

### Leader's Account

When Moses came down and asked Aaron what the people had done to him that he had brought such a great sin on them, Aaron said:

"You know the people, that they are set on evil."

He said the people had asked him for gods. He told them to give him their gold.

"I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf."

I have recorded this explanation exactly as given.

I don't believe it needs any commentary from me.

### Immediate Concerns

- A leader left in charge gave the people what they demanded, instead of what God had commanded.
- Aaron did not only allow the calf. He made it, built the altar and announced the feast.
- Scripture says Aaron had let the people get out of control.
- Aaron's account moves responsibility onto the people and onto the fire.

### Consequences

- Moses burned the calf, ground it to powder, scattered it on the water and made the people drink it.
- About three thousand people died that day.
- The LORD struck the people with a plague because of the calf Aaron made.

### Follow-up

- Crowd pressure is not a defence for a leader who builds the altar.
- Accountability for Aaron rests with Moses and with God, not with me.
- This record stays restricted to the people named.

### Current Assessment

The most important thing Moses did after this was not his anger. It was his intercession.

The next day, Moses went back up to the LORD and said, "Oh, this people have sinned a great sin, and have made themselves gods of gold. Yet now, if you will, forgive their sin — and if not, please blot me out of your book which you have written."

A leader under pressure made an idol for the people. Another leader offered himself for them.

That contrast is the lesson I'm keeping from this file.

### Prayer

Courage for leaders to refuse what the crowd demands when it is against what God commands.

Honesty to own our part instead of saying "out came this calf."

And intercessors like Moses, who stand before God for people who have failed badly.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 32:1–6, 19–35.

## Equipment Fit Review — David and Saul's Armor

**Record type:** Equipment / Procurement Lesson  
**Date:** 2026-04-20  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** David  
**Status:** Returned  
**Tags:** #administrative #funny #courage #faith

I need to document an equipment issue before the battle with Goliath.

David volunteered to fight the Philistine.

Saul's first response was that David could not do it, because David was only a youth and Goliath had been a warrior since his youth.

David explained his experience. When a lion or a bear took a lamb from his father's flock, he went after it, struck it and rescued the lamb. The LORD who delivered him from the lion and the bear would deliver him from this Philistine.

Saul agreed, and said, "Go! The LORD will be with you."

He then issued David his own equipment.

### Equipment Issued

- Bronze helmet.
- Coat of mail.
- Saul's clothing.
- Sword.

### Equipment Test

David put on the sword and tried to walk.

He said, "I can't go with these, for I have not tested them."

He took everything off.

### Equipment Selected Instead

- One staff.
- Five smooth stones from the brook.
- One shepherd's bag.
- One sling.

### Procurement Findings

- Senior leadership equipment is not automatically suitable for the next leader.
- Equipment a person has never used is not preparation.
- All issued items were returned in full. No damage reported.

### Current Assessment

Saul's offer was not foolish. He gave David the best he had.

David's refusal was not disrespect. He knew what he could move in.

But the real finding of this review is not about equipment. When David faced Goliath, he said: "You come to me with a sword, with a spear, and with a javelin; but I come to you in the name of the LORD of Armies." He said the whole assembly would know that "the LORD doesn't save with sword and spear; for the battle is the LORD's."

David did not win because he found better equipment. He won because he trusted God with the equipment God had already trained him to use.

### Prayer

Faith for every leader facing something that looks too big.

Wisdom to use what God has already put in our hands, instead of borrowing someone else's armour.

And for me, a better equipment request form, one that includes the question "Have you tested this?"

**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 17:32–40, 45–47.

### Comments

**David:** The helmet was very heavy.

## Staffing Reduction Review — Gideon's Army

**Record type:** Resourcing / Staffing Review  
**Date:** 2026-05-11  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Gideon  
**Status:** Approved (Not by Me)  
**Tags:** #administrative #funny #fear #faith #victory

I need to document a staffing plan that I would not have approved.

Gideon had gathered an army of thirty-two thousand men against Midian.

The LORD said that was too many.

The reason given was that if Israel won with that many, they would boast, "My own hand has saved me."

### Round One

Everyone who was afraid was told they could go home.

Twenty-two thousand went home.

Ten thousand remained.

### Round Two

The LORD said there were still too many.

The men were brought down to the water. Three hundred lapped the water with their hands to their mouths. Everyone else knelt down to drink.

The LORD said He would save Israel with the three hundred.

Everyone else was sent home.

### Staffing Summary

- Starting force: 32,000.
- Final force: 300.
- Reduction: over ninety-nine percent.
- Opposing force: Midianites, Amalekites and the people of the east, lying in the valley like locusts, with camels as numerous as the sand on the seashore.

### Equipment Issued to the Three Hundred

- Trumpets.
- Empty jars.
- Torches inside the jars.

I have checked this list several times. It does not include swords.

### Immediate Concerns

- By every resourcing standard I use, this is the wrong decision.
- Gideon was afraid. God knew it, and told him that if he was afraid, he could go down to the enemy camp with his servant Purah and listen.
- Gideon went. He heard a Midianite describe a dream about a barley loaf rolling into the camp and flattening a tent, and another Midianite say it meant God had given Midian into Gideon's hand.
- Gideon worshipped.

### Outcome

In the middle of the night, the three hundred surrounded the camp in three companies. They blew the trumpets, smashed the jars, held up the torches and shouted, "The sword of the LORD and of Gideon!"

Every man stood in his place.

The LORD set the Midianite soldiers against each other, and the whole army fled.

### Current Assessment

I have flagged this staffing plan three times. Each time, the reduction came from God, on purpose, for the reason He stated.

The point was never efficiency. The point was that nobody could look at the result and say, "We did this ourselves."

I also want to record that God did not scold Gideon for being afraid. He gave him a way to hear encouragement before the battle.

### Prayer

Faith for leaders when God's plan leaves them with fewer resources than they wanted.

Encouragement for anyone who is afraid, like Gideon was.

And humility for me, because my resourcing spreadsheet has no column for "so that Israel won't boast."

**Biblical account:** Judges 7:1–22.

### Comments

**Gideon:** I was afraid too. God sent me to hear a dream in their camp.

## Audit Finding — Saul and the Amalekite Livestock

**Record type:** Compliance / Audit Finding  
**Date:** 2026-06-08  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Saul, Samuel  
**Status:** Finding Upheld  
**Tags:** #serious #administrative #obedience #regret #leadership

I need to record an audit finding from Samuel's review of Saul's campaign against Amalek.

Samuel had brought Saul a clear instruction from the LORD: destroy Amalek completely, and spare nothing that belonged to them.

Saul attacked the Amalekites.

Saul and the people spared King Agag.

They also spared the best of the sheep, the cattle, the fattened calves, the lambs, and everything that was good. They destroyed only what was worthless.

The LORD told Samuel that Saul had turned back from following Him and had not carried out His commands. Samuel was troubled, and cried out to the LORD all night.

In the morning, Samuel was told that Saul had gone to Carmel and set up a monument in his own honour.

### Completion Report Submitted

When Samuel arrived, Saul greeted him: "You are blessed by the LORD! I have performed the commandment of the LORD."

### Auditor's Observation

Samuel said: "Then what does this bleating of the sheep in my ears and the lowing of the cattle which I hear mean?"

I don't think I have ever seen an audit finding announce itself so clearly.

### Leader's Response

Saul's first explanation was that the people had spared the best animals to sacrifice to the LORD, and that the rest had been destroyed.

When Samuel pressed him, Saul insisted that he had obeyed, and again said it was the people who took the plunder.

Only later did Saul say: "I have sinned; for I have transgressed the commandment of the LORD and your words, because I feared the people and obeyed their voice."

### Finding

- The report of completion was not accurate.
- The evidence was audible.
- The leader's first response placed responsibility on the people.
- The underlying cause, in Saul's own words, was fear of the people.

### Current Assessment

This is not a light matter.

Samuel's answer is one of the most important sentences in this file: "Has the LORD as great delight in burnt offerings and sacrifices, as in obeying the LORD's voice? Behold, to obey is better than sacrifice, and to listen than the fat of rams."

Saul tried to turn disobedience into worship by relabelling it as a sacrifice. God was not asking for a better offering. He was asking to be obeyed.

Samuel told Saul that because he had rejected the word of the LORD, the LORD had rejected him as king.

The sheep made the finding obvious. The monument at Carmel had already shown where Saul's heart was.

### Prayer

Honest reports from leaders, even when the evidence isn't audible.

Hearts that fear God more than they fear people.

And for all of us, the wisdom to understand that no amount of religious activity can replace simply doing what God said.

**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 15:1–3, 7–26.

## Incident Report — Eutychus

**Record type:** Safety Incident / Leadership Follow-up  
**Date:** 2026-06-14  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Follow-up Required  
**Tags:** #serious #administrative #funny #safety #leadership #relief

We had a serious incident during the evening gathering involving Eutychus.

Paul was teaching.

For a long time.

The meeting continued until midnight.

Eutychus was sitting in a window on the third floor and gradually fell into a deep sleep while Paul continued speaking.

He then fell from the window.

This immediately changed the nature of the meeting.

The people went downstairs and he was taken up dead. Paul went down, embraced him, and assured everyone that his life was in him.

I am grateful to report that Eutychus was restored alive and there were no remaining consequences from the fall.

I would also like to document what happened next.

Paul returned upstairs.

Broke bread.

Ate.

And continued speaking until daylight.

I have confirmed this sequence.

### Immediate Concerns

- Eutychus should not have been seated in an open third-story window during a late-night gathering.
- Fatigue risk was not adequately considered.
- The gathering had already extended well beyond normal evening hours.
- Paul appears to regard midnight as an intermediate point in a teaching session rather than a natural stopping point.
- Future venues should not combine elevated open windows with extended preaching.

### Follow-up

- Confirm Eutychus has no outstanding care needs following the fall.
- Review venue seating and window safety before future evening gatherings.
- Establish safer seating for anyone showing signs of fatigue.
- Discuss reasonable teaching-duration expectations with Paul.
- Clarify that "I am leaving tomorrow" does not automatically authorize an all-night sermon.
- Ensure leaders understand that successful recovery from an incident does not eliminate the need to review why the incident occurred.

### Current Assessment

This was a genuine and potentially fatal incident.

I do not want the fact that Eutychus survived to cause us to minimize the seriousness of the fall.

At the same time, the event raises some practical questions regarding gathering duration, venue safety and pastoral awareness of exhausted participants.

Paul's desire to make full use of his remaining time with the believers is understandable.

His definition of "full use" may require further discussion.

I am particularly interested in understanding at what point during an evening meeting Paul believes a speaker should reasonably conclude.

So far, the available evidence suggests the answer is not midnight.

### Prayer

Thank God for preserving Eutychus.

Wisdom for our leaders in recognizing physical limits as well as spiritual hunger.

Wisdom for Paul in distinguishing between having more to say and needing to say all of it before sunrise.

And wisdom for me as I prepare to tell one of the most effective teachers in the early church that a third-floor fall may be an indicator that the meeting has gone long enough.

**Attention:** Review late-night gathering safety and teaching-duration expectations.  
**Attention to:** Cris  
**Biblical account:** Acts 20:7–12.

## Equipment Incident — Borrowed Axe Head

**Record type:** Facilities / Equipment Incident  
**Date:** 2026-08-17  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Elisha, Nehemiah  
**Status:** Recovered  
**Tags:** #funny #relief #provision #gratitude

I need to report an equipment loss during a building project.

The company of prophets told Elisha that the place where they met with him had become too small.

They proposed a volunteer building project: go to the Jordan, each man cut a beam, and build a new meeting place.

Elisha approved the project.

One of them asked Elisha to come along. Elisha agreed.

While one of the men was cutting down a tree, the iron axe head flew off the handle and fell into the river.

He cried out: "Alas, my master! For it was borrowed."

### Incident Details

- Item: iron axe head.
- Ownership: borrowed.
- Location of loss: the Jordan River.
- Recovery method: see below.

### Recovery

Elisha asked where it fell.

The man showed him the place.

Elisha cut a stick, threw it into the water at that spot, and the iron floated.

Elisha said, "Take it."

The man reached out and picked it up.

### Follow-up

- Item recovered and presumably returned to its owner.
- I would like to commend the worker's first report. He did not say, "I lost an axe head." He said, "It was borrowed." That is the correct priority for borrowed equipment.
- Recommendation for Facilities: keep a register of every borrowed tool. Nehemiah already has one.
- No recommendation regarding floating iron as a standard recovery method.

### Current Assessment

This is one of the smallest miracles in Scripture.

Nobody was sick. Nobody was in danger. No army was defeated.

A man lost something that wasn't his, and he was distressed because he could not repay it. God cared about that, and Scripture thought it was worth recording.

I find that deeply encouraging. The same God who parts seas also notices a borrowed tool at the bottom of a river.

### Prayer

Thank God that no problem is too small for His care.

Integrity for everyone who uses what belongs to someone else.

And for me, the faith to bring small problems to God before I have filled out the replacement form.

**Biblical account:** 2 Kings 6:1–7.

### Comments

**Nehemiah:** Our borrowed-tool register has 41 items. None of them have entered a river.
