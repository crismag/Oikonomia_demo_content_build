## Equipment Replacement Review — Stone Tablets

**Record type:** Equipment / Replacement Review  
**Date:** 2026-02-16  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Moses  
**Status:** Approved  
**Tags:** #administrative #light #serious

Item: two stone tablets, written by the finger of God.

Cause of damage, as recorded: damaged by requester.

This is technically accurate. Moses came down the mountain, saw the golden calf and the dancing, and threw the tablets out of his hands, breaking them beneath the mountain.

### Replacement

- God instructed Moses to chisel two tablets like the first, and He would write on them the words that were on the first tablets, "which you broke."
- The replacement instruction itself names the requester. I've noted that no further cause analysis is needed.

### Current Assessment

The breakage is funny on a form. The reason for it isn't. The people had made an idol within weeks of hearing the commandments, and the broken tablets are what that looked like.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 32:15–20; Exodus 34:1–4.

### Comments

**Moses:** "Damaged by requester" is fair.

## Serious Incident — The Golden Calf

**Record type:** Serious Incident / Leadership Accountability  
**Date:** 2026-03-02  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Aaron, Moses  
**Status:** Recorded  
**Tags:** #serious #grief #conviction

While Moses was on the mountain, the people pressed Aaron to make gods to go before them. Aaron collected their gold earrings, fashioned a calf and built an altar before it. The people worshipped it.

This was idolatry. It was grave, and the consequences were grave, including deaths among the people and a plague. None of that is humorous, and this record doesn't treat it as such.

### Leader's Account

When Moses asked what the people had done to him that he brought such a great sin on them, Aaron said: "You know the people, that they are set on evil." He said they had asked for gods, and that he told them to give him their gold. "I threw it into the fire, and out came this calf."

I've recorded that explanation exactly as given. I don't need to comment on it. The account speaks for itself.

### Follow-up

- Crowd pressure is not a defence for a leader who builds the altar.
- Accountability for Aaron sits with Moses and with God, not with me.

**Biblical account:** Exodus 32:1–6, 21–24, 30–35.

## Equipment Fit Review — Saul's Armor

**Record type:** Equipment / Procurement Lesson  
**Date:** 2026-04-20  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** David  
**Status:** Returned  
**Tags:** #administrative #light #confidence

Saul dressed David in his own equipment: bronze helmet, coat of mail, sword.

David tried to walk in it and said, "I can't go with these, for I have not tested them." He took it all off.

He selected instead: his staff, five smooth stones from the brook, a shepherd's bag and a sling.

### Procurement Lesson

- Senior leadership equipment is not automatically suitable for the next leader.
- Equipment a person hasn't tested isn't preparation.
- Returned in full. No damage.

### Current Assessment

Saul's offer wasn't foolish; he gave the best he had. David's refusal wasn't disrespect; he knew what he could move in. The giant was real, and the courage was in trusting God with the equipment he knew.

**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 17:38–40.

### Comments

**David:** The helmet was very heavy.

## Audit Finding — Livestock Noise

**Record type:** Compliance / Audit Finding  
**Date:** 2026-06-08  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Saul, Samuel  
**Status:** Finding Upheld  
**Tags:** #serious #administrative #regret

Instruction: completely destroy Amalek and everything that belonged to them.

Reported outcome, from Saul: "I have performed the commandment of the LORD."

Auditor's observation, from Samuel: "Then what does this bleating of the sheep in my ears and the lowing of the cattle which I hear mean?"

Response: the people spared the best sheep and cattle, to sacrifice to the Lord. The rest were destroyed. King Agag was also spared.

### Finding

The report of completion was not accurate. The evidence was audible.

### Current Assessment

This is the rare audit where the finding announces itself. It isn't a light matter: Samuel said that to obey is better than sacrifice, and that because Saul rejected the word of the Lord, the Lord had rejected him as king. Saul's first explanation blamed the people; his confession came after.

**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 15:13–26.

## Incident Report — Eutychus

**Record type:** Safety Incident / Leadership Follow-up  
**Date:** 2026-06-14  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Follow-up Required  
**Tags:** #serious #administrative #funny #safety #leadership #relief

We had a serious incident during the evening gathering involving Eutychus.

Paul was teaching.

For a long time.

The meeting continued until midnight.

Eutychus was sitting in a window on the third floor and gradually fell into a deep sleep while Paul continued speaking.

He then fell from the window.

This immediately changed the nature of the meeting.

The people went downstairs and he was taken up dead. Paul went down, embraced him, and assured everyone that his life was in him.

I am grateful to report that Eutychus was restored alive and there were no remaining consequences from the fall.

I would also like to document what happened next.

Paul returned upstairs.

Broke bread.

Ate.

And continued speaking until daylight.

I have confirmed this sequence.

### Immediate Concerns

- Eutychus should not have been seated in an open third-story window during a late-night gathering.
- Fatigue risk was not adequately considered.
- The gathering had already extended well beyond normal evening hours.
- Paul appears to regard midnight as an intermediate point in a teaching session rather than a natural stopping point.
- Future venues should not combine elevated open windows with extended preaching.

### Follow-up

- Confirm Eutychus has no outstanding care needs following the fall.
- Review venue seating and window safety before future evening gatherings.
- Establish safer seating for anyone showing signs of fatigue.
- Discuss reasonable teaching-duration expectations with Paul.
- Clarify that "I am leaving tomorrow" does not automatically authorize an all-night sermon.
- Ensure leaders understand that successful recovery from an incident does not eliminate the need to review why the incident occurred.

### Current Assessment

This was a genuine and potentially fatal incident.

I do not want the fact that Eutychus survived to cause us to minimize the seriousness of the fall.

At the same time, the event raises some practical questions regarding gathering duration, venue safety and pastoral awareness of exhausted participants.

Paul's desire to make full use of his remaining time with the believers is understandable.

His definition of "full use" may require further discussion.

I am particularly interested in understanding at what point during an evening meeting Paul believes a speaker should reasonably conclude.

So far, the available evidence suggests the answer is not midnight.

### Prayer

Thank God for preserving Eutychus.

Wisdom for our leaders in recognizing physical limits as well as spiritual hunger.

Wisdom for Paul in distinguishing between having more to say and needing to say all of it before sunrise.

And wisdom for me as I prepare to tell one of the most effective teachers in the early church that a third-floor fall may be an indicator that the meeting has gone long enough.

**Attention:** Review late-night gathering safety and teaching-duration expectations.  
**Attention to:** Cris  
**Biblical account:** Acts 20:7–12.

## Facilities Incident — Borrowed Axe Head

**Date:** 2026-08-17  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Elisha, Nehemiah  
**Status:** Recovered  
**Tags:** #light #relief #gratitude

The company of prophets were building a larger meeting place by the Jordan. Elisha went with them.

While one man was cutting a tree, the iron axe head flew off into the water. He cried out, "Alas, my master! For it was borrowed."

Elisha asked where it fell, cut a stick, threw it in, and the iron floated. The man picked it up.

### Follow-up

- Item: axe head, iron, borrowed. Status: recovered.
- The worker's first report was that it was borrowed, not that it was lost. That's the correct priority for borrowed equipment.
- Note for Facilities: every borrowed tool on the register. Nehemiah already has one.

### Current Assessment

A small miracle for a man worried about someone else's property. The account thought that was worth recording, and so do I.

**Biblical account:** 2 Kings 6:1–7.

### Comments

**Nehemiah:** Our borrowed-tool register has 41 items. None have entered a river.
