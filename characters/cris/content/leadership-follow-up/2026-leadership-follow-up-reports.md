# Cris — Leadership Follow-up Reports

**Oikonomia section:** Leadership Report

Cris files these as a 2026 coordinator. Each report takes a situation from a leader's lived history and follows it up the way Cris would follow up any leadership matter: what happened, who needs to talk to whom, what not to force, and what to watch. The events are the leaders' history. Cris was not there, and doesn't pretend to have been.

**Record type:** Leadership Capacity / Follow-up  
**Date:** 2026-01-19  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Follow-up Required  
**Tags:** #administrative #serious #exhaustion

## Gilgal Follow-up — Authority Taken Under Deadline Pressure

**Record type:** Leadership Conduct / Follow-up  
**Date:** 2026-05-04  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Saul, Samuel  
**Status:** Recorded  
**Tags:** #serious #fear #regret

Saul was told to wait seven days at Gilgal for Samuel, who would offer the sacrifices.

He waited the seven days. Samuel hadn't arrived. The Philistines were gathering. His troops were hiding and scattering. Saul said, "Bring the burnt offering to me," and offered it himself.

Samuel arrived just as he finished. Samuel asked, "What have you done?" Saul explained the pressure: the people were scattering, Samuel was late, and he forced himself.

### Follow-up

- Deadline pressure is real. It doesn't transfer authority.
- When the person responsible is late, the next step is to ask, not to act in their place.
- Saul's 2026 check-ins with Samuel include every "I had to" decision.

### Current Assessment

Saul waited almost long enough. The consequence Samuel announced was grave, and I'm not softening it. The lesson for any leader under pressure is that "I forced myself" is how it feels from inside, and "you have done foolishly" is how it looked to the one sent by God.

**Attention:** Watch for decisions made outside a leader's authority under time pressure.  
**Attention to:** Samuel  
**Biblical account:** 1 Samuel 13:8–14.

### Comments

**Saul:** I did wait the seven days. I know that isn't the point.

## Team Conflict Follow-up — Paul and Barnabas

**Record type:** Leadership Relationship / Team Conflict  
**Date:** 2026-05-18  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Status:** Follow-up Required  
**Tags:** #serious #administrative #conflict #discernment #restoration #leadership

I need to document the disagreement between Paul and Barnabas concerning John Mark.

Paul does not want Mark included in the next ministry assignment because Mark previously withdrew from the team in Pamphylia.

Barnabas wants to bring him.

Both have understandable reasons.

Neither considers the other's reason sufficient.

The disagreement became sharp enough that Paul and Barnabas separated and continued ministry through different teams.

This is difficult because both men have demonstrated considerable maturity in other situations.

Barnabas was the person who stood beside Paul when many believers were still afraid of him.

Paul, therefore, understands better than most people what it means for someone to advocate for a person whose future usefulness is not yet obvious.

I have decided not to make that observation during the first five minutes of the meeting.

### Follow-up

- Speak with Paul regarding the difference between legitimate reliability concerns and permanently defining someone by a previous failure.
- Speak with Barnabas regarding the difference between encouragement and ignoring legitimate team concerns.
- Follow up with Mark separately without treating him as the object being passed between two senior leaders.
- Avoid forcing reconciliation merely so the organizational chart looks tidy.
- Monitor whether the separation produces bitterness or whether both teams remain able to honor one another.
- Revisit this report when Paul's later assessment of Mark becomes relevant.

### Current Assessment

I do not believe this situation requires choosing which leader is entirely right.

Paul is taking mission reliability seriously.

Barnabas is taking restoration seriously.

Both concerns matter.

The more important question is whether disagreement can remain disagreement without becoming contempt.

There is also something worth watching in Mark himself.

The young man currently at the center of a leadership split may eventually become someone Paul describes as useful to him for ministry.

If that happens, I would like everyone involved to remember this report before pretending the process was obvious.

### Prayer

Wisdom for Paul.

Grace and discernment for Barnabas.

Courage and maturity for Mark.

And wisdom for me in mediating two respected leaders who are both accustomed to being the person in the room with strong convictions.

**Attention:** Monitor relationship health and Mark's restoration without forcing premature resolution.  
**Attention to:** Cris  
**Biblical account:** Acts 13:13; Acts 15:36–41; Colossians 4:10; 2 Timothy 4:11.
