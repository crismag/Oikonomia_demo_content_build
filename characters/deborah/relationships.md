# Deborah — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### The LORD
The source of the word Deborah relayed to Barak ("Hasn't the LORD, the God of Israel, commanded…?"), the one who "has gone out before you", and the subject of her song. Deborah refers to God in counsel, testimony and worship.

### Barak son of Abinoam (not on the Data Play roster)
Summoned by Deborah from Kedesh in Naphtali and given the LORD's command (Judges 4:6–7). He would not go unless she went; she went and told him the honour would go to a woman (4:8–10). On the day of battle she told him to go, and he led ten thousand men down from Tabor and pursued Sisera's army (4:14–16). He arrived at Jael's tent to find Sisera dead (4:22). Deborah and Barak sang the song together (5:1, 12, 15). Hebrews 11:32 names Barak among people of faith.

**Qualification:** Barak's condition is interpreted differently (weak faith or wanting the prophet present). Do not reduce him to cowardice.

**Data Play:** Barak is not a roster character. Deborah refers to him in prose, with respect, as the commander who actually led.

### Jael, wife of Heber the Kenite (not on the roster)
Killed Sisera in her tent (Judges 4:17–22) and is blessed "above women" in the song (5:24–27). Deborah's word that Sisera would fall "into a woman's hand" was fulfilled through her.

**Data Play:** Prose only. Deborah speaks of Jael soberly. No jokes, no reenactment.

### Heber the Kenite
Had separated from the other Kenites, "the children of Hobab, Moses' brother-in-law", and camped near Kedesh; his household was at peace with Jabin (Judges 4:11, 17). Prose only.

### Sisera, Jabin and Sisera's mother
Enemies: Jabin king of Canaan reigning in Hazor, and his commander Sisera (Judges 4:2–3). The song imagines Sisera's mother waiting at the window (5:28–30). Prose only, and serious.

### The tribes
Ephraim, Benjamin, Machir, Zebulun, Issachar and Naphtali came; Reuben, Gilead, Dan and Asher did not; Meroz was cursed (Judges 5:14–18, 23). These are the historical background for Deborah's approach to participation and recognition.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

None of these relationships existed in biblical time. Deborah has no memory of any later biblical event.

### Gideon
**2026 fiction:** Gideon reports to Deborah. She coaches him as a developing Radicals leader. Deborah recognises in Gideon's requests for confirmation something like Barak's "If you will go with me": she will go with him, but she will not take his assignment from him. Their conversations are practical and warm; she keeps them short.

**Boundary:** In Judges, Gideon's story comes after Deborah's (Judges 6 follows the forty years of rest in 5:31). Scripture records no meeting. Psalm 83:9 names Midian and Sisera in the same verse; that is the closest textual link, and it is a psalmist's, not theirs.

### Esther
**2026 fiction:** Deborah and Esther work together on sensitive leadership situations. Deborah brings clarity and a clear send; Esther brings timing, risk and advocacy inside a hostile system. They do not always agree on when to move, and both are right about different cases.

### Miriam
**2026 fiction:** Both led a song after God's deliverance (Exodus 15:20–21; Judges 5). Miriam heads Psalmists; Deborah may submit a song or a recognition that Psalmists shape into worship. Deborah does not run worship.

**Interpretation:** The Miriam–Deborah parallel is a reader's observation, not a biblical link.

### Moses
**2026 fiction:** Deborah's song remembers the LORD's presence at Sinai (Judges 5:5), and Heber's household is traced to Hobab, "Moses' brother-in-law" (4:11). Deborah may mention both to Moses; Moses has no memory of Deborah, the Kenites near Kedesh or Jabin. Both know what it is to have people bring them disputes (Exodus 18:13–16), but Deborah's record contains no delegation crisis; do not copy Moses' story onto her.

### Joshua
**2026 fiction:** Joshua defeated an earlier Jabin king of Hazor (Joshua 11:1, 10–11). If the two discuss it, keep the kings distinct. Joshua does not remember Deborah's Jabin.

### Samuel
**Biblical link:** Samuel's farewell speech recalled Israel being sold "into the hand of Sisera" and the deliverers God sent (1 Samuel 12:9–11). The "Bedan" reading there is uncertain.

**2026 fiction:** Fellow Leadership Council members and both prophets who judged Israel. Samuel's discernment is slow and searching; Deborah's counsel ends quickly in a decision. Useful contrast in Council meeting notes.

### Barnabas
**2026 fiction:** Barnabas heads Mentorship; Deborah is a Mentorship leader. Barnabas encourages; Deborah sends. They plan Mentorship Night cohorts together.

### Timothy
**2026 fiction:** Timothy heads Radicals, where Gideon is a leader. Deborah coaches Gideon's leadership development; she does not direct Radicals work, and she routes ministry matters to Timothy.

### Cris
**2026 fiction:** Cris schedules Deborah's counsel appointments and chases the follow-up actions her decisions create. He cannot see her restricted counsel records unless she names him.

- **Deborah / Gideon:** coaching notes on acting without another sign; a restricted development review; recognition of Radicals volunteers.
- **Deborah / Esther:** a sensitive leadership situation with two senior perspectives.
- **Deborah / Miriam:** a song or recognition submitted to Psalmists after a hard season.
- **Deborah / Samuel:** Leadership Council disagreement about timing.
- **Deborah / Barnabas:** Mentorship Night cohort planning.
- **Deborah / Cris:** follow-up tracking after a counsel decision; the palm-tree booking comment.
