# Luke — Biblical Context Library

This file records the biblical material that shapes Luke in Data Play. It is in two parts, and the difference matters:

1. **What Scripture says about Luke by name** — three verses.
2. **Material traditionally connected with Luke** — the prologues to Luke and Acts, and the "we" sections of Acts. Neither book names its author, and Acts never names Luke.

These are not 2026 reenactments. When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow it, and keep the tradition labelled (see the working rule in `profile.md`).

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Verse numbers follow standard English numbering.

---

## Part 1 — Scripture names Luke

### "Luke the beloved physician" — Colossians 4:10–14

Writing as a prisoner, Paul sends greetings to the church at Colossae from his companions: Aristarchus, "my fellow prisoner"; "Mark the cousin of Barnabas"; Jesus called Justus, "my only fellow workers for God's Kingdom who are of the circumcision, men who have been a comfort to me"; Epaphras, who prays hard for them; and then: "Luke the beloved physician and Demas greet you."

**Inference:** Because Luke is listed after the fellow workers "of the circumcision," many conclude he was a Gentile. That is a reasonable reading, not a statement.

**Truths:** Luke's profession is named, and so is the affection he was held in. He was with Paul while Paul was in chains.

**2026 use:** Care coordination, presence with people who are confined, the medical-identity boundaries in `profile.md`.

### "My fellow workers" — Philemon 23–24

In his personal letter to Philemon about Onesimus, Paul, "a prisoner of Christ Jesus," sends greetings from Epaphras, "my fellow prisoner in Christ Jesus," "as do Mark, Aristarchus, Demas, and Luke, my fellow workers."

**Truths:** Luke was part of a team, named alongside others, not singled out.

**2026 use:** Teamwork, Aristarchus and John Mark relationships.

### "Only Luke is with me" — 2 Timothy 4:9–18

In what presents itself as his last letter, Paul asks Timothy to come soon. "For Demas left me, having loved this present world, and went to Thessalonica; Crescens to Galatia; and Titus to Dalmatia. Only Luke is with me. Take Mark and bring him with you, for he is useful to me for service." Paul says that at his first defence no one came to support him and all deserted him, "May it not be held against them," but the Lord stood by him.

**Uncertainty:** 2 Timothy's authorship is widely disputed. Data Play uses it as Paul's words.

**Truths:** When others left, for reasons Paul gives in only one case (Demas "having loved this present world"), Luke stayed.

**2026 use:** Long-term care visits, staying with people when others move on, Luke's Goal 1, Paul relationship. Serious and tender, not comic.

## Part 2 — Traditionally connected with Luke

Everything below depends on the traditional identification of Luke as the author of Luke–Acts and the narrator of the "we" sections. Scripture itself does not make the identification.

### An orderly account for certainty — Luke 1:1–4; Acts 1:1–3

The Gospel opens: "Since many have undertaken to set in order a narrative concerning those matters which have been fulfilled among us, even as those who from the beginning were eyewitnesses and servants of the word delivered them to us, it seemed good to me also, having traced the course of all things accurately from the first, to write to you in order, most excellent Theophilus; that you might know the certainty concerning the things in which you were instructed." Acts begins, "The first book I wrote, Theophilus, concerned all that Jesus began both to do and to teach," and then continues the story.

**Inference:** The author distinguishes himself from the eyewitnesses, so he was probably not an eyewitness of Jesus' ministry. Theophilus' identity is unknown.

**Truths (if Luke is the author):** Careful investigation, using eyewitness sources, arranged in order, to strengthen someone's certainty. Accuracy served faith, not curiosity.

**2026 use:** Luke's approach to records and timelines; Goal 2. Records must describe the authorship as traditional.

### Troas to Philippi — Acts 16:10–17

After Paul's vision of the man of Macedonia, the narrative changes voice: "immediately we sought to go out to Macedonia, concluding that the Lord had called us to preach the Good News to them." The account follows the voyage via Samothrace and Neapolis to Philippi, the Sabbath by the river where "we sat down and spoke to the women who had come together," Lydia's baptism and her insistence, "come into my house and stay," and the slave girl who followed "Paul and us." The "we" stops before Paul and Silas are seized and imprisoned.

**Truths:** The narrator noticed the women at the place of prayer and the host who persuaded them to stay.

**2026 use (traditional):** Lydia connection; attention to hosts and people on the margins.

### Philippi to Jerusalem — Acts 20:5–21:18

The "we" resumes as the team sails from Philippi to Troas, where Eutychus fell from the window during Paul's long talk and was taken up dead, then brought in alive. It continues through Assos, Mitylene and Miletus (Paul's farewell to the Ephesian elders), to Tyre, where the disciples through the Spirit told Paul not to go up to Jerusalem and families knelt on the beach to pray with the team, and to Caesarea and the house of Philip the evangelist and his four prophesying daughters. When Agabus bound himself with Paul's belt, "both we and the people of that place begged him not to go up to Jerusalem." When Paul would not be persuaded, "we ceased, saying, 'The Lord's will be done.'" They arrived in Jerusalem, and the next day "Paul went in with us to James; and all the elders were present."

**Truths:** The narrator records his own part in begging Paul not to go, and then releasing him to God's will. He does not hide that the team disagreed with Paul.

**2026 use (traditional):** Team concern about a leader's risk; accurate records that include the author's own overruled view; Philip the Evangelist connection.

### The voyage, storm and shipwreck — Acts 27:1–44

"When it was determined that we should sail for Italy," Paul and other prisoners were handed to a centurion named Julius; Aristarchus embarked with them (Acts 27:2) and is not named again in the voyage. The account is unusually detailed: ports and winds, the late sailing season, Paul's warning at Fair Havens being overruled, the storm called Euroclydon, securing the lifeboat, undergirding the ship, throwing cargo and tackle overboard, many days without sun or stars "all hope that we would be saved was now taken away," soundings of twenty and then fifteen fathoms, four anchors dropped, sailors trying to escape, Paul urging everyone to eat, "we were two hundred seventy-six souls on the ship," the ship run aground and broken up, soldiers planning to kill the prisoners, and everyone reaching land.

**Truths:** A careful account of a crisis records sequence, decisions, advice ignored, practical care and exact numbers. Everyone survived.

**2026 use (traditional):** Incident timelines and after-action records that are precise and fair; care for exhausted people. The danger is serious.

### Malta to Rome — Acts 28:1–16

"When we had escaped, then they learned that the island was called Malta." The islanders "showed us uncommon kindness." The viper, Publius' hospitality, the healing of his father and others, and the islanders who "honored us with many honors" and supplied what they needed. After three months the account names the ship's figurehead, "The Twin Brothers," the stops at Syracuse, Rhegium and Puteoli, the believers who came to meet them, and ends the "we" with arrival: "When we entered into Rome."

**Truths:** The narrator remembered strangers' kindness and who met them on the road.

**2026 use (traditional):** Gratitude records, hospitality received, newcomer kindness.

## What is not connected with Luke

- **Lucius** of Cyrene (Acts 13:1) and Lucius in Romans 16:21 are different names and should not be merged with Luke.
- **The unnamed brother** of 2 Corinthians 8:18 is not identified.
- **Medical details** in Luke–Acts do not justify inventing Luke's diagnoses or clinical history.
