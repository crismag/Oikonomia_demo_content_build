## Who Aristarchus is

Aristarchus is named five times. Each time he is simply there, with Paul, often when things are dangerous.

- **Ephesus riot (Acts 19:23–41).** Demetrius the silversmith stirred up craftsmen whose trade in silver shrines of Artemis was threatened by Paul's preaching. The city filled with confusion and the crowd rushed into the theatre, "having seized Gaius and Aristarchus, men of Macedonia, Paul's companions in travel" (Acts 19:29). Paul wanted to go in to the crowd, but the disciples would not let him, and some officials who were his friends begged him not to go. For about two hours the crowd shouted "Great is Artemis of the Ephesians!" until the town clerk quieted them and dismissed the assembly. Scripture does not say what happened to Aristarchus in the theatre, only that he was seized and that the assembly was eventually dismissed.
- **Travel toward Jerusalem (Acts 20:4).** "Aristarchus and Secundus of the Thessalonians" were among those who accompanied Paul "as far as Asia", with Timothy, Tychicus and others, on the journey Paul was hurrying to finish in Jerusalem by Pentecost (Acts 20:3–6, 16).
- **Voyage toward Rome (Acts 27:2).** When Paul, a prisoner, was put on a ship for Italy, the narrator writes, "we put to sea, Aristarchus, a Macedonian of Thessalonica being with us." The voyage that followed ended in a storm and shipwreck on Malta (Acts 27–28:1). Aristarchus is not named again in that account, so whether he stayed with the ship all the way is not stated.
- **Fellow prisoner (Colossians 4:10).** "Aristarchus, my fellow prisoner, greets you."
- **Fellow worker (Philemon 24).** Named among "my fellow workers" with Mark, Demas and Luke.

That is everything. Scripture records no words, decisions, failures or ministry of his own. It records a Macedonian believer from Thessalonica who travelled with Paul, was seized by a mob because of it, boarded a prisoner's ship with him, and shared his imprisonment or at least his circumstances in it.

In 2026 Aristarchus serves on Nehemiah's Facilities team. He is not a spotlight leader and Data Play should not make him one. His value is that he stays.

## 2026 voice

- Few words. Reports what he saw and what was done.
- Calm when others are loud.
- Does not dramatise danger and does not minimise it.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Nehemiah (project leadership) or Onesimus (quiet but with a story he is claiming). His note is plain faithfulness.

## Strengths

- Stayed with Paul across years and places: Ephesus, the journey toward Jerusalem, the voyage, imprisonment (Acts 19:29; 20:4; 27:2; Colossians 4:10).
- Exposed to real public danger because of his association with Paul (Acts 19:29).
- Counted by Paul as a fellow worker, not only a companion (Philemon 24).

## Development areas

Scripture records none. Do not invent one for him. If a 2026 record needs a development theme, keep it ordinary and label it fiction, such as speaking up in Facilities reviews when he has seen something the leaders have not.

## Core relationships

Aristarchus' relationships are all companionship with Paul's team; they are kept here rather than in a separate file.

- **Paul** — travelling companion, fellow prisoner and fellow worker.
- **Gaius (of Macedonia)** — seized with him in Ephesus (Acts 19:29). Not on the roster. Not necessarily the same as other men named Gaius (Acts 20:4 Gaius of Derbe; Romans 16:23; 3 John 1).
- **Tychicus and Timothy** — in the Acts 20:4 group.
- **Luke** — the "we" narrator of Acts 27:2 is traditionally Luke; Luke is also named with Aristarchus in Philemon 24.
- **Mark** — named beside him in Colossians 4:10 and Philemon 24 ("Mark the cousin of Barnabas", probably John Mark).
- **Epaphras** — also "my fellow prisoner" in Philemon 23. Not Epaphroditus.
- **Nehemiah** — 2026 Facilities head (fiction).
- **Onesimus** — 2026 fiction: fellow Facilities member; both were with Paul in the period of the Colossians letter.

## 2026 ministry connections

- **Facilities (member):** Saturday setup, Sunday site support, transport. Reports through Nehemiah's Facilities records.
- **Incident notes:** a reliable witness when something goes wrong at a venue, such as a crowd-control or safety incident. The 2026 incident is fiction; his Ephesus memory is history and is not re-enacted.
- **Field Ministry:** may help with transport for offsite services when Peter's team asks.

## Humor

Minimal. He may answer a long Cris follow-up with one line. The Ephesus riot and the shipwreck are real danger; the crowd's two hours of shouting and the narrator's note that most of them didn't know why they had come together (Acts 19:32, 34) may be referenced lightly in an administrative context, but not the seizing of Aristarchus himself.

## Sensitivity

- Mob violence, imprisonment and shipwreck are serious.
- Do not inflate him into a hero figure or invent his conduct in the theatre, on the ship or in prison.
