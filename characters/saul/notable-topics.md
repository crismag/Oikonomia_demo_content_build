# Saul — Notable Topics

Use these as source topics for later Oikonomia records; they are not prompts and do not imply exclusive ownership. Fuller retellings, truths and fact/inference notes are in `biblical-context.md`. Do not reduce Saul to a cartoon villain, and do not soften his gravest acts.

**Namesake:** King Saul son of Kish only. Saul of Tarsus is Paul.

## Calling and early promise
- **Lost donkeys and Samuel's table** — 1 Samuel 9. Ordinary errand; concern for his father; "Am I not a Benjamite, of the smallest of the tribes of Israel?"
- **Chosen as first king** — 1 Samuel 9–10. Private anointing; "God gave him another heart"; prophesying with the prophets; told his uncle about the donkeys, not the kingdom.
- **Hidden among the baggage; held his peace** — 1 Samuel 10:17–27. Public selection, insecurity, restraint toward critics.
- **Jabesh Gilead rescue** — 1 Samuel 11. Came in from the field behind his oxen; decisive early leadership and national unity; "No man shall be put to death today." Jabesh Gilead's loyalty at his death (31:11–13).
- **Victories on every side** — 1 Samuel 14:47–52. Sustained defence of Israel; delivered Israel from plunderers; recruited valiant men.

## Pressure, disobedience and rejection
- **Unlawful sacrifice / impatience** — 1 Samuel 13:1–15. Pressure, waiting, role boundaries, "I forced myself." Whether Samuel was late is debated.
- **Jonathan's victory and Saul's rash oath** — 1 Samuel 14. Poor policy under pressure; faint troops eating with the blood; Saul's first altar; nearly executing Jonathan; the people's intervention.
- **Amalek / incomplete obedience** — 1 Samuel 15. Selective obedience, monument at Carmel, blame, "I feared the people," "please honor me now before the elders," torn robe, rejection as king. The destruction command is grave and never applied to 2026.

## The troubling spirit, David and jealousy
- **The Spirit departs; troubling spirit; David's music** — 1 Samuel 16:14–23. Servants seek help; Saul loves David and makes him armour-bearer. No modern diagnosis; not comedy.
- **Goliath: fear and "Go!"** — 1 Samuel 17:11, 31–39, 55–58. Saul afraid; offers his armour; releases David.
- **The song and jealousy** — 1 Samuel 18:6–9. "What can he have more but the kingdom?" Another leader's success becoming a perceived threat.
- **Spear incidents** — 1 Samuel 18:10–11; 19:9–10; 20:33. Against David twice and against Jonathan. Serious.
- **Daughters used in plots** — 1 Samuel 18:17–29; 25:44. Merab withdrawn; Michal's bride-price; Michal later given to Palti.
- **Orders to kill David; Jonathan's appeal; broken oath** — 1 Samuel 19:1–17.
- **Naioth: prophesying before Samuel** — 1 Samuel 19:18–24. Stopped by the Spirit; never humor.
- **Jonathan conflict** — 1 Samuel 20:24–34. Family and succession tensions; insult and spear at his heir.

## Suspicion, pursuit and failed confession
- **Nob and Doeg** — 1 Samuel 21:1–9; 22:6–23. "there is none of you who is sorry for me"; guards refuse; eighty-five priests and a town killed. Catastrophic consequences of suspicion and command; never humor.
- **Pursuit of David** — 1 Samuel 23–26. Keilah, Ziph, Maon; "you have had compassion on me." Abuse of authority, unsafe leadership, obsession and repeated pursuit.
- **En Gedi confession** — 1 Samuel 24:16–22. "You are more righteous than I"; oath for his descendants.
- **The camp at Hachilah and "I have played the fool"** — 1 Samuel 26:21–25. Confession without lasting change; Saul stops pursuing when David goes to Gath (27:4).

## The end
- **Endor** — 1 Samuel 28; 1 Chronicles 10:13–14. Fear, God's silence, prohibited spiritual practice he had himself banned, desperation; the medium's meal. What appeared is debated.
- **Gilboa and death** — 1 Samuel 31; 1 Chronicles 10. Three sons killed; Saul takes his own life; body desecrated; Jabesh Gilead's night journey and burial. Grave; no dramatised detail.
- **The Amalekite's conflicting report** — 2 Samuel 1:1–16. Unresolved discrepancy; David executes him.
- **David's lament** — 2 Samuel 1:17–27. "Saul and Jonathan were lovely and pleasant in their lives."

## After Saul
- **House of Saul and long war** — 2 Samuel 2:8–4:12. Ishbosheth, Abner; decline of Saul's house.
- **Mephibosheth and Saul's land** — 2 Samuel 9. Kindness to Saul's grandson for Jonathan's sake.
- **Gibeonites, famine and Rizpah** — 2 Samuel 21:1–14. Saul's broken oath with consequences for his descendants; Rizpah's vigil; burial in Kish's tomb. Grave.
- **Later memory** — 2 Samuel 7:15; Psalms 18, 52, 54, 57, 59 superscriptions; Acts 13:21–22.

## Strong Oikonomia surfaces
- **Personal development (restricted):** waiting inside a role; honest accountability without image management; comparison and jealousy; rash commitments; suspicion.
- **Check-ins with Samuel (`Named people`):** the reporting line as a place of truth-telling, not punishment or restoration.
- **Mentorship Night:** a former senior leader learning in a room with emerging leaders; the danger of treating another's success as a threat.
- **Facilities work:** ordinary, practical service; setup and transport records that are simply good Facilities records.
- **Relationship reports (restricted):** Saul's perspective on David, written in 2026 without contradicting David's canonical accounts.
- **Crisis and legacy:** rarely, and seriously: fear when God seems silent; what a leader's decisions cost others.
