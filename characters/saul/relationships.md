# Saul — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Samuel
Samuel anointed Saul privately, kissed him, gave him signs, presented him to Israel and renewed the kingdom with him (1 Samuel 9:15–10:25; 11:14–15). Samuel confronted him at Gilgal (13:8–14) and after Amalek (15:10–33), where Saul grabbed and tore Samuel's robe and pleaded to be honoured before the elders. Samuel went back with him once to worship, then "came no more to see Saul until the day of his death, but Samuel mourned for Saul" (15:35). Saul lay prophesying before Samuel at Naioth (19:23–24). After Samuel's death Saul sought him at Endor (28, debated).

**2026 fiction:** Saul reports to Samuel. It is the most delicate reporting line in the church. Samuel prays for Saul and keeps a fortnightly restricted check-in; Saul arrives on time and says little at first. Neither pretends Gilgal, Amalek or the torn robe did not happen. The line is not a punishment and not a restoration of kingship; it is supervision with truth and prayer. Their records are `Only me` or `Named people`. Progress, if any, is slow and plain.

### David
Saul brought David into his service as a musician, "loved him greatly," made him armour-bearer (1 Samuel 16:21–22), released him to fight Goliath and offered his armour (17:37–39), and set him over the men of war (18:5). After the women's song Saul became jealous, threw spears at him (18:10–11; 19:9–10), plotted his death through bride-prices (18:17–27), ordered him killed (19:1), and pursued him for years (23–26). Twice David spared Saul's life, and twice Saul confessed and blessed him (24:16–22; 26:21–25), then pursuit resumed until David went to Gath (27:1–4). Saul gave Michal to another man (25:44). David mourned Saul and wrote a lament (2 Samuel 1).

**Canonical constraint:** David's existing records (see `targets/ot/saul.md`) state that Saul's apologies did not rebuild trust, that David kept his distance, and that he genuinely grieved Saul. Saul's records must not contradict them or claim a reconciliation Scripture does not record.

**2026 fiction:** David is a Bishop and Council member; Saul does not report to him. They are colleagues in the same church, and both are careful. David will not discuss Saul's history casually, and Saul does not ask David for absolution. Saul can read David's `Shared` reflections about him; he cannot read David's `Only me` records. Any 2026 interaction between them is rare, restricted and dignified. It must not become a redemption arc that implies their biblical story ended differently.

### Jonathan — son and heir (not on the roster)
Jonathan fought beside Saul (1 Samuel 13:2–3; 14), nearly died because of Saul's oath (14:24–45), loved David and defended him to Saul (19:1–7), and was insulted and targeted with a spear by his father (20:30–33). He died with Saul at Gilboa (31:2). David's lament says of them, "In their death, they were not divided" (2 Samuel 1:23). Refer to Jonathan in prose only.

**2026 note:** Jonathan is Saul's deepest grief and his clearest regret. Records about Jonathan are serious and restricted.

### Michal and Merab — daughters (not on the roster)
Saul promised Merab to David, then gave her to Adriel (1 Samuel 18:17–19). He gave Michal to David hoping the bride-price would get David killed (18:20–27), sent men to kill David at their house (19:11–17), and later gave her to Palti (25:44). Five of Merab's sons were among the seven descendants handed to the Gibeonites (2 Samuel 21:8).

### Ahinoam, Rizpah and Saul's household (not on the roster)
Saul's wife was Ahinoam daughter of Ahimaaz (1 Samuel 14:50). Rizpah, his concubine, guarded the bodies of her sons after their execution (2 Samuel 21:8–11). Ishbosheth reigned briefly over Israel after Saul's death (2 Samuel 2:8–10). Mephibosheth, Jonathan's son and Saul's grandson, received Saul's land from David (2 Samuel 9).

### Abner (not on the roster)
Commander of Saul's army, beside Saul at the table and in the camp (1 Samuel 14:50; 20:25; 26:5–7); later made Ishbosheth king (2 Samuel 2:8).

### Ahimelech, Doeg and the priests of Nob (not on the roster)
Saul accused Ahimelech of conspiracy, sentenced him and his father's house, and had Doeg kill them after the guards refused (1 Samuel 22:11–19). Abiathar escaped to David (22:20).

### The people of Jabesh Gilead (not on the roster)
Rescued by Saul at the start of his reign (1 Samuel 11); recovered and buried his body at the end (31:11–13); blessed by David for it (2 Samuel 2:4–7).

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Nehemiah
Facilities head. Nehemiah gives Saul clear assignments, a rota and honest feedback, and does not treat him as a former king or as a problem. Saul respects that Nehemiah leads through work rather than status.

### Caleb and Joshua
Facilities colleagues and experienced military men. Joshua knows what it is to wait for a senior leader (Exodus 24:13; 32:17); Caleb is known for wholehearted loyalty over a long life (Numbers 14:24; Joshua 14:6–14). Useful contrasts without turning them into lectures.

### Barnabas
Mentorship head. Barnabas' encouragement is real and not naive. At Mentorship Night Barnabas makes room for Saul in a group of emerging leaders without making him the object lesson.

### Gideon
Both began by protesting their family's smallness (Judges 6:15; 1 Samuel 9:21), and both have serious later complications. Their conversations at Mentorship Night can be honest about how early humility does not guarantee a faithful finish.

### Paul
Namesake and fellow Benjamite. Paul referred to Saul son of Kish in his Antioch sermon (Acts 13:21–22). **Saul has no memory of Paul.** In 2026 the shared name can cause a small, harmless roster clarification; it must never merge the two or make Saul's history a joke.

### Solomon
Both were kings who began with humility and whose kingdoms were torn because of disobedience (1 Samuel 15:28; 1 Kings 11:11). Saul's rejection is recorded in Samuel; Solomon's in Kings. Neither remembers the other historically.

### Cris
Cris schedules Facilities rotas and Samuel's check-ins and keeps Saul's ordinary records moving. Cris cannot see Saul's restricted records unless Saul names Cris.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Saul / Samuel:** restricted check-in notes; a slow, honest accountability thread; Samuel's prayer goal and Saul's truth-telling goal meeting in the same conversation.
- **Saul / Nehemiah:** ordinary Facilities setup and transport records where Saul is simply dependable.
- **Saul / Barnabas / Gideon:** Mentorship Night on comparison, humility and finishing.
- **Saul / David:** at most a rare, restricted acknowledgment that respects David's canonical records.
- **Saul / Paul / Cris:** a harmless roster name clarification.
