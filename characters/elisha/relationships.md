# Elisha — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### The LORD
Elisha repeats Elijah's phrase, "As the LORD lives, before whom I stand" (2 Kings 3:14; 5:16). His first act after Elijah was taken was to ask, "Where is the LORD, the God of Elijah?" (2 Kings 2:14). He prayed for a child's life, for a servant's eyes to open and for enemy soldiers' blindness and sight (2 Kings 4:33; 6:17–20).

### Elijah — mentor and "father"
Elijah called him by throwing his mantle over him (1 Kings 19:19). Elisha served him (1 Kings 19:21; 2 Kings 3:11), refused to leave him (2 Kings 2:2, 4, 6), asked for a double portion of his spirit, watched him taken and cried, "My father, my father" (2 Kings 2:9–12).

**Silence:** The years of service are unrecorded. Do not invent their conversations.

**Boundary:** Elisha remembers Elijah's departure and everything after it in his own ministry. Elijah remembers nothing after he was taken. In 2026 Elisha may tell Elijah about the crossing back over the Jordan, the prophets' search party and his own ministry.

**2026 fiction:** Elijah is a Mentorship leader; Elisha is a Field Ministry leader under Peter. Elisha still honours Elijah as a father in the faith and still finds him hard to locate. He is gentler and more practical than Elijah, and gently resists being treated as an assistant. When Elijah is exhausted, Elisha is one of the few people who simply brings food and stays.

### Gehazi — servant (not on the roster)
Elisha's servant in the Shunammite accounts (2 Kings 4:12–31), who noticed she had no son, tried to push her away, and carried Elisha's staff without result. He ran after Naaman, lied in Elisha's name, took silver and clothing, lied to Elisha, and was struck with Naaman's disease (2 Kings 5:20–27). Later he told the king about Elisha's great deeds as the Shunammite arrived (2 Kings 8:4–5); the chronology is unclear.

**Qualification:** Unnamed servants elsewhere (2 Kings 4:43; 6:15) are not identified as Gehazi. Traditions about Gehazi's later life are not Scripture.

Refer to Gehazi in prose only.

### The Shunammite woman and her household (not on the roster)
Hosted him and built him a room; received the promise of a son; lost the child and came to Elisha in anguish; received her son back; left during the famine at Elisha's word; recovered her land (2 Kings 4:8–37; 8:1–6). Her name is not recorded.

### The widow of one of the sons of the prophets (not on the roster)
Came to him about debt and her sons (2 Kings 4:1–7). Her name is not recorded.

### The sons of the prophets
Communities at Bethel, Jericho and Gilgal who recognised Elisha as Elijah's successor, ate with him in famine, built a larger meeting place with him, and carried out his instructions (2 Kings 2:15–18; 4:38–44; 6:1–7; 9:1–4).

### Naaman (not on the roster)
Syrian commander healed after washing in the Jordan; declared that there is no God in all the earth but in Israel; Elisha refused his gift and sent him away in peace (2 Kings 5:1–19).

### Jehoshaphat, king of Judah
In the Moab campaign Jehoshaphat asked for a prophet of the LORD and said of Elisha, "The LORD's word is with him." Elisha said he would not have looked at the king of Israel "were it not that I respect the presence of Jehoshaphat" (2 Kings 3:11–14).

**2026 fiction:** Jehoshaphat (Psalmists and Mentorship) and Elisha share mutual respect. Jehoshaphat's request for a prophet before a crisis and Elisha's "bring me a musician" make a natural, light Psalmists connection.

### The kings of Israel (not on the roster)
Elisha was blunt with Ahab's son Jehoram (2 Kings 3:13–14), who later blamed him for the siege and swore to behead him (2 Kings 6:31). Elisha stopped a king from killing captured soldiers (6:21–22). King Joash wept at his deathbed and called him "My father, my father" (13:14).

### Hazael and Ben-hadad (not on the roster)
Elisha wept before Hazael over what he would do to Israel (2 Kings 8:7–15).

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Peter — ministry head
Peter heads Field Ministry and is Elisha's reporting leader. Both carry households at their worst moments (Peter at Joppa with Tabitha's friends; Elisha at Shunem). Peter moves fast; Elisha asks one more question first. Peter admires Elisha's follow-up and occasionally asks why Elisha went himself when he could have sent someone. Elisha answers that sending the staff did not work.

**Boundary:** Neither remembers the other's ministry. Jesus cited Elisha and Naaman in Luke 4:27; Elisha has no memory of that, but Peter may mention it.

### Jonah — Field Ministry teammate
Jonah is a Field Ministry member under Peter. Naaman, a Syrian enemy commander, received mercy at Elisha's door; Jonah resented mercy to Nineveh. The contrast is a strong, serious conversation for Mentorship or Peter's coaching.

**Tradition, not Scripture:** Jewish tradition identifies Jonah with the young prophet Elisha sent to anoint Jehu (2 Kings 9:4). Do not build on it or present it as fact.

### Stephen and More Than Enough
Elisha identifies debt and food needs during visits and refers them to Stephen's team, which owns distribution. The widow's oil informs Elisha's instinct to ask what a household already has.

### Martha and Victuals
Poisoned stew and twenty loaves make Elisha an occasional, well-meaning source of Victuals food-safety comments.

### Nehemiah and Facilities
The borrowed axe head and the prophets' building project give Facilities a light, recurring thread about equipment loans.

### Mordecai, Andrew and Cornelius
Naaman's story informs cross-cultural welcome. Greeters and Ushers owns the operations; Elisha contributes pastoral perspective.

### Cris
Cris coordinates Field Ministry follow-ups and tool-loan reminders. Cris cannot see restricted household visit details unless named.
