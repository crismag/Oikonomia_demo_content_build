# Elisha — Biblical Context Library

This file records Elisha's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are Elisha's memories, testimony, service, hard moments and leadership formation.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

**Order note:** Many of the accounts in 2 Kings 4–8 may be grouped by theme rather than in strict time order. Do not build a record on their exact sequence.

---

## The call and the cloak — 1 Kings 19:16, 19–21

At Horeb God told Elijah to anoint "Elisha the son of Shaphat of Abel Meholah to be prophet in your place." Elijah found Elisha plowing with twelve yoke of oxen, Elisha himself driving the twelfth, and threw his mantle over him. Elisha left the oxen, ran after him and said, "Let me please kiss my father and my mother, and then I will follow you." Elijah answered, "Go back again; for what have I done to you?" Elisha went back, slaughtered his yoke of oxen, boiled the meat using the oxen's equipment and gave it to the people to eat. Then he "went after Elijah, and served him."

**Interpretation:** Elijah's answer is ambiguous. Burning the plowing equipment is widely read as leaving no way back; the text records the act, not Elisha's reason.

**Truths:** Elisha's calling began with ordinary work interrupted. He honoured his family, fed his community, and then entered years of service before he held any title.

**2026 use:** Testimony, Mentorship, calling from ordinary work, farewell meals and transitions (Events or Victuals operationally).

## Serving Elijah — 2 Kings 3:11

Years later, when kings needed a prophet in the desert, one of the king of Israel's servants described Elisha simply as the man "who poured water on the hands of Elijah."

**Silence:** Scripture records almost nothing of the years Elisha served Elijah. Do not invent their conversations.

**Truths:** His reputation began as a servant's reputation.

**2026 use:** Mentorship, humility in leadership development, the value of unseen years.

## "I will not leave you"; the double portion — 2 Kings 2:1–14

On the day the LORD was to take Elijah, Elijah told Elisha three times to stay behind: at Gilgal, Bethel and Jericho. Each time Elisha said, "As the LORD lives, and as your soul lives, I will not leave you." At Bethel and Jericho the sons of the prophets asked him whether he knew his master would be taken today. He said, "Yes, I know it. Hold your peace." At the Jordan Elijah struck the water with his rolled-up mantle and they crossed on dry ground.

Elijah said, "Ask what I shall do for you, before I am taken from you." Elisha said, "Please let a double portion of your spirit be on me." Elijah said it was a hard thing, but if Elisha saw him taken it would be granted. A chariot of fire and horses of fire separated them, and Elijah went up by a whirlwind into heaven. Elisha cried, "My father, my father, the chariots of Israel and its horsemen!" He saw him no more and tore his clothes in two. He picked up Elijah's mantle, went back to the Jordan, struck the water and said, "Where is the LORD, the God of Elijah?" The water parted and he crossed.

**Interpretation:** The double portion is often read in light of the firstborn son's inheritance (Deuteronomy 21:17): Elisha asked to be Elijah's true heir and successor.

**Truths:** Elisha stayed when he was given permission to leave. He grieved openly. His first act as successor was to ask where God was.

**2026 use:** Succession, Mentorship, grief for a mentor, testimony. Elijah has no memory of Elisha's cry or the crossing back; Elisha can tell him.

## Searching for Elijah; healing Jericho's water — 2 Kings 2:15–22

The prophets at Jericho said, "The spirit of Elijah rests on Elisha," and bowed before him. They wanted to send fifty strong men to search for Elijah in case the Spirit had dropped him on a mountain or in a valley. Elisha said, "Don't send them." They pressed him until he was embarrassed, so he let them go. They searched three days and found nothing. He said, "Didn't I tell you, 'Don't go'?"

The men of Jericho told him the city was pleasant but the water was bad and the land unproductive. He asked for a new jar with salt in it, threw the salt into the spring and said the LORD had healed the water. It stayed healed.

**Truths:** A new leader can know something and still give way under pressure; Elisha let them learn. His first public act of care was for a town's water supply.

**2026 use:** Gentle humor about a search party sent against advice; leadership under pressure from a team; practical community needs.

## The youths at Bethel — 2 Kings 2:23–25

On the road up to Bethel, "some youths came out of the city and mocked him, and said to him, 'Go up, you baldy! Go up, you baldy!'" Elisha turned, looked at them and "cursed them in the LORD's name. Then two female bears came out of the woods and mauled forty-two of those youths." He went on to Mount Carmel and then back to Samaria.

**Interpretation and uncertainty:** The Hebrew phrase translated "youths" can refer to boys or young men; their age is disputed. Some readers see the event as judgment on organised contempt for God's prophet at Bethel, where Jeroboam had set up a golden calf (1 Kings 12:28–29), and read "Go up" as mocking Elijah's being taken up. Others find it deeply troubling. The text does not say whether any died, and does not comment further.

**Truths:** Scripture records this without softening it. The account is part of Elisha's history and must be handled honestly, not explained away or exploited.

**2026 use:** Rarely, and seriously: difficult passages in teaching or Lifegroup. **Never comedy. Never a joke about baldness, bears or mockery.** Do not use it to model a response to criticism in 2026.

## Water in the desert; Jehoshaphat and the Moab campaign — 2 Kings 3:9–27

The kings of Israel, Judah and Edom marched against Moab and ran out of water for seven days. Jehoshaphat of Judah asked whether a prophet of the LORD was there, and Elisha was named. Jehoshaphat said, "The LORD's word is with him." Elisha told the king of Israel to go to the prophets of his father and mother, and said, "were it not that I respect the presence of Jehoshaphat the king of Judah, I would not look toward you." He asked for a musician. While the musician played, "the LORD's hand came on him." He told them to dig trenches in the valley; water would fill it without wind or rain, and Moab would be handed over. Water came in the morning. The Moabites saw the sun reflecting red on it, thought the kings had slaughtered one another, rushed to plunder and were defeated. At the end, the king of Moab sacrificed his oldest son on the city wall, and Israel withdrew.

**Truths:** Elisha's respect for a faithful king shaped his willingness to help. He was blunt with an unfaithful one.

**2026 use:** Jehoshaphat relationship (fiction), worship and prophetic ministry (the musician, light), preparing trenches before the water comes. The sacrifice of the Moabite king's son is grave.

## The widow's oil — 2 Kings 4:1–7

The widow of one of the sons of the prophets cried out to Elisha: her husband, who feared the LORD, had died, and a creditor was coming to take her two children as slaves. Elisha said, "What should I do for you? Tell me, what do you have in the house?" She had nothing except a pot of oil. He told her to borrow empty containers from all her neighbours, "Don't borrow just a few," shut the door, and pour. She poured until her son said there were no more containers, and the oil stopped. Elisha said, "Go, sell the oil, and pay your debt; and you and your sons live on the rest."

**Truths:** Elisha started with a question about what she had. The neighbours were part of the provision. The outcome was debt cleared, children kept, and a household able to live, not a one-off handout.

**2026 use:** Field Ministry household visits, debt and practical need, referral to More Than Enough (which owns distribution), Lifegroup on provision. Debt and the threat of losing children to slavery are serious.

## The Shunammite woman: a room, a promise, a death and a restoration — 2 Kings 4:8–37

A prominent woman in Shunem persuaded Elisha to eat with her household whenever he passed. She said to her husband, "I perceive that this is a holy man of God who passes by us continually," and suggested a small room on the roof with "a bed, a table, a chair, and a lamp stand." Elisha, through his servant Gehazi, asked what could be done for her in return, even a word to the king or the army commander. She answered, "I dwell among my own people." Gehazi observed that she had no son and her husband was old. Elisha told her that at this time next year she would hold a son. She said, "No, my lord, you man of God, do not lie to your servant." She bore a son as he had said.

When the child was older, he went out to his father among the reapers and cried, "My head! My head!" He was carried to his mother, sat on her lap until noon, and died. She laid him on Elisha's bed, shut the door, and rode to Elisha at Mount Carmel, telling her husband only, "It's all right," and her servant not to slow down. When Gehazi was sent to ask whether all was well, she said, "It is well." When she reached Elisha she caught hold of his feet. Gehazi came to push her away, but Elisha said, "Leave her alone, for her soul is troubled within her; and the LORD has hidden it from me, and has not told me." She said, "Did I ask you for a son, my lord? Didn't I say, 'Do not deceive me'?"

Elisha sent Gehazi ahead with his staff to lay on the child's face. The mother said, "As the LORD lives, and as your soul lives, I will not leave you," so Elisha went himself. Gehazi's attempt brought no response. Elisha went in, shut the door, prayed, and lay on the child, mouth to mouth, eyes to eyes, hands to hands. The child's body grew warm. Elisha walked back and forth in the house, stretched himself on the child again, and the boy sneezed seven times and opened his eyes. Elisha called her and said, "Take up your son." She fell at his feet, bowed to the ground, picked up her son and went out.

**Note:** The mother's words "I will not leave you" are the same words Elisha had said to Elijah (2 Kings 2:2, 4, 6).

**Truths:** Hospitality was offered freely and without asking for anything. Elisha did not pretend to know what God had not shown him. Grief was allowed to be angry. Sending a helper was not the same as going himself.

**2026 use:** Serious: Field Ministry, grief care, hospitality, honesty about not knowing. The rooftop room can be light as a hospitality example; the child's death never is. Never reenact a raising from the dead in 2026.

## Famine at Gilgal: poisoned stew and twenty loaves — 2 Kings 4:38–44

During a famine, Elisha was at Gilgal with the sons of the prophets. He told his servant to put on the large pot and cook stew. Someone gathered wild gourds without recognising them and cut them into the pot. As the men ate they cried, "Man of God, there is death in the pot!" Elisha had meal thrown in and said to serve it, and "there was nothing harmful in the pot." A man from Baal Shalishah brought twenty barley loaves of firstfruits and fresh grain. Elisha said to give it to the people. His servant said, "What, should I set this before a hundred men?" Elisha repeated the instruction with the LORD's word that they would eat and have some left over, and they did.

**Truths:** Elisha shared meals with the community he led during famine. A gift intended for the prophet went to feed everyone.

**2026 use:** Victuals (food safety, feeding a group from limited supplies) and More Than Enough (donations shared outward). Operational work belongs to those ministries. The stew can carry gentle humor; the famine cannot.

## Naaman — 2 Kings 5:1–19; Luke 4:27

Naaman commanded the army of Syria and was highly honoured, but he had leprosy (a serious skin disease). A young Israelite girl, taken captive in a Syrian raid, served his wife and said she wished her master could see the prophet in Samaria. The king of Syria sent Naaman with a letter and great wealth to the king of Israel, who tore his clothes: "Am I God, to kill and to make alive?" He assumed it was a pretext for war. Elisha sent word: "Let him come now to me, and he shall know that there is a prophet in Israel."

Naaman arrived with horses and chariots at Elisha's door. Elisha sent a messenger: "Go and wash in the Jordan seven times." Naaman left in a rage. He had expected Elisha to come out, call on God and wave his hand over the place, and he said the rivers of Damascus were better. His servants said, "My father, if the prophet had asked you do some great thing, wouldn't you have done it? How much rather then, when he says to you, 'Wash, and be clean'?" He washed seven times and his flesh was restored "like the flesh of a little child."

He returned and said, "See now, I know that there is no God in all the earth, but in Israel," and urged Elisha to accept a gift. Elisha said, "As the LORD lives, before whom I stand, I will receive none." Naaman asked for two mule-loads of earth to take home for worshipping the LORD, and asked pardon in advance for bowing when his master leaned on his arm in the temple of Rimmon. Elisha said, "Go in peace."

**Interpretation:** Elisha's "Go in peace" is not explained. Readers differ on whether it approves Naaman's request or simply sends him off with a blessing. Do not settle it.

**Truths:** God's help reached a foreign enemy commander through a captive girl and his own servants. Humility meant accepting a simple instruction. Elisha refused payment so that the healing could not be treated as a transaction.

**2026 use:** Cross-cultural welcome (Greeters and Ushers, Reach-Out), humility, integrity about gifts, the voice of junior people. The captive girl's situation is serious. Jesus later cited Naaman (Luke 4:27); Elisha has no memory of that.

## Gehazi — 2 Kings 5:20–27

Gehazi said to himself that his master had let Naaman off without taking anything: "As the LORD lives, I will run after him, and take something from him." Naaman got down from his chariot to meet him. Gehazi said Elisha had sent him because two young prophets had just arrived and needed a talent of silver and two changes of clothing. Naaman insisted on two talents and sent servants to carry them. Gehazi hid the goods at the house and went in to Elisha. "Where did you come from, Gehazi?" "Your servant went nowhere." Elisha said, "Didn't my heart go with you when the man turned from his chariot to meet you? Is it a time to receive money, and to receive garments, and olive groves and vineyards, and sheep and cattle, and male servants and female servants?" He declared that Naaman's leprosy would cling to Gehazi and his descendants forever, and Gehazi left "a leper, as white as snow."

**Truths:** Gehazi used his master's name to take what his master had refused, invented a need to justify it, and lied when asked directly. The damage was not only money: it misrepresented God's free mercy to a new believer.

**2026 use:** Serious: staff and volunteer integrity, misuse of a leader's name, receiving gifts in ministry, restricted accountability reviews. Gehazi's disease is not a joke.

## The floating axe head — 2 Kings 6:1–7

The sons of the prophets told Elisha that the place where they met with him was too small, and asked to go to the Jordan to cut beams and build a new place. He said, "Go!" One asked him to come, and he did. While cutting a tree, one man's iron axe head fell into the water: "Alas, my master! For it was borrowed." Elisha asked where it fell, cut a stick, threw it in, and the iron floated. "Take it."

**Truths:** A borrowed tool mattered to the man who lost it, and Elisha treated it as worth attention. He went along when invited.

**2026 use:** Light: equipment loans, Facilities, a team building its own space. The man's distress about a borrowed item is real but not grave.

## Seeing the army around Dothan; feeding the enemy — 2 Kings 6:8–23

The king of Syria kept planning ambushes, and Elisha kept warning the king of Israel where not to go. The Syrian king suspected a traitor, but was told that Elisha told Israel's king even the words spoken in his bedroom. He sent horses, chariots and a large army by night to surround Dothan. Elisha's servant saw them in the morning: "Alas, my master! What shall we do?" Elisha said, "Don't be afraid, for those who are with us are more than those who are with them." He prayed, "LORD, please open his eyes, that he may see," and the servant saw the mountain full of horses and chariots of fire around Elisha.

Elisha prayed for the soldiers to be struck with blindness, told them this was not the right city, and led them into Samaria, where their eyes were opened. The king of Israel asked, "My father, shall I strike them? Shall I strike them?" Elisha said, "You shall not strike them," and told him to set bread and water before them and send them home. The king prepared a great feast, sent them back, "So the bands of Syria stopped raiding the land of Israel."

**Note:** The servant here is unnamed. Do not identify him as Gehazi.

**Truths:** Elisha's calm came from seeing what was actually there, and he prayed for his frightened helper to see it too. Mercy to captured enemies ended the raids.

**2026 use:** Leading a frightened team, mentoring, prayer, reconciliation and hospitality to opponents (Victuals operationally for any meal).

## The siege of Samaria and the end of the famine — 2 Kings 6:24–7:20

Ben-hadad of Syria besieged Samaria. Food prices became absurd: a donkey's head for eighty pieces of silver. A woman cried to the king on the wall. She said she and another woman had agreed to eat their sons; they had boiled and eaten hers, and now the other woman had hidden her son. The king tore his clothes, revealing sackcloth underneath, and swore to have Elisha beheaded that day. Elisha, sitting with the elders in his house, knew the messenger was coming and had the door held shut.

Elisha announced that by the same time tomorrow fine flour and barley would sell cheaply at Samaria's gate. The officer on whose arm the king leaned scoffed that it could not happen even if the LORD made windows in heaven. Elisha said he would see it but not eat it. That night four men with leprosy at the city gate decided to surrender to the Syrians rather than starve, and found the camp empty: the Lord had made the Syrians hear the sound of a great army, and they had fled. After eating and hiding some plunder, the four men said, "We aren't doing right. Today is a day of good news, and we keep silent," and told the city. The people plundered the camp, prices fell exactly as Elisha had said, and the scoffing officer was trampled to death in the gate.

**Truths:** In extreme suffering, the king's anger landed on the prophet. Relief came first to outsiders at the gate, and they chose not to keep good news to themselves.

**2026 use:** Serious: crisis reports, famine relief themes for More Than Enough, sharing good news, blame in crisis. **The account of the mothers is grave and must never be used lightly.** The officer's death is not a joke.

## The Shunammite's land restored — 2 Kings 8:1–6

Elisha had warned the Shunammite woman of a seven-year famine and told her to leave. She lived in Philistine territory for seven years. When she returned to appeal to the king for her house and land, the king happened to be asking Gehazi to tell him Elisha's great deeds, and Gehazi was describing the restoration of her son as she walked in. The king appointed an officer to restore all her property and the income of her fields.

**Truths:** Elisha's care for the household continued long after the crisis. Timing and testimony worked together for a woman seeking justice.

**2026 use:** Long-term follow-up with families, advocacy, housing and property concerns (handled with care and confidentiality).

## Hazael and Elisha's tears — 2 Kings 8:7–15

Elisha came to Damascus while Ben-hadad was ill. Hazael brought forty camel-loads of gifts to ask whether the king would recover. Elisha said to tell him he would recover, "however the LORD has shown me that he will surely die." He stared at Hazael until Hazael was ashamed, "Then the man of God wept." He told Hazael he knew the evil Hazael would do to Israel: burning strongholds, killing young men, and killing children and pregnant women. Hazael protested; Elisha said the LORD had shown him Hazael would be king. The next day Hazael smothered Ben-hadad and took the throne.

**Interpretation:** The meaning of Elisha's double message about recovery is debated.

**Truths:** Elisha wept over suffering he could see coming to his people.

**2026 use:** Serious only: lament, foresight and grief in leadership. Never light.

## Sending a young prophet to anoint Jehu — 2 Kings 9:1–10

Elisha sent one of the sons of the prophets to Ramoth Gilead with a flask of oil to anoint Jehu king over Israel privately and then run. Jehu's rise brought the end of Ahab's house, with great bloodshed.

**Truths:** Elisha delegated a dangerous task with very clear instructions.

**2026 use:** Background only. The bloodshed that followed is not material for delegation humor.

## The arrows and Elisha's death — 2 Kings 13:14–21

Elisha fell sick with the illness from which he died. King Joash of Israel came and wept over him: "My father, my father, the chariots of Israel and its horsemen!", the same words Elisha had cried when Elijah was taken. Elisha had the king take a bow, placed his hands on the king's hands, had him shoot an arrow east as "the LORD's arrow of victory" over Syria, then told him to strike the ground with the arrows. Joash struck three times and stopped. Elisha was angry: five or six strikes would have meant complete victory; now there would be only three.

Elisha died and was buried. Later, when a burial party saw raiders, they threw a dead man into Elisha's tomb, and when he touched Elisha's bones he came to life.

**Truths:** Elisha was still mentoring a king on his deathbed, including hands on hands. He grieved half-hearted effort.

**Boundary:** The man revived at the tomb is after Elisha's death; Elisha has no memory of it. In 2026 Elisha is alive and serving; his death is reflection, not an event.

**2026 use:** Legacy, finishing well, mentoring through illness, encouraging wholehearted follow-through. Not light.
