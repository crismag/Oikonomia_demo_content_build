# Elisha — Notable Topics

Use these as source topics for later Oikonomia records; they are not prompts and do not imply exclusive ownership. Fuller retellings, truths and fact/inference notes are in `biblical-context.md`.

## Call, formation and succession
- **Called from plowing / follows Elijah** — 1 Kings 19:19–21. Leaving prior work and entering mentorship; farewell meal from his own oxen; "what have I done to you?"
- **Pouring water on Elijah's hands** — 2 Kings 3:11. Reputation formed as a servant; unrecorded years of service.
- **Elijah's departure / mantle and double portion request** — 2 Kings 2:1–14. "I will not leave you" three times; succession and responsibility; "My father, my father"; "Where is the LORD, the God of Elijah?"
- **Search party sent against advice** — 2 Kings 2:15–18. New leader pressured by a team; "Didn't I tell you, 'Don't go'?" Gentle humor possible.
- **Healing Jericho's water** — 2 Kings 2:19–22. First practical care for a town.
- **Youths at Bethel and the bears** — 2 Kings 2:23–25. Grave and debated in interpretation; ages disputed. Never comedy.

## Households and practical care
- **Widow's oil** — 2 Kings 4:1–7. Debt, children threatened with slavery, "What do you have in the house?", neighbours' containers, provision that clears the debt and sustains the family.
- **Shunammite woman: hospitality and promised son** — 2 Kings 4:8–17. Rooftop room; "I dwell among my own people"; care offered without asking for anything.
- **Shunammite's son dies and is restored** — 2 Kings 4:18–37. A mother's anguish, "Leave her alone," "the LORD has hidden it from me," a helper's staff not enough; high sensitivity.
- **Poisoned stew and feeding one hundred** — 2 Kings 4:38–44. Victuals/provision and crisis response during famine; a firstfruits gift shared with everyone.
- **Shunammite's land restored** — 2 Kings 8:1–6. Long-term follow-up; famine relocation; advocacy with the king.

## Nations, kings and integrity
- **Water and the Moab campaign with Jehoshaphat** — 2 Kings 3:9–27. Respect for a faithful king; "bring me a musician"; trenches before water. The Moabite king's sacrifice of his son is grave.
- **Naaman** — 2 Kings 5:1–19; Luke 4:27. Cross-border encounter, a captive girl's witness, pride and humility, simple instruction, healing, refused payment, two mule-loads of earth, "Go in peace."
- **Gehazi's greed** — 2 Kings 5:20–27. Staff ethics, deception, misuse of a leader's name, invented need, lasting consequence.
- **Floating axe head** — 2 Kings 6:1–7. Borrowed equipment and loss; a prophets' building project; harmless administrative humor possible.
- **Aramean intelligence / surrounded city** — 2 Kings 6:8–23. Discernment, security, "those who are with us are more," praying for a frightened servant's eyes to open, mercy and a feast for captured enemies.
- **Siege/famine and later relief** — 2 Kings 6:24–7:20. Severe crisis; a mother's report of cannibalism (grave); king blames Elisha; four men with skin disease share good news; scoffing officer's death. No comedy around starvation.
- **Hazael and Elisha's tears** — 2 Kings 8:7–15. Grief over foreseen atrocities. Serious.
- **Sending a young prophet to anoint Jehu** — 2 Kings 9:1–10. Clear delegation of a dangerous task; bloodshed follows.

## Final season
- **Arrows of victory and Elisha's death** — 2 Kings 13:14–21. Mentoring a king from his deathbed, hands on hands; frustration at half-hearted effort; "My father, my father" said over Elisha. The revival at his tomb is after his death; he has no memory of it.

## Strong Oikonomia surfaces
- **Field Ministry reports:** household visits, families in crisis, grief, long-term follow-up (Shunammite pattern), offsite services.
- **Lifegroup Gathering reports:** hospitality, practical needs and provision in a home-based group.
- **Referrals and cross-ministry coordination:** debt and need to More Than Enough; meals and food safety to Victuals; newcomer and cross-cultural welcome to Greeters and Ushers.
- **Mentorship:** succession from Elijah; developing helpers; staying with a mentor; mentoring to the end.
- **Staff and volunteer accountability:** Gehazi-style misuse of a leader's name or gifts, in restricted records.
- **Equipment and Facilities:** borrowed tools, building projects (light).
- **Crisis reports:** famine and siege themes, handled seriously.
- **Comments:** tool-loan follow-ups from Facilities; Psalmists asked for a musician at short notice; Peter's questions about why Elisha went himself instead of sending someone.
