# Caleb — 2026 Goals

**Oikonomia section:** Goals

These are Caleb's 2026 Oikonomia goals. They come from his lived biblical history and are meant to gather progress through Facilities work, Mentorship Night, his Lifegroup and his friendship with Joshua. Progress entries are added as records are written; none are recorded yet.

---

## Goal 1 — Report what I saw, and what God said

**Status:** Active  
**Ministry:** Facilities  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #serious #courage #faith

### Why this matters

When Moses sent twelve of us into Canaan, we all saw the same things: fruit so heavy two men carried one cluster on a pole, and the sons of Anak at Hebron behind walls. I told Moses the truth as it was in my heart and said we could go up at once. Ten of my brothers made the people's hearts melt. The congregation wanted to stone Joshua and me.

Honest reporting was not pretending the walls were small. It was refusing to leave God out of the report.

### 2026 actions

- Write Facilities assessments that name every real risk plainly, and say what we believe we can do about it.
- Don't soften a report to keep a room comfortable, and don't exaggerate one to win an argument.
- When I disagree with a majority recommendation, say so in the meeting and in the record, with reasons.
- Ask Joshua to tell me if my certainty is running ahead of the evidence.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 13:17–33; Numbers 14:6–10; Joshua 14:7–8.

## Goal 2 — Keep asking for the hill country

**Status:** Active  
**Ministry:** Facilities  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #confidence #courage #gratitude

### Why this matters

I waited forty-five years for what God promised at Kadesh. When the time came, I was eighty-five. I told Joshua I was as strong as the day Moses sent me, and I didn't ask for the easy ground. I asked for the hill country where the Anakim lived behind great fortified cities: it may be that the LORD will be with me, and I will drive them out.

Experience is not a reason to take the comfortable assignment. It is a reason to take the one that needs someone who has seen hard ground before.

### 2026 actions

- Volunteer for the difficult Facilities jobs: outdoor sites, heavy moves, problem rooms, the long setup days.
- Keep the humility in "it may be": pray before I commit, and accept Nehemiah's safety decisions without sulking.
- Finish old commitments that have been sitting open, especially ones other people have forgotten.
- Say no to an assignment only when it belongs better to someone else, not because it is hard.

**Biblical account:** Numbers 14:24; Joshua 14:6–15; Joshua 15:13–14.

## Goal 3 — Give the next generation a real Debir, and ask what they need

**Status:** Active  
**Ministry:** Mentorship  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #pastoral #hope #compassion

### Why this matters

After Hebron there was still Debir to take, and I didn't take it myself. Othniel did, and later God raised him up to deliver Israel. When my daughter Achsah came to me and I asked her, "What do you want?", she asked for springs of water, because the land I had given her was dry. I gave her the upper springs and the lower springs.

I am good at taking the hard job. I need to be as good at handing a hard job to someone younger, and at listening when they tell me what they actually need to do it.

### 2026 actions

- At Mentorship Night, give at least two younger leaders a real Facilities responsibility with a hard edge to it, not just carrying.
- Ask each of them, early and plainly, what they need, and provide it.
- Stop stepping in to finish their job for them because I could do it faster.
- Share testimony about waiting and honest reporting when it helps someone afraid of their assignment.

**Biblical account:** Joshua 15:15–19; Judges 1:12–15; Judges 3:9–11.
