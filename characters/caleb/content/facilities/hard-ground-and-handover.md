## 2026-04-12 — Outdoor Site Assessment: Name the Slope, Then Make a Plan

**Record type:** Facilities / Site and Risk Assessment  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Joshua, Nehemiah  
**Status:** Approved with Conditions  
**Tags:** #administrative #serious #courage #discernment

The east-lawn service site is usable, but it is not level. The access route narrows after rain, the tent anchors need a marked boundary, and the loading path cannot take both the sound equipment and the hospitality delivery at once. None of that is a reason to call the site impossible. It is a reason to stop describing it from the parking lot and assign the work in order.

I recommend that Facilities own the ground preparation and transport window, while Production confirms its own equipment load-in and Events owns the gathering layout. I will take the early site walk and the heavy anchor work; that does not make the rest of the work mine.

When Joshua and I explored Canaan, we did not deny the fortified cities or the people who lived there. Caleb's report in Numbers says the obstacles were real; our disagreement with the other scouts was about whether those obstacles had the final word against God's promise. This 2026 assessment is not that ancient expedition. It is a small current example of the same discipline: tell the truth about the terrain, then act faithfully with the truth.

**Attention:** Do not schedule simultaneous deliveries through the east access route.

## 2026-06-23 — Mentorship Night: Give Someone Else the Hard End

**Oikonomia section:** Meeting Notes  
**Kind:** Minutes  
**Meeting type:** Coaching  
**Facilitator:** Caleb  
**Note taker:** Caleb  
**Participants:** Caleb, Joshua, Hezekiah  
**Tags:** #pastoral #serious #hope

### Discussion

I brought the outdoor-site work because I was about to take every difficult piece myself. Joshua reminded me that being willing is not the same as being the only person who can be trusted. Hezekiah asked for the load-plan responsibility and named the information he needed: the confirmed equipment list, the access times, and one experienced person for the first unload.

I remembered Debir, where Othniel—not I—took the remaining hard place. Scripture later names him as a deliverer. The account does not turn delegation into a slogan, and it says nothing about a 2026 Facilities team. It does show that a hard responsibility can become an opportunity for another person to act. Achsah's request for springs also corrects my habit of assigning a task before asking what support is needed.

### Decisions

- Hezekiah will lead the first unload and report what needs changing afterward.
- Caleb will remain available for the anchor work but will not take over the load plan unless a safety issue requires it.
- Joshua will review the handover after the event, not only the event outcome.
