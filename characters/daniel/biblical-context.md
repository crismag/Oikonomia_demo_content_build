# Daniel — Biblical Context Library

This file records Daniel's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are Daniel's memories, prayer, service under foreign kings, and visions he did not fully understand.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or inference rather than explicit text, it is marked. Daniel's visions are symbolic and widely debated; 2026 records must never treat them as predictions about current events.

---

## Taken, trained and renamed — Daniel 1:1–7

In the third year of Jehoiakim king of Judah, Nebuchadnezzar of Babylon besieged Jerusalem. Some of the vessels from God's house were taken to the treasury of Nebuchadnezzar's god. The king told Ashpenaz, master of his eunuchs, to bring young men "of the royal offspring and of the nobles… in whom was no defect, but well-favored, skillful in all wisdom," to be taught the language and literature of the Chaldeans for three years, fed from the king's food and wine, and then to serve the king. Among them were Daniel, Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah. The chief official renamed them: Daniel became Belteshazzar; the others became Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego.

**Silence:** Daniel's age, parents and whether he was made a eunuch are not stated.

**Truths:** Exile took Daniel's home, his name and his education and put them under a foreign king's control. What he did next shows he had not given up who he was.

**2026 use:** Radicals conversations with young adults entering institutions that shape identity; testimony. Exile is not comedy.

## A ten-day test — Daniel 1:8–16

"But Daniel purposed in his heart that he would not defile himself with the king's delicacies, nor with the wine which he drank." He asked the chief official's permission. God gave Daniel favour and compassion with the official, but the official was afraid: if the young men looked worse than the others, "you would endanger my head with the king." Daniel went to the steward assigned over the four and proposed: "Test your servants, I beg you, ten days; and let them give us vegetables to eat and water to drink. Then let our faces be examined before you." After ten days they looked healthier than the others, and the steward kept giving them vegetables.

**Silence:** The reason eating the king's food would defile him is not stated.

**Truths:** Daniel decided first, in his heart. He asked rather than defied. He took the official's fear seriously and proposed a limited, testable alternative that protected the official too.

**2026 use:** Radicals; Mentorship on conscience with courtesy; personal development. **Not** diet advice; Victuals owns food.

## Wisdom and standing before the king — Daniel 1:17–21

"God gave them knowledge and skill in all learning and wisdom; and Daniel had understanding in all visions and dreams." At the end of the training the king found none equal to the four, and "ten times better than all the magicians and enchanters who were in all his realm." "Daniel continued even to the first year of King Cyrus."

**Truths:** Faithfulness and excellence were not opposites. Daniel's service outlasted the empire that took him.

**2026 use:** Integrity and competence together; long faithfulness through leadership changes (light humor available about many reporting-line changes).

## The dream no one could tell — Daniel 2:1–23

Nebuchadnezzar had a troubling dream and demanded that his magicians, enchanters, sorcerers and Chaldeans tell him both the dream and its meaning, threatening to have them "cut in pieces" if they could not. They protested that no one on earth could do that. The king, furious, ordered all the wise men of Babylon destroyed, and Daniel and his companions were sought to be killed.

"Then Daniel returned answer with counsel and prudence to Arioch the captain of the king's guard." He asked why the decree was so urgent, went in and asked the king for time, then went home and told Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah, so they would "desire mercies of the God of heaven concerning this secret." The secret was revealed to Daniel in a night vision, and he blessed God: "He changes the times and the seasons. He removes kings and sets up kings. He gives wisdom to the wise, and knowledge to those who have understanding. He reveals the deep and secret things."

**Truths:** In a crisis Daniel stayed calm, asked a good question, asked for time, and asked his friends to pray. He praised God before he went to the king.

**2026 use:** Crisis response; team prayer; Radicals leadership under pressure. The threatened executions are not comedy.

## Not revealed for any wisdom of his own — Daniel 2:24–49

Daniel asked Arioch not to destroy the wise men and to bring him to the king. The king asked if he could reveal the dream. Daniel answered that no wise man could, "but there is a God in heaven who reveals secrets… But as for me, this secret is not revealed to me for any wisdom that I have more than any living." He described a great statue of gold, silver, bronze, iron and iron mixed with clay, struck by a stone cut out without hands that became a mountain filling the earth, and interpreted it as a succession of kingdoms after Nebuchadnezzar's, ended by a kingdom God would set up "which will never be destroyed."

The king fell on his face before Daniel, ordered offerings to him, and said, "Of a truth your God is the God of gods, and the Lord of kings, and a revealer of secrets." He made Daniel ruler over the province of Babylon and chief over its wise men. At Daniel's request his three companions were appointed over the province's affairs, "but Daniel was in the king's gate."

**Interpretation:** The identity of the kingdoms is debated. Do not settle it.

**Truths:** Daniel refused credit and gave it to God. He saved the lives of the wise men who had failed. He used his promotion to advocate for his companions.

**2026 use:** Giving credit; advocating for teammates; Radicals leadership. Not a 2026 prophecy chart.

## The furnace (Daniel not present) — Daniel 3

Nebuchadnezzar set up a golden image and commanded all officials to worship it at the sound of music, on pain of the furnace. Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego refused: "our God whom we serve is able to deliver us… But if not… we will not serve your gods." They were thrown into the furnace, which killed the men who threw them in, and were seen walking unharmed with a fourth figure "like a son of the gods."

**Boundary:** Daniel is not mentioned in chapter 3. Do not place him there or explain his absence. Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah are not on the Data Play roster.

**2026 use:** Daniel may speak of his companions' courage with admiration, but not as an eyewitness.

## The tree cut down and hard counsel — Daniel 4

Nebuchadnezzar tells, in his own voice, of a dream of a great tree that fed and sheltered all living things, cut down by a heavenly watcher's decree, its stump bound, and "seven times" passing over it, "to the intent that the living may know that the Most High rules in the kingdom of men." When the king told Daniel the dream, "Daniel… was stricken mute for a while, and his thoughts troubled him." The king told him not to be troubled. Daniel answered, "My lord, may the dream be for those who hate you, and its interpretation to your adversaries." Then he told the king plainly that the tree was the king, who would be driven from people to live like an animal until he acknowledged that the Most High rules. He added: "O king, let my counsel be acceptable to you, and break off your sins by righteousness, and your iniquities by showing mercy to the poor. Perhaps there may be a lengthening of your tranquility."

A year later, boasting over "great Babylon, which I have built… by the might of my power," the king was driven out and lived like an animal until his understanding returned and he praised the Most High.

**Qualification:** Do not attach a medical or psychological diagnosis to Nebuchadnezzar's condition.

**Truths:** Daniel did not enjoy delivering bad news to a powerful man; he wished it were for the king's enemies. He still told the whole truth, and urged repentance expressed in justice and mercy to the poor.

**2026 use:** Delivering difficult counsel to a senior leader respectfully; Mentorship on truth-telling with care.

## The writing on the wall — Daniel 5

King Belshazzar held a feast for a thousand officials and, while drinking, had the gold and silver vessels taken from Jerusalem's temple brought so that he, his officials, wives and concubines could drink from them while praising idols. Fingers appeared and wrote on the plaster wall. The terrified king offered purple, a gold chain and the third place in the kingdom to anyone who could read it; none of his wise men could. The queen remembered Daniel, in whom "light and understanding and wisdom" had been found in Nebuchadnezzar's day.

Daniel told the king, "Let your gifts be to yourself, and give your rewards to another. Nevertheless, I will read the writing." He reminded Belshazzar how Nebuchadnezzar had been humbled, and said: "You, his son, Belshazzar, have not humbled your heart, though you knew all this." The writing was "MENE, MENE, TEKEL, UPHARSIN": God had numbered his kingdom and ended it; he had been weighed and found wanting; his kingdom was divided and given to the Medes and Persians. Belshazzar had Daniel clothed in purple anyway. "In that night Belshazzar the Chaldean King was slain."

**Qualification:** Extra-biblical sources name Belshazzar as Nabonidus' son; "father" and "son" may mean predecessor and successor.

**Truths:** Daniel was not for sale. He named the specific wrong: knowing the history and not humbling himself. Institutional memory (the queen's) brought the right person into the room.

**2026 use:** Integrity about gifts and favours; learning from history rather than repeating it; gentle humor about declined gifts being given anyway. Belshazzar's death is not comedy.

## No fault found; the decree; prayer as before — Daniel 6:1–10

Darius the Mede appointed 120 satraps under three presidents, one of whom was Daniel, "that the king should suffer no loss." Daniel so distinguished himself that the king planned to set him over the whole kingdom. The other officials "sought to find occasion against Daniel as touching the kingdom; but they could find no occasion or fault, because he was faithful. There wasn't any error or fault found in him." They concluded, "We won't find any occasion against this Daniel, unless we find it against him concerning the law of his God." They persuaded Darius to sign a decree, unalterable under the law of the Medes and Persians, that anyone who petitioned any god or man except the king for thirty days would be thrown to the lions.

"When Daniel knew that the writing was signed, he went into his house (now his windows were open in his room toward Jerusalem) and he kneeled on his knees three times a day, and prayed, and gave thanks before his God, as he did before."

**Truths:** Daniel's integrity was complete enough that only his faithfulness to God could be used against him. He did not change his prayer to avoid danger, nor did he stage a protest; he did what he had always done.

**2026 use:** Personal development on consistency; Radicals on keeping prayer steady when rules or schedules change; the style reference's Daniel prayer report. Hostile policy is serious.

## The lions' den — Daniel 6:11–23

The officials found Daniel praying and reported him. Darius was deeply distressed and "labored until the going down of the sun to rescue him," but the officials reminded him the decree could not be changed. Daniel was thrown into the den; the king said, "Your God whom you serve continually, he will deliver you." A stone was placed over the mouth and sealed with the king's signet. The king spent the night fasting and sleepless. At dawn he hurried to the den and called out in anguish. Daniel answered: "O king, live forever! My God has sent his angel, and has shut the lions' mouths, and they have not hurt me, because innocence was found in me before him; and also before you, O king, I have done no harm." He was lifted out with no injury, "because he had trusted in his God."

**Truths:** Daniel answered the king who had condemned him with courtesy. He claimed innocence before God and loyalty to the king at the same time.

**2026 use:** Testimony; prayer under threat; Radicals teaching on faith and courage. The den is not comedy.

## The accusers and their families; the decree — Daniel 6:24–28

"The king commanded, and they brought those men who had accused Daniel, and they cast them into the den of lions—them, their children, and their wives; and the lions mauled them, and broke all their bones in pieces before they came to the bottom of the den." Darius then decreed that everyone in his kingdom should fear "the God of Daniel. For he is the living God." "So this Daniel prospered in the reign of Darius and in the reign of Cyrus the Persian."

**Seriousness:** The killing of the accusers' wives and children was the king's order. Scripture reports it without comment. Data Play must not present it as justice served or as a punchline.

**2026 use:** Serious reflection only, if at all.

## Four beasts and one like a son of man — Daniel 7

In the first year of Belshazzar, Daniel had a dream and "wrote the dream and told the sum of the matters." Four great beasts rose from the sea, the fourth terrifying, with ten horns and a boastful little horn. Thrones were set and "one who was Ancient of Days" took his seat; "The judgment was set. The books were opened." Then "there came with the clouds of the sky one like a son of man," who was given an everlasting dominion. An attendant explained that the beasts were kings, and that "the saints of the Most High will receive the kingdom." The horn would make war on the saints "until a time and times and half a time." Daniel writes: "my spirit was grieved within my body… my thoughts troubled me greatly, and my face was changed in me; but I kept the matter in my heart."

**Interpretation:** The identities of the beasts and horns are debated. Christians read "one like a son of man" in light of Jesus' use of the phrase (Mark 14:62); Daniel has no memory of NT events.

**Truths:** Earthly powers are real and terrifying, and they are not final. Daniel wrote down what he saw and still carried distress.

**2026 use:** Careful Lifegroup or teaching context; testimony about carrying heavy things. Never a 2026 prediction.

## The ram and the goat; back to the king's business — Daniel 8

In a vision at Susa by the river Ulai, Daniel saw a two-horned ram overpowered by a goat with one great horn, which broke into four, and from one came a little horn that grew great, took away the daily offering and cast down the sanctuary. Gabriel was told to explain the vision: the ram was the kings of Media and Persia, the goat the king of Greece. Daniel fell on his face in fear. At the end: "I, Daniel, fainted, and was sick for some days. Then I rose up and did the king's business. I wondered at the vision, but no one understood it."

**Interpretation:** Many interpreters see Antiochus IV in the little horn; others differ.

**Truths:** Daniel went back to work carrying something no one else understood.

**2026 use:** Leaders who keep serving while carrying something unresolved. No medical labels.

## Reading Jeremiah and confessing for the people — Daniel 9:1–19

In the first year of Darius, "I, Daniel, understood by the books the number of the years about which the LORD's word came to Jeremiah the prophet for the accomplishing of the desolations of Jerusalem, even seventy years." He turned to God "with fasting and sackcloth and ashes," and prayed: "we have sinned, and have dealt perversely, and have done wickedly, and have rebelled… Lord, righteousness belongs to you, but to us confusion of face." He acknowledged that disaster had come as written in the law of Moses, then pleaded, "for we do not present our petitions before you for our righteousness, but for your great mercies' sake. Lord, hear. Lord, forgive. Lord, listen and do. Don't defer, for your own sake, my God."

**Fact:** Daniel's prayer includes himself: "confessing my sin and the sin of my people Israel" (9:20).

**Truths:** Reading Scripture led to prayer, not to calculation alone. A man with no recorded failure confessed as "we." He appealed to God's mercy, not his own record.

**2026 use:** Corporate confession at a prayer day; Lifegroup teaching on Scripture-shaped prayer (compare Ezra 9 and Nehemiah 1).

**Cross-era note:** Jeremiah is a Data Play character. Jeremiah has no memory of Daniel reading his words. In 2026 Daniel may tell him.

## Gabriel and the seventy weeks — Daniel 9:20–27

While Daniel was still praying, about the time of the evening offering, Gabriel came "to give you wisdom and understanding… for you are greatly beloved," and spoke of "seventy weeks" decreed "to finish disobedience, to make an end of sins… to bring in everlasting righteousness," of an anointed one cut off, and of desolations.

**Interpretation:** The seventy weeks are among the most debated passages in Scripture. Data Play never uses them to set dates or predict events.

**2026 use:** "Greatly beloved" as encouragement; otherwise teaching context only, handled with care.

## Three weeks of mourning; the man in linen — Daniel 10

In the third year of Cyrus, Daniel mourned three weeks: "I ate no pleasant food. No meat or wine came into my mouth." By the Tigris he saw a man clothed in linen, with a face like lightning. The men with him fled; "no strength remained in me." A hand touched him and set him on his knees and the palms of his hands; told to stand, he stood trembling. "Daniel, you greatly beloved man… Don't be afraid, Daniel; for from the first day that you set your heart to understand, and to humble yourself before your God, your words were heard." The messenger spoke of being withstood by "the prince of the kingdom of Persia" for twenty-one days until Michael helped. Daniel could not speak and had no breath until he was touched again: "Greatly beloved man, don't be afraid. Peace be to you. Be strong. Yes, be strong."

**Interpretation:** The "princes" of Persia and Greece are often understood as spiritual powers; details are debated.

**Truths:** Prayer was heard from the first day, even when the answer took weeks. Strength was given to Daniel; he did not produce it.

**2026 use:** Prayer when answers are slow; encouragement for exhausted leaders.

## Kings of the north and south; the wise who teach — Daniel 11 (selected)

A long vision describes kings of Persia and Greece and a series of conflicts between "the king of the south" and "the king of the north," ending with a king who exalts himself above every god. Within it: "the people who know their God will be strong and take action. Those who are wise among the people will instruct many; yet they will fall by the sword and by flame, by captivity and by plunder, many days" (11:32–33).

**Interpretation:** Commonly read as describing Persian and Hellenistic kings, especially Antiochus IV, with the final section debated. Do not map it to modern nations.

**2026 use:** The faithful wise who teach others under pressure; Mentorship. Not geopolitical commentary.

## "I heard, but I didn't understand"; go your way — Daniel 12

Michael will arise in a time of unprecedented trouble; those "found written in the book" will be delivered; "Many of those who sleep in the dust of the earth will awake, some to everlasting life, and some to shame and everlasting contempt. Those who are wise will shine as the brightness of the expanse. Those who turn many to righteousness will shine as the stars forever and ever." Daniel was told to seal the book. When he asked how long, he was told "a time, times, and a half." "I heard, but I didn't understand. Then I said, 'My lord, what will be the outcome of these things?'" The answer: "Go your way, Daniel; for the words are shut up and sealed until the time of the end… But go your way until the end; for you will rest, and will stand in your inheritance at the end of the days."

**Truths:** Daniel was allowed to not understand. He was given rest and a promised inheritance, not a timetable. Those who lead others to righteousness are honoured.

**2026 use:** Testimony about unanswered questions; encouragement for Radicals leaders who invest in others; resurrection hope. Never date-setting.

## Later references to Daniel

- **Ezekiel 14:14, 20; 28:3** name a Daniel alongside Noah and Job as righteous, and as proverbially wise. Whether this is the same Daniel is debated.
- **Matthew 24:15; Mark 13:14** — Jesus refers to "the abomination of desolation, which was spoken of through Daniel the prophet." Daniel has no memory of this.
- **Hebrews 11:33** refers to those who by faith "stopped the mouths of lions," a probable allusion to Daniel, who is not named.
