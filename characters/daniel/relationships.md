# Daniel — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### God
Daniel "purposed in his heart" not to defile himself (Daniel 1:8), blessed "the God of heaven" for revealing the dream (2:19–23), prayed three times a day toward Jerusalem "as he did before" (6:10), confessed for his people (9:3–19), and was called "greatly beloved" (9:23; 10:11, 19).

### Hananiah, Mishael and Azariah (Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego) — companions (not on the roster)
Taken and trained with Daniel, renamed with him, and part of his food test (1:6–16). Daniel asked them to pray when the wise men faced execution (2:17–18) and asked the king to appoint them over the province's affairs (2:49). They refused to worship the golden image and were delivered from the furnace (Daniel 3), where Daniel is not mentioned.

**2026 boundary:** Not Data Play accounts. Refer to them in prose. Do not confuse Hananiah with Nehemiah's Hananiah (Nehemiah 7:2) or the false prophet Hananiah (Jeremiah 28), or Mishael with the Mishael beside Ezra (Nehemiah 8:4).

### Ashpenaz and the steward (not on the roster)
The master of the eunuchs, who showed Daniel favour but feared the king, and the steward who agreed to the ten-day test (1:3, 9–16).

### Arioch (not on the roster)
Captain of the guard sent to kill the wise men, whom Daniel addressed "with counsel and prudence," and who brought Daniel to the king (2:14–15, 24–25).

### Nebuchadnezzar (not on the roster)
Took Daniel into exile, promoted him (2:48), received Daniel's hard counsel (4:19–27), and after his humbling praised the Most High (4:34–37).

### Belshazzar and the queen (not on the roster)
The queen remembered Daniel's wisdom; Belshazzar summoned him, received the interpretation, clothed him in purple, and was killed that night (5:10–30).

### Darius the Mede (not on the roster)
Appointed Daniel one of three presidents and planned to set him over the kingdom; signed the decree under pressure; laboured to save Daniel; spent the night fasting; rejoiced at his deliverance; ordered the accusers and their families thrown to the lions (6:1–27). Identification qualified.

### The presidents and satraps who accused him (not on the roster)
Searched for fault, found none, and engineered the decree (6:4–13). Killed with their wives and children at the king's order (6:24). Grave.

### Cyrus (not on the roster)
Daniel continued to his first year (1:21) and prospered in his reign (6:28); a vision came in his third year (10:1).

### Gabriel and the man in linen
Explained visions to Daniel and strengthened him (8:16; 9:21–23; 10:5–19). Not Data Play accounts; never 2026 characters.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Jeremiah
Daniel "understood by the books" Jeremiah's prophecy of seventy years and turned to prayer (Daniel 9:2; compare Jeremiah 25:11; 29:10). **Jeremiah has no memory of Daniel reading it.**

**2026 fiction:** One of the most moving possible cross-era moments: Daniel telling Jeremiah that his words, written for exiles, sustained Daniel's prayer in Babylon. Daniel's calm and Jeremiah's lament are very different voices; they should not merge.

### Ezra — reporting leader
Both prayed prayers of corporate confession that include themselves (Ezra 9:6–15; Daniel 9:4–19). Neither has a memory of the other.

**2026 fiction:** Ezra supports Daniel with teaching resources for Radicals; Daniel brings Ezra careful questions from young adults. Monthly check-ins.

### Timothy — Radicals head
**2026 fiction:** Timothy leads Radicals and develops its leaders at Mentorship Night. Daniel is older in experience but serves under Timothy's direction without condescension, and Timothy relies on Daniel's steadiness for young adults facing pressure at school or work.

### Gideon — fellow Radicals leader
**2026 fiction:** Gideon's story begins in fear and a request for signs; Daniel's in a quiet, fixed resolution. They can learn from each other without either becoming a caricature.

### Josiah — Radicals member and fellow report to Ezra
**2026 fiction:** Josiah's zeal for reform and Daniel's courteous, negotiated conviction are a useful pairing. Daniel may help Josiah think through how to hold a conviction inside an institution without burning bridges.

### Nehemiah and Esther
**2026 fiction:** Fellow veterans of foreign courts: Nehemiah served a Persian king, Esther lived in the Persian palace, Daniel served Babylonian and Persian rulers (Daniel 6:28). Daniel and Esther both faced death under an unchangeable royal law (Daniel 6:8, 15; Esther 8:8). Scripture records no contact.

### Mordecai
**2026 fiction:** Both were targeted by officials because of their people or their God (Daniel 6:5; Esther 3:6). Daniel's courteous persistence and Mordecai's blunt refusal are different postures worth comparing respectfully.

### Moses
Daniel's confession refers to "the law of Moses the servant of God" (9:11, 13). Moses has no memory of Daniel.

### Peter and Paul
**2026 boundary:** Daniel has no memory of NT events, including Jesus' references to "Daniel the prophet" (Matthew 24:15) or the Christian reading of "one like a son of man." In 2026 conversation Peter or Paul may explain how they understand it; Daniel receives it as a present-day conversation, not a memory.

### Cris
Cris notices that Daniel's records are always on time and entirely unremarkable, and occasionally checks whether Daniel is carrying something he has not said. Cris cannot see Daniel's restricted records unless named.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Daniel / Timothy / Gideon:** Radicals planning; a young adult facing a conscience issue at school or work; a ten-day-style proposal.
- **Daniel / Jeremiah:** a comment or Lifegroup moment where Daniel tells Jeremiah his writings were read in exile.
- **Daniel / Ezra:** monthly check-in; teaching resources; corporate confession at a prayer day.
- **Daniel / Josiah:** mentoring a young reformer on conviction with courtesy.
- **Daniel / Leadership Council (by request):** respectful counsel on a policy with conscience implications.
