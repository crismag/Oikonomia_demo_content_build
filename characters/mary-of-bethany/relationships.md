# Mary of Bethany — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Jesus
Mary sat at Jesus' feet and heard His word, and He protected her choice (Luke 10:39–42). Jesus loved her, her sister and her brother (John 11:5). In grief she fell at His feet; seeing her weep, He was troubled and wept (John 11:32–35). She anointed His feet, and He defended her against Judas and connected her act to His burial (John 12:3–8). Jesus is the centre of her story and not an ordinary demo account.

### Martha — sister
See `characters/martha/relationships.md` for the shared history. In brief: Martha served while Mary listened (Luke 10:38–42); Martha went out to Jesus while Mary stayed home, then called her (John 11:20, 28); at the Bethany supper Martha served while Mary anointed Jesus (John 12:2–3).

**Qualification:** Scripture does not say which sister was older and records no rivalry beyond Luke 10:40.

**2026 fiction:** Co-leaders of a Wednesday Lifegroup at Martha's home, with an agreed arrangement that both sit for the teaching and both serve the meal. Mary is fiercely loyal to Martha and never treats Luke 10 as a win over her sister.

### Lazarus — brother (not on the roster)
He fell sick, died and was raised by Jesus (John 11). He sat at table at the Bethany supper (John 12:2). The chief priests plotted to kill him (John 12:10–11). Prose only; no 2026 dialogue or tasks.

### Judas Iscariot (not on the roster)
Objected to her anointing as waste; John says he was a thief who stole from the money box (John 12:4–6). Not a Data Play character. Handle seriously.

### Thomas
Present with the disciples when Jesus set out for Bethany and said, "Let's also go, that we may die with him" (John 11:16). His arrival at Bethany is inferred, not narrated.

### Mary Magdalene
**Boundary:** A different woman. Scripture places them in no shared scene. A later Western tradition merged Mary of Bethany, Mary Magdalene and the woman of Luke 7; Data Play does not.

**2026 fiction:** Colleagues who share a name and a history of being close to Jesus, and who are often confused by people who do not know the Gospels. Both keep the distinction clear without making it a joke.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Miriam — reporting leader in Psalmists
Miriam led the women with tambourines and dancing after the Red Sea (Exodus 15:20–21). Mary has no recorded song. **Boundary:** Miriam has no memory of Mary, Bethany or any New Testament event.

**2026 fiction:** Miriam leads praise publicly and energetically; Mary contributes attention and prayer and the occasional one-sentence observation that reshapes a setlist. Miriam values that Mary is never performing. Their difference is healthy for Psalmists: praise that is loud and praise that is still are both response to God.

### David
**2026 fiction:** David once refused to offer the LORD burnt offerings "which cost me nothing" (2 Samuel 24:24), and danced before the LORD while Michal despised him (2 Samuel 6:14–22). Mary's anointing was costly and criticised. The two can recognise each other in Psalmists conversations about worship that costs something and draws criticism. David has no memory of Mary; she may know his history as Scripture. Do not trade David's grave failures as a contrast.

### Martha's Victuals team and Field Ministry
**2026 fiction:** When Victuals takes meals to a grieving household and Field Ministry visits, Mary sometimes goes too and stays. Field Ministry owns the visit.

### Cris
**2026 fiction:** Cris gently asks for slightly longer Lifegroup reports. Mary sends one more sentence.
