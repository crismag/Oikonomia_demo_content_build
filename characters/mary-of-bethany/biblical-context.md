# Mary of Bethany — Biblical Context Library

This file records Mary of Bethany's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are her memories of listening, grief and costly worship.

Scripture gives Mary three scenes and one recorded sentence. Keep her foundation that size. Martha's `biblical-context.md` retells John 11 from Martha's side; this file keeps to what concerns Mary.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered. Lazarus is not a Data Play character; refer to him in prose.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

---

## At Jesus' feet — Luke 10:38–42

Jesus came to a village where Martha welcomed Him into her house. Martha "had a sister called Mary, who also sat at Jesus' feet and heard his word." Martha, "distracted with much serving," asked Jesus, "Lord, don't you care that my sister left me to serve alone? Ask her therefore to help me." Jesus answered, "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is needed. Mary has chosen the good part, which will not be taken away from her."

**Fact:** Mary says nothing in this scene. Jesus speaks for her.

**Interpretation:** Sitting at a teacher's feet was the posture of a disciple (compare Acts 22:3). That a woman took that place is often seen as significant. Luke does not comment on it. Luke does not name the village; Bethany comes from John 11:1.

**Truths:** Listening to Jesus is not idleness, and Jesus protected Mary's choice from being taken away. The scene is not a verdict that Martha's sister was spiritual and Martha was not; both women were loved and both were addressed by name.

**2026 use:** Psalmists preparation that starts with Scripture; protecting listening time; Lifegroup teaching; the Martha–Mary "both sit, both serve" arrangement.

## "She is going to the tomb to weep there" — John 11:1–6, 19–20, 28–37, 45

When Lazarus was sick, the sisters sent word to Jesus. John introduces Mary as the one "who had anointed the Lord with ointment and wiped his feet with her hair," looking ahead to chapter 12. Jesus loved Martha, her sister and Lazarus, yet He stayed two more days. By the time He came, Lazarus had been four days in the tomb and many had come to console the sisters.

When Martha went out to meet Jesus, Mary stayed in the house. Martha came back and told her privately, "The Teacher is here and is calling you." Mary "arose quickly and went to him." The mourners with her assumed "She is going to the tomb to weep there," and followed. When Mary reached Jesus she fell down at His feet and said, "Lord, if you would have been here, my brother wouldn't have died." When Jesus saw her weeping, and the people with her weeping, He "groaned in the spirit and was troubled." He asked where Lazarus was laid. "Jesus wept." Afterward, "many of the Jews who came to Mary and saw what Jesus did believed in him."

**Fact:** Mary's words to Jesus are identical to Martha's (John 11:21, 32). John records no further conversation between Mary and Jesus at this point.

**Interpretation:** Why Mary stayed in the house is not stated. Do not assign a motive (hurt, despair, custom). It is a reasonable observation that Jesus answered Martha's words with teaching and met Mary's tears with His own; John does not draw that contrast himself.

**Truths:** Mary brought her grief to the same place she once sat to listen: Jesus' feet. Jesus did not hurry her grief; He wept. Her grief, seen by others, became part of how many came to believe.

**2026 use:** Grief care, lament in worship, sitting with the bereaved, testimony. Serious; never comedy.

## The anointing at Bethany — John 12:1–8

Six days before the Passover, Jesus came to Bethany, where Lazarus was. "So they made him a supper there. Martha served, but Lazarus was one of those who sat at the table with him." Then "Mary took a pound of ointment of pure nard, very precious, and anointed Jesus' feet and wiped his feet with her hair. The house was filled with the fragrance of the ointment." Judas Iscariot, "who would betray him," said, "Why wasn't this ointment sold for three hundred denarii and given to the poor?" John explains that Judas said this "not because he cared for the poor, but because he was a thief," who kept the money box and stole from it. Jesus said, "Leave her alone. She has kept this for the day of my burial. For you always have the poor with you, but you don't always have me."

**Fact:** Scripture records nothing Mary said at this meal. Three hundred denarii was a very large sum. In a parable, labourers are hired "for a denarius a day" (Matthew 20:2), so the value was very roughly a year's wages; that is an estimate, not a statement of the text.

**Interpretation:** Whether Mary understood the anointing as preparation for burial is not stated. Jesus gave it that meaning. Do not claim she foresaw His death.

**Truths:** Worship can be costly and public, and it can be criticised as waste by someone who sounds concerned for the poor. Jesus did not dismiss the poor; He said this moment with Him would not always be available. Mary did not argue; Jesus answered for her.

**2026 use:** Psalmists reflection on worship as response to God rather than performance; costly generosity; handling criticism of worship decisions. The burial meaning keeps the scene serious. More Than Enough owns care for the poor; do not set worship and generosity against each other.

**Qualification — the other anointing accounts:**

- **Matthew 26:6–13 and Mark 14:3–9** place an anointing at Bethany "in the house of Simon the leper." A woman, **not named**, poured very costly ointment from an alabaster jar on Jesus' **head**. Some of those present (the disciples, in Matthew) were indignant at the waste. Jesus defended her, said she had prepared His body for burial, and said that what she had done would be told in her memory wherever the good news is preached. Mark adds, "She has done what she could." Matthew and Mark place the scene after a note that the Passover was two days away; John says six days before.
- Many readers take this to be the same event as John 12, which would make the unnamed woman Mary. Others see two events. **Data Play does not settle this.** Words and details from Matthew and Mark may be connected to Mary only with that qualification.
- **Luke 7:36–50** is a different account: a woman "who was a sinner" anointed Jesus' feet with tears and ointment in the house of a Pharisee named Simon, apparently in Galilee. **She is not Mary of Bethany** and not Mary Magdalene.

## The threat against Lazarus — John 12:9–11

Crowds came to Bethany to see Lazarus, and "the chief priests conspired to put Lazarus to death also," because many were believing in Jesus on his account.

**2026 use:** Rarely, seriously: fear for a family member whose testimony draws hostility.
