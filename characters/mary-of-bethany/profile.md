## Who Mary of Bethany is

Mary lived in Bethany, near Jerusalem, with her sister Martha and her brother Lazarus. Scripture shows her in three scenes. In Luke 10 she sat at Jesus' feet listening to His word while Martha served, and when Martha asked Jesus to send her to help, Jesus said Mary had "chosen the good part, which will not be taken away from her." In John 11, when Lazarus died, she stayed in the house until Martha told her Jesus was calling for her; then she got up quickly, went to Him, fell at His feet weeping, and said, "Lord, if you would have been here, my brother wouldn't have died." Jesus, seeing her weep, was deeply troubled, and wept. In John 12, at a supper in Bethany six days before the Passover, she anointed Jesus' feet with a pound of very costly ointment and wiped them with her hair. Judas objected that it should have been sold and the money given to the poor. Jesus said, "Leave her alone. She has kept this for the day of my burial."

Scripture records only one sentence spoken by Mary, and it is the same sentence her sister had already said. Almost everything we know of her is what she did: listened, wept, went when called, gave something extravagant. She is not passive. She took a disciple's place at a teacher's feet, grieved in public, and made a costly, public act of worship that drew open criticism from one of the Twelve.

In 2026 she serves in Psalmists under Miriam and co-leads a Lifegroup with Martha. Scripture never records her singing or playing an instrument, and Data Play does not pretend it does. Her part in worship is what the Gospels show: attention, devotion and giving that costs something.

## 2026 voice

- Few words. Her reports are short and specific; when she writes a longer one, it matters.
- Notices people. Her comments are often a single observation nobody else made.
- Unhurried, not vague. She says plainly what happened and what she thinks it means.
- Does not defend herself at length. When criticised she tends to go quiet and keep doing what she believes is right, and she is learning when she does need to explain.
- Warm with grieving people; comfortable sitting in silence.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation and no dreamy mysticism.

She should not sound like Miriam (public leader of praise and prophetic proclamation), David (poetry and emotion at length), Martha (direct, practical, detailed) or Mary Magdalene (plain eyewitness testimony, someone who goes and tells). Mary of Bethany is the one who stays, listens and then acts.

## Strengths

- Listening: she chose to sit and hear Jesus' word (Luke 10:39).
- Devotion that is willing to cost something (John 12:3).
- Honest, embodied grief: she wept and brought her grief to Jesus' feet (John 11:32–33).
- Responsiveness: when told Jesus was calling, she "arose quickly" (John 11:29).
- Steadiness under criticism: Scripture records no defence from her when Judas objected; Jesus defended her (John 12:7).
- Her grief and Jesus' response were seen by many, and many who came to her believed (John 11:45).

## Development areas

Scripture records no correction of Mary. Do not invent faults and present them as biblical. The following are **Data Play development themes**, reasonable for a 2026 leader with her temperament, not claims about her biblical conduct:

- Making sure her choice to listen does not leave someone else carrying the work alone. Jesus defended her choice in Luke 10; in 2026 she and Martha have arranged their Lifegroup so both sit and both serve.
- Explaining herself when teammates need to understand a worship decision, rather than relying on silence.
- Staying in the house: in John 11 she remained at home until called. In 2026 grief care she may need prompting to be first out the door.

## Humor

Very gentle, and only from personality and administrative framing:

- two-sentence Lifegroup reports that Martha's comment then expands into eleven bullet points;
- Cris asking whether "It was good" can be given a Record type;
- Mary being the only person in a Psalmists planning meeting who has not yet spoken, and whose one comment changes the setlist.

Humor must not come from Lazarus' death, her weeping, the anointing (which Jesus connected to His burial), Judas' criticism or the plot against Lazarus. She is never the dreamy sister who doesn't help.

## Sensitivity

- **Lazarus' death** (John 11): real grief, four days in the tomb, public mourning. Serious.
- **The anointing** (John 12:1–8) is costly worship and, in Jesus' words, preparation for His burial. It is not a romantic scene and not a scene about hair or perfume for their own sake.
- **Do not confuse her** with Mary Magdalene or with the unnamed "woman in the city who was a sinner" who anointed Jesus in a Pharisee's house (Luke 7:36–50). Do not suggest Mary of Bethany had a sinful past; Scripture says nothing of it.
- **Judas' objection:** John states Judas was a thief who stole from the money box (John 12:6). Handle as serious moral fact.

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| Sister of Martha and Lazarus; from Bethany | Biblical fact (John 11:1–2, 19) |
| The Mary of Luke 10:39 is the same as the Mary of John 11–12 | Strong inference: sister of a Martha in both Gospels. Luke does not name the village. |
| Sat at Jesus' feet and heard His word | Biblical fact (Luke 10:39) |
| Sitting at a teacher's feet was a disciple's posture | Reasonable interpretation (compare Acts 22:3) |
| Her only recorded words: "Lord, if you would have been here, my brother wouldn't have died." | Biblical fact (John 11:32) |
| Anointed Jesus' feet with a pound of costly nard and wiped them with her hair | Biblical fact (John 12:3; also referred to in John 11:2) |
| The woman who anointed Jesus' head in the house of Simon the leper at Bethany (Matthew 26:6–13; Mark 14:3–9) was Mary | Possible and widely held, since the setting, the objection and Jesus' reply are similar. Matthew and Mark do not name the woman, place the anointing on the head, and differ from John in timing. Do not state as fact. |
| Mary of Bethany is the "sinner" woman of Luke 7:36–50 | No basis. Different setting (a Pharisee named Simon's house, apparently in Galilee), different framing. Keep separate. |
| Mary of Bethany is Mary Magdalene | No biblical basis. A later Western tradition merged them (together with the Luke 7 woman). Do not follow it. |
| Mary's worship involved music or song | Not recorded. 2026 Psalmists role is fiction. |
| Age, marital status, children, occupation, which sister was older, family wealth | Not recorded. The value of the ointment (about three hundred denarii, John 12:5) does not establish the family's wealth. |
| Mary had a sinful past or needed correction | Not recorded. Do not invent. |
