# Peter — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Jesus
The centre of Peter's story: calling, confession, correction, prayer for Peter before his failure, restoration and commission. Jesus is not an ordinary demo account. Peter refers to Him in testimony, prayer and teaching; Jesus does not receive tasks, comments or reports.

### Andrew — brother
Andrew heard John the Baptist, followed Jesus, and first brought Simon to Him (John 1:40–42). They fished together and were called together (Mark 1:16–18). Their house in Capernaum is where Jesus healed Peter's mother-in-law (Mark 1:29–31). Andrew was also with Peter, James and John when they asked Jesus privately about the temple's destruction (Mark 13:3–4).

**2026 fiction:** Andrew connects people and doesn't need the spotlight; Peter is naturally visible. Andrew may quietly bring newcomers to Peter's Lifegroup or to Field Ministry. Peter knows he is in the story because his brother went looking for him.

### James and John, sons of Zebedee — partners
Fishing partners (Luke 5:10). With Peter, the three were taken into Jairus' house, up the mountain of the Transfiguration and further into Gethsemane (Mark 5:37; 9:2; 14:33). Herod killed James with the sword; shortly after, Peter was imprisoned (Acts 12:1–3). John and Peter prepared the Passover (Luke 22:8), went to the temple together, were arrested together and spoke together before the council (Acts 3–4), and were sent together to Samaria (Acts 8:14). Paul names James (the Lord's brother), Cephas and John as pillars (Galatians 2:9).

**Qualification:** "The disciple whom Jesus loved" ran with Peter to the tomb, told Peter "It's the Lord!", and prompted Peter's "Lord, what about this man?" (John 20:2–10; 21:7, 20–22). That disciple is traditionally identified as John, but the text does not name him. Records should either say "the disciple whom Jesus loved" or state that the identification is traditional.

**2026 fiction:** Peter and John remain close collaborators with different instincts. Peter acts and speaks first; John observes and documents carefully. Their friction is affectionate and useful: John records more detail than Peter would like; Peter moves before John has finished writing. Peter's loss of James is serious grief and not a source of banter.

### Paul
Paul visited Peter in Jerusalem and stayed fifteen days (Galatians 1:18). Later the pillars, including Cephas, gave Paul and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship (Galatians 2:9). At Antioch Paul opposed Peter to his face for withdrawing from Gentile believers under pressure (Galatians 2:11–14). 2 Peter calls him "our beloved brother Paul" and admits his letters contain hard things (2 Peter 3:15–16).

**Silence:** Scripture does not record Peter's reply at Antioch or any reconciliation conversation.

**2026 fiction:** Two senior leaders with very different styles who respect each other and can disagree sharply. Paul's written, argued correction and Peter's spoken, relational instinct produce substantive accountability records. Their 2026 disagreement must be a new, present-day matter; it must not replay Antioch.

### Barnabas
Barnabas sold a field and laid the money at the apostles' feet (Acts 4:36–37). At Antioch he was "carried away" and joined Peter's withdrawal (Galatians 2:13). At the Jerusalem Council Barnabas and Paul reported after Peter spoke (Acts 15:12).

**2026 fiction:** Barnabas and Peter both know what it is to be corrected in public and to keep going. Encouragement flows both ways.

### John Mark
Peter went to the house of Mary, John Mark's mother, after his release from prison (Acts 12:12). 1 Peter 5:13 sends greetings from "Mark, my son."

**Qualification:** That this Mark is John Mark is probable, not explicit. The tradition that Mark's Gospel draws on Peter's preaching is not Scripture.

**2026 fiction:** Peter treats Mark as a son in the faith and a developing leader. He does not define Mark by his earlier withdrawal from Paul's mission; Peter knows how that feels.

### Silas / Silvanus
Peter wrote 1 Peter "through Silvanus, our faithful brother" (1 Peter 5:12).

**Qualification:** Silvanus is probably Paul's companion Silas.

**2026 fiction:** A trusted co-worker for letters, messages and careful delivery.

### Cornelius
The Roman centurion whose household received the Holy Spirit while Peter was still preaching (Acts 10). Peter refused to be worshipped by him, stayed with his household, and defended the decision in Jerusalem (Acts 11).

**2026 fiction:** A mutual friendship across cultural lines. Cornelius' hospitality and prayer, and Peter's willingness to cross thresholds, shape newcomer and cross-cultural welcome records. Neither treats the other as a project.

### Philip the Evangelist
Philip preached in Samaria; the apostles then sent Peter and John, who prayed for the believers there and dealt with Simon the sorcerer (Acts 8:5–25).

**Qualification:** This Philip is one of the Seven (Acts 6:5), not Philip the apostle. Do not merge them.

**2026 fiction:** Reach-Out follow-up and handoff: Philip starts, Peter supports, neither takes the other's credit.

### Matthias
Chosen by lot after Peter led the prayerful process to replace Judas (Acts 1:15–26).

**2026 fiction:** Peter takes an interest in how Matthias is supported in a role he entered by appointment rather than by long visibility.

### Thomas and Nathanael
Present with Peter when he said "I'm going fishing" and at the breakfast by the sea (John 21:2).

**Qualification:** Nathanael's identification with Bartholomew is traditional, not explicit.

### Mary Magdalene
Ran to tell Simon Peter and the other disciple that the tomb was empty (John 20:2).

### Apollos
Named alongside Paul and Cephas in the Corinthian factions (1 Corinthians 1:12; 3:22). There is no record of Peter and Apollos meeting.

**2026 fiction:** Both refuse to become the name people rally around. Useful for leader-loyalty and faction situations in Lifegroups.

### James, the Lord's brother (not on the Data Play roster)
Peter asked that James be told of his release (Acts 12:17). James gave judgment at the Jerusalem Council after Peter spoke (Acts 15:13–21). The men who came "from James" preceded Peter's withdrawal at Antioch (Galatians 2:12). Refer to James in prose only.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Moses
Peter saw Moses (and Elijah) with Jesus on the mountain of the Transfiguration (Mark 9:4). Scripture records this from the disciples' side only. **Moses must not be given a memory of it.** In 2026 Peter may mention it with awe and some embarrassment about the tents; Moses may respond as a colleague hearing it described. Otherwise, Moses and Peter share the experience of carrying people and of failure followed by continued calling.

### Elijah
Same Transfiguration boundary as Moses.

### David
At Pentecost Peter preached from David's psalms and spoke of David's tomb (Acts 2:25–35; also Acts 1:16; 4:25). In 2026 David may be moved or amused that Peter preached his psalms; neither claims historical contact. Both know public failure, repentance and continued leadership. Peter's and David's restrained or restricted records should not trade those stories as entertainment.

### Joshua
Both began close to a greater leader and had to act after that leader's departure. Useful for Mentorship Night conversations about stepping into responsibility.

### Timothy and Titus
Peter is not their biblical mentor; Paul is. Peter may contribute at Mentorship Night without displacing Paul.

### Cris
Cris coordinates follow-ups from Peter's Lifegroup and Field Ministry visits and mediates when Peter and John disagree about what a record should contain. Cris cannot see Peter's restricted pastoral records unless Peter names Cris as a reader.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Peter / John:** documentation versus discretion (the Malchus incident is already named in Scripture), shared Lifegroup or Field Ministry visits, a comment thread about who arrived first.
- **Peter / Paul:** a substantive 2026 accountability disagreement about inclusion or consistency, handled in a restricted leadership record; later mutual respect.
- **Peter / Andrew:** newcomer invitations, quiet support.
- **Peter / Cornelius:** cross-cultural welcome and hospitality; newcomer pathway improvements with Greeters and Ushers.
- **Peter / John Mark:** mentoring after failure.
- **Peter / Philip the Evangelist:** Reach-Out follow-up handoffs.
- **Peter / Moses / David:** Leadership Council conversations about failure, restoration and trust.
