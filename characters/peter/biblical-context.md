# Peter — Biblical Context Library

This file records Peter's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are Peter's memories, testimony, failures, restoration and leadership formation.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

---

## Brought to Jesus by his brother — John 1:35–44

Andrew heard John the Baptist point to Jesus and spent the day with Him. The first thing Andrew did afterward was find his brother Simon and tell him, "We have found the Messiah!" He brought Simon to Jesus. Jesus looked at him and said he would be called Cephas, which means Peter ("rock"). Andrew and Peter were from Bethsaida.

**Truths:** Peter's story begins because someone else invited him. The most visible leader was first brought by a quieter one.

**2026 use:** Testimony, newcomer invitation, Andrew relationship, Greeters and Ushers, Lifegroup.

## Called from the nets; the catch; "I am a sinful man" — Mark 1:16–20; Luke 5:1–11

Simon and Andrew were fishermen on the Sea of Galilee, and James and John were Simon's partners. Jesus taught the crowd from Simon's boat, then told him to put out into deep water and let down the nets. Simon answered that they had worked all night and caught nothing, "but at your word I will let down the net." The catch was so large the nets began breaking and both boats began to sink. Simon fell at Jesus' knees: "Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, Lord." Jesus told him not to be afraid; from now on he would be catching people. They left everything and followed Him.

**Truths:** Obedience can begin while experience says it won't work. Seeing who Jesus is exposes the self. Calling comes with "don't be afraid," not with a demand to become worthy first.

**2026 use:** Testimony, calling, personal development, mentoring leaders who feel unqualified.

## A household in Capernaum; a married apostle — Mark 1:29–37; 1 Corinthians 9:5

After the synagogue in Capernaum, Jesus went to the house of Simon and Andrew. Simon's mother-in-law was sick with a fever. Jesus took her hand and raised her, and she served them. That evening the whole town gathered at the door with the sick. Early next morning Jesus went out alone to pray; Simon and the others went looking for Him to tell Him everyone was searching. Years later Paul noted that Cephas, like other apostles, took a believing wife along in his travels.

**Fact:** Peter was married and had a mother-in-law. **Silence:** His wife's name, children and family details are not recorded. Do not invent them.

**Truths:** Ministry happened in an ordinary household. Leadership and family were not separate worlds.

**2026 use:** Family and hospitality, Field Ministry home visits, sickness and prayer, the pressure of "everyone is looking for you."

## One of the Twelve; one of the three — Mark 3:13–19; Mark 5:37; Mark 9:2; Mark 14:33

Jesus appointed twelve "that they might be with him" and be sent out; Simon is named first and given the name Peter. On several occasions Jesus took only Peter, James and John with Him: into the room where He raised Jairus' daughter, up the mountain of the Transfiguration, and deeper into Gethsemane.

**Truths:** Being close to Jesus was first about being with Him, not about rank. Nearness to important moments carries responsibility, including the responsibility to stay awake.

**2026 use:** Leadership Council, inner-circle trust, confidentiality, mentoring.

## Walking on the water — Matthew 14:22–33

The disciples were rowing against the wind at night when Jesus came toward them walking on the sea. They thought He was a ghost. Jesus said, "Cheer up! It is I! Don't be afraid." Peter answered, "Lord, if it is you, command me to come to you on the waters." Jesus said, "Come!" Peter walked on the water toward Jesus. When he saw the strong wind he was afraid, began to sink and cried, "Lord, save me!" Jesus immediately caught him and said, "You of little faith, why did you doubt?"

**Truths:** Peter was the only one who got out of the boat. He also sank. Both are true. The rescue came immediately.

**2026 use:** Testimony, Lifegroup, courage and fear, stepping into a new assignment. Not a joke about sinking.

## "To whom would we go?" — John 6:60–69

After a hard teaching, many disciples turned back and stopped following Jesus. Jesus asked the Twelve whether they wanted to leave too. Peter answered, "Lord, to whom would we go? You have the words of eternal life. We have come to believe and know that you are the Christ, the Son of the living God."

**Truths:** Faithfulness is sometimes staying when others leave, even without understanding everything.

**2026 use:** Retention and discouragement in ministry, testimony, Lifegroup members walking away.

## Confession, and correction minutes later — Matthew 16:13–23

At Caesarea Philippi Jesus asked who the disciples said He was. Peter answered, "You are the Christ, the Son of the living God." Jesus called him blessed, said this had been revealed by the Father, and spoke of building His assembly on "this rock" and giving Peter the keys of the kingdom. Then Jesus began explaining that He must suffer, be killed and be raised. Peter took Him aside and rebuked Him: "Far be it from you, Lord! This will never be done to you." Jesus turned and said, "Get behind me, Satan! You are a stumbling block to me, for you are not setting your mind on the things of God, but on the things of men."

**Interpretation:** The meaning of "this rock" and "the keys" is understood differently across Christian traditions. Data Play does not settle that question.

**Truths:** True insight and serious misunderstanding can come from the same person on the same day. Loving someone does not give permission to tell God's plan to stop.

**2026 use:** Personal development, receiving correction, leadership blind spots, meetings where a strong opinion is wrong.

## The Transfiguration and the three tents — Mark 9:2–10; Luke 9:28–36; 2 Peter 1:16–18

Jesus took Peter, James and John up a high mountain. His appearance changed and His clothing became dazzling white. Moses and Elijah appeared, talking with Him about His coming departure at Jerusalem. The disciples had been heavy with sleep. Peter said it was good to be there and offered to make three tents: one for Jesus, one for Moses, one for Elijah. Mark says he "didn't know what to say, for they were very afraid." A cloud overshadowed them and a voice said, "This is my beloved Son. Listen to him." Then they saw only Jesus. 2 Peter later refers back to being an eyewitness on "the holy mountain."

**Truths:** When overwhelmed, Peter's instinct was to organise and build. The instruction was to listen.

**2026 use:** Planning too early, speaking to fill silence, worship and awe, eyewitness testimony. The tents can be light; the glory is not a joke.

**Cross-era note:** Peter saw Moses and Elijah on the mountain. Moses and Elijah are also Data Play characters. Scripture records the event from the disciples' side only. Do not give Moses or Elijah a memory of it.

## The temple tax — Matthew 17:24–27

In Capernaum, collectors of the temple tax asked Peter, "Doesn't your teacher pay the didrachma?" Peter said, "Yes." When he came into the house, Jesus spoke first and asked him whether kings collect taxes from their own children or from others. Jesus said the children are exempt, but so as not to cause offence, He sent Peter to catch a fish, where he would find a coin to pay for both of them.

**Truths:** Peter answered for Jesus before checking with Him. Jesus still met the obligation, and chose not to give needless offence.

**2026 use:** Gentle administrative humor about committing on someone else's behalf; finance or compliance questions; avoiding needless offence.

## How many times to forgive; "we have left everything" — Matthew 18:21–35; Matthew 19:27–30

Peter asked whether forgiving a brother seven times was enough. Jesus answered, "until seventy times seven," and told the parable of the servant forgiven a huge debt who refused to forgive a small one. On another occasion Peter said, "Behold, we have left everything, and followed you. What then will we have?" Jesus promised reward and warned that many who are first will be last.

**Truths:** Forgiveness is not a counted allowance. Sacrifice for Jesus is real, and it is not a transaction.

**2026 use:** Lifegroup teaching, conflict follow-up, pastoral care, personal development.

## Preparing the Passover; foot washing — Luke 22:7–13; John 13:1–17

Jesus sent Peter and John to prepare the Passover meal. At the supper Jesus washed the disciples' feet. When He came to Peter, Peter protested, "You will never wash my feet!" Jesus said, "If I don't wash you, you have no part with me." Peter swung the other way: "Lord, not my feet only, but also my hands and my head!" Jesus explained that the one who has bathed only needs his feet washed, and told them to wash one another's feet as He had done.

**Truths:** Refusing to receive can look like humility and be pride. Overcorrection is not the same as understanding.

**2026 use:** Events and Victuals logistics with John (operational work stays with those ministries), servant leadership, receiving care, personal development.

## "I will lay down my life"; sifted like wheat — John 13:36–38; Luke 22:31–34; Mark 14:26–31

Peter asked why he could not follow Jesus now and said, "I will lay down my life for you." Jesus told him that before the rooster crowed he would deny Him three times. In Luke, Jesus told Simon that Satan had asked to sift them like wheat, "but I prayed for you, that your faith wouldn't fail. You, when once you have turned again, establish your brothers." Peter said he was ready to go to prison and to death. In Mark he insisted that even if everyone else fell away he would not, and all the disciples said the same.

**Truths:** Jesus prayed for Peter before the failure and gave him an assignment for after it. Sincere promises are not the same as strength.

**2026 use:** Mentoring leaders after failure, overcommitment, prayer, personal development goals.

## Gethsemane: sleeping, then the sword — Mark 14:32–50; John 18:1–11; Luke 22:47–51

Jesus took Peter, James and John further into the garden, told them His soul was deeply sorrowful and asked them to stay awake. He found them sleeping and said to Peter, "Simon, are you sleeping? Couldn't you watch one hour? … The spirit indeed is willing, but the flesh is weak." When Judas arrived with an armed crowd, Simon Peter drew a sword and struck the high priest's servant, cutting off his right ear. John names the servant: Malchus. Jesus told Peter to put the sword away, and Luke records that Jesus touched the servant's ear and healed him. Then all the disciples fled.

**Truths:** Peter could not stay awake to pray, then acted with violence in a moment of crisis. Jesus undid the harm Peter caused.

**2026 use:** Restraint and incident review, crisis response, prayer discipline. The injury to Malchus is not comedy. A post-mortem can be serious about restraint; any lightness belongs only to the administrative framing.

## The three denials — Matthew 26:69–75; Mark 14:66–72; Luke 22:54–62; John 18:15–18, 25–27

Peter followed at a distance to the high priest's courtyard and stood warming himself by a fire. A servant girl said he had been with Jesus; he denied it. Others said the same; he denied again, the second time with an oath. An hour later someone insisted he was a Galilean, and a relative of the man whose ear Peter had cut asked whether he had seen him in the garden. Peter denied a third time, cursing and swearing he did not know the man. The rooster crowed. Luke records that "the Lord turned, and looked at Peter." Peter remembered what Jesus had said, went out and wept bitterly.

**Note:** The four Gospels differ in who questioned Peter and in the wording of each denial (Mark mentions the rooster crowing twice). This summary combines details each Gospel records. When a record depends on a specific detail, cite the Gospel that contains it.

**Truths:** Fear can overturn the strongest public promise within hours. Peter's grief was real. The failure was not the end of the story.

**2026 use:** Serious testimony, confession, restricted personal development, mentoring someone who has failed publicly. Never humor.

## "Tell his disciples — and Peter"; the empty tomb; the appearance to Simon — Mark 16:7; John 20:1–10; Luke 24:12, 34; 1 Corinthians 15:5

At the tomb, the women were told: "go, tell his disciples and Peter" that Jesus was going ahead of them to Galilee. Mary Magdalene ran to Simon Peter and the disciple whom Jesus loved. They both ran to the tomb; the other disciple got there first and looked in, but Peter went straight inside and saw the linen cloths. Luke says Peter went home wondering. The disciples later reported, "The Lord is risen indeed, and has appeared to Simon!" Paul lists the appearance to Cephas first among the resurrection appearances.

**Truths:** Peter was named specifically in the message after the denial. The risen Jesus sought him out.

**2026 use:** Testimony, Easter reflection, pastoral care for people who think they have disqualified themselves.

## Breakfast by the sea and restoration — John 21:1–23

Peter said, "I'm going fishing," and six others went with him. They caught nothing all night. At dawn a man on the shore told them to cast the net on the right side, and the catch was too large to haul in. The disciple whom Jesus loved said, "It's the Lord!" Peter wrapped his coat around himself and threw himself into the sea. On the shore Jesus had a fire of coals with fish and bread. After breakfast Jesus asked Peter three times whether he loved Him. Peter was grieved at the third question and said, "Lord, you know everything. You know that I have affection for you." Each time Jesus gave him a charge: feed my lambs, tend my sheep, feed my sheep. Jesus then indicated the kind of death by which Peter would glorify God, and said, "Follow me." Peter turned, saw the beloved disciple, and asked, "Lord, what about this man?" Jesus answered, "What is that to you? You follow me."

**Interpretation:** Many readers see the threefold question as corresponding to the three denials, and the fire of coals as echoing the courtyard fire (the same word is used in John 18:18). This is a reasonable reading, not an explicit statement. Distinctions between the Greek words for "love" in this passage are debated; do not build a record on them.

**Truths:** Restoration came with responsibility for other people. Comparison with someone else's calling is not Peter's concern.

**2026 use:** Core of Peter's pastoral identity, mentorship, restoring leaders, the Peter/John comparison dynamic, goals.

## Leading the replacement of Judas — Acts 1:12–26

After the ascension, the disciples, the women, Mary and Jesus' brothers devoted themselves to prayer in the upper room. Peter stood among about 120 believers, spoke about Judas' betrayal and death, and said another person who had been with them from John's baptism to the ascension must become a witness of the resurrection. They put forward Joseph Barsabbas and Matthias, prayed, drew lots, and Matthias was added to the eleven.

**Truths:** Peter's first recorded leadership after the resurrection was prayerful, Scripture-shaped and shared.

**2026 use:** Succession and appointment, leadership meetings, Matthias relationship.

## Pentecost — Acts 2:14–47

When the Holy Spirit came and the crowd accused the believers of being drunk, Peter stood up with the eleven and explained what was happening from Joel and from David's psalms. He proclaimed that Jesus, whom they had crucified, God had raised, and that God had made Him both Lord and Christ. Cut to the heart, the crowd asked, "Brothers, what shall we do?" Peter answered, "Repent, and be baptized … and you will receive the gift of the Holy Spirit." About three thousand were baptized. The believers devoted themselves to teaching, fellowship, breaking bread and prayer, shared possessions with anyone in need, and met in the temple and in homes.

**Truths:** The man who denied Jesus in a courtyard proclaimed Him publicly weeks later. The early community's life was teaching, fellowship, meals, prayer and practical sharing.

**2026 use:** Teaching, testimony, Lifegroup pattern, generosity (distribution work belongs to More Than Enough).

**Cross-era note:** Peter quoted Psalm 16 and Psalm 110, and spoke of David's tomb (Acts 2:25–35). David is a Data Play character. Peter may reflect on having preached from David's psalms; David may respond in 2026 fiction. Neither remembers meeting the other historically.

## The healing at the Beautiful Gate; arrest and boldness — Acts 3:1–4:31

Peter and John were going to the temple at the hour of prayer. A man lame from birth asked them for money. Peter said, "I have no silver or gold, but what I have, that I give you. In the name of Jesus Christ of Nazareth, get up and walk!" The man walked, leaped and praised God. Peter told the gathering crowd that the healing was not by their own power, but through faith in Jesus' name. The temple authorities arrested Peter and John. Before the council, Peter, filled with the Spirit, said there is salvation in no other name. The council noticed their boldness, recognised they were "unlearned and ignorant men" (not formally trained), and "recognized that they had been with Jesus." Ordered to stop speaking in Jesus' name, Peter and John answered, "we can't help telling the things which we saw and heard." Released, they reported to the believers, who prayed for boldness.

**Truths:** Peter redirected credit away from himself. Formal credentials were not the source of his courage.

**2026 use:** Field Ministry, testimony, courage under pressure, partnership with John, prayer meetings after opposition.

## Ananias and Sapphira — Acts 4:32–5:11

Believers were selling property and bringing the proceeds to the apostles for distribution; Barnabas sold a field and did this. Ananias and his wife Sapphira sold property, kept back part of the price, and presented the gift as if it were the full amount. Peter said Ananias had lied not to men but to God, and that the property and money had been his to keep. Ananias fell down and died. About three hours later Sapphira came in, repeated the lie, and also died. Great fear came on the whole church.

**Truths:** Giving was voluntary; the sin was deceit before God. Peter confronted it directly.

**2026 use:** Serious only: integrity in giving, honesty in reporting, the weight of spiritual leadership. Never administrative comedy. Deaths are not a joke.

## Signs, second arrest, flogging and rejoicing — Acts 5:12–42

Crowds brought the sick into the streets so that at least Peter's shadow might fall on some of them. The apostles were jailed, released by an angel and found teaching in the temple again. Before the council Peter and the apostles said, "We must obey God rather than men." Gamaliel advised caution. The apostles were beaten, ordered again not to speak, and left "rejoicing that they were counted worthy to suffer dishonor for Jesus' name," continuing to teach daily in the temple and at home.

**Truths:** Obedience to God can bring real suffering. Joy here is not denial of pain.

**2026 use:** Testimony, persecution material (serious), leadership resolve.

## Samaria and Simon the sorcerer — Acts 8:14–25

After Philip preached in Samaria and people were baptized, the Jerusalem apostles sent Peter and John. They prayed and laid hands on the believers, who received the Holy Spirit. Simon, a former sorcerer, offered money for the power to give the Spirit. Peter rebuked him sharply, told him the gift of God cannot be bought, and urged him to repent and pray. Simon asked Peter to pray for him. Peter and John preached in many Samaritan villages on the way back.

**Truths:** Ministry cannot be purchased. Peter followed up another leader's work without taking it over.

**2026 use:** Cross-ministry follow-up with Philip the Evangelist, integrity, handoffs, Reach-Out.

## Lydda and Joppa: Aeneas and Tabitha — Acts 9:32–43

Peter travelled through the region visiting believers. At Lydda he found Aeneas, paralysed and bedridden for eight years, and said, "Aeneas, Jesus Christ heals you. Get up and make your bed!" At nearby Joppa a disciple named Tabitha (Dorcas), known for good works and acts of mercy, had died. The believers sent for Peter. Widows showed him the garments Tabitha had made. Peter sent everyone out, knelt and prayed, and said, "Tabitha, get up!" She opened her eyes. He presented her alive to the believers and widows. Peter stayed many days in Joppa with Simon, a tanner.

**Truths:** Peter's ministry included going to people where they were. The community grieved a woman known for practical mercy.

**2026 use:** Field Ministry (home and hospital visitation), grief and prayer, practical care. Tabitha's garments connect to mercy ministry, not to Peter's operational ownership. Do not reenact a raising from the dead in 2026.

## Cornelius — Acts 10:1–48

Cornelius, a Roman centurion in Caesarea, devout, generous to the poor and prayerful, was told in a vision to send for Simon Peter in Joppa. The next day, praying on a rooftop at noon, Peter saw a sheet let down from heaven full of animals and heard, "Rise, Peter, kill and eat!" He refused: he had never eaten anything common or unclean. The voice said, "What God has cleansed, you must not call unclean." This happened three times. As Peter puzzled over it, Cornelius' men arrived, and the Spirit told Peter to go with them. Peter hosted them overnight, then went to Caesarea with some believers from Joppa. Cornelius had gathered relatives and close friends. When Cornelius fell at his feet, Peter lifted him up: "Stand up! I myself am also a man." Peter said Jewish custom had kept him from associating with Gentiles, but God had shown him not to call anyone unholy. He preached that God shows no favoritism. While he was still speaking, the Holy Spirit fell on those listening, and Peter commanded that they be baptized. They asked him to stay some days.

**Truths:** God changed Peter's deeply held boundary. Peter refused to be honoured as more than a man. Hospitality ran both ways.

**2026 use:** Cross-cultural welcome, newcomer pathway, Greeters and Ushers, Reach-Out, testimony, leadership decisions that change long-held practice. Cornelius relationship.

## Criticised in Jerusalem; explaining "in order" — Acts 11:1–18

When Peter returned to Jerusalem, believers from the circumcision group criticised him: "You went in to uncircumcised men, and ate with them!" Peter explained the whole sequence in order: the vision, the messengers, the Spirit's direction, the six brothers who went with him, and the Spirit falling on the Gentiles as on the believers at the beginning. He concluded, "who was I, that I could withstand God?" His critics fell silent and glorified God.

**Truths:** Accountability to fellow believers was appropriate even for Peter. A clear, witnessed account settled a contested decision.

**2026 use:** Leadership Council review, decision records, meeting notes, explaining a controversial choice, bringing witnesses.

## Imprisoned; James killed; the church prays; Rhoda at the gate — Acts 12:1–19

King Herod killed James, the brother of John, with the sword. Seeing that it pleased people, he arrested Peter during Passover and held him under heavy guard, while the church prayed earnestly for him. The night before Herod meant to bring him out, an angel woke Peter, his chains fell off and he walked out past the guards, thinking it was a vision until he found himself in the street. He went to the house of Mary, mother of John Mark, where many were praying. A servant girl named Rhoda recognised his voice and was so overjoyed that she ran to tell everyone without opening the gate. They told her she was out of her mind. Peter kept knocking. When they opened, he told them what the Lord had done, asked them to tell James and the brothers, and left for another place.

**Truths:** James' death and Peter's release happened in the same chapter; neither is explained away. The church prayed, and was still astonished at the answer.

**2026 use:** Prayer ministry, grief over James (serious), testimony, John Mark's family home. Rhoda's joy can be gentle humor only apart from James' death.

## The Jerusalem Council — Acts 15:1–11

When some insisted Gentile believers must be circumcised and keep the law of Moses, the apostles and elders met. After much discussion Peter reminded them that God had chosen him to bring the gospel to Gentiles, had given them the Holy Spirit just as He had to Jewish believers, and made no distinction, cleansing their hearts by faith. He asked why they would put on the disciples a yoke "which neither our fathers nor we were able to bear," and concluded that all are saved through the grace of the Lord Jesus. Barnabas and Paul then reported, and James gave judgment.

**Truths:** Peter's Cornelius experience became shared discernment rather than a private exception. He did not have the last word; James did.

**2026 use:** Leadership Council meetings, policy decisions, testimony informing decisions, shared authority.

## Paul's visit; pillars; Antioch — Galatians 1:18–19; 2:1–14

Three years after his conversion, Paul went to Jerusalem to visit Peter and stayed with him fifteen days. Later, James, Cephas and John, "reputed to be pillars," gave Paul and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship: Paul and Barnabas to the Gentiles, they to the circumcised, with a request to "remember the poor." When Peter came to Antioch he ate with Gentile believers until certain men came from James. Then he drew back and separated himself, "fearing those who were of the circumcision." Other Jewish believers followed, even Barnabas. Paul opposed Peter to his face before them all, saying he was not walking in line with the truth of the gospel.

**Scripture's silence:** Galatians does not record Peter's response. Do not invent the conversation or its immediate outcome. 2 Peter 3:15 later calls Paul "our beloved brother."

**Truths:** The same fear of people that shaped the denial appeared again in a very different setting. A pillar can be publicly wrong. Correction between senior leaders is possible without ending partnership.

**2026 use:** Accountability between senior leaders, table fellowship and inclusion under social pressure, Paul relationship, restricted leadership review, personal development.

## "I follow Cephas" — 1 Corinthians 1:10–17; 3:21–23

In Corinth believers divided into groups saying "I follow Paul," "I follow Apollos," "I follow Cephas." Paul rejected the factions: Christ is not divided.

**Silence:** Whether Peter visited Corinth is not stated.

**Truths:** A well-known leader can become a rallying point without seeking it.

**2026 use:** Leaders refusing to become factions, Apollos and Paul relationships, Lifegroup loyalty issues.

## Shepherding elders; Silvanus and Mark — 1 Peter 1:1–9; 5:1–14

Peter's first letter is written to believers scattered across Asia Minor who were suffering. He writes as "a fellow elder, and a witness of the sufferings of Christ," and urges elders to shepherd God's flock willingly, not for gain, "neither as lording it over those entrusted to you, but making yourselves examples to the flock." He calls everyone to humility, to cast their anxieties on God, and to stay alert. He wrote "through Silvanus," and sends greetings from "she who is in Babylon" and from "Mark, my son."

**Interpretation:** Silvanus is probably Silas; "Mark" is probably John Mark; "Babylon" is commonly understood to mean Rome. None is explicit.

**Truths:** The shepherd charge from John 21 became Peter's charge to other leaders. Humility is written by someone who once refused to have his feet washed.

**2026 use:** Leadership development, elder/shepherd standards, Mentorship, John Mark and Silas relationships, pastoral care in suffering.

## Remembering before departure — 2 Peter 1:12–18; 3:15–18

2 Peter speaks of the writer's approaching death, as the Lord had made clear, and of wanting believers to remember the truth after his departure. It appeals to being an eyewitness of Jesus' majesty on the holy mountain. It refers to "our beloved brother Paul," acknowledging that some things in Paul's letters are hard to understand and are twisted by some readers.

**Uncertainty:** 2 Peter's authorship is debated. Use it as Peter's words in Data Play without making authorship claims in records.

**Truths:** Leaders prepare others to remember after they are gone. Respect for a colleague can include honesty that his writing is difficult.

**2026 use:** Succession and legacy planning, documentation, Paul relationship, testimony.

## Peter's death — John 21:18–19

Jesus told Peter that when he was old he would stretch out his hands and another would dress him and carry him where he did not want to go, indicating the kind of death by which he would glorify God.

**Tradition:** Church tradition says Peter was crucified in Rome. Scripture does not describe his death.

**2026 use:** Rarely. Peter in 2026 is alive and serving. Use only for reflection on faithfulness to the end, and never as comedy.
