## Proposal — A Second Wednesday Group for Newer Families

**Record type:** Leadership Proposal  
**Date:** 2026-05-04  
**Visibility:** Leadership  
**Shared with:** Cornelius, Mordecai  
**Status:** Withdrawn  
**Tags:** #administrative #uncertainty

### Situation

My Wednesday Lifegroup has grown from six to fifteen people since January. Two newcomer households, the Adeyemis and the Lins, now attend regularly. The Lin grandparents speak Mandarin and some English. For three gatherings their sixteen-year-old granddaughter translated everything. Last week she didn't, and discussion slowed considerably.

Several longer-standing members have told me privately they miss the old pace. One asked whether the group had "become an ESL class." Emily looked exhausted after the gathering she translated.

### Proposal

Start a second Wednesday group in the room next door for newer families and anyone who wants discussion with translation support. I would move between the two groups. Both groups would share the meal.

### Reasoning

- Newcomers get a slower pace and dedicated support.
- Longer-standing members keep the discussion depth they value.
- Emily no longer carries the translation load.

### Request

Feedback from leadership before I raise this with the group.

### Comments

**Paul:** Peter, I've read this three times, and I want to answer it carefully, because I think the problem is real and the solution is wrong.

You describe the Lins as a translation load and the longer-standing members as people with a pace to protect. Then you put them in different rooms and say they will share the meal. They'll share the food and not the table.

I'm not writing this to win an argument. You know as well as I do what it looks like when believers eat in separate groups because some people are uncomfortable. You also know what it communicates to the people who get moved. Mr. Lin won't hear "dedicated support." He'll hear that he slowed the group down.

The pace isn't what makes the group. The people at the table are. If the discussion is slower, let it be slower. If Emily is carrying too much, then the group should carry it with her, not hand it back to her family in another room.

I would be glad to think this through with you. But I don't think you should bring this to the group.

**Cornelius:** I would ask one question before any decision: have the Lins been asked what they want?

**Mordecai:** Greeters and Ushers sees newcomers who don't come back after a first month. The most common reason they give is that they felt placed somewhere, rather than welcomed. I can share the notes if helpful.

**Peter:** I've read these. Give me the week.

## I Was About to Build Two Tables

**Record type:** Personal Development / Leadership Reflection  
**Date:** 2026-05-11  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Paul, Barnabas  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #conviction #regret #humility

I'm withdrawing the proposal for a second Wednesday group.

Paul was right. I read his comment on Monday and was irritated. I'd already defended my reasons to myself on Tuesday. By Thursday I couldn't pretend anymore.

God once had to show me three times, in a vision on a rooftop in Joppa, that I must not call anyone unclean whom He had cleansed. I went into Cornelius' house. I defended that decision in Jerusalem, step by step, to people who criticised me for eating with Gentiles.

Then, years later, in Antioch, I was eating with Gentile believers until certain men arrived from James. I drew back and separated myself, because I was afraid of what that group would think. Other Jewish believers followed me. Even Barnabas was carried along. Paul opposed me to his face in front of everyone.

This month I didn't withdraw from anyone's table out of fear. But I wrote a well-organised plan to give the newcomers their own room, because some people at the table were uncomfortable and I didn't want to lose them. It had a reasonable purpose and three tidy bullet points. It would still have left Mr. Lin believing he was the problem.

I'm grateful Paul wrote to me before I brought it to the group, not afterward in front of everyone. That was a kindness I didn't give him credit for on Monday.

### What I'll do instead

- Tell the group plainly on Wednesday that I was about to divide them, and apologise.
- Ask the Lins and the Adeyemis what would help, and ask the whole group to carry the answer together.
- Ask the Leadership Council to make it a church principle that Lifegroups aren't split by language or by how long people have attended.

### What I want Paul and Barnabas to watch for

Whenever I describe people as a "load," or suggest moving someone to another room, ask me who is uncomfortable and why.

**Related goal:** Goal 3 — Keep the same table when different people are watching  
**Biblical account:** Acts 10:9–35; Acts 11:1–18; Galatians 2:11–14.

### Comments

**Paul:** Thank you. For what it's worth, I didn't write my comment as someone who has never divided a table. Barnabas and I once disagreed so sharply about who should come with us that we went separate ways. I'm glad you asked for this to be watched. I'll watch.

**Barnabas:** I'll watch too. And for the record, Peter, I remember Antioch from where I was standing. I went along with you. This time you stopped before anyone had to follow.
