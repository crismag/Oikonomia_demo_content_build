## Hospital Visit Review — I Raised My Voice Before I Understood

**Record type:** Personal Development / Incident Review  
**Date:** 2026-03-16  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Barnabas  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #regret #conviction #humility

### What happened

On Thursday, March 12, John and I went to see Edith Alvarez, one of our oldest members, who is in end-of-life care at the hospital. A staff member at the nursing station told us visiting hours had ended. I told her, loudly, that Edith might not have another day and that we were going in. Another nurse came over. John quietly asked if we could step into the hallway. I went, but I was still angry.

Twenty minutes later the charge nurse confirmed that pastoral visitors could see patients in end-of-life care, and we went in. Edith was asleep most of the visit. She woke once and held my hand.

On Friday I went back and apologised to the staff member. She said she had been given the new policy that morning and had already been shouted at twice before we arrived.

### What I recognise

My concern for Edith was real. That did not make the way I acted right.

I have done this before. When the soldiers came for Jesus in the garden, the disciples asked whether we should strike with the sword. I didn't wait for His answer. I drew and struck the high priest's servant, Malchus, and cut off his ear. Jesus told me to put the sword away, and He touched the man's ear and healed him. He had to undo the harm I did while defending Him.

On Thursday a nurse was the one who had to deal with what I did, and John was the one who stepped in.

### Pattern

- The trigger is urgency: someone vulnerable, a closed door, not enough time.
- My response is force: volume, pressure, my body in the doorway.
- The cost lands on people who are just doing their job, and on whoever is with me.

### Commitments

- I don't lead hospital visits alone until the end of April.
- When I'm told no at a door, I ask one question before I say anything else: "What would make this possible?"
- I talk through any visit where I felt that heat with Barnabas at Mentorship Night.

### Development note

This isn't the kind of failure that ends a ministry. It is the kind that becomes character if I keep calling it zeal.

**Related goal:** Goal 1 — Listen all the way through before I answer  
**Biblical account:** John 18:10–11; Luke 22:49–51.

### Comments

**Barnabas:** Thank you for sharing this with me. I noticed you wrote "I went back and apologised" as one sentence. It was probably the hardest part. We'll talk Tuesday.

## What Belongs in a Visit Note

**Record type:** Relationship / Documentation Review  
**Date:** 2026-03-23  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** John, Cris  
**Status:** Open  
**Tags:** #reflective #frustration #light

John's restricted visit note from March 12 describes the incident at the nursing station in full: what I said, how loud I was, where I was standing, how long it took me to calm down, and his exact words asking me to step away.

The note is accurate. I asked John whether it needed to be that complete. The note goes to Field Ministry leadership and sits in Edith's care history, and I don't think the detail of my raised voice helps anyone caring for Edith. John thinks a visit record that leaves out why we were delayed twenty minutes isn't a true record.

We both agreed to ask Cris what the record actually needs, since Cris isn't the one who shouted or the one who wrote it.

For what it's worth, I've been here before. A servant of the high priest once lost his ear in a garden, and one of the written accounts of that night names both me and the servant. The other accounts don't. I have made my peace with that one.

### Comments

**John:** I'm not trying to embarrass you. A care record should say why a visit started twenty minutes late, and who was responsible. If I leave it out, the next team won't know the staff at that station have had a hard week.

**Peter:** I agree they should know. I'm asking whether they need to know the volume.

**John:** I wrote "raised voice." I did not write the volume.

**Peter:** You wrote "raised voice, sustained."

**John:** It was sustained.

**Cris:** I have read both of your positions and the note. Proposal: Edith's care history says, "Visit delayed by 20 minutes due to a visitor-policy dispute at the nursing station; resolved by the charge nurse; staff member received an apology the following day." The full description stays in John's restricted visit note, shared with Peter and Luke only. The incident review stays in Peter's personal record.

**John:** That is accurate.

**Peter:** That is fair.

**Cris:** Then I will make the change. For the record, neither of you asked me to decide who was right, and I have not.

**John:** Peter was wrong at the nursing station and right about the care history.

**Cris:** Noted. Not in the care history.

## Apology to John — Counting Chairs

**Record type:** Relationship / Personal Development  
**Date:** 2026-08-26  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** John  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #light #humility #regret

In July I asked John how many people came to his Wednesday Lifegroup, which has a waiting list. John says the tone was "competitive." I asked a second time in August, which John says confirmed the tone.

My own Lifegroup talked about comparison on August 19, and I told them I would apologise. I did, today, over coffee.

I have asked this kind of question before. After Jesus restored me and told me to follow Him, I turned around, saw the disciple whom Jesus loved following, and asked, "Lord, what about this man?" Jesus said, "What is that to you? You follow me."

John's group is bigger because John's group is good. Mine is smaller because mine is good in a different way, and because a sixteen-year-old translator and a man with strong opinions about chairs don't fit fifteen more people.

**Related goal:** Goal 1 — Listen all the way through before I answer  
**Biblical account:** John 21:20–22.

### Comments

**John:** Apology accepted. For the record, I wrote down the tone the first time and did not mention it until you did.

**Peter:** Of course you wrote it down.

**John:** I write most things down.
