### Discussion

**Where newcomers get lost.** Mordecai walked through the pathway from the door to a second visit. Andrew described the moment after the service: newcomers stand near the coffee table for about four minutes, and if nobody speaks to them in that time, most leave. Philip said many first questions are practical (parking, children's rooms, whether they have to give money) and nobody has an easy answer sheet.

**Language.** Cornelius reported that three interpreters have volunteered (Mandarin, Spanish, Yoruba), none related to the families they would help. The Lin family has asked for one interpreter on the first Sunday of each month rather than every week.

**Lifegroup handoff.** Peter said the most successful newcomer handoff this year was Andrew physically walking the Adeyemi family to his Lifegroup, not handing them a card. Andrew said he did that because Tunde looked like he was about to leave.

**Honour.** Cornelius mentioned, as a gentle joke about his own history, that when newcomers bow slightly or call leaders by titles, greeters should do what Peter once did and tell them to stand up. Peter said that is correct and that he is still telling Cornelius to stand up.

### Decisions

- Every newcomer is spoken to within four minutes of the service ending. Greeters own this.
- A one-page answer sheet for practical first questions, in English, Mandarin, Spanish and Yoruba.
- Lifegroup introductions are made in person, by walking people to the leader, not by handing out cards.
- Interpreters are scheduled on request, starting with the first Sunday of each month.
