## 2026-06-07 — Leadership Council: Language and Newcomers in Lifegroups

**Kind:** Minutes  
**Meeting type:** Leaders  
**Facilitator:** Moses  
**Note taker:** John  
**Participants:** Moses, David, Peter, Paul, Samuel, Deborah, Ezra, Esther, Cornelius, Mordecai  
**Tags:** #administrative #serious #hope

### Agenda

1. Peter's withdrawn proposal and what prompted it.
2. Greeters and Ushers observations about newcomers who don't return.
3. A church principle for Lifegroups.
4. Practical support.

### Discussion

**1. What prompted it.** Peter explained in order: two newcomer households joined his Wednesday group; one family's grandparents speak Mandarin; a teenage granddaughter carried translation; longer-standing members became impatient; he drafted a proposal for a separate group; Paul challenged it; Cornelius asked whether the family had been consulted; Peter withdrew it and told the group. He said he brought it to the Council "so it becomes a decision on purpose, not a habit by accident."

**2. Greeters and Ushers.** Mordecai reported that of 31 first-time households since January, 12 have not returned after a month. In follow-up calls, the most common reason given is feeling "placed" in a group rather than welcomed into one. Cornelius added that, as someone whose household was once the outsiders, being given a separate room is kind in intention and isolating in effect.

**3. Principle.** Paul spoke in favour of a clear principle. Ezra asked that it be written so that it serves understanding, not only attendance: people should be able to understand what is read and discussed. Esther asked that the principle protect people who are hesitant to ask for help, not only those who ask. David said the Psalmists had faced a similar question about songs in other languages and chose to include them rather than hold a separate service. Samuel cautioned against using this as a reason to criticise the members who were impatient; they need patience too. Deborah asked who would own it after today.

**4. Practical support.** Discussion of printed bilingual materials, paired discussion, and volunteer interpreters who are not family members.

### Decisions

- Lifegroups at this church are not divided by language, culture or length of attendance. Where language is a barrier, the group adapts together.
- Groups that need it receive printed bilingual passages and questions, and use paired discussion before whole-group discussion.
- Family members, especially young people, are not expected to act as regular interpreters.
- Greeters and Ushers, with Cornelius, reviews the newcomer pathway and reports back by August.
