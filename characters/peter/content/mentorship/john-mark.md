## Missed Visit — Late Communication

**Record type:** Mentorship Record / Follow-up  
**Date:** 2026-06-16  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** John Mark, Barnabas  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #pastoral #compassion

Mark missed his Thursday visit on June 11, and didn't tell anyone until Friday afternoon. The household waited. James visited them Saturday morning.

Mark came to me before I went to him, which I noticed.

He said that week had been hard. He had taken on two extra shifts at work and felt he couldn't admit he'd overcommitted. So he kept planning to make the visit until it was too late, and then he was ashamed to call.

I told him that shame was the reason he waited, not the reason he missed it. I know that pattern. After the denial, there came a night when all I knew to say was, "I'm going fishing." It was Jesus who came to the shore and called to us.

We didn't pretend it was nothing. The household was let down. Mark called them himself on Monday and apologised.

### Agreed

- The "tell someone as soon as you know" rule applies to embarrassing reasons too.
- Mark keeps his route. Nobody is taking it away over one missed visit.
- The team adopted a backup visitor for every Thursday route (see Field Ministry report of June 15). This is not because of Mark; it should have existed already.

**Related goal:** Goal 4 — Strengthen others who have failed  
**Biblical account:** John 21:1–7.

### Comments

**Barnabas:** He came to you first. That's the development. Thank you for not making it smaller or bigger than it was.
