## Storeroom — I Wanted to Throw It All Out

**Record type:** Personal Development / Anger Review  
**Date:** 2026-04-17  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Ezra  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #anger #restraint

On Thursday Hezekiah showed me the main storeroom full of Events decorations, with our cleaning supplies stacked in the corridor.

What I wanted to do, honestly: carry every box out onto the pavement myself, text Solomon a photograph, and raise it in front of the Council on Sunday.

When I returned to Jerusalem and found that Eliashib had given Tobiah a room in the temple courts, I was very grieved. I threw Tobiah's household goods out of the room, ordered it cleaned and restored the offerings. Years later, when I found people breaking the covenant they had sworn, I contended with them, cursed them, struck some of them and pulled out their hair.

Scripture records what I did. It doesn't say I was right to do all of it that way. I know the man who did it. I'd prefer he doesn't run Facilities.

### What I did instead

- Called Ezra on Friday morning, before speaking to anyone.
- Didn't text Solomon a photograph.
- Asked for a meeting with Solomon for Tuesday.

### What Ezra said

That the storeroom is a real problem and my anger isn't wrong. That Solomon will fix it faster if he isn't humiliated. That I should ask what Events actually needs, because they wouldn't have taken the room if they had somewhere else.

**Related goal:** Goal 3 — Confront without losing control of myself  
**Biblical account:** Nehemiah 13:4–9; Nehemiah 13:23–27.

### Comments

**Ezra:** You called on a Friday morning, not a Thursday night. That is the progress. We'll talk after Tuesday.

## Ladder Near-Miss — Anger That Waited

**Record type:** Personal Development / Anger Review  
**Date:** 2026-08-02  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Ezra  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #serious #fear #anger #restraint

Yesterday a volunteer climbed a ladder alone in the children's wing, against the rule, and the ladder slipped. He caught himself. It could have been a head injury on a concrete floor.

I was angrier than I've been all year, and it was mostly fear. I wanted to shout. I stopped the work, sent people for water, and called Ezra from the car park. Then I held the stand-down about the rule, not the person.

Today, with Hezekiah present, I spoke to the volunteer privately. He was already frightened and ashamed. I told him I was angry because I was afraid for him, that the rule exists because I would rather lose a Saturday than lose a person, and that he is still welcome on the team. He asked to lead the next safety briefing.

When the builders were afraid, I didn't shame them. I told them to remember the Lord, who is great and awesome, and to fight for their brothers, sons, daughters, wives and homes. The same care that makes me angry about a ladder should make me careful with the man who climbed it.

**Related goal:** Goal 3 — Confront without losing control of myself  
**Biblical account:** Nehemiah 4:14.

### Comments

**Ezra:** You called from the car park before you said anything to anyone. That's the goal, practised under real pressure.
