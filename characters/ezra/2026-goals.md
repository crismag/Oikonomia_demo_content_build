# Ezra — 2026 Goals

**Oikonomia section:** Goals

These are Ezra's 2026 Oikonomia goals. They come from his lived biblical history and are meant to gather progress through Lifegroup reports, Mentorship Night, Leadership Council work, document reviews and his check-ins with Nehemiah, Daniel and Josiah. Progress entries are added as records are written; none are recorded yet.

---

## Goal 1 — Study it, do it, then teach it

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #reflective #devotional #conviction

### Why this matters

When the book summed me up, it said I had set my heart to seek the LORD's law, to do it, and to teach it. The order matters. I am a scribe. It would be easy for me to know the text thoroughly and teach it well while my own obedience lagged behind my explanations.

### 2026 actions

- Keep a daily reading pattern that comes before preparing anything I teach.
- Before I teach a passage in Lifegroup or on Sunday, write down one thing I have changed because of it.
- Ask a trusted leader each quarter whether my teaching and my conduct match.
- Protect study time on my calendar the way I would protect a Council meeting.

**Biblical account:** Ezra 7:6, 10.

## Goal 2 — Help people understand, not only hear

**Status:** Active  
**Ministry:** Mentorship  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #pastoral #hope #joy

### Why this matters

When the people asked me to bring the book of the Law, I read from early morning until midday. The reading alone would not have been enough. Levites helped the people understand: they read distinctly and gave the sense, so that they understood. The people wept, and then they went home to eat, send food to those who had none, and celebrate, because they had understood.

I want more explainers, not a louder reader.

### 2026 actions

- Develop at least four Lifegroup leaders at Mentorship Night who can explain a passage clearly in their own words.
- In my Lifegroup, stop after each reading and ask what people heard before I say anything.
- Prepare plain-language study notes for Radicals with Daniel.
- Measure success by questions answered, not by pages covered.

**Biblical account:** Nehemiah 8:1–12.

## Goal 3 — Weigh it out, write it down, hand it over

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #administrative #accountability #gratitude

### Why this matters

Before we left the river Ahava I weighed the silver, gold and vessels into the hands of twelve leading priests and told them that they and the vessels were holy to the LORD. In Jerusalem everything was weighed again, counted, and the whole weight written down. Nobody had to wonder where anything had gone, and nobody carrying it could be accused.

The church trusts leaders with money, equipment, documents and people's private information. A clear handover protects all of them.

### 2026 actions

- Put a simple custody and handover record in place for shared equipment and documents with Facilities and Production.
- Review the church's volunteer forms and written standards so that each one says plainly what it is for and who can see it.
- Reconcile any resources I am responsible for before the end of each quarter.
- Keep my own records short enough that Cris will actually read them.

**Biblical account:** Ezra 8:24–34.

## Goal 4 — Ask who bears the cost before a corrective decision

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #serious #humility #discernment

### Why this matters

When leaders told me that returned exiles, including priests, Levites and officials, had married women from the surrounding peoples, I tore my clothes, pulled out my hair and sat appalled until evening. I prayed and confessed. Shecaniah proposed a covenant to send away the wives and the children born to them, and told me to be courageous and do it. I had the leaders swear to it. The people stood trembling in the rain and asked for time and an orderly process. Four men stood against the arrangement; the book does not say why. The book ends by recording that some of those men had children with these wives.

Scripture records what we did and why. It does not record the women's or children's voices. I will not tell that story as though it were simple, and I will not let anyone use it as a pattern for decisions about families today.

What I can do in 2026 is make sure that when the Council considers a decision meant to correct something, someone asks who will bear its cost, and that the people who bear it are heard.

### 2026 actions

- On any Leadership Council decision that affects families or vulnerable people, ask who bears the cost and whether they have been heard.
- Take time over grave decisions, even when a room full of grieving people wants action now.
- Welcome dissent in the record, as the text records the four who opposed.
- Keep conversations about this history restricted and pastoral, never a debate for its own sake.

**Biblical account:** Ezra 9:1–10:44.
