## 2026-05-25 — Teach the Procedure, Not Only the Correction

**Record type:** Leadership Development / Coaching Report  
**Visibility:** Named people  
**Shared with:** Nehemiah  
**Status:** Follow-up Required  
**Tags:** #serious #reflective #accountability

Nehemiah and I agreed that he needs a Facilities process that can carry correction before his anger enters the room. The shared coaching note is owned in his workspace, dated 2026-05-11; I am not duplicating the minutes here.

My part is to read the wording of a procedure with him, ask who can understand it, and ask who bears the cost when it fails. That is not softness. A rule that nobody can explain will be enforced only by the person with the loudest voice.

My own history makes this question weighty. When leaders reported covenant unfaithfulness in Jerusalem, I grieved, confessed, and later acted on a proposal that led to wives and children being sent away. Scripture does not tell us their side or what became of them. I will not use that account as a policy template. I remember it whenever a leader wants a fast answer whose cost will fall elsewhere.

**Attention:** Ezra to review Nehemiah's first two correction notes for clarity and restraint.  
**Attention to:** Ezra  
**Related goal:** Goal 4 — Ask who bears the cost before a corrective decision

## 2026-06-18 — Thursday Lifegroup: Understanding Before Agreement

**Oikonomia section:** Lifegroup Gathering
**Led by:** Ezra  
**Venue:** Fellowship hall  
**Attendance:** Nehemiah, Daniel, Josiah, Martha  
**Tags:** #reflective #devotional #joy

We read part of Nehemiah 8 slowly. The people asked Ezra to bring the Book of the Law; he read from morning until midday, and Levites helped the people understand what was being read. When the people wept, Nehemiah, Ezra, and the Levites told them not to mourn, but to celebrate and to share food with those who had none prepared.

The group noticed that understanding did not end in analysis. It led to worship, generosity, and a changed practice. Josiah asked whether explaining a text can ever be too slow. I said that explanation can be slow; incomprehension is slower, because it asks people to obey words they have not heard.

## 2026-08-13 — Entrusted Items Reconciled, With One Useful Discrepancy

**Record type:** Documents / Handover Reconciliation  
**Visibility:** Shared  
**Status:** Complete  
**Tags:** #administrative #gratitude #discernment

The teaching-event equipment was counted at handover and counted again on return. One adapter was listed under an old label; it was present, but the label would have made the next handover needlessly uncertain. The record has been corrected.

Before Ezra travelled from Babylon to Jerusalem, he weighed silver, gold, and temple vessels to entrusted priests, charged them to guard the items, and had everything weighed and recorded again on arrival. This 2026 reconciliation is not that journey. It is a small practice shaped by the same conviction: entrusted things should be handed over in a way another person can verify.
