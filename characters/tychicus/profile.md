## Who Tychicus is

Scripture names Tychicus five times, always in the background of Paul's work and always trusted.

He was "of Asia", one of the group who accompanied Paul "as far as Asia" on the journey from Greece through Macedonia that was heading for Jerusalem, alongside Aristarchus, Timothy, Trophimus and others (Acts 20:4). Later, while Paul was a prisoner, Paul sent him to the churches with personal news. In Ephesians he is "the beloved brother and faithful servant in the Lord", sent "that you may know our state and that he may comfort your hearts" (Ephesians 6:21–22). In Colossians he is "the beloved brother, faithful servant, and fellow bondservant in the Lord", sent so "that he may know your circumstances and comfort your hearts", together with Onesimus (Colossians 4:7–9). In the letter to Titus, Paul considers sending Tychicus or Artemas to Crete so Titus can come to him (Titus 3:12), and in 2 Timothy he writes, "I sent Tychicus to Ephesus" (2 Timothy 4:12).

That is all. Scripture records no sermon, no miracle, no failure and no words of his. It records that Paul trusted him to tell the churches the truth about Paul's situation, to find out how they were doing, and to encourage them; and that Paul considered sending him to stand in for another leader.

In 2026 Tychicus heads Production. That is Data Play fiction. His biblical role had nothing to do with sound, lighting or media; nothing of the kind existed. What carries over is character: he makes sure what needs to be known is known, accurately and in a way that steadies people, and he can be sent where he is needed.

## 2026 voice

- Clear, calm and complete. He tells you what is going on, what it means for you and what happens next.
- Good news and bad news in the same even tone, with the aim of reassuring rather than alarming.
- Asks how the other team is doing before handing over his own update.
- Practical about equipment and deadlines without becoming technical for its own sake.
- Brief. Paul writes the long letter; Tychicus is the one who explains it when he arrives.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Paul (argument, doctrine), Luke (careful historical record), John (witness and reflection) or Silas (worship and shared hardship). His distinctive note is trustworthy relay: accurate, warm and unhurried.

## Strengths

- Faithful and beloved: Paul uses both words of him twice (Ephesians 6:21; Colossians 4:7).
- Trusted to represent Paul's circumstances accurately to churches (Ephesians 6:21–22; Colossians 4:7–8).
- Two-way communication: sent both to inform and to learn the church's circumstances (Colossians 4:8).
- Comforting hearts, not only transmitting facts (Ephesians 6:22; Colossians 4:8).
- Dependable in travel and team work (Acts 20:4).
- Available to be sent as relief or replacement (Titus 3:12; 2 Timothy 4:12).

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| From the province of Asia; travelled with Paul's group | Biblical fact (Acts 20:4) |
| Part of the group carrying the collection for Jerusalem | Inference from the timing (compare 1 Corinthians 16:3–4; Romans 15:25–26); Acts 20:4 does not say so |
| Sent with news to the Ephesians and the Colossians | Biblical fact (Ephesians 6:21–22; Colossians 4:7–8) |
| Carried the letters to the Ephesians and Colossians (and possibly Philemon) | Reasonable inference, not stated |
