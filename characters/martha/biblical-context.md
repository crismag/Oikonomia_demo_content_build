# Martha — Biblical Context Library

This file records Martha's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are Martha's memories, grief, correction, confession and service.

Scripture gives Martha three scenes: Luke 10:38–42, John 11 and John 12:1–2. That is enough for a real person, and not enough for a biography. Do not fill the gaps.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered. Lazarus is not a Data Play character; refer to him in prose.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

---

## Welcoming Jesus; "left me to serve alone" — Luke 10:38–42

As Jesus and His disciples travelled, He came to a village where a woman named Martha "received him into her house." Her sister Mary "sat at Jesus' feet and heard his word." Martha "was distracted with much serving." She came to Jesus and said, "Lord, don't you care that my sister left me to serve alone? Ask her therefore to help me." Jesus answered, "Martha, Martha, you are anxious and troubled about many things, but one thing is needed. Mary has chosen the good part, which will not be taken away from her."

**Fact and inference:** Luke does not name the village or say how many guests there were. That it was Bethany is inferred from John 11:1. Scripture does not record what Martha said or did next.

**Interpretation:** Sitting at a teacher's feet was the posture of a disciple (compare Acts 22:3, where Paul was brought up "at the feet of Gamaliel"). Many readers see significance in a woman taking that place. This is a reasonable reading; Luke does not comment on it.

**Truths:** Jesus did not tell Martha that hospitality was wrong. He named anxiety and distraction, and He protected Mary's place at His feet. Martha brought her frustration straight to Jesus instead of nursing it; that honesty is part of the scene too. A workload problem framed as "make her help me" is not the same as a request for help.

**2026 use:** Victuals volunteer rota and workload; asking for help without accusing; protecting volunteers' time to sit under teaching; personal development; Lifegroup discussion. Warm and lightly self-aware at most; never a joke at Martha's expense.

## The message about Lazarus; Jesus waits — John 11:1–16

Lazarus of Bethany, brother of Martha and Mary, was sick. The sisters sent word to Jesus: "Lord, behold, he for whom you have great affection is sick." Jesus said the sickness was "not to death, but for the glory of God." John adds, "Now Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus." Yet Jesus stayed two more days where He was. Then He told the disciples plainly, "Lazarus is dead," and set out for Judea, although the disciples reminded Him that people there had recently tried to stone Him. Thomas said to the others, "Let's also go, that we may die with him."

**Truths:** Being loved by Jesus and experiencing His delay are both in the same paragraph. The sisters' message asked for nothing specific; it simply told Him who was sick.

**2026 use:** Prayer requests when a family member is ill; waiting; pastoral care. Serious.

## Martha meets Jesus outside the village — John 11:17–27, 30

By the time Jesus arrived, Lazarus had been in the tomb four days. Bethany was close to Jerusalem, and many people had come to console the sisters. When Martha heard Jesus was coming, she went out to meet Him; Mary stayed in the house. Martha said, "Lord, if you would have been here, my brother wouldn't have died. Even now I know that whatever you ask of God, God will give you." Jesus said, "Your brother will rise again." Martha answered, "I know that he will rise again in the resurrection at the last day." Jesus said, "I am the resurrection and the life. He who believes in me will still live, even if he dies. Whoever lives and believes in me will never die. Do you believe this?" She said, "Yes, Lord. I have come to believe that you are the Christ, God's Son, he who comes into the world."

**Truths:** Grief and faith are spoken in the same breath. Martha is honest about the loss and clear about who Jesus is. Jesus' "I am the resurrection and the life" is spoken to Martha, in a conversation she started by going out to meet Him.

**Interpretation:** Martha's confession closely parallels Peter's in Matthew 16:16. This is an observation many readers make, not a claim John states.

**2026 use:** Testimony; grief care; a Lifegroup gathering on John 11; the theological weight of Martha beyond hospitality. Serious; never comedy.

## "The Teacher is here and is calling you" — John 11:28–37

Martha went back and called Mary privately: "The Teacher is here and is calling you." Mary got up quickly. The mourners, thinking she was going to the tomb to weep, followed her. Mary fell at Jesus' feet and said the same words Martha had said. When Jesus saw her weeping, and the others weeping with her, He "groaned in the spirit and was troubled." He asked where Lazarus had been laid. "Jesus wept." Some said, "See how much affection he had for him!" Others asked why He had not kept Lazarus from dying.

**Truths:** Martha did not keep Jesus to herself; she went to get her sister. Jesus wept with people whose grief He was about to answer.

**2026 use:** Sibling care in grief; bringing someone else to Jesus; Martha and Mary's different ways of grieving without either being wrong.

## At the tomb: "by this time there is a stench" — John 11:38–44

The tomb was a cave with a stone against it. Jesus said, "Take away the stone." Martha, "the sister of him who was dead," said, "Lord, by this time there is a stench, for he has been dead four days." Jesus said to her, "Didn't I tell you that if you believed, you would see God's glory?" They took away the stone. Jesus thanked the Father aloud for the sake of the crowd, then called, "Lazarus, come out!" The dead man came out, bound hand and foot, his face wrapped in a cloth. Jesus said, "Free him, and let him go."

**Truths:** Faith that had just confessed Jesus as the Christ still hesitated at the practical, physical reality of death. Jesus reminded her of His word rather than rebuking her. Grief-realism and faith sit together in the same person.

**2026 use:** Personal development (realism that must not have the final word); testimony. The detail of decay is part of the reality of death and is never a joke.

## Consequences: the council and the threat to Lazarus — John 11:45–57; 12:9–11

Many who had come to Mary and seen what Jesus did believed in Him; others reported it to the Pharisees. The chief priests and Pharisees met, and from that day they plotted to kill Jesus. Later, when crowds came to Bethany to see Lazarus, "the chief priests conspired to put Lazarus to death also," because many were believing in Jesus on his account.

**Truths:** The miracle that restored Martha's family also brought danger to it.

**2026 use:** Rarely, and seriously: the cost of testimony, fear for a family member. Never comedy.

## "Martha served" — John 12:1–8

Six days before the Passover, Jesus came to Bethany. "So they made him a supper there. Martha served, but Lazarus was one of those who sat at the table with him." At the same meal Mary anointed Jesus' feet with a pound of costly ointment and wiped them with her hair, and Judas Iscariot objected that it could have been sold for three hundred denarii and given to the poor. John says Judas said this because he was a thief. Jesus said, "Leave her alone. She has kept this for the day of my burial."

**Fact:** John does not say whose house this was or record Martha saying anything at this meal.

**Truths:** Martha is serving again after Luke 10, and this time nothing in the text corrects her. Serving was never the problem. The same meal held Martha's work, Lazarus' presence and Mary's worship.

**2026 use:** Victuals as honoured, ordinary faithfulness; a shared celebratory meal; Martha and Mary each giving in her own way. See Mary of Bethany's files for the anointing.

**Note on the other anointing accounts:** Matthew 26:6–13 and Mark 14:3–9 record a woman anointing Jesus at Bethany in the house of Simon the leper. They do not name her. Do not place Martha in those accounts.
