# Martha — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Jesus
Martha received Jesus into her house (Luke 10:38), brought Him her frustration (Luke 10:40), was corrected by name (Luke 10:41–42), sent Him word when her brother was sick (John 11:3), went out to meet Him in grief, heard Him say "I am the resurrection and the life," and confessed Him as the Christ (John 11:20–27). John records that "Jesus loved Martha, and her sister, and Lazarus" (John 11:5). Jesus is the centre of Martha's story and not an ordinary demo account. He does not receive Victuals requests, comments or tasks.

### Mary of Bethany — sister
Mary sat at Jesus' feet while Martha served (Luke 10:39–40). When Lazarus died, Martha went out to meet Jesus and Mary stayed in the house; Martha then called her privately, and Mary said the same words to Jesus that Martha had said (John 11:20, 28–32). At the Bethany supper Martha served and Mary anointed Jesus (John 12:2–3).

**Qualification:** Scripture does not say which sister was older, and records no conversation between them apart from Martha's message in John 11:28. Do not invent a history of rivalry.

**2026 fiction:** Martha and Mary co-lead a Wednesday Lifegroup at Martha's home. They have deliberately arranged the evening so both sit for the teaching and both serve the meal. They are loyal to each other and very different: Martha writes long, practical gathering reports; Mary writes two sentences. Martha occasionally feels the old pull to ask someone to tell Mary to help, and sometimes names it herself before anyone else can. Their disagreements are real, affectionate and never a morality play in which one sister is the spiritual one.

### Lazarus — brother (not on the roster)
Lazarus fell sick, died and was four days in the tomb before Jesus raised him (John 11). He later sat at table with Jesus while Martha served (John 12:2), and the chief priests plotted to kill him too (John 12:10–11). Refer to him in prose only.

**2026 use:** Martha may mention her brother in testimony or grief-care records. Do not give Lazarus 2026 dialogue or tasks, and do not use his death or the plot against him lightly.

### Mary Magdalene
**Boundary:** Scripture places Martha and Mary Magdalene in no shared scene. Mary Magdalene is a different Mary from Martha's sister. Do not merge them.

### Thomas
Thomas was with the disciples when Jesus decided to go to Bethany after Lazarus died, and said, "Let's also go, that we may die with him" (John 11:16).

**Qualification:** John does not narrate Thomas at Bethany; his presence there is inferred from the disciples travelling with Jesus.

**2026 fiction:** Thomas can respect Martha as someone who also said the brave, blunt thing on the way to Bethany. Light connection only.

### The disciples; Judas Iscariot (not on the roster)
The disciples travelled with Jesus to Bethany (John 11:7–16). Judas objected at the Bethany supper where Martha served (John 12:4–6). Judas is not a Data Play character.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Moses — reporting leader
Moses is Martha's reporting leader. His March 2026 reports already reflect on manna, provision and food-handling with Victuals (see `characters/moses/content/2026-reports/2026-01-to-03.md` and `characters/moses/content/special-reports/provision-water-and-victuals.md`). Martha can treat those as conversations she took part in as Victuals head.

**Boundary:** Moses has no memory of Martha, Bethany or any New Testament event. Martha may know the manna account as Scripture; Moses hears her story as a colleague in 2026.

**2026 fiction:** Moses once told God he could not carry the people alone (Numbers 11:14), and Jethro told him the work was too heavy to do alone (Exodus 18:18). Martha once told Jesus her sister had left her to serve alone. Moses is the leader most likely to take Martha's workload concern seriously and least likely to tell her simply to try harder. He is also the one who has to say, kindly, that more volunteers will not fix anxiety.

### Lydia — Victuals leader
Lydia, who persuaded Paul's team to stay in her house (Acts 16:15) and later welcomed them again after prison (Acts 16:40), serves as a Victuals leader under Martha.

**2026 fiction:** Two hosts with different styles. Lydia's instinct is to invite more people and persuade them to stay; Martha's is to make sure there is enough for the people already coming. Their collaboration produces good hospitality planning and occasional headcount friction.

### Mary of Bethany's ministry head — Miriam
Mary serves in Psalmists under Miriam. Martha and Miriam coordinate when Sunday worship timings and refreshment timings collide and when the sisters' Lifegroup overlaps with Psalmists commitments. Miriam's history as a sister is her own; do not draw parallels Scripture does not make.

### Solomon (Events), Nehemiah (Facilities), Stephen (More Than Enough)
Operational neighbours. Events owns celebrations and Victuals owns their food; Facilities owns the kitchen building and equipment; More Than Enough owns outreach food giving, and Victuals passes surplus to Stephen's team. Stephen was among the Seven appointed so the widows would not be neglected "in the daily service" (Acts 6:1–5), which gives him and Martha a natural shared interest in food and fairness without merging their ministries.

### Mary Magdalene and Field Ministry
When a household is bereaved, Field Ministry (Peter's ministry, with Mary Magdalene among its leaders) owns the visit and Victuals provides meals when asked. Martha brings grief experience to the arrangement; she does not take over pastoral care.

### Cris
Cris receives cross-ministry follow-ups from Martha and never assigns work inside Victuals (see `characters/cris/relationships.md`). Martha likes Cris because his follow-ups have due dates.
