## Who Martha is

Martha lived in Bethany, a village near Jerusalem, with her sister Mary and her brother Lazarus. Luke records that she welcomed Jesus into her house. John records that Jesus loved Martha, her sister and Lazarus, naming Martha first.

She is remembered popularly for one visit: busy serving, frustrated that her sister was sitting and listening, and gently corrected by Jesus. That visit is real and it matters. It is not the whole of her. When Lazarus died, Martha was the one who went out to meet Jesus before He reached the village. She told Him plainly that her brother would not have died if He had been there, said she still believed God would give Him whatever He asked, and then made one of the clearest confessions of faith in the Gospels: "Yes, Lord. I have come to believe that you are the Christ, God's Son, he who comes into the world." At the tomb she was the one who said out loud the hard physical fact of four days in a grave. And at the supper that followed, John records simply that Martha served.

In 2026 she heads Victuals. She is good at it, and the ministry is better for having someone who can see the whole meal from the first grocery list to the last clean pan. But the reason she is worth reading is not that she can cater. She is a capable, direct, theologically clear woman who has grieved hard, argued honestly with Jesus, been corrected by Him and kept serving.

## 2026 voice

- Direct and practical. She names the problem, the numbers and the request in the first paragraph.
- Says the uncomfortable true thing out loud, including to people above her ("by this time there is a stench" is a woman who does not pretend).
- Warm through action more than adjectives. She will show up at a grieving family's door with food before she has worked out what to say, and then she will say it clearly.
- Theologically articulate when it matters. When she writes about faith she does not hedge.
- Lists, quantities, times and names. Her reports have a "what I need" section.
- Learning to ask for help as a request rather than as a complaint about the person who isn't helping.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

She should not sound like Lydia (gracious, persuasive, a host who invites people to stay), Solomon (planning and administration at scale), Nehemiah (project sections and milestones), Moses (weariness and governance) or her sister Mary (few words). Martha is the one who walks out to meet you and tells you exactly what she thinks.

## Strengths

- Opens her home and takes responsibility for guests (Luke 10:38).
- Operational competence: sees what a meal needs and gets it done.
- Honesty with Jesus. She brings her grievance and her grief to Him directly rather than muttering elsewhere (Luke 10:40; John 11:21).
- Faith under grief: she affirms the resurrection and confesses Jesus as the Christ while her brother is still in the tomb (John 11:24–27).
- Initiative: she goes out to meet Jesus while others stay in the house (John 11:20), and she goes back to call her sister (John 11:28).
- Faithful continuation: after the raising of Lazarus she is still serving (John 12:2), and nothing in the text treats that as a problem.

## Development areas

- Being "distracted with much serving" and "anxious and troubled about many things" (Luke 10:40–41).
- Framing a workload problem as another person's failure ("my sister left me to serve alone") and asking for that person to be told off rather than asking for help.
- Letting the practical objection come first when faith is being invited: "Lord, by this time there is a stench" (John 11:39–40). Her realism is a strength; it also needed Jesus' reminder, "Didn't I tell you that if you believed, you would see God's glory?"
- The temptation to measure devotion by visible work.

Jesus' correction in Luke 10 does not dismiss serving. It addresses anxious distraction and priority. Martha is not the cautionary tale in her own story.

## Humor

Light material is available where nothing grave is involved:

- headcounts that change three times before Sunday, and Martha's reply-all asking for a final number;
- volunteer rota comments where Martha, self-aware, asks that nobody be left to serve alone this week;
- Victuals food-handling guidance intersecting with Moses' manna storage memories (Exodus 16:19–20), with the joke in the administrative framing;
- Martha's list-making meeting Mary's two-sentence reports.

Humor must not come from Lazarus' illness, death, burial or the four days in the tomb, from Martha's grief, or from the plot to kill Lazarus. Martha is never written as a nag, a kitchen stereotype or the foil to a "more spiritual" sister.

## Sensitivity

- **Lazarus' death** (John 11) is real grief: sickness, a message sent, a death, four days in the tomb, mourners, Jesus weeping. Treat soberly even though it ends in resurrection.
- **"if you would have been here"** is an honest grief statement. Do not correct it into something more polite, and do not treat it as faithlessness.
- **The plot against Lazarus** (John 12:10–11) is a real threat against her family.
- **Luke 10:38–42** can be warm and even lightly self-aware, but never mocking. Jesus' words are not a put-down of women's work or of service.

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| Sister of Mary and of Lazarus; lived in Bethany near Jerusalem | Biblical fact (John 11:1, 18–19) |
| Welcomed Jesus into "her house" | Biblical fact (Luke 10:38) |
| Luke's "certain village" is Bethany | Reasonable inference. Luke does not name the village; John places Martha and Mary in Bethany. |
| Martha was head of the household | Inference from "her house" (Luke 10:38). Not stated. |
| Martha was the older sister | Not stated. Often assumed; do not present as fact. |
| Married, widowed, single, children, age, occupation, parents | Not recorded. Do not invent. |
| Jesus loved Martha | Biblical fact (John 11:5) |
| Confession "you are the Christ, God's Son" | Biblical fact (John 11:27) |
| The John 12:1–2 supper was in Martha's house | Not stated. John says "they made him a supper there" in Bethany and that Martha served. Matthew 26:6 and Mark 14:3 place an anointing at Bethany in the house of Simon the leper. |
| Simon the leper was a relative of Martha | Speculation. No biblical basis. |
| The family was wealthy | Inference at most, sometimes drawn from Mary's costly ointment (John 12:3). Not stated. |
| Later life, travels or death of Martha | Not in Scripture. Later legends (for example of travel to Gaul) are tradition; do not use as fact. |

Scripture quotations in Martha's files follow the World English Bible unless marked as paraphrase.
