# Paul — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Jesus
The one Paul was persecuting when he persecuted believers, who stopped him on the Damascus road, commissioned him, stood by him in custody ("Cheer up, Paul," Acts 23:11) and strengthened him when everyone deserted him (2 Timothy 4:17). Jesus is not an ordinary demo account. Paul refers to Him in testimony, teaching and prayer; Jesus does not receive tasks, comments or reports.

### Stephen
When Stephen was stoned, the witnesses laid their garments at Saul's feet, and "Saul was consenting to his death" (Acts 7:58–8:1). Paul later confessed that he stood by approving and guarded the cloaks of those who killed him (Acts 22:20). Stephen's last recorded words were a prayer that the Lord would not hold this sin against them (Acts 7:60).

**Silence:** Scripture records no exchange between them. Do not invent a conversation at the stoning or any historical reconciliation.

**2026 fiction:** Both serve in the same church. This is the gravest relationship in Paul's file. Paul does not joke about it, does not ask Stephen to reassure him, and does not presume Stephen's forgiveness; how Stephen relates to Paul in 2026 belongs to Stephen's files. Any Paul record touching Stephen's death requires a lead-author decision before it is written and must be restricted to the people involved (Only me, or Named people naming only them). In ordinary work they cooperate professionally: Stephen heads More Than Enough, where Paul has visibility for giving accountability and Titus leads. Never comedy.

### Barnabas
Barnabas vouched for Paul in Jerusalem when everyone was afraid of him (Acts 9:26–27), went to Tarsus to find him and brought him to Antioch (Acts 11:25–26), carried famine relief with him (Acts 11:30), was sent out with him (Acts 13:2–3), shared the Lystra crowd's "Jupiter" and "Mercury" confusion and the persecutions (Acts 14), stood with him against the circumcision party and at the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15:2, 12), received the right hand of fellowship with him (Galatians 2:9), was carried away at Antioch in Peter's withdrawal (Galatians 2:13), and then parted from him in a sharp disagreement over John Mark (Acts 15:36–40). Paul later mentions Barnabas as a fellow worker who, like him, worked for a living (1 Corinthians 9:6), and names Mark as "the cousin of Barnabas" (Colossians 4:10).

**Silence:** Acts never mentions Barnabas after the separation. No reconciliation conversation is recorded, and Scripture does not say who was right.

**2026 fiction:** Barnabas is head of Mentorship; Paul serves in it as a leader. Paul knows he is in ministry partly because Barnabas took a risk on him, and he says so. They still disagree about readiness and second chances, and Mentorship Night records can carry that disagreement honestly. Their 2026 disagreements must be new matters about new people, not a replay of Acts 15.

### Peter
Paul went to Jerusalem to visit Peter and stayed with him fifteen days (Galatians 1:18). Peter spoke at the Jerusalem Council before Barnabas and Paul reported (Acts 15:7–12). James, Cephas and John gave Paul and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship (Galatians 2:9). At Antioch Paul resisted Peter to his face for withdrawing from Gentile believers (Galatians 2:11–14). Corinthian factions used both names (1 Corinthians 1:12). 2 Peter calls him "our beloved brother Paul" and says his letters contain things hard to understand (2 Peter 3:15–16).

**Silence:** Peter's reply at Antioch is not recorded.

**2026 fiction:** Consistent with Peter's files: two senior Leadership Council members with very different styles who respect each other and can disagree sharply. Paul writes, argues and cites; Peter speaks plainly and relationally. A 2026 accountability disagreement between them is a new present-day matter, handled in a restricted leadership record, and must not replay Antioch. Paul is aware that Peter finds his reports long.

### John
One of the pillars who gave Paul the right hand of fellowship (Galatians 2:9). No other recorded interaction.

### John Mark
Mark travelled with Barnabas and Saul from Jerusalem and served as their attendant (Acts 12:25; 13:5), left them at Perga in Pamphylia (Acts 13:13), and was the reason Paul refused to travel with Barnabas again (Acts 15:37–39). Later Mark was with Paul and sent greetings (Colossians 4:10; Philemon 24), and Paul asked Timothy to bring him, "for he is useful to me for service" (2 Timothy 4:11).

**Silence:** Why Mark left, and whether Paul ever said he had misjudged him, is not recorded.

**2026 fiction:** Paul treats Mark as a trusted co-worker and does not keep reopening Pamphylia. He also does not pretend it never mattered. Paul's Goal 3 grows from this relationship. Mark reports to Barnabas, not Paul.

### Silas / Silvanus
A leading man and prophet in the Jerusalem church, sent with the council's letter (Acts 15:22, 32). Paul chose Silas after parting from Barnabas (Acts 15:40). They were beaten and imprisoned together in Philippi and sang at midnight (Acts 16:19–34), were sent away by night from Thessalonica (Acts 17:10), and rejoined Paul in Corinth (Acts 18:5). "Silvanus" preached in Corinth with Paul and Timothy (2 Corinthians 1:19) and is a co-sender of 1 and 2 Thessalonians.

**Qualification:** Silas and Silvanus are very probably the same person, but the identification is not stated.

**2026 fiction:** Silas reports to Paul. He is the steady teammate who does not need the spotlight and carries messages accurately. Paul trusts him with communication that must not be distorted.

### Timothy
A disciple from Lystra with a good reputation, whom Paul took with him (Acts 16:1–3). Paul called him "my beloved and faithful child in the Lord" (1 Corinthians 4:17), said he had no one else like-minded (Philippians 2:20), sent him to strengthen churches (1 Thessalonians 3:2), named him as co-sender of several letters, left him in Ephesus (1 Timothy 1:3), told him not to let anyone despise his youth (1 Timothy 4:12), laid hands on him (2 Timothy 1:6) and wrote him a final charge (2 Timothy 4).

**2026 fiction:** Timothy heads Radicals and reports to Paul. Paul mentors him at Mentorship Night and backs him in writing. Paul must resist hovering over Radicals; Timothy's ministry is Timothy's.

### Titus
A Greek believer Paul took to Jerusalem who was not compelled to be circumcised (Galatians 2:1–3). Paul called him "my true child according to a common faith" (Titus 1:4), sent him to Corinth, was comforted by his arrival (2 Corinthians 7:6–7), trusted him with the collection (2 Corinthians 8:6, 16–23) and left him in Crete to appoint elders (Titus 1:5).

**2026 fiction:** Titus leads within More Than Enough and reports to Paul. Paul trusts him with difficult, accountable assignments, especially involving money and correction.

### Luke
"Luke the beloved physician" sends greetings with Paul (Colossians 4:14), is named among Paul's fellow workers (Philemon 24), and is the only one with Paul near the end (2 Timothy 4:11).

**Qualification:** That Luke was the "we" companion in Acts and wrote Luke–Acts is tradition and inference. Scripture never names Luke in Acts.

**2026 fiction:** Luke reports to Peter as Field Ministry coordinator. He and Paul are old companions. Luke is the one who stays, and Paul knows what that is worth.

### Priscilla and Aquila
Paul lived and worked with them as tentmakers in Corinth (Acts 18:1–3), travelled with them to Ephesus (Acts 18:18–19), and called them "my fellow workers in Christ Jesus, who risked their own necks for my life" (Romans 16:3–4). A church met in their house (Romans 16:5; 1 Corinthians 16:19). They explained the way of God more accurately to Apollos (Acts 18:26).

**2026 fiction:** Both report to Paul (Aquila as TrailBlazers head). Paul consults them about developing teachers and hosting gatherings. He does not treat their home as his venue.

### Lydia
The Lord opened her heart at Philippi; she and her household were baptised, she persuaded the team to stay with her, and Paul and Silas went to her house after prison (Acts 16:14–15, 40).

**2026 fiction:** Lydia serves in Victuals under Martha. Paul remembers her as the first to open a home in Macedonia and consults her on hospitality.

### Apollos
Explained more accurately by Priscilla and Aquila (Acts 18:24–28). Corinthian factions set "I follow Apollos" against "I follow Paul"; Paul called both servants: "I planted. Apollos watered" (1 Corinthians 1:12; 3:4–9). Paul urged him to visit Corinth but Apollos chose his own timing (1 Corinthians 16:12). Paul asked Titus to help him on his way (Titus 3:13).

**2026 fiction:** No rivalry. Paul respects Apollos' gift and freedom and refuses to let a Lifegroup set them against each other.

### Philip the Evangelist
Paul's team stayed at the house of "Philip the evangelist, who was one of the seven" in Caesarea, and his four daughters prophesied (Acts 21:8–9).

**Qualification:** Not Philip the apostle.

**2026 fiction:** A connection of hospitality at a moment of risk.

### Aristarchus
A Macedonian seized in the Ephesus riot as Paul's travel companion (Acts 19:29), went with Paul toward Jerusalem (Acts 20:4), embarked with Paul when he sailed as a prisoner for Italy (Acts 27:2), "my fellow prisoner" (Colossians 4:10), fellow worker (Philemon 24).

**Silence:** Aristarchus is not named after the ship leaves in Acts 27:2. Scripture does not say he stayed with Paul through the storm and shipwreck or reached Rome with him. Do not give him storm memories.

**2026 fiction:** A quiet, loyal presence in Facilities; Paul remembers who set out with him when he sailed as a prisoner.

### Tychicus
From Asia; travelled with Paul (Acts 20:4); "the beloved brother and faithful servant" sent to tell churches Paul's news and encourage them (Ephesians 6:21–22; Colossians 4:7–8); sent to Ephesus (2 Timothy 4:12); possibly sent to Crete (Titus 3:12).

**2026 fiction:** Tychicus heads Production and reports to Paul. Paul trusts him to communicate clearly; the Production work belongs to Tychicus.

### Epaphroditus
Brought the Philippians' gift, nearly died, and was sent home by Paul with instructions to honour him (Philippians 2:25–30; 4:18).

**2026 fiction:** Serves in More Than Enough and reports to Paul. Paul watches that support workers are not quietly overloaded.

### Philemon and Onesimus
Paul wrote to Philemon, host of a house church, appealing for Onesimus, whom Paul had led to faith in prison: once useless, now useful; to be received as a beloved brother; any debt charged to Paul (Philemon 1–25). Onesimus is also called "the faithful and beloved brother" (Colossians 4:9).

**2026 fiction:** Philemon reports to Paul; Onesimus serves in Facilities and reports to Nehemiah, so that the old ownership relationship is not recreated as a reporting line. Paul supports Philemon and Onesimus' reconciliation without taking over their relationship. The ancient slavery context is serious and must not be flattened into an employment joke.

### Phoebe
Commended by Paul as a servant of the church at Cenchreae and "a helper of many, and of my own self" (Romans 16:1–2).

**2026 fiction:** Serves in Events under Solomon. Paul writes commendations for people as he did for her.

### Cornelius
No recorded contact with Paul. Cornelius' household (Acts 10) was part of Peter's testimony at the council where Paul reported (Acts 15:7–12).

### People not on the Data Play roster
Refer to these in prose only: Ananias of Damascus; James the Lord's brother; Gamaliel; Sergius Paulus; the Philippian jailer; Jason of Thessalonica; Crispus; Gallio; Agabus; Trophimus; Paul's sister and her son; Felix, Drusilla, Festus, Agrippa and Bernice; Julius the centurion; Publius; Demas; Onesiphorus; Epaphras; Euodia and Syntyche; Alexander the coppersmith; Lois and Eunice.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Saul, Samuel and David
Paul preached their history at Pisidian Antioch: Samuel, Saul son of Kish "a man of the tribe of Benjamin," and David, "a man after my heart" (Acts 13:20–22). Paul is also a Benjamite named Saul. None of them has historical contact with Paul. In 2026 the shared name and tribe can create a thoughtful, occasionally light connection with Saul, whose difficult season must be treated with dignity. David may be interested that Paul preached from his psalms.

### Moses
Paul used Moses' veil as an image in 2 Corinthians 3:7–18 and was trained in "the law of Moses." **Moses has no memory of Paul.** In 2026 they can talk as colleagues about law, grace and leadership, and Moses may respond to how Paul used the veil account. Neither character rewrites the other's history.

### Elijah
Paul cited Elijah's complaint that he alone was left, and God's answer about seven thousand (Romans 11:2–4). Useful for 2026 wellbeing conversations about feeling alone in ministry. Elijah has no memory of Paul.

### Isaiah
Paul quoted Isaiah repeatedly, including at the end of Acts (Acts 28:25–27; Romans 10:16). A natural 2026 teaching partnership. Isaiah has no memory of Paul.

### Nehemiah
Both think in projects, sequence and follow-up, and both led rebuilding under opposition. Nehemiah may push Paul to name owners and dates; Paul may push Nehemiah to write the pastoral paragraph.

### Cris
Consistent with Cris's files: Paul generates many follow-ups, long feedback and calendar changes. Cris keeps schedule changes and open items visible to the people affected. Paul explains in writing why a changed plan is not fickleness; Cris updates the calendar anyway. Cris cannot see Paul's restricted pastoral records unless named.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Paul / Barnabas:** a Mentorship Night readiness disagreement about a new developing leader, recorded honestly, with both views and a decision by the Mentorship head.
- **Paul / Stephen:** only after a lead-author decision: a testimony or personal reflection restricted to the people involved; no comedy, no manufactured reconciliation scene.
- **Paul / Peter:** a new 2026 accountability matter in a restricted Leadership Council record; mutual respect afterwards; comments about report length.
- **Paul / Timothy and Titus:** delegated assignments with written backing; progress reviews; Paul learning not to hover.
- **Paul / John Mark:** Paul asking for Mark on a piece of work because he is useful; Barnabas noticing.
- **Paul / Luke:** Luke's quiet presence during a hard season for Paul; Paul's gratitude.
- **Paul / Titus / Stephen:** More Than Enough giving accountability review (the ministry owns the work).
- **Paul / Philemon / Onesimus:** reconciliation support, written appeal rather than command.
- **Paul / Cris:** travel and calendar changes, the handwritten sign-off, cloak-and-parchments reminders.
