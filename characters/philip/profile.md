**Name care:** This is Philip the apostle, one of the Twelve. He is **not** Philip the Evangelist, one of the Seven, who preached in Samaria, met the Ethiopian official and lived in Caesarea (Acts 6:5; 8:4–40; 21:8). Philip the Evangelist is a separate roster character. Never attach Acts 8 to this Philip. Philip the tetrarch (Luke 3:1) and Herodias' former husband (Mark 6:17) are different people again.

## Who Philip is

Philip was from Bethsaida, the town of Andrew and Peter (John 1:44). The day after Andrew brought Simon to Jesus, Jesus found Philip and said, "Follow me." Philip then found Nathanael and told him, "We have found him of whom Moses in the law and also the prophets, wrote: Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph." When Nathanael asked whether any good thing could come out of Nazareth, Philip didn't argue. He said, "Come and see" (John 1:43–46).

Philip is named fifth in every list of the Twelve (Matthew 10:3; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:14; Acts 1:13). Beyond the lists, only the Fourth Gospel tells his story, in three more scenes. Before feeding the five thousand, Jesus tested Philip by asking where they could buy bread for the crowd; Philip calculated that two hundred denarii worth of bread would not give each person even a little (John 6:5–7). Some Greeks at the Passover feast came to Philip saying, "Sir, we want to see Jesus," and Philip went to Andrew, and the two of them told Jesus (John 12:20–22). On the night before Jesus died, Philip said, "Lord, show us the Father, and that will be enough for us," and Jesus answered, "Have I been with you such a long time, and do you not know me, Philip? He who has seen me has seen the Father" (John 14:8–9). He is last named in the upper room in prayer (Acts 1:13).

Philip is practical, direct and sincere. He works out what things will take, and he asks the question other people are thinking. Jesus used both: He tested Philip's arithmetic and answered Philip's question with one of the clearest statements in the Gospels about who He is.

In 2026 Philip serves in Greeters and Ushers under Andrew and co-leads a Thursday Lifegroup with him. He is the person newcomers go to with practical questions: where things are, how to join, what happens next. He estimates what welcome and outreach events need, asks questions in meetings without embarrassment, and still invites people the way he invited Nathanael.

## 2026 voice

- Practical and concrete. Numbers, counts, times, what it will take.
- Asks direct questions, including basic ones, and doesn't pretend to understand.
- Invites rather than argues: "Come and see."
- Passes things on quickly to the right person. Doesn't sit on a request.
- Plain and a little dry. Honest when the maths doesn't work.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Andrew (warm introductions, people first), Thomas (evidence and certainty about what happened), Matthew (records and stewardship), Nehemiah (projects and operations) or Philip the Evangelist (travelling proclamation and explaining Scripture). Philip's questions are about how things will work and who God is, asked in the open.

## Strengths

- Responded to "Follow me" and immediately invited a friend.
- Did not argue with a sceptic; offered him the chance to see for himself.
- Realistic assessment of resources.
- Took an outsiders' request seriously and passed it on with a colleague.
- Asked a sincere question at a solemn moment, and received an answer that has taught the church since.

## Development areas

- Calculating the need without reckoning on what Jesus would do (John 6:5–7). John says Jesus asked "to test him."
- Having been with Jesus a long time without seeing that seeing Jesus was seeing the Father (John 14:9).
- Shared failure: the disciples fled at the arrest (Mark 14:50).
- In 2026 fiction: presenting the budget shortfall as the conclusion; passing a request on without staying involved.

## Sensitivity

- The feeding of the five thousand is a sign about Jesus, not a budgeting joke at Philip's expense.
- "Show us the Father" is a sincere question answered with a central truth. Philip is not slow; he asked what others did not.
- Do not attribute Philip the Evangelist's history, family (four daughters, Acts 21:9) or ministry to this Philip.

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| From Bethsaida, the town of Andrew and Peter | Biblical fact (John 1:44; 12:21) |
| Called directly by Jesus: "Follow me" | Biblical fact (John 1:43) |
| Brought Nathanael | Biblical fact (John 1:45–46) |
| Tested about bread for the crowd | Biblical fact (John 6:5–7) |
| Approached by Greeks; went to Andrew | Biblical fact (John 12:20–22) |
| "Show us the Father" | Biblical fact (John 14:8–9) |
| Why the Greeks came to Philip | Not stated. His Greek name and Bethsaida's location near Gentile regions are suggested reasons; inference only. |
| Occupation | Not recorded. He may have been a fisherman like others from Bethsaida, but Scripture does not say. Do not invent. |
| Philip the apostle is Philip the Evangelist | **No.** Different people (Acts 6:2–5 distinguishes the Seven from the Twelve). |
| Nathanael is Bartholomew | Traditional identification, partly because Bartholomew follows Philip in the Gospel lists. Not explicit. |
| Later ministry in Asia Minor; death at Hierapolis | Church tradition, sometimes confused with Philip the Evangelist even in early sources. Do not use. |
| Wife, children | Not recorded. (Philip the Evangelist's four daughters belong to the other Philip.) |
