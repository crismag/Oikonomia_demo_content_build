# Philip — Biblical Context Library

This file records the lived biblical history of Philip the apostle that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments.

**This is not Philip the Evangelist.** Acts 6:5, Acts 8 and Acts 21:8–9 belong to that Philip, one of the Seven. Philip the apostle's story is told only in the Fourth Gospel beyond the lists of the Twelve.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB).

---

## "Follow me" and "Come and see" — John 1:43–51

The day after Andrew brought Simon to Jesus, Jesus decided to go to Galilee. "He found Philip. Jesus said to him, 'Follow me.'" Philip was from Bethsaida, the town of Andrew and Peter. Philip found Nathanael and said, "We have found him of whom Moses in the law and also the prophets, wrote: Jesus of Nazareth, the son of Joseph." Nathanael said, "Can any good thing come out of Nazareth?" Philip said to him, "Come and see." When Nathanael came, Jesus called him an Israelite without deceit, and told him He had seen him under the fig tree before Philip called him. Nathanael confessed Jesus as the Son of God and King of Israel.

**Fact:** Jesus sought Philip out directly. Philip connected Jesus to what "Moses in the law and also the prophets" wrote.

**Truths:** Philip's first act as a follower was to invite someone. He met scepticism with an invitation, not an argument. Jesus already knew the person Philip was inviting.

**2026 use:** Reach-Out invitations, newcomer welcome, testimony, Nathanael relationship, Goal 2.

**Cross-era note:** Philip speaks of what Moses wrote. Moses is a Data Play character. Moses has no memory of Philip or of Jesus' ministry; Philip may reflect in 2026 on having recognised Jesus from Moses' writings.

## Among the Twelve — Matthew 10:2–4; Mark 3:13–19; Luke 6:12–16

Philip is named fifth in each list of the Twelve, and in each Gospel list Bartholomew is named immediately after him.

**2026 use:** Background only.

## "Where are we to buy bread?" — John 6:1–14

Near Passover, a great crowd came toward Jesus on a mountainside. Jesus said to Philip, "Where are we to buy bread, that these may eat?" John adds, "He said this to test him, for he himself knew what he would do." Philip answered, "Two hundred denarii worth of bread is not sufficient for them, that every one of them may receive a little." Andrew pointed out a boy with five barley loaves and two fish, "but what are these among so many?" Jesus had the people sit down, about five thousand men, gave thanks, and distributed the food through the disciples until everyone had as much as they wanted. Twelve baskets of pieces were gathered so that nothing would be lost.

**Background:** A denarius was roughly a day's wage for a labourer (see Matthew 20:2), so two hundred denarii was a large sum.

**Truths:** Philip's arithmetic was accurate. The test was not whether he could count, but whether he would reckon with Jesus. Jesus used both Philip's honest assessment and Andrew's small find.

**2026 use:** Planning estimates, resource shortfalls, Lifegroup, Goal 1. Food and distribution belong to Victuals and More Than Enough. Do not reenact the miracle.

## "Sir, we want to see Jesus" — John 12:20–33

Some Greeks who had come up to worship at the Passover feast came to Philip, "who was from Bethsaida of Galilee," and asked, "Sir, we want to see Jesus." "Philip came and told Andrew, and in turn, Andrew came with Philip, and they told Jesus." Jesus answered that the hour had come for the Son of Man to be glorified: unless a grain of wheat falls into the earth and dies, it remains alone, but if it dies it bears much fruit. He said that when He was lifted up from the earth He would draw all people to Himself.

**Silence:** Scripture does not say why the Greeks approached Philip, why Philip went to Andrew first, or whether the Greeks met Jesus. Suggestions (his Greek name; Bethsaida near Gentile territory; checking with Andrew because of Matthew 10:5) are inference.

**Truths:** An outsider's request was carried to Jesus, not filtered out. Jesus' answer pointed to the cross as the way all people would be drawn to Him.

**2026 use:** Cross-cultural newcomer requests, escalation and handoff, Andrew relationship, Goal 2.

## "Show us the Father" — John 14:1–14

On the night before His death, Jesus told the disciples He was going to prepare a place for them and that they knew the way. Thomas said they didn't know where He was going. Jesus said, "I am the way, the truth, and the life. No one comes to the Father, except through me. If you had known me, you would have known my Father also." Philip said, "Lord, show us the Father, and that will be enough for us." Jesus said, "Have I been with you such a long time, and do you not know me, Philip? He who has seen me has seen the Father. How do you say, 'Show us the Father'?" He told them to believe that He is in the Father and the Father in Him, or else to believe because of the works themselves, and promised that whoever believes in Him will do the works He does, and greater, because He was going to the Father.

**Truths:** Philip asked for what he thought would settle everything. Jesus' answer was that Philip had already been looking at it for years. A sincere question can open a central truth for everyone listening.

**2026 use:** Lifegroup questions, teaching, personal development, questions from newcomers treated with respect.

## The arrest and the upper room — Mark 14:50; Acts 1:12–14

All the disciples fled at Jesus' arrest. After the ascension Philip is named among the apostles praying in the upper room with the women, Mary the mother of Jesus and His brothers. This is the last time Scripture names Philip the apostle.

**Caution:** The Philip of Acts 6 onward is Philip the Evangelist.

**2026 use:** Shared failure and restoration of the group; prayer.
