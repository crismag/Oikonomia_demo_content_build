## Who James son of Alphaeus is

James son of Alphaeus was one of the Twelve. He appears in all four lists of the apostles, always in the ninth position (Matthew 10:3; Mark 3:18; Luke 6:15; Acts 1:13). The name of his father, Alphaeus, is the only personal detail given, and it exists mainly to distinguish him from James son of Zebedee.

Everything else Scripture says about him is shared with the Twelve:

- **Chosen.** Jesus prayed all night and then chose twelve from His disciples, appointing them "that they might be with him, and that he might send them out to preach" (Luke 6:12–13; Mark 3:14).
- **Sent.** The Twelve went out in pairs, preached repentance, anointed the sick with oil and healed them, then reported back to Jesus (Mark 6:7–13, 30).
- **Failed together.** At Jesus' arrest "all the disciples left him and fled" (Matthew 26:56).
- **Commissioned together.** The eleven met the risen Jesus on the mountain in Galilee and were sent to make disciples of all nations (Matthew 28:16–20).
- **Prayed together.** After the ascension he was in the upper room with the others who "with one accord continued steadfastly in prayer" (Acts 1:13–14).
- **Very probably in Acts 6.** "The twelve" asked the church to choose seven men to oversee the daily distribution (Acts 6:2–6). He is not named there, but no record says he had left.

**Truths:** Being chosen by Jesus did not depend on being remembered by name for great deeds. A person can be fully part of the mission without a single recorded line. Faithfulness is often invisible in the record and still real.

**2026 use:** Attendance, Sunday welcome, shared meetings, team records and quiet faithfulness. His biblical memory is the shared life of the Twelve, described honestly as shared.

In 2026 he serves on Andrew's Greeters and Ushers team. He is there early, knows where the empty seats are, and rarely writes anything longer than a paragraph.

## Sensitivity

- His biblical silence is not a joke and not a gap to fill.
- Do not import the stories of other Jameses: James son of Zebedee (killed by Herod, Acts 12:2), James the Lord's brother (Jerusalem leader, Acts 15:13; Galatians 1:19; traditionally the James named in James 1:1), or James the father of Judas (Luke 6:16).
