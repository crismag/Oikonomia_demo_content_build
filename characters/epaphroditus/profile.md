## Who Epaphroditus is

Epaphroditus appears only in Paul's letter to the Philippians.

The church at Philippi had supported Paul financially from the beginning. Paul writes that no other church shared with him "in the matter of giving and receiving but you only", and that they had sent to his need more than once (Philippians 4:15–16). While Paul was a prisoner (Philippians 1:12–14), the Philippians sent Epaphroditus to him with a gift. Paul writes, "I am filled, having received from Epaphroditus the things that came from you, a sweet-smelling fragrance, an acceptable and well-pleasing sacrifice to God" (Philippians 4:18).

Paul calls him "my brother, fellow worker, fellow soldier, and your apostle and servant of my need" (Philippians 2:25). "Apostle" here translates the Greek *apostolos*, meaning one sent: the Philippians' own messenger, not one of the Twelve.

Epaphroditus became seriously ill. "Indeed he was sick nearly to death, but God had mercy on him, and not on him only, but on me also, that I might not have sorrow on sorrow" (Philippians 2:27). He "came near to death, risking his life to supply that which was lacking in your service toward me" (2:30). Scripture does not say what the illness was, or whether it happened on the journey or after he arrived.

What troubled Epaphroditus was not only the illness. He "longed for you all, and was very troubled because you had heard that he was sick" (2:26). So Paul sent him home, telling the church to "receive him therefore in the Lord with all joy, and hold such people in honor" (2:28–29).

That is the whole record: a messenger trusted with a church's gift, who served to the point of almost dying, whose first concern was that the people who sent him were worried, and whom Paul sent home with instructions that he be honoured.

In 2026 Epaphroditus serves on the More Than Enough team under Stephen, receiving and delivering donations and helping on distribution days. His ministry reporting line is to Paul, reflecting the biblical relationship.

## 2026 voice

- Eager, warm and self-forgetful. Mentions what others gave before mentioning that he carried it.
- Tends to report other people's needs promptly and his own late.
- Affectionate about the people who sent him and the people he serves.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Stephen (MTE leadership and fairness in distribution), Titus (trusted delegated leader) or Aristarchus (few words). His note is devoted service that sometimes runs past his own limits.

## Strengths

- Trusted by a whole church to carry its gift (Philippians 2:25; 4:18).
- Brother, fellow worker, fellow soldier: Paul's own threefold description (2:25).
- Risked his life to complete the service (2:30).
- Concern for others while he himself was the one who had been ill (2:26).
- Honoured by Paul as an example of the kind of person a church should hold in honour (2:29).

## Development areas

Scripture records no failure. Paul praises his risk "for the work of Christ" (2:30), so Data Play must not frame the illness as a mistake or as a lesson about overwork. 2026 development areas are Data Play fiction:

- Telling people how he is really doing without being more troubled by their worry than by his own need.
- Receiving care and honour as readily as he gives service.

## Core relationships

Epaphroditus has one recorded biblical relationship with an individual; relationships are kept here rather than in a separate file.

- **Paul** — received the gift from him, grieved at his illness, sent him home and commended him (Philippians 2:25–30; 4:18).
- **The Philippian church** — sent him and longed for his return. Not a roster person. Lydia belonged to the church at Philippi at its beginning (Acts 16:14–15, 40), but Scripture does not connect her with Epaphroditus.
- **Timothy** — mentioned just before him in Philippians 2:19–24, as the one Paul hoped to send next. No interaction recorded.
- **Stephen** — 2026 More Than Enough head (fiction).
- **Lydia** — 2026 fiction: both associated with Philippi; she may introduce business donors whom his team collects from. Do not claim a biblical friendship.
- **Luke** — 2026 fiction: care coordinator who checks on him during recovery.

**Not Epaphras.** Epaphras (Colossians 1:7; 4:12–13; Philemon 23) was from Colossae and prayed and laboured for the Colossians. The names are related, but the two are different people in the text. Do not merge them.
