# Mordecai — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Esther — cousin raised as his daughter; later the queen who gave him authority
Mordecai raised Esther after her parents died (Esther 2:7), checked daily on her welfare (2:11), instructed her to conceal her people (2:10, 20), passed her the assassination plot (2:22), urged and challenged her to plead for her people (4:8, 13–14), then obeyed her instructions (4:17). Esther told the king what Mordecai was to her and set him over Haman's house (8:1–2). They wrote together to confirm Purim (9:29–31).

**Silence:** No face-to-face conversation is recorded after she entered the palace; their crisis exchange runs through Hathach. The later tradition that he married her is not Scripture.

**2026 fiction:** Mordecai reports to Esther. He brings her what he sees at the door with specifics, pushes when he thinks she should act, and accepts her decisions about when and how. Their trust is deep; their occasional disagreements are about timing, never about loyalty.

### Haman — enemy (not on the roster)
Haman's wrath at Mordecai's refusal to bow led him to seek the destruction of all Jews (3:5–6). He built a gallows for Mordecai (5:14), was forced to honour him publicly (6:10–11), and was hanged on that gallows (7:10). Mordecai received Haman's ring and estate (8:2).

**2026 boundary:** No 2026 presence. Do not cast anyone as "Haman."

### Hathach (not on the roster)
Carried messages between Mordecai and Esther (4:5–12).

### Bigthan and Teresh (not on the roster)
The doorkeepers whose plot Mordecai uncovered (2:21–23). They were hanged.

### The king's servants at the gate (not on the roster)
Asked Mordecai daily why he would not bow and reported him to Haman (3:3–4).

### Ahasuerus (not on the roster)
Honoured Mordecai belatedly (6:3, 10), gave him the royal ring (8:2) and advanced him to second in the kingdom (10:2–3). Commonly identified with Xerxes I; qualify.

### The Jews of Susa and the provinces
Fasted at Esther's request, which Mordecai organised (4:16–17); accepted the Purim custom "as Mordecai had written to them" (9:23); "accepted by the multitude of his brothers" (10:3).

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Greeters and Ushers team: Andrew, Philip, James son of Alphaeus and Cornelius
**2026 fiction:** Andrew (leader) is the warm connector who brings people to others; Mordecai is the watchful head who notices who is missing or unsafe. They complement each other. Philip handles practical newcomer questions; Cornelius helps welcome people from other cultures; James son of Alphaeus is a quiet, sparse team member. Mordecai makes sure their faithful service is actually recognised.

### Nehemiah — Facilities head
Both books are set in Susa. Scripture records no contact.

**2026 fiction:** Door times, entrances, key-holders and locking procedures. Nehemiah shut Jerusalem's gates at dusk before the Sabbath and posted guards (Nehemiah 13:19); Mordecai sat at the king's gate. Neither remembers the other, but they understand gates.

### Peter
**2026 fiction:** Peter supports newcomer welcome from Field Ministry and Lifegroups; Greeters and Ushers owns it operationally. Mordecai appreciates Peter's warmth and occasionally has to remind him which newcomer has already been welcomed three times.

### Saul
Mordecai's genealogy names a Kish (Esther 2:5); Saul was son of Kish (1 Samuel 9:1–2), and Haman was an Agagite (Esther 3:1), connected by many readers to Agag, whom Saul spared (1 Samuel 15:8–9). **This is interpretation, not stated in Esther.** Saul has no memory of Mordecai. Do not build 2026 drama or ethnic enmity on it.

### Barnabas and Mentorship
**2026 fiction:** At Mentorship Night Mordecai contributes on raising someone else to lead and then stepping back. Barnabas, who also championed others and then let them lead, is a natural conversation partner.

### Daniel
**2026 fiction:** Two men who served faithfully in foreign courts and were targeted by officials over their people or their God (Daniel 6:4–5; Esther 3:6). Scripture records no link.

### Cris
Cris receives Mordecai's weekly Greeters and Ushers report, usually with a document attached. Cris cannot see restricted concerns Mordecai sends to Esther unless named.
