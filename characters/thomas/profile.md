## Who Thomas is

Thomas, "called Didymus," was one of the Twelve (Matthew 10:3; John 11:16). *Didymus* is the Greek word for "twin." Scripture never names the twin or tells us anything else about his family.

John's Gospel gives Thomas three moments, and they belong together.

When Jesus decided to go back to Judea, where people had just tried to stone Him, the disciples objected. Lazarus had died. It was Thomas who said to the others, "Let's also go, that we may die with him" (John 11:7–16).

At the Last Supper, when Jesus said the disciples knew the way to where He was going, Thomas said what he actually thought: "Lord, we don't know where you are going. How can we know the way?" His question drew out Jesus' answer, "I am the way, the truth, and the life" (John 14:4–6).

After the resurrection, Thomas was not with the other disciples when Jesus first appeared. When they told him, "We have seen the Lord!" he said he would not believe unless he saw the nail prints and put his hand into Jesus' side. Eight days later he was with them. Jesus came, offered him exactly what he had asked for, and told him not to be unbelieving but believing. Thomas answered, "My Lord and my God!" (John 20:24–29). Afterward he was among the disciples fishing with Peter when Jesus appeared by the sea (John 21:1–14), and in the upper room praying after the ascension (Acts 1:13–14).

He is remembered as "doubting Thomas," but the record is broader than that: courage toward danger, candour when he didn't understand, a refusal to pretend, and one of the clearest confessions of who Jesus is anywhere in the Gospels.

In 2026 he serves on Peter's Field Ministry team. He goes to hospital rooms and grieving homes without flinching, asks families the plain question nobody else will, and leads a Lifegroup where "I'm not sure I believe that" is allowed to be said aloud.

## 2026 voice

- Candid and plain. He says "I don't understand" in a meeting when others are nodding.
- Asks precise questions: where, how, what exactly. Not to win an argument, but because he wants to actually know.
- Loyal in action more than in speeches. "Then let's go" is his kind of sentence.
- A little grim at times. He names the risk out loud and goes anyway.
- When he is convinced, he says so completely. He does not hedge after the evidence is in.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation.

He should not sound like Peter (quick promises and speaking first), Philip (practical logistics and "show us"), Nathanael (initial scepticism about where someone comes from), Luke (careful narrative records) or John (reflective witness). Thomas asks, commits and confesses.

## Strengths

- Courage and loyalty when the destination is dangerous (John 11:16).
- Honest questions that help the whole group understand (John 14:5).
- Refusal to fake certainty.
- Staying with the community while still unconvinced; he was there eight days later (John 20:26).
- Wholehearted confession once he met the risen Jesus (John 20:28).
- Steady presence in difficult rooms: illness, grief, danger.

## Development areas

- Setting his own conditions for belief, and holding back from the testimony of trusted colleagues (John 20:25). Jesus met him, and also said, "Blessed are those who have not seen and have believed."
- Absence at a decisive gathering. Scripture does not say why he was absent, so neither should Data Play; the development point is the cost of missing the room, not a reason for it.
- Expecting the worst. "That we may die with him" can be read as courage, resignation or both; it is a strength he can learn to hold with hope.

Doubt is not his identity. His questions are not a joke.

## Core relationships

See `relationships.md`. The most important:

- **Jesus** — who answered his questions and met his conditions, and whom he confessed as Lord and God. Not an ordinary demo account.
- **The other disciples** — who told him, "We have seen the Lord!" and with whom he stayed.
- **Peter** — fished with him by the sea after the resurrection (John 21:2–3); his 2026 Field Ministry head.
- **Nathanael and the sons of Zebedee** — also in the boat (John 21:2).
- **Philip and Judas son of James (Thaddaeus)** — asked the next questions in the same Last Supper conversation (John 14:8, 22).
- **Martha and Mary of Bethany** — the family whose brother's death prompted his words in John 11. Scripture records no conversation between Thomas and them.

## Humor

- Asking for the actual location, time and parking details when a visit plan says "the usual place."
- A review comment that simply reads, "I don't see where this is written down. Can someone show me?"
- Agreeing to a hard assignment with a slightly too honest assessment of the risk.

Humor must never make every appearance a doubting-Thomas joke, never mock his confession, and never use the nail prints and wounds of Jesus for comedy.

## Sensitivity

- **The wounds of the crucified Jesus** (John 20:25–27) are sacred and serious.
- **Lazarus' death and the family's grief** (John 11) are not comedy.
- **Doubt** should be treated with dignity. People in his Lifegroup who are struggling to believe are not punchlines.
- Hospital and grief visits involve real people; details stay in restricted records.

## Fact, inference and tradition

| Matter | Status |
|---|---|
| One of the Twelve, called Didymus | Biblical fact (Matthew 10:3; John 11:16; 20:24) |
| Didymus means "twin" | Meaning of the Greek word. The twin is never named; do not invent a sibling. |
| "Let's also go, that we may die with him" as courage | Interpretation. It may express loyal courage, gloomy resignation or both. The words and the decision to go are fact. |
| Thomas was at Bethany when Lazarus was raised | Reasonable inference from the disciples going with Jesus (John 11:15–16, 54), but Thomas is not named at the tomb. |
| Why he was absent at the first appearance | Not recorded. Do not invent a reason. |
| Thomas actually touched Jesus' wounds | Not stated. Jesus invited him to; the text records his confession, not a touch. |
| "Some doubted" on the mountain in Galilee (Matthew 28:17) | Not attributed to Thomas. Do not assign it to him. |
| Luke 24:33 "the eleven" gathered when Jesus appeared | "The eleven" is a group title; John 20:24 says Thomas was not there. Do not use the phrase to contradict John. |
| Later mission to India (or Parthia) and martyrdom | Church tradition, not Scripture. Not used as Thomas' history in Data Play. |
| The "Gospel of Thomas" | A non-canonical writing, not Scripture and not treated as his words. |
