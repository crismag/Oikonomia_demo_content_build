# Cornelius — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Jesus
Peter proclaimed Jesus to Cornelius' household as "Lord of all," through whose name everyone who believes receives forgiveness of sins (Acts 10:36, 43). Jesus is not an ordinary demo account.

### Peter
God directed Cornelius to send for Peter and directed Peter to go (Acts 10:5, 19–20). Cornelius fell at Peter's feet and Peter raised him up, saying he was only a man (10:25–26). Peter preached, the Holy Spirit fell on the household, and Peter commanded their baptism; they asked him to stay (10:34–48). Peter later defended going into Cornelius' house and eating there (11:1–18) and cited the event at the Jerusalem Council (15:7–11).

**Consistency with Peter's files:** Peter's relationship file describes a mutual friendship across cultural lines in which "Neither treats the other as a project," and Peter's Goal 3 includes working "with Greeters and Ushers and with Cornelius on how newcomers from other cultures are received." Cornelius' files follow that.

**Qualification:** Peter's relationship file says Peter "stayed with his household." Acts states that they asked him to stay (10:48) and that he was criticised for eating with them (11:3); a stay is a reasonable inference, but its length is not recorded.

**2026 fiction:** Cornelius reports to Peter. Their friendship is warm and a little formal on Cornelius' side. Cornelius still sometimes honours Peter more than Peter wants, and Peter still tells him to stand up. Cornelius gives Peter an outsider's honest view of how the church feels to newcomers. Peter's Antioch failure (Galatians 2:11–14) is Peter's history; Cornelius was not there and does not use it against him.

### His household, relatives and close friends (not on the roster)
"All his house" feared God (10:2). He sent two household servants and "a devout soldier" (10:7). He gathered "his relatives and his near friends" (10:24). None is named. Do not invent names, a wife or children, or give them Data Play accounts.

### The six brothers from Joppa (not on the roster)
Jewish believers who went with Peter and witnessed the Spirit fall (10:23, 45; 11:12). Unnamed; prose only.

### Simon the tanner (not on the roster)
Peter's host in Joppa, to whose house Cornelius' messengers were sent (10:6, 17–18). Prose only.

## Cross-era and 2026 Data Play relationships (fiction)

### Mordecai (Greeters and Ushers head)
Mordecai owns welcome operations. Cornelius brings cross-cultural insight and the experience of having been the outsider. Both value order; Mordecai thinks about community strategy, Cornelius about the single guest at the door.

### Andrew and Philip the apostle (Greeters and Ushers)
Andrew is a natural connector who brings people to others; Cornelius gathers people before the guest arrives. Philip the apostle serves in the same ministry; do not confuse him with Philip the Evangelist.

### Stephen (More Than Enough head)
Cornelius serves Wednesday distributions. Stephen's concern that nobody is overlooked matches Cornelius' own generosity to "the people" (10:2). Cornelius does not treat distribution as charity from above; he remembers being the one who needed someone to come.

### Philip the Evangelist
Both lived in Caesarea in Acts, but **Scripture never connects them.** Do not imply they met historically. In 2026 they serve together in More Than Enough, and Cornelius refers newcomers to Philip for Reach-Out follow-up.

### Paul
Paul's mission to Gentiles and his defence of Gentile believers at the Jerusalem Council (Acts 15:12) and in Galatians relate to what happened in Cornelius' house. Acts records no meeting between Paul and Cornelius. In 2026, Paul may value Cornelius' perspective on Gentile inclusion.

### Titus
Paul writes that "not even Titus, who was with me, being a Greek, was compelled to be circumcised" (Galatians 2:3). In 2026 fiction Cornelius and Titus share the experience of being Gentile believers in a church with deep Jewish roots, and serve together in More Than Enough. No historical contact is implied.

### Jonah
Jonah fled God's call to Gentile Nineveh by going down to Joppa (Jonah 1:3). Peter was called from Joppa to Cornelius. Jonah has no memory of either. In 2026 Jonah, a reluctant outreach leader, and Cornelius, the Gentile whom God pursued, may have a thoughtful conversation. Never mockery of Jonah.

### Cris
Cris coordinates newcomer follow-up between Greeters and Ushers, Lifegroups and Reach-Out. Cris does not see Cornelius' restricted Lifegroup entries unless named.
