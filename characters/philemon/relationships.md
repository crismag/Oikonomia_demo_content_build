# Philemon — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Paul
Paul calls Philemon "our beloved fellow worker" and "brother", counts him a partner, says his love has given Paul "much joy and comfort", and tells him "you owe to me even your own self" (Philemon 1, 7, 17, 19). Paul appeals rather than commands and asks for a guest room (verses 8–9, 22).

**Inference:** Philemon probably came to faith through Paul. **Silence:** How and where they met.

**2026 fiction:** Philemon reports to Paul. Paul still tends to write long, careful appeals; Philemon still answers with hospitality. Their relationship holds real affection and a shared memory of a request that cost Philemon something.

### Onesimus
Onesimus had been enslaved in Philemon's household. He became a believer through Paul in prison, and Paul sent him back asking Philemon to receive him "no longer as a slave, but more than a slave, a beloved brother" (Philemon 10–17). Colossians calls Onesimus "the faithful and beloved brother, who is one of you" (Colossians 4:9).

**Silence:** Why Onesimus left, whether he had wronged Philemon, and how Philemon responded. Do not fill these gaps as if Scripture settled them.

**2026 fiction:** They serve in the same church as brothers. Onesimus does not report to Philemon (he reports to Nehemiah in Facilities), and nothing in 2026 should read as ownership, obedience or debt. Philemon does not speak for Onesimus, narrate Onesimus' feelings, or present him as a success story of Philemon's generosity. Records touching their history are restricted to the people involved and must be written with Onesimus' dignity in view.

### Timothy
Named with Paul as co-sender of the letter (Philemon 1). No personal interaction is recorded.

**2026 fiction:** A friendly colleague; nothing more is needed.

### Apphia and Archippus
Addressed with Philemon (Philemon 2). Archippus is Paul's "fellow soldier" and is charged in Colossians 4:17 to fulfil his ministry.

**Qualification:** Often assumed to be Philemon's wife and son. The text does not say so. They are not on the Data Play roster; mention them in prose only, without assigning family roles.

### Epaphras, Mark, Aristarchus, Demas and Luke
They send greetings at the end of the letter (Philemon 23–24).

**Qualification:** "Mark" is Mark the cousin of Barnabas (Colossians 4:10), usually identified with John Mark. That identification is probable, not explicit. Epaphras is not Epaphroditus.

## Cross-era and 2026 Data Play relationships (fiction)

### Lydia
Two hosts whose homes became gathering places (Acts 16:15, 40; Philemon 2). In 2026 they compare notes on hosting home Lifegroups. Lydia is quicker and more decisive; Philemon is gentler and slower to push.

### Solomon
Philemon serves on Events Ministry under Solomon. Receptions, guest arrangements and welcome occasions.

### Martha
Victuals owns food preparation. Philemon hosts; Martha's team supports catered occasions. A light friction thread about Philemon feeding people outside any Victuals plan is acceptable.

### Tychicus
Tychicus travelled with Onesimus to Colossae (Colossians 4:7–9). Tychicus is not recorded meeting Philemon. In 2026 they are colleagues; Tychicus is not a go-between in Philemon and Onesimus' history.

### Cris
Cris coordinates Lifegroup follow-ups. Cris has no access to restricted records about Philemon and Onesimus unless one of them names Cris as a reader.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Philemon / Onesimus:** a restricted reflection by Philemon on what he was asked to give up, written without narrating Onesimus' inner life. Onesimus' own perspective belongs in his own records.
- **Philemon / Paul:** a guest-room and hosting thread for a visiting-leaders weekend; a leadership reflection on being appealed to rather than commanded.
- **Philemon / Lydia:** home-Lifegroup hosting practice.
- **Philemon / Solomon / Martha:** Events reception planning with Victuals.
