## Who Simon the Zealot is

Simon was one of the Twelve. Luke calls him "Simon who was called the Zealot" (Luke 6:15) and Acts "Simon the Zealot" (Acts 1:13). In Matthew and Mark the WEB also reads "Simon the Zealot" (Matthew 10:4; Mark 3:18); the Greek word there is *Kananaios*, generally understood as coming from an Aramaic word for "zealous." Older translations rendered it "Canaanite," which is not the meaning.

### What "Zealot" might mean

Scripture does not explain the title. There are two main readings:

- **A political or nationalist movement.** Some Jews resisted Roman rule, sometimes violently, and later the name "Zealots" belonged to a party in the war against Rome. Simon may have belonged to, or sympathised with, an earlier form of that resistance. Historians debate whether an organised Zealot party existed as early as Jesus' ministry.
- **Personal zeal.** The word may describe Simon's intense devotion to God and the Law, without any political membership.

Data Play keeps both open. It never gives Simon violent acts, revolutionary plots or a political biography.

### Shared life with the Twelve

He shared what Scripture records of the Twelve together: chosen after Jesus' night of prayer (Luke 6:12–16), sent out to preach and heal (Mark 6:7–13), scattered at the arrest (Matthew 26:56), commissioned on the mountain in Galilee (Matthew 28:16–20), praying in the upper room after the ascension (Acts 1:13–14), and very probably among "the twelve" who asked the church to appoint seven men over the daily distribution (Acts 6:2–6).

**Truths:** Jesus chose a man known for zeal and placed him in the same small group as a tax collector, without Scripture recording any explanation or conflict. Whatever Simon's zeal had been directed toward, he was with the others, in one accord, praying.

**2026 use:** Fairness and justice in More Than Enough distribution, team unity across backgrounds, conviction channelled into service. Always as 2026 fiction grounded in the title, not as recorded biography.

In 2026 he serves on Stephen's More Than Enough team. He cares intensely about who is being overlooked in the distribution line and about whether a donation comes with strings attached.

## Sensitivity

- Do not fabricate extremist conduct, violence or political plots.
- Do not use "Zealot" as a label for a hothead or troublemaker.
- Do not attach "Zeal for your house will eat me up" (John 2:17) to Simon; the disciples applied it to Jesus.
- Do not import other Simons: Simon Peter; Simon the brother of Jesus (Mark 6:3); Simon the leper (Mark 14:3); Simon of Cyrene (Mark 15:21); Simon the Pharisee (Luke 7:40); Simon the sorcerer (Acts 8:9); Simon the tanner (Acts 9:43); Simon, father of Judas Iscariot (John 6:71).
- People served by More Than Enough are never comedy material.
