# Mary Magdalene — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Jesus
Jesus set her free from seven demons (Luke 8:2). She travelled with Him and served (Luke 8:1–3; Mark 15:41), watched Him die (John 19:25), saw where He was buried (Mark 15:47), came to anoint His body (Mark 16:1), and was called by name by the risen Jesus and sent to tell His brothers (John 20:16–17). Jesus is the centre of her story and not an ordinary demo account.

### Peter
She ran to Simon Peter with the news of the empty tomb (John 20:2). The message she and the other women received named him: "go, tell his disciples and Peter" (Mark 16:7). Luke records that after the women's report, which the apostles did not believe, Peter ran to the tomb (Luke 24:10–12).

**Canonical note:** Peter's files already record that Mary Magdalene ran to tell him (`characters/peter/biblical-context.md`, `characters/peter/relationships.md`). This file agrees.

**2026 fiction:** Peter heads Field Ministry and is her reporting leader. She was the one who brought him the news that morning, after the denial; in 2026 she brings him the visit reports that need a pastor's attention. Mutual respect: she does not remind him of his failure, and he does not doubt her reports. Her plainness and his speed make good visit partnerships.

### The disciple whom Jesus loved; John
She ran to Peter "and to the other disciple whom Jesus loved" (John 20:2); that disciple outran Peter to the tomb (John 20:4). The beloved disciple also stood near the cross with Jesus' mother while Mary Magdalene was there (John 19:25–26).

**Qualification:** The beloved disciple is traditionally identified as John son of Zebedee, but the text does not name him. Records should say "the disciple whom Jesus loved" or state that the identification is traditional.

**2026 fiction:** John and Mary Magdalene share a memory of standing near the cross (with the qualification above). They speak of it rarely and only seriously.

### James son of Zebedee and John — through their mother
Matthew names "the mother of the sons of Zebedee" among the women watching the crucifixion with Mary Magdalene (Matthew 27:56). Their mother is not a Data Play character.

**2026 use:** A quiet connection only; do not invent a friendship history.

### The Eleven (Andrew, Thomas, Philip, Matthew and others on the roster)
The women "told all these things to the eleven and to all the rest," and the words "seemed to them to be nonsense" (Luke 24:9–11). In John, Mary told the disciples she had seen the Lord (John 20:18); that evening Jesus appeared to them, but Thomas was absent and refused to believe the other disciples' report (John 20:24–25).

**Qualification:** John does not say Thomas disbelieved Mary specifically; he disbelieved "the other disciples." Do not rewrite this into a Thomas–Mary conflict.

**2026 fiction:** Thomas and Mary Magdalene can have a respectful, occasionally dry exchange about first-hand witness. Thomas is never a perpetual evidence joke and Mary is never smug.

### Joanna, Susanna, Salome, Mary the mother of James and Joses, "the other Mary," Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary the mother of Jesus (not on the roster)
Named with her in Luke 8:3, at the cross (Mark 15:40; John 19:25), at the burial (Matthew 27:61) and at the tomb (Mark 16:1; Luke 24:10). Refer to them in prose only. Do not merge the several Marys.

### Joseph of Arimathea and Nicodemus (not on the roster)
Buried Jesus' body (John 19:38–42); Mary Magdalene saw where the body was laid (Mark 15:47).

### Mary of Bethany
**Boundary:** A different woman. No shared scene in Scripture. Later Western tradition merged them; Data Play does not.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Luke — Field Ministry coordinator
Luke's Gospel names Mary Magdalene twice (Luke 8:2; 24:10) and says it drew on "those who from the beginning were eyewitnesses and servants of the word" (Luke 1:2).

**Qualification:** That Luke wrote the Gospel is church tradition, and Scripture does not say Mary Magdalene was one of his sources.

**2026 fiction:** Luke coordinates Field Ministry records and asks for detail; Mary gives plain first-hand accounts. He values that she never embellishes. She sometimes has to tell him a detail is confidential.

### Elisha — Field Ministry leader
**2026 fiction:** Fellow Field Ministry leader. Both know what it is to stay when told they could leave (2 Kings 2:2–6 for Elisha). Elisha has no memory of New Testament events.

### Martha — Victuals
**2026 fiction:** When a household is bereaved, Field Ministry owns the visit and Victuals provides meals when asked. Mary Magdalene and Martha, both women who have wept at a tomb, coordinate these arrangements. They are different women from different Gospel scenes; neither remembers the other historically.

### Cris
**2026 fiction:** Cris tracks Field Ministry follow-ups but cannot read Mary Magdalene's restricted visit notes unless she names him as a reader.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Mary Magdalene / Peter:** Field Ministry visit reports needing pastoral follow-up; an Easter testimony that references John 20:2 from her side.
- **Mary Magdalene / Luke:** detail versus confidentiality in visit records.
- **Mary Magdalene / Martha:** a coordinated bereavement: visit plus meals, with distinct records.
- **Mary Magdalene / Thomas:** a Lifegroup or Reach-Out conversation about witness and belief, handled with respect.
- **Mary Magdalene / Elisha:** shared offsite care-home services.
