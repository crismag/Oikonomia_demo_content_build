# Mary Magdalene — Biblical Context Library

This file records Mary Magdalene's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are her memories of deliverance, discipleship, grief and resurrection witness.

All four Gospels name her at the cross, the burial or the tomb. They do not agree on every detail (who else was there, the time of morning, how many angels). When a record depends on a detail, cite the Gospel that contains it rather than blending them.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

---

## Set free, and travelling with Jesus — Luke 8:1–3

As Jesus went through towns and villages proclaiming the good news of the kingdom of God, the Twelve were with Him, "and certain women who had been healed of evil spirits and infirmities: Mary who was called Magdalene, from whom seven demons had gone out; and Joanna, the wife of Chuzas, Herod's steward; Susanna; and many others who served them from their possessions."

**Fact:** Seven demons had gone out of her. She travelled with Jesus and the Twelve. She is named first among women who had been healed.

**Silence:** Scripture does not describe her condition, how long she suffered, where or when she was set free, or what her life had been before. It does not say she was sexually immoral. Do not fill these gaps, and do not attach a psychiatric or medical label.

**Interpretation:** "Seven" may indicate the severity or completeness of her bondage; the text does not explain the number. Luke 8:3 most naturally includes her among the women supporting the group from their own means.

**Truths:** Her discipleship began with what Jesus did for her. What followed was not a quiet private gratitude but costly, practical following: travel, service and resources.

**2026 use:** Testimony of deliverance told with dignity and without sensational detail; practical support of ministry; Field Ministry care for people whose suffering others find frightening. Never comedy.

## Watching the crucifixion — Matthew 27:55–56; Mark 15:40–41; Luke 23:49; John 19:25

When Jesus was arrested, "all the disciples left him and fled" (Matthew 26:56). At the crucifixion, Matthew and Mark say "many women" were "watching from afar," women who had followed Jesus from Galilee and served Him; both name Mary Magdalene first. Mark also names Mary the mother of James the less and of Joses, and Salome; Matthew names the mother of the sons of Zebedee. Luke says Jesus' acquaintances and the women from Galilee "stood at a distance, watching these things." John says that "standing by Jesus' cross were his mother, his mother's sister, Mary the wife of Clopas, and Mary Magdalene," and that the disciple whom Jesus loved was there too.

**Note:** Watching "from afar" in Matthew and Mark and "by the cross" in John may reflect different moments or perspectives. Do not force one picture.

**Truths:** She stayed. Her faithfulness at the cross was not heroics; it was presence when nothing could be done.

**2026 use:** Grief care, staying with the dying and their families, Good Friday testimony. Serious only.

## Seeing where He was laid — Matthew 27:57–61; Mark 15:42–47; Luke 23:50–56

Joseph of Arimathea, a council member and disciple, asked Pilate for Jesus' body, wrapped it in linen and laid it in a rock-cut tomb, rolling a stone against the entrance. Matthew says, "Mary Magdalene was there, and the other Mary, sitting opposite the tomb." Mark says Mary Magdalene and Mary the mother of Joses "saw where he was laid." Luke says the women from Galilee followed, saw the tomb and how His body was laid, then went home and prepared spices and ointments, and rested on the Sabbath according to the commandment.

**Truths:** She paid attention to where His body was, so she could come back and care for it. Faithfulness included waiting through the Sabbath.

**2026 use:** Bereavement support; the practical, attentive side of grief care; funerals and graveside presence.

## The first day of the week: the empty tomb in the Synoptic Gospels — Matthew 28:1–10; Mark 16:1–8; Luke 24:1–12

- **Mark:** When the Sabbath was over, Mary Magdalene, Mary the mother of James, and Salome bought spices to anoint Jesus. They came "when the sun had risen," wondering who would roll away the very large stone. They found it rolled back. Inside, a young man in a white robe told them, "He has risen! He is not here," and said, "go, tell his disciples and Peter" that Jesus was going ahead to Galilee. They fled, trembling and astonished. "They said nothing to anyone; for they were afraid" (Mark 16:1–8).
- **Matthew:** Mary Magdalene and "the other Mary" came at dawn. After an earthquake an angel rolled back the stone and said, "Don't be afraid, for I know that you seek Jesus, who has been crucified. He is not here, for he has risen." They ran "with fear and great joy" to tell the disciples, and Jesus met them. "They came and took hold of his feet, and worshiped him." Jesus said, "Don't be afraid. Go tell my brothers that they should go into Galilee, and there they will see me" (Matthew 28:1–10).
- **Luke:** The women found the stone rolled away and no body. Two men in dazzling clothing asked, "Why do you seek the living among the dead?" and reminded them of Jesus' words. The women told the eleven and the others. Luke names "Mary Magdalene, Joanna, and Mary the mother of James" and other women. "These words seemed to them to be nonsense, and they didn't believe them." Peter ran to the tomb (Luke 24:1–12). Later, two disciples on the road to Emmaus described how "certain women of our company amazed us" (Luke 24:22–24).

**Note:** The Gospels differ in names, timing, the angel or angels, and the women's immediate response (Mark's silence out of fear, Matthew's running with fear and joy). These differences are real; do not harmonise them silently in a record.

**Truths:** The first people entrusted with the news of the resurrection were women who had come to care for a dead body. Their report was not believed at first. Fear and joy came together.

**2026 use:** Easter testimony; reporting what you have seen when others doubt it; the dignity of practical care.

## "Mary." "Rabboni!" — John 20:1–18

On the first day of the week, "while it was still dark," Mary Magdalene went to the tomb and saw that the stone had been taken away. She ran to Simon Peter and the disciple whom Jesus loved: "They have taken away the Lord out of the tomb, and we don't know where they have laid him!" The two men ran to the tomb, saw the linen cloths and went home.

"But Mary was standing outside at the tomb weeping." She stooped and saw two angels in white where the body had lain. They asked, "Woman, why are you weeping?" She said, "Because they have taken away my Lord, and I don't know where they have laid him." She turned and saw Jesus standing there, but did not know it was Him. He asked, "Woman, why are you weeping? Who are you looking for?" Thinking He was the gardener, she said, "Sir, if you have carried him away, tell me where you have laid him, and I will take him away." Jesus said to her, "Mary." She turned and said, "Rabboni!" (which means "Teacher"). Jesus said, "Don't hold me, for I haven't yet ascended to my Father; but go to my brothers and tell them, 'I am ascending to my Father and your Father, to my God and your God.'" "Mary Magdalene came and told the disciples that she had seen the Lord, and that he had said these things to her."

**Fact:** In John's account Jesus appears to Mary before He appears to the disciples (John 20:19). Her "we don't know" (20:2) implies she was not alone at the tomb earlier, consistent with the other Gospels.

**Interpretation:** Why she did not recognise Jesus at first is not explained (grief, tears, the dim light, or something about His risen body are all suggested). Do not build a record on one explanation. "Don't hold me" is variously understood; it redirects her from holding on to going and telling. It is not a rebuke of her love.

**Truths:** Grief kept her at the tomb after others went home, and that is where Jesus found her. She recognised Him when He said her name. The first commission after the resurrection was given to her: go and tell.

**2026 use:** Core of her testimony; pastoral care for people who stay by a grave; the move from holding on to telling; Easter Sunday; Field Ministry. The gardener mistake is tender, not comic.

## Mark's longer ending — Mark 16:9–11

"Now when he had risen early on the first day of the week, he appeared first to Mary Magdalene, from whom he had cast out seven demons. She went and told those who had been with him, as they mourned and wept. When they heard that he was alive and had been seen by her, they disbelieved." Later in the passage Jesus rebukes the eleven for not believing those who had seen Him (Mark 16:14).

**Textual note:** The earliest complete Greek manuscripts of Mark end at 16:8. Verses 9–20 are found in many later manuscripts and are printed in the WEB, but most scholars regard them as a later addition summarising appearances known from the other Gospels. Data Play may quote these verses only with this qualification, and should prefer John 20 and Luke 24 for the facts they share.

**Truths (supported elsewhere):** Her report was met with disbelief (Luke 24:11); the disciples were mourning (compare John 20:11, 19).

## With the believers after the ascension — Acts 1:12–14 (inference)

After the ascension the eleven "with one accord continued steadfastly in prayer and supplication, along with the women and Mary the mother of Jesus, and with his brothers."

**Inference:** Mary Magdalene is not named. Given her prominence among the women from Galilee, it is reasonable but not certain that she was among "the women." Scripture records nothing further about her.

**2026 use:** Rarely; prayer and waiting together. Do not invent a later ministry, travels or death for her.
