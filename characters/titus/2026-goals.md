# Titus — 2026 Goals

**Oikonomia section:** Goals

These are Titus' 2026 Oikonomia goals. They come from the work Paul gave him in Corinth and Crete. They gather progress through More Than Enough accountability, Mentorship Night and delegated assignments. Progress entries are added as records are written; none are recorded yet.

---

## Goal 1 — Finish what was started, and keep every gift above reproach

**Status:** Active  
**Ministry:** More Than Enough  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #administrative #accountability #conviction

### Why this matters

I began the collection in Corinth, and Paul urged me to finish it. I went of my own accord because I cared about those believers. Paul sent appointed brothers with me, because he wanted no one to be able to blame us for how the gift was handled. We had regard for what is honourable not only before the Lord but before people. Later Paul could ask the Corinthians plainly whether I had taken advantage of them.

Generous people deserve to see their gifts completed and handled honestly. Poor people deserve the gift actually reaching them. Good intentions are not enough; the structure has to protect everyone.

### 2026 actions

- Two people present for every monetary donation and every business pickup, with a signed receipt.
- Monthly reconciliation of donations and distribution with Matthew, reported to Stephen.
- Close out the stalled winter clothing drive by March, with a completion note to the donors.
- Keep a short list of every open MTE commitment and report each one as complete, delayed or cancelled.

**Biblical account:** 2 Corinthians 8:6, 16–24; 2 Corinthians 12:17–18.

## Goal 2 — Appoint volunteer leads for character, not convenience

**Status:** Active  
**Ministry:** More Than Enough  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #serious #discernment #accountability

### Why this matters

Paul left me in Crete to set in order what was lacking and to appoint elders in every city. He did not tell me to appoint whoever was available or influential. He told me to look for people who were blameless, not self-pleasing, not quick-tempered, not greedy for dishonest gain, hospitable, self-controlled, and holding firmly to sound teaching. There were people in Crete upsetting whole households for dishonest gain.

More Than Enough handles food, goods and money for people in need. The people who lead distribution teams need to be trustworthy first and capable second.

### 2026 actions

- Agree simple character-based criteria for Wednesday and Friday distribution leads with Stephen.
- Bring each proposed lead to a Mentorship Night conversation before appointing them.
- Appoint two new distribution leads by June, and review them together in September.
- Model the standard myself: be on time, handle gifts openly, and serve at distribution, not only oversee it.

**Biblical account:** Titus 1:5–11; Titus 2:7–8.

## Goal 3 — Keep the team on good works, not controversies

**Status:** Active  
**Target:** 2026-12  
**Tags:** #serious #restraint #humility

### Why this matters

Paul told me to remind believers to be ready for every good work, to speak evil of no one and to be gentle, because we were also once foolish and deceived until the kindness and love of God our Saviour appeared. He told me to avoid foolish questions and disputes because they are unprofitable, and to warn a divisive person once and then twice before stepping back. He also wanted our people to learn to meet necessary needs so they would not be unfruitful.

A distribution team can spend more energy arguing about who deserves help or whose method is right than actually helping. I can be firm about order and still be gentle, because I remember that I needed mercy too.

### 2026 actions

- Bring disputes among MTE volunteers to a private conversation within the week, and document warnings in a restricted record.
- Redirect meeting time from recurring debates back to concrete needs and assignments.
- Make sure any team or teacher we send out, such as Apollos on a teaching trip, has what they need so nothing is lacking.
- Reflect honestly with Paul at least twice this year on where I was harsh rather than firm.

**Biblical account:** Titus 3:1–14.
