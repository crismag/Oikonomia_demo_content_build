# Titus — Biblical Context Library

This file records Titus' lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are his memories, assignments and the charges he received.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

**Source note:** Titus does not appear in Acts. Every account below comes from Paul's letters. The letter to Titus names Paul as author; scholars debate this. Data Play uses it as Paul's words and does not argue the question.

---

## A Greek in Jerusalem — Galatians 2:1–10

Fourteen years after an earlier visit, Paul went up to Jerusalem with Barnabas, "taking Titus also with me." Paul laid out privately before the respected leaders the gospel he preached among the Gentiles. "But not even Titus, who was with me, being a Greek, was compelled to be circumcised." Paul explains that false brothers had been secretly brought in to spy out their freedom in Christ, and "we gave no place in the way of subjection, not for an hour." James, Cephas and John, reputed to be pillars, gave Paul and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship and asked only that they remember the poor.

**Silence:** Titus' own words and feelings are not recorded. Scripture does not say he met Peter, James or John personally, only that he was with Paul on the visit. **Uncertainty:** Which visit in Acts this corresponds to is debated.

**Truths:** Titus' presence made the question concrete: a real Greek believer, accepted without becoming Jewish. Sometimes a person's simple presence is the test case for what a church believes. The request to "remember the poor" was attached to that same meeting.

**2026 use:** Serious reflection on belonging and identity, newcomers from other backgrounds (with Cornelius), MTE's roots in remembering the poor. Not comedy. Contrast with Timothy's circumcision (Acts 16:3) belongs in careful conversation, not banter.

## Waited for at Troas, welcomed in Macedonia — 2 Corinthians 2:1–13; 7:2–16

Paul's relationship with the Corinthian church had become painful. He had written to them "out of much affliction and anguish of heart … with many tears," and a person who had caused sorrow had been disciplined by the church (2:4–7). Paul then went to Troas to preach, and "a door was opened," yet "I had no relief for my spirit, because I didn't find Titus my brother," so he left for Macedonia. There, "our flesh had no relief, but we were afflicted on every side. Fightings were outside. Fear was inside. Nevertheless, he who comforts the lowly, God, comforted us by the coming of Titus." Titus brought news of the Corinthians' longing, mourning and zeal for Paul. Paul rejoiced even more "for the joy of Titus, because his spirit has been refreshed by you all," and wrote that Titus' affection for them grew as he remembered how they had received him "with fear and trembling." Paul's boasting about them to Titus had proved true.

**Inference:** Titus had been in Corinth dealing with the aftermath of the painful letter; whether he carried that letter is not stated. **Silence:** The identity of the offender and the details of the offence are not recorded. Do not invent them.

**Truths:** Titus was trusted with a fragile relationship. God comforted a distressed leader through a co-worker's arrival and an honest report. The one sent was himself refreshed by the people he had been sent to.

**2026 use:** Delegated reconciliation or follow-up assignments, restricted assignment reports, encouragement between co-workers, the value of an accurate report.

## Finishing the collection — 2 Corinthians 8:1–24

Paul described the Macedonian churches, who in "deep poverty" gave generously and begged to take part in the relief for the saints. "So we urged Titus, that as he had made a beginning before, so he would also complete in you this grace." Paul reminded the Corinthians that they had started a year earlier and should now complete what they had been willing to do, according to their means, for the sake of equality. "Thanks be to God, who puts the same earnest care for you into the heart of Titus. For he indeed accepted our exhortation, but being himself very earnest, he went out to you of his own accord." Paul sent with him a brother praised throughout the churches and appointed by them, and another proven brother, explaining: "We are avoiding this, that any man should blame us concerning this abundance which is administered by us. Having regard for honorable things, not only in the sight of the Lord, but also in the sight of men." And: "As for Titus, he is my partner and fellow worker for you."

**Silence:** The two brothers are not named. Identifying one as Luke is speculation.

**Truths:** Generosity needs follow-through. Integrity is protected by structure, not only by good intentions: more than one trusted person, appointed openly. Doing right before people matters as well as before God.

**2026 use:** More Than Enough stewardship and accountability, completing a donation drive that stalled, two-person handling of gifts, Titus' Goal 1.

## "Did Titus take any advantage of you?" — 2 Corinthians 12:14–18

Paul, preparing a third visit, insisted he sought the Corinthians themselves, not their possessions. Some may have suggested he had exploited them through others. He answered: "I exhorted Titus, and I sent the brother with him. Did Titus take any advantage of you? Didn't we walk in the same spirit? Didn't we walk in the same steps?"

**Truths:** Titus' conduct was so plainly honest that Paul could appeal to it as evidence. A delegate's integrity protects the sender's reputation.

**2026 use:** Financial accountability reports, reference for trustworthy volunteers.

## Left in Crete to set things in order — Titus 1:1–16

Paul wrote "to Titus, my true child according to a common faith." "I left you in Crete for this reason, that you would set in order the things that were lacking and appoint elders in every city, as I directed you." An elder must be blameless, "the husband of one wife," with believing children not accused of unruly behaviour; as God's steward, not self-pleasing, not quick-tempered, not given to wine, not violent, not greedy for dishonest gain, but hospitable, a lover of good, sober-minded, fair, holy and self-controlled, holding firmly to the trustworthy word so he can encourage with sound teaching and refute those who contradict it. There were many rebellious people, empty talkers and deceivers, "especially those of the circumcision," upsetting whole households for dishonest gain. Paul quoted one of the Cretans' own prophets, "Cretans are always liars, evil beasts, and idle gluttons," and told Titus to rebuke them sharply so they would be sound in the faith.

**Sensitivity:** The quotation is Paul citing a Cretan's saying in dealing with specific false teachers. It must not be used for ethnic humor or stereotypes about anyone. **Uncertainty:** How Titus came to be in Crete, and when, is not recorded in Acts.

**Truths:** Order and leader selection were Titus' job, not an afterthought. Character comes before gifting. Correction aims at soundness.

**2026 use:** Selecting MTE volunteer leads, Mentorship Night on leader standards, restricted records about a volunteer who is exploiting a ministry, Titus' Goal 2.

## Teaching every group, and being the example — Titus 2:1–15

Titus was to teach what fits sound doctrine to older men, older women (who were to teach what is good and train younger women), younger men and servants. "In all things show yourself an example of good works. In your teaching, show integrity, seriousness, incorruptibility, and soundness of speech that can't be condemned, that he who opposes you may be ashamed, having no evil thing to say about us." The grace of God has appeared, training believers to live self-controlled, upright and godly lives while waiting for Christ, who gave himself to purify a people "zealous for good works." "Say these things and exhort and reprove with all authority. Let no one despise you."

**Truths:** The leader who sets standards has to embody them. Grace is the reason for good works, not a rival to them.

**2026 use:** Titus as an example in MTE service; generational discipleship; parallel with Timothy's "Let no man despise your youth."

## Good works, not foolish controversies — Titus 3:1–11

Titus was to remind believers to submit to rulers, be ready for every good work, speak evil of no one, avoid quarrelling and show gentleness to everyone. "For we were also once foolish, disobedient, deceived … But when the kindness of God our Savior and his love toward mankind appeared, not by works of righteousness which we did ourselves, but according to his mercy, he saved us." Paul wanted Titus to insist on these things "so that those who have believed God may be careful to maintain good works." He was to "shun foolish questionings, genealogies, strife, and disputes about the law," and to "avoid a factious man after a first and second warning."

**Truths:** Mercy remembered produces gentleness. Some disputes are unprofitable. Divisiveness gets warnings and then a boundary.

**2026 use:** Handling a divisive volunteer, keeping MTE focused on practical good works, Titus' Goal 3.

## Travel arrangements, and "that nothing may be lacking" — Titus 3:12–15

"When I send Artemas to you, or Tychicus, be diligent to come to me to Nicopolis, for I have determined to winter there. Send Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their journey speedily, that nothing may be lacking for them. Let our people also learn to maintain good works to meet necessary needs, that they may not be unfruitful."

**Qualification:** This Apollos is very probably the Alexandrian teacher of Acts 18, but the text does not say so. **Silence:** Whether Titus went to Nicopolis is not recorded.

**Truths:** Supporting other workers' travel and needs is itself a good work. The church learns to meet necessary needs by doing it.

**2026 use:** Practical provision for outgoing teams (MTE and Victuals may supply; Facilities handles transport), Apollos relationship, handover when a replacement (Artemas or Tychicus) arrives.

## Gone to Dalmatia — 2 Timothy 4:10

In his last letter Paul told Timothy: "Demas left me, having loved this present world, and went to Thessalonica; Crescens to Galatia; and Titus to Dalmatia."

**Boundary:** Only Demas is said to have loved this present world. The text gives no reason for Titus' going and does not suggest desertion.

**2026 use:** Rarely. Titus as someone still being sent.
