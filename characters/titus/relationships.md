# Titus — Relationships

Biblical relationships come first. 2026 Data Play relationships are marked as fiction and never rewrite what Scripture records. Titus' biblical relationships are known only from Paul's letters, so they are few and short.

---

## Biblical relationships

### Paul — spiritual father and sender
Paul took Titus to Jerusalem (Galatians 2:1–3), sent him to Corinth and was comforted by his arrival and report (2 Corinthians 7:6–7, 13–15), urged him to complete the collection and called him "my partner and fellow worker" (8:6, 23), cited his integrity (12:18), left him in Crete with a hard assignment and called him "my true child according to a common faith" (Titus 1:4–5), and later noted he had gone to Dalmatia (2 Timothy 4:10).

**Boundary:** Paul's foundation is written separately. Describe Paul in Titus' files only from these biblical facts.

**2026 fiction:** Paul shows his trust by sending Titus into work that needs order. Titus reports to Paul for those assignments and serves day to day in More Than Enough under Stephen. Paul's impatience for news and Titus' careful, complete reports are a recurring, affectionate pattern.

### Barnabas
Went to Jerusalem with Paul and Titus (Galatians 2:1).

**Silence:** No interaction between Barnabas and Titus is recorded.

**2026 fiction:** Barnabas, as Mentorship head, values Titus' clarity on leader standards and sometimes softens how Titus delivers it.

### The Corinthian believers
Titus was received by them "with fear and trembling," was refreshed by them, and his affection for them grew (2 Corinthians 7:13–15). They are not individual roster characters.

### Apollos
Paul asked Titus to send Zenas the lawyer and Apollos on their journey "that nothing may be lacking for them" (Titus 3:13).

**Qualification:** Very probably the Alexandrian Apollos of Acts 18, but not explicit.

**2026 fiction:** Titus makes sure Apollos has what he needs when he travels to teach elsewhere, and checks that Apollos' teaching trips have clear handover.

### Tychicus
Paul planned to send Artemas or Tychicus to Titus in Crete, after which Titus was to come to Nicopolis (Titus 3:12).

**2026 fiction:** Titus and Tychicus (Production head) both carry messages and handovers well. Titus hands a completed assignment to Tychicus when Tychicus relieves him on a cross-ministry project.

### Timothy
Both are Paul's "true child" (1 Timothy 1:2; Titus 1:4); both were left to set a church in order; both were told not to let anyone despise them (1 Timothy 4:12; Titus 2:15).

**Silence:** Scripture never places them together.

**2026 fiction:** Peers at Mentorship Night who compare delegated assignments. Titus is quicker to put a structure in place; Timothy is quicker to notice how people are doing.

### Peter, James (the Lord's brother) and John
Titus was with Paul on the Jerusalem visit where the pillars gave Paul and Barnabas the right hand of fellowship (Galatians 2:1–9).

**Silence:** Scripture does not say Titus met them. James the Lord's brother is not on the roster; refer to him in prose only.

## Cross-era Data Play relationships (2026 fiction)

### Stephen — More Than Enough head
Stephen was chosen among the Seven to oversee the daily distribution to widows (Acts 6:1–6); Titus completed a collection for poor believers. In 2026 Stephen heads MTE and owns the weekly report; Titus leads distribution teams and owns stewardship integrity. They agree on the goal and occasionally differ on how much process is enough.

### Matthew — More Than Enough coordinator
Matthew keeps records; Titus insists on a second signature. Their monthly reconciliation is a natural home for administrative humor that never mocks recipients.

### Philip the Evangelist, Cornelius, Simon the Zealot and Epaphroditus
Fellow More Than Enough team members in 2026. Cornelius, a Gentile welcomed by Peter, and Titus, a Greek not compelled to be circumcised, can share serious reflection on belonging.

### Moses
Moses appointed capable, trustworthy leaders over groups on Jethro's advice (Exodus 18). In 2026 the two can compare notes on appointing leaders by character. Neither has any memory of the other's history; Moses never gains a memory of Crete, and Titus was not at Sinai.

### Nehemiah — Facilities head
Both put disordered situations back in order. Nehemiah provides transport and site logistics when MTE runs large pickups.

### Cris
Cris receives MTE attention items that need cross-ministry coordination. Cris cannot see restricted assignment reports unless Titus or Paul names Cris.

## Relationship-driven record opportunities

- **Paul / Titus:** assignment brief and completion report; Paul waiting for Titus' report.
- **Titus / Stephen / Matthew:** donation accountability, stalled drive completed, monthly reconciliation.
- **Titus / Barnabas:** Mentorship Night on selecting volunteer leads by character.
- **Titus / Apollos:** provision and handover for a teaching trip.
- **Titus / Timothy:** comparing notes on setting things in order.
- **Titus / Cornelius:** belonging and identity reflection.
