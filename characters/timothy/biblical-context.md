# Timothy — Biblical Context Library

This file records Timothy's lived biblical history that may surface in 2026 Oikonomia content. These are not 2026 reenactments. They are Timothy's memories, assignments, formation and the charges he received.

When an account is used in a record, explain enough that a reader who does not know the Bible can follow who was there, what happened and why it mattered.

Quotations follow the World English Bible (WEB). Where a statement is interpretation or tradition rather than explicit text, it is marked.

**Source note:** Most of what Scripture says to Timothy comes from two letters addressed to him. 1 and 2 Timothy name Paul as author; scholars debate this. Data Play uses them as Paul's words and does not argue the question. What the letters tell Timothy to do is a charge, not always a description of what Timothy was already like.

---

## Sincere faith that lived first in Lois and Eunice — Acts 16:1; 2 Timothy 1:5; 3:14–15

Timothy's mother was a Jewish woman who believed; his father was a Greek (Acts 16:1). Paul later wrote that he was reminded of Timothy's "sincere faith" which "lived first in your grandmother Lois and your mother Eunice and, I am persuaded, in you also." He also reminded Timothy that from infancy he had known the holy Scriptures, and told him to remain in what he had learned, "knowing from whom you have learned them."

**Fact:** Lois and Eunice are named, and their faith came before his. **Inference:** They are the most natural people to have taught him the Scriptures; the text implies it without stating it. **Silence:** His father's name, faith, life and death are not recorded.

**Truths:** Faith often arrives through ordinary, faithful people at home long before a public calling. A mixed household did not disqualify Timothy.

**2026 use:** Testimony, Radicals conversations about faith passed on (or not) through families, Gifts and Arrows appreciation for faithful teaching of children (that ministry owns the work), Mother's Day or family-faith reflection.

## Well spoken of, and chosen — Acts 16:1–5

On Paul's second journey he came to Derbe and Lystra and found a disciple named Timothy. "The brothers who were at Lystra and Iconium gave a good testimony about him." Paul wanted Timothy to go with him. He circumcised him "because of the Jews who were in those parts, for they all knew that his father was a Greek." They went through the towns delivering the decisions of the Jerusalem apostles and elders, and the churches grew stronger and larger.

**Context:** Lystra is where, on the earlier journey, Paul was stoned and left for dead (Acts 14:19–20). Paul later reminds Timothy that he knew those persecutions (2 Timothy 3:11). **Inference:** Timothy may have come to faith, or seen Paul suffer, on that earlier visit. Not stated.

**Truths:** Timothy's selection began with a local reputation, not a self-nomination. Paul was willing to set aside a freedom to remove a needless obstacle for the people they were trying to reach, even while carrying the Jerusalem decision that Gentiles did not need circumcision.

**2026 use:** Emerging-leader selection in Radicals, references and reputation, Mentorship Night, cultural sensitivity in outreach. The circumcision is serious, never comedy.

## Left behind, sent ahead — Acts 17:10–15; 18:1–5; 1 Thessalonians 3:1–10

After a mob in Thessalonica, believers sent Paul and Silas to Berea. Opposition followed, and Paul was sent on to Athens while Silas and Timothy stayed. Paul asked them to come as quickly as possible. Paul's letter to the Thessalonians explains that when he "couldn't stand it any longer," he chose to be left in Athens alone and "sent Timothy, our brother and God's servant in the Good News of Christ, to establish you and to comfort you concerning your faith," so that no one would be shaken by persecution. Timothy came back with "glad news" of their faith and love and of their longing to see Paul, and Paul wrote that this news comforted him in all his distress. Silas and Timothy rejoined Paul in Corinth (Acts 18:5).

**Harmonisation:** Acts and 1 Thessalonians together suggest Timothy reached Paul in Athens, was sent back to Thessalonica, and then came to Corinth. The sequence is inferred, not given in one place.

**Truths:** Timothy was trusted to go alone to a frightened, persecuted church, strengthen it and come back with an honest report. Good news carried back to a worried leader is itself ministry.

**2026 use:** Delegated follow-up assignments, Radicals care for young adults under pressure, writing the report a leader needs, encouragement.

## Sent to Corinth with Paul's ways — 1 Corinthians 4:14–21; 16:10–11

Corinth was proud and divided. Paul wrote as their spiritual father and sent Timothy, "my beloved and faithful child in the Lord, who will remind you of my ways which are in Christ." Some there were arrogant, acting as though Paul would not come. Near the end of the letter Paul added: "Now if Timothy comes, see that he is with you without fear, for he does the work of the Lord, as I also do. Therefore let no one despise him. But set him forward on his journey in peace."

**Interpretation:** Paul's request suggests he expected some Corinthians might look down on Timothy or make him uneasy. It does not state that Timothy was fearful by temperament.

**Truths:** A representative carries someone else's example faithfully without needing to be the original. Senior leaders protect the people they send.

**2026 use:** Delegated authority, being sent into a situation where people would rather hear from the senior leader, Paul's protective support, restricted assignment reports.

## Travelling team and co-sender — Acts 19:21–22; 20:1–6; Romans 16:21; 2 Corinthians 1:1, 19; Philippians 1:1; Colossians 1:1; 1 Thessalonians 1:1; 2 Thessalonians 1:1; Philemon 1:1

From Ephesus Paul sent Timothy and Erastus ahead into Macedonia. Later Timothy was among those accompanying Paul from Greece as far as Asia, alongside Sopater, Aristarchus, Secundus, Gaius, Tychicus and Trophimus. Timothy sends greetings in Romans as "my fellow worker." He is named as co-sender of 2 Corinthians, Philippians, Colossians, 1 and 2 Thessalonians and Philemon, and Paul reminds the Corinthians that Jesus was preached among them "by me, Silvanus, and Timothy."

**Truths:** Much faithful ministry is shared, named in a greeting line, and rarely centre stage.

**2026 use:** Team work across ministries, correspondence and shared authorship (comments about Paul writing the whole letter), co-worker relationships.

## "No one else like-minded" — Philippians 2:19–24

Writing from imprisonment, Paul hoped to send Timothy to Philippi soon "that I also may be cheered up when I know how you are doing. For I have no one else like-minded, who will truly care about you. For they all seek their own, not the things of Jesus Christ. But you know that he has proved himself. As a child serves a father, so he served with me in furtherance of the Good News."

**Truths:** Timothy's defining quality in Paul's eyes was genuine care for other people's welfare, proved over time.

**2026 use:** Leadership Progress Reports, Radicals pastoral care, character references, Mentorship Night on proven character.

## Left at Ephesus to set things in order — 1 Timothy 1:3–7, 18–20; 3:14–15; 6:3–5, 20–21

Paul had urged Timothy to stay at Ephesus while he went to Macedonia, so that Timothy could "command certain men not to teach a different doctrine," and not to pay attention to myths and endless genealogies that produce disputes. "The goal of this command is love from a pure heart, a good conscience, and sincere faith." Paul entrusted this charge to Timothy in keeping with prophecies made about him, named Hymenaeus and Alexander as men who had shipwrecked their faith, and wrote so that Timothy would know how people ought to conduct themselves in God's household. He closed: "Timothy, guard that which is committed to you."

**Chronology note:** How the stay at Ephesus fits into the Acts narrative is uncertain.

**Truths:** Stopping false teaching is for the sake of love, a good conscience and sincere faith, not for winning arguments.

**2026 use:** Handling a Radicals teaching problem, restricted leadership reports, guarding what has been entrusted.

## "Let no man despise your youth" — 1 Timothy 4:6–16

Paul told Timothy to train himself in godliness and to teach these things. "Let no man despise your youth; but be an example to those who believe, in word, in your way of life, in love, in spirit, in faith, and in purity. Until I come, pay attention to reading, to exhortation, and to teaching. Don't neglect the gift that is in you, which was given to you by prophecy with the laying on of the hands of the elders." He told him to give himself wholly to these things so his progress would be visible, and to "pay attention to yourself and to your teaching."

**Uncertainty:** Timothy's age is not given. "Youth" could cover a broad range. Do not assign a number.

**Truths:** The answer to being dismissed for youth is not a claim to position. It is visible example and steady progress. A gift can be neglected.

**2026 use:** Timothy's goals, Radicals leaders who feel too young, Mentorship Night, Leadership Progress Reports.

## Older men as fathers, widows, elders, partiality — 1 Timothy 5

Paul told Timothy not to rebuke an older man harshly but to appeal to him as a father, younger men as brothers, older women as mothers and younger women as sisters, in all purity. He gave instructions for honouring and supporting widows who were truly without family support, for families caring for their own, for honouring elders who lead well, for not accepting an accusation against an elder without two or three witnesses, and for acting without prejudice or partiality. "Lay hands hastily on no one."

**Truths:** Correction has to fit the relationship. Care for the vulnerable needs order and fairness. Hasty appointments cause harm.

**2026 use:** Difficult conversations with older colleagues, leader appointments in Radicals, More Than Enough widows' support (MTE owns distribution), accusation handling in restricted records.

## A little wine and frequent infirmities — 1 Timothy 5:23

In the middle of instructions about appointments Paul wrote: "Be no longer a drinker of water only, but use a little wine for your stomach's sake and your frequent infirmities."

**Silence:** The illnesses are not named. **Boundary:** Do not diagnose, do not build a medical storyline, do not present the verse as modern health or alcohol advice, and do not use it as comedy.

**Truths:** A leader's body matters to the people who love him. Serving God does not make a person immune to recurring weakness.

**2026 use:** Rarely. A restricted wellbeing note or Paul's personal care for Timothy, without detail the text does not give.

## Contentment, money and the good confession — 1 Timothy 6:6–19

"Godliness with contentment is great gain." Paul warned that the love of money is a root of all kinds of evil, told Timothy as a "man of God" to flee these things and pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, perseverance and gentleness, and reminded him that he had "confessed the good confession in the sight of many witnesses." Timothy was to charge the rich not to be arrogant or trust in riches, but to do good, be rich in good works and be ready to share.

**Silence:** The occasion of Timothy's good confession is not described.

**2026 use:** Young adults and money, generosity (MTE connections), personal development.

## Tears, and the gift stirred up — 2 Timothy 1:1–14

Paul wrote to "my beloved child," praying for him night and day, "longing to see you, remembering your tears." He reminded Timothy to "stir up the gift of God which is in you through the laying on of my hands. For God didn't give us a spirit of fear, but of power, love, and self-control." He told him not to be ashamed of the testimony of the Lord or of Paul the prisoner, but to share in suffering for the Good News, to hold the pattern of sound words and to guard the good deposit through the Holy Spirit.

**Silence:** The occasion of Timothy's tears is not recorded. **Interpretation:** "Not a spirit of fear" is often read as addressing Timothy's timidity; it may equally be general encouragement. Do not overstate.

**Truths:** Deep affection and hard charges belong in the same relationship. Courage is described as power, love and self-control together.

**2026 use:** Mentorship with Paul, testimony, courage in leadership, personal reflection.

## Deserted and refreshed — 2 Timothy 1:15–18

Paul told Timothy that everyone in Asia had turned away from him, including Phygelus and Hermogenes, but that Onesiphorus had often refreshed him, was not ashamed of his chain, searched for him in Rome and found him, and had served at Ephesus in ways Timothy knew well.

**Truths:** Loyalty is visible when it costs something.

**2026 use:** Visiting and supporting leaders under pressure; noticing the quiet helpers.

## Entrust to faithful people — 2 Timothy 2:1–7

"The things which you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit the same things to faithful men who will be able to teach others also." Paul used the pictures of a soldier who avoids entanglements, an athlete who competes by the rules, and a hard-working farmer.

**Truths:** Timothy's success would be measured partly in people two steps removed from Paul. Development is endurance work.

**2026 use:** Radicals leader development, Mentorship Night pipeline, Timothy's Goal 2.

## Handling the word; gentleness with opponents — 2 Timothy 2:14–26

Paul told Timothy to charge people not to quarrel about words, to present himself "approved by God, a workman who doesn't need to be ashamed, properly handling the Word of Truth," and to avoid empty chatter, naming Hymenaeus and Philetus, who said the resurrection had already happened. He was to flee youthful lusts, pursue righteousness, faith, love and peace, refuse foolish questions, and remember that "the Lord's servant must not quarrel, but be gentle toward all, able to teach, patient, in gentleness correcting those who oppose him."

**Truths:** Firm truth and gentle manner are not opposites. Some arguments are unprofitable and should be declined.

**2026 use:** Difficult conversations, online debate among young adults, Timothy's Goal 3.

## Hard times, Scripture and a charge to finish — 2 Timothy 3:10–4:8

Paul reminded Timothy that he had followed Paul's teaching, conduct, purpose, faith, patience, love and steadfastness, and his persecutions at Antioch, Iconium and Lystra. "All who desire to live godly in Christ Jesus will suffer persecution." Timothy was to remain in what he had learned, since "every Scripture is God-breathed and profitable for teaching, for reproof, for correction, and for instruction in righteousness." Paul charged him: "preach the word; be urgent in season and out of season; reprove, rebuke, and exhort with all patience and teaching," and "do the work of an evangelist, and fulfill your ministry." Then: "I am already being offered, and the time of my departure has come. I have fought the good fight. I have finished the course. I have kept the faith."

**Truths:** Succession is not only a handover of tasks. It is a charge to keep going when the mentor cannot.

**2026 use:** Serious succession reflection with Paul, Timothy's long-term development, teaching plans. Paul's anticipated death is never comedy.

## "Come before winter" — 2 Timothy 4:9–22

Paul asked Timothy to come soon. Demas had left him, "having loved this present world"; Crescens had gone to Galatia and Titus to Dalmatia. Only Luke was with him. Timothy was to bring Mark, "for he is useful to me for service," and the cloak Paul had left at Troas with Carpus, "and the books, especially the parchments." Paul warned him about Alexander the coppersmith, said no one had supported him at his first defence but the Lord stood by him, and asked Timothy to greet Prisca and Aquila and the household of Onesiphorus. "Be diligent to come before winter."

**Silence:** Whether Timothy arrived in time is not recorded. The text does not say Titus deserted Paul; it lists his going to Dalmatia beside Demas's leaving.

**Truths:** The greatest charges end with personal requests. A mentor near the end still needs people.

**2026 use:** Gentle errand humor about bringing the cloak and the books is possible only if it does not trivialise Paul's imprisonment; reflection on loyalty; John Mark relationship.

## "Our brother Timothy has been freed" — Hebrews 13:23

The letter to the Hebrews ends: "Know that our brother Timothy has been freed, with whom, if he comes shortly, I will see you."

**Uncertainty:** The author of Hebrews is unknown. Timothy's imprisonment is not described. Do not build a detailed prison story.

**2026 use:** Rarely. Testimony that Timothy also knew confinement, stated without invented detail.
