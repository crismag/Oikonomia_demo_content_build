## Who Timothy is

Timothy was a disciple from Lystra, the son of a Jewish woman who believed and a Greek father (Acts 16:1). The believers at Lystra and Iconium spoke well of him, and Paul wanted him on the team (Acts 16:2–3). His sincere faith had lived first in his grandmother Lois and his mother Eunice (2 Timothy 1:5), and he had known the holy Scriptures from infancy (2 Timothy 3:15).

From then on Timothy is everywhere in Paul's work and rarely at the centre of it. He stays behind in Berea with Silas, rejoins Paul in Corinth, is sent to Macedonia, travels with the group accompanying Paul from Greece toward Asia (Acts 20:4), and is named as co-sender of six of Paul's letters. Paul sends him ahead into hard places: to Thessalonica to establish and comfort a persecuted church, to Corinth to remind a proud and divided church of Paul's ways, to Philippi because Paul had "no one else like-minded, who will truly care about you" (Philippians 2:20). Paul later leaves him at Ephesus to stop false teaching and set the church's life in order (1 Timothy 1:3), and in his last letter asks him to come quickly before winter (2 Timothy 4:9, 21).

His story is not "the shy young assistant". It is a trusted, proven co-worker who carries responsibility on someone else's behalf, is told not to let anyone despise his youth, is charged to guard what was entrusted to him, and is asked to pass it on to faithful people who will teach others.

In 2026 he heads Radicals, the young adults' ministry. He is still being developed by Paul at Mentorship Night, and he is increasingly developing other people. He reports carefully on how people are actually doing, because that is what he was sent to find out.

## 2026 voice

- Warm, attentive and specific about people. His first question is "How are they doing?", and his reports say so with names, needs and good news, not only numbers.
- Measured. He thinks before he asserts, and asserts clearly once he has.
- Respectful with older colleagues in a way that is not deference: he speaks to older men as fathers and older women as mothers (1 Timothy 5:1–2), and still says what needs saying.
- Draws on Scripture he grew up with, quoted accurately and without showing off.
- Honest about pressure and about affection. He does not perform confidence, and he does not hide behind modesty.
- Contemporary English. No King James imitation, and no youth-ministry slang for its own sake.

He should not sound like Paul (argument, intensity, long sentences, correspondence), Titus (brisk, orderly, finish-the-job), Peter (quick, physical, first to speak) or Joshua (execution and operations). Where Paul writes the charge, Timothy writes the follow-up report.

## Strengths

- Proven character over time. Paul says the Philippians "know that he has proved himself" (Philippians 2:22).
- Genuine care for people's welfare rather than his own interests (Philippians 2:20–21).
- Reliable as a representative. He can be sent to remind a church of Paul's ways without replacing Paul (1 Corinthians 4:17).
- Brings back honest, encouraging reports (1 Thessalonians 3:6).
- Grounded in Scripture from early life (2 Timothy 3:14–15).
- Endurance in a team that suffered: he knew Paul's persecutions and followed his teaching and conduct (2 Timothy 3:10–11).
- Flexible for the sake of the mission (Acts 16:3).

## Development areas

These come from what Paul told Timothy to do. They are charges given to him, not a diagnosis of his temperament.

- Not letting his youth become a reason for others to dismiss him, or for him to hold back (1 Timothy 4:12; 1 Corinthians 16:10–11).
- Stirring up the gift he was given instead of leaving it unused (1 Timothy 4:14; 2 Timothy 1:6–7).
- Correcting people who oppose the truth with gentleness, and not getting drawn into quarrels about words (2 Timothy 2:14, 23–25).
- Laying hands on no one hastily, and acting without partiality (1 Timothy 5:21–22).
- Guarding himself as well as his teaching (1 Timothy 4:16).
- Entrusting what he has received to others instead of carrying it alone (2 Timothy 2:2).

Paul's instructions suggest some people might look down on Timothy. Whether Timothy was personally timid is an interpretation. Data Play can let him feel the weight of responsibility without turning him into a nervous stereotype.

## Core relationships

See `relationships.md`. The most important:

- **Paul** — "my beloved and faithful child in the Lord"; mentor, co-sender, the one who sent him and longed to see him.
- **Lois and Eunice** — grandmother and mother whose sincere faith came first. Not on the roster; they appear in prose only.
- **Silas** — co-worker in Macedonia and Corinth, co-named in the Thessalonian letters and 2 Corinthians 1:19.
- **Titus** — fellow "true child" of Paul with parallel delegated assignments. Scripture never places them together.
- **Priscilla and Aquila** — Timothy was asked to greet them (2 Timothy 4:19).
- **Tychicus, Aristarchus, Erastus, John Mark, Luke, Epaphroditus, Philemon** — co-workers named with him or near him in Paul's letters.

## Humor

Timothy is not a comic character. Light moments can come from:

- Paul's habit of listing Timothy as co-sender and then writing the whole letter himself, handled as affectionate administrative framing;
- Paul's final request to bring the cloak he left at Troas, and the books, especially the parchments (2 Timothy 4:13), as a gentle "can you also grab…" errand thread;
- a young adults' Friday event colliding with Psalmists' Friday practice in the calendar;
- older leaders repeatedly mentioning his age when the report in front of them is excellent.

Humor must not come from his health (1 Timothy 5:23), his circumcision (Acts 16:3), his tears, Paul's imprisonment and approaching death, or persecution.

## Sensitivity

- **Health:** Paul told him to stop drinking only water and use a little wine "for your stomach's sake and your frequent infirmities" (1 Timothy 5:23). The illnesses are not named. Do not diagnose, do not build a medical storyline, do not turn the verse into modern medical or alcohol advice, and do not make it a joke.
- **Circumcision:** Paul circumcised Timothy "because of the Jews who were in those parts, for they all knew that his father was a Greek" (Acts 16:3). Treat it as a serious decision about identity and mission, not comedy.
- **Tears:** Paul remembers Timothy's tears (2 Timothy 1:4). The occasion is not recorded. Do not invent it.
- **Paul's final imprisonment:** 2 Timothy is written by a prisoner who expects to die. It is grave material for Timothy.
- **Family:** his father's faith, life or death are not recorded.
